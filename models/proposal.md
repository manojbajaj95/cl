# Research proposal: protect what the model can do, not what it used to say

**Lead: verifier-measured skill retention across continual updates.** Preserve the closed-loop probability of solving old tasks, not the identities or probabilities of all their tokens. Test whether this buys plasticity at a fixed forgetting budget, then determine whether the protection can be audited at rollout cost low enough to use in long streams. The CPU pilot tests the first premise, not the full method or publication novelty.

**Scope relative to the [shared scan](../docs/frontier-scan.md#ranked-research-bets).** This project audits the effects of candidate language-model updates on skill execution. It does not diagnose which mechanism changed in a world model, retract learned dependent writes, or choose whether to reset an optimizer or representation. It uses the [shared playbook](../docs/research-process.md) for falsification, stream splits, and compute accounting.

## Why this might be a frontier result

Let $V_j(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ be a fixed verifier for old skill $j$, and let $p_{j,g}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim D_{j,g},,y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[V_j(x,y)]$ for prompt group $g$. Use the same bounded rollout policy and stopping rule throughout. For each prompt, the chain rule for KL after partitioning complete trajectories by the verifier gives:

$$
D_{KL}(P\Vert Q) =
D_{KL}(P(V)\Vert Q(V)) +
\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}}P(V=v)D_{KL}(P(Y|V=v)\Vert Q(Y|V=v)).
$$

Full trajectory KL on old prompts bounds verification drift, but can penalize harmless substitutions arbitrarily strongly; KL on new prompts alone need not bound old-prompt behavior. Conversely, two policies can have identical correctness-conditioned distributions and arbitrarily different success probabilities. These are distinctions among objectives, not claims that any one dominates in practice: multiple valid strategies may itself be a capability worth verifying.

**Hypothesis.** Across controlled families with many valid solutions and sparse consequential decisions, constraining regression in verified old behavior improves the acquisition–retention frontier versus equally resourced KL and verifier replay. At equal forgetting and sample budgets, a sequential audit of candidate updates will reduce missed regressions versus a rolling token-KL gate. The advantage should narrow on tasks where only one trajectory is valid or where it is cheap to replay essentially all old data.

**Pilot decision.** Prioritize the audit, not a new training loss: the event guard recovered severe forgetting but did not beat canonical replay on the shared qualified seeds. With a known prefix partition in the separate CPU audit, sampling the unresolved half reached about the same decision rate with one quarter of the verifier calls; whether useful cheap certificates exist on real workloads is the next falsifiable test. See [results](pilot/README.md).

**Closest work.** [CoKL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01743) anchors the second term within correct outputs without directly preserving the first; [OPR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29495) replays successful generations; [MOPD replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02746) distills toward the previous student. Reward rehearsal already maximizes verified old success, and [GEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840) and [constrained policy optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10528) already express behavioral constraints. None of those premises is novel. [FRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08813) buys upstream robustness, while [TTT settlement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076) gates persistent prediction updates on independent text. [Semantic probabilistic control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01954) estimates verifier satisfaction for decoding control. The proposed contribution is the combination of **persistent, group-level acquisition-reference retention contracts** and **variance-reduced sequence-event auditing** during training: retain an interpretable budget over many writes without replaying canonical trajectories, an ensemble of teachers, or assuming that token drift predicts regression.

[Retention-centric developmental safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03955) already constrains measured old-task capability in image classification; [Provably Safe Model Updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01899) certifies updates through invariant parameter domains. [Declared Change Envelopes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04192) certifies and composes bounds on **decision changes outside a declared region**, not lower bounds on generated-solution mass that allow different successful outputs. [Probabilistic Model Checking of Autoregressive Neural Sequence Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00838) obtains sound per-input behavior bounds by refining an under-approximation; compare its refinement-only approach with certified-prefix boundaries plus residual sampling under repeat-update cost. If it already supplies an equally cheap stream-level audit, there is no method claim. These are targeted primary-paper checks, not exhaustive novelty clearance; log and independently review a dedicated forward/backward citation search before scale.

## Method to test

1. **Record contracts at acquisition.** Keep a small budget of old prompt families, verifier/version hashes, and uncertainty intervals for acquisition-time group success; evaluate the frozen initial model similarly for inherited skills. Keep a separate, bounded optimization-anchor pool. Absent verifiers are out of scope, not proof of retention. No permanent model snapshot per task.
2. **Propose and shape updates.** Use SFT, OPSD, or verifiable RL for the new domain. On optimization anchors, estimate closed-loop success and penalize regression beyond its per-group tolerance (smooth max or dual weights). First compare exact mean and worst-group probability objectives where both are tractable; use verifier-reward replay as the deliberately strong control. Any approximate gradient is a proposal tool, not the statistical audit.
3. **Audit before committing.** Use independent draws for the candidate. For short, verifier-certified prefix trees, sum probabilities of disjoint accepted leaves exactly. For longer generations, split off a finite, disjoint set of prefixes on which acceptance/rejection is known. If exact accepted mass is $a$ and unresolved mass is $r$, then $p=a+r\Pr[V=1\mid\text{unresolved}]$. Sample the residual by choosing a frontier prefix in proportion to its probability and continuing autoregressively; compare to a plain rollout estimator. Candidate audit draws must be inaccessible to training and fresh after adaptive proposals, with multiplicity control across groups and candidate checks (e.g., preregistered alpha spending over looks with bounded confidence intervals). Accept only if the candidate's simultaneous lower confidence bounds clear the stored upper acquisition bounds minus the allowed drops; also require new-task gain. Tighten acquisition estimates upfront, since overly wide intervals can halt adaptation. A rolling previous-checkpoint reference is an ablation that can silently accumulate small losses; any guarantees concern the audited prompt populations and verifier, not the whole model.
4. **Fail safely and measure it.** If the check is inconclusive or fails, defer, retry a shorter update, or retain the old checkpoint. Count all rejected proposals and audit compute. Test whether the decomposition actually removes enough unresolved mass to reduce verifier cost; do not call a guessed verifier certificate exact. General transformer prefix histories cannot be merged merely because they share an automaton state.

## Best way to pursue it

| Gate | Work | Go / no-go |
|---|---|---|
| **CPU: isolate the effect** | Run the 10K–20K-parameter [autoregressive pilot](pilot/README.md) in under one CPU-hour. Enumerate two legal paths per prompt exactly, start all methods from the same neural policy, use the same frozen hinted teacher and new-task data, and separate adaptation anchors, development selection, and test prompts. Re-run on more seeds and cross the number of valid paths, prefix horizon, group balance, and teacher quality before treating any edge as robust. | Establish reproducible forgetting under unguarded OPSD and recovery at comparable acquisition. If a tuned token-KL or verified replay control explains the recovery, carry forward only the estimator/certification question, not a claim of a superior training objective. |
| **GPU screening: test the mechanism** | Profile one baseline and one candidate on **1× 24 GB GPU** for 0.5B LoRA, then one **80 GB A100/H100** if parallel rollouts or 1.5B full updates need it. Provision 32 CPU cores, 128 GB RAM, and 0.5 TB scratch for verifier output and checkpoints. Use synthetic bounded-output domains plus verifier-backed instruction following, executable coding tests in a sandbox, and answer-checked math. Run four interleaved stages, at least five paired held-out streams, and 50M–150M combined processed/rollout tokens; plan **50–150 GPU-hours**, revising after measured throughput. | Preregister a primary endpoint of at least 2× fewer verifier calls without higher total time at matched regression-detection error and at most 1 pp loss of new-task accuracy. Rule out a cheap refinement-only explanation. Test a scripted verifier with almost no certifiable prefixes as a negative control. |
| **GPU replication: make the paper case** | Request **4–8× 80 GB A100/H100 with NVLink**, 64–128 CPU cores, 256–512 GB RAM and 1–2 TB fast storage if screening works. Run 7–8B LoRA with a co-located or separately sharded rollout service; include a second model family and multiple distinguishable stream orders. Target 6–10 stages, 5 seeds/orders, TRACE plus independent verifier-backed slices and evolving-fact paraphrase evaluation. Budget **400–1,200 GPU-hours**, contingent on base rollout throughput and all audit tokens. | Require cross-family replication, paired uncertainty intervals, and no consistent loss of mean final accuracy. Reject a mechanism claim if it does not change under the predicted manipulation of valid-path multiplicity or unresolved mass. No GPU reservation is needed to run the existing CPU pilot. |

**Controls and ablations.** Compare sequential SFT, standalone OPSD/SDFT, GRPO where rewards exist, SFT + random replay, OPR, verifier-reward replay with the same verifiers, token KL on old student rollouts, CoKL, and a donor-grafting control when checkpoints are available. Include frozen and offline-joint references. Ablate success marginal vs. conditional vs. full-policy matching; mean vs. worst-group contracts; acquisition vs. rolling snapshots; audit vs. no audit; residual vs. plain sampling; fresh vs. reused guard prompts; and exogenous vs. self-generated data for optional test-time persistence. Match stored bytes, verifier access, total rollout/backprop tokens, and tuning runs. Preregister paired comparisons and verifier versions before the final held-out run.

**Metrics and risks.** Report average accuracy, average BWT, max-to-final forgetting, worst-group BWT and drift across repeated writes, forward transfer, matched-acquisition frontiers, commit rate, false accepts/rejects, verifier calls, latency, and calibration of confidence bounds. Bootstrap paired task/order/seed blocks, not individual correlated completions. Check contamination, duplicate prompts, changed decoding, learned verifier loopholes, degraded alternative strategies, measurement granularity, and cumulative selection effects. Kill the approach if simple replay matches the accuracy–retention–cost frontier; if verifiers or fresh draws are unavailable; or if audit overhead consumes the plasticity budget. A reproducible negative result separating token-level stability from behavioral stability is worth retaining, but is not a positive paper claim.

**Chronology.** Predict and score before update on each new event; permit only evidence available at that time. Keep invariant, current-target, and historical-recurrence probes distinct; isolate changes that deliberately invalidate old answers. The CPU toy exposes the transition as a controlled simplification. The streaming experiment must withhold task boundaries, regime IDs, and delayed targets, and log per-event acquisition, audit outcome, and retention on independent probes.

## Other discovery tracks

1. **Two-timescale test-time writes.** Keep disposable fast memory for self-generated text; promote to slow weights only when evidence from a causally independent stream persists across recurrences. Test *which source* actually provides independent evidence with a fixed-generator/exogenous-stream intervention. [TTT settlement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076) already audits individual writes, so the novelty would need to be delayed consolidation and a predictive failure law across episodes, not merely another gate.
2. **Sparse memory with counterfactual collision repair.** Ask whether slot conflicts are negative transfer or necessary concept sharing by randomly reassigning colliding updates to equivalent spare slots at matched byte budgets. Test repair on long episodic fact streams with recurrence and replacements. [SMF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103) and [LoRA versus N-gram Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14921) make simple sparsity incremental; pursue only if an intervention distinguishes harmful collisions from useful sharing and improves actual downstream behavior.
