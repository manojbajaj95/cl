# Continual learning for language models: frontier and open questions

Scope: persistent **model-side** adaptation, from pretraining and post-training to test-time writes. This is a selective literature scan through November 2026, not a benchmark replication. Links are primary paper records; 2026 preprints are evidence from the authors' settings, not established general results.

| Area | What works in reported settings | Gap worth testing |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgetting in SFT and RL post-training** | [An empirical study of catastrophic forgetting during continual fine-tuning (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08747) tracks losses across knowledge, reasoning, and reading comprehension. [RL's Razor (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259) reports better retention for online RL than SFT at comparable new-task performance, and a relationship with smaller base-policy KL on **new-task** prompts. [FRPO (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08813) instead trains the *upstream* RLHF policy to be robust to subsequent changes in a KL neighborhood. | Online RL is not automatically retention-safe; objective, rollout source, reward quality, and initialization are confounded across recipes. New-prompt KL has no distribution-free guarantee on unobserved old prompts. Separate observed behavior loss from inability to recall under a particular decoding or question format. |
| **OPD, OPSD, and feedback** | [On-policy distillation (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649) queries a teacher on student-generated prefixes. [Self-Distilled Reasoner (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734) uses a self-teacher with privileged solution traces; [SDFT (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897) uses demonstration-conditioned self-teaching and reports gains over SFT in sequential learning. [SDPO (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20802) conditions the teacher on feedback. [Adaptive Supervised Anchoring (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07935) falls back toward canonical-prefix supervision when sampled prefixes corrupt the teaching signal. [SR-OPSD (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09745) interpolates reference and teacher distributions. | [Denser ≠ Better (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01763) reports forgetting and occasional collapse for SDPO despite on-policy data; [Rethinking OPSD for Thinking Models (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05184) observes fewer reasoning forks and worse long-rollout accuracy under privileged supervision; [Privileged Information Does Not Guarantee Privileged Guidance (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16064) directly audits teacher signals. Hold rollout budget and new-task competence fixed; cross teacher conditioning, update rule and rollout source. Audit old-task success on fresh rollouts, not only next-token agreement. |
| **Replay and regularization** | [EWC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796) regularizes important weights; [GEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840) constrains gradients using episodic memory; [MIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742) retrieves examples likely to suffer from a candidate update. [Revisiting Replay and Gradient Alignment (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01908) finds small replay fractions highly competitive at scale for multilingual continual pretraining. [On-Policy Replay (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29495) trains on verified self-generations from historical prompts and improves TRACE backward transfer. [Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation Replay (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02746) replays old-domain prompts using the previous student as teacher. | Compare against tuned replay at the same **total** tokens and retained bytes. [CoKL (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01743) preserves the relative distribution *within* correct outputs without anchoring overall correctness mass. This leaves a distinct question: is tracking and protecting that mass more efficient than matching identities of specific outputs? Verifier access is extra information and must be budgeted. |
| **Model merging and task arithmetic** | [Task arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089) composes task deltas; [TIES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708) trims and resolves sign conflicts. [Off-Policy Merging Beats OPSD (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05872) reports that learning deltas on an earlier donor and grafting scaled, optionally masked updates onto a post-trained checkpoint can beat both OPSD and SFT in its settings. | Task-isolated checkpoint availability, donor compatibility, continual tuning of merge coefficients, and cumulative interference matter. Report regimes with and without task boundaries and account for donor storage. Merging is a baseline here, not the primary research topic. |
| **LoRA continual learning** | [O-LoRA (2023)](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.715/) learns new tasks in approximately orthogonal low-rank spaces. [Adaptive Budget Allocation (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22358) adjusts orthogonal-adapter capacity over the stream. [ELLA (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02232) selectively penalizes overlap instead of enforcing hard separation. [Bilinear Optimization Divergence (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23594) diagnoses issues arising from factor-constrained LoRA CL. | Orthogonality in factor space is not functional independence; insisting on separation can suppress positive transfer. Compare one shared adapter, merged fresh adapters, and router-based isolated adapters with matched rank, memory, and no oracle task ID unless explicitly granted. |
| **Sparse model memory** | [Memory Layers at Scale (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764) motivates trainable sparse memory. [Sparse Memory Finetuning (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103) updates sparse slots selected for activation on new information relative to background; on one reported comparison NaturalQuestions F1 falls 11% (relative), versus 71% with LoRA at similar new knowledge learning. [Improving SMF (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05248) retrofits Qwen2.5-0.5B. [LoRA versus N-gram Memory (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14921) observes less backbone drift but interference within learned memory across seven tasks. | Track slot collision/eviction, retention of learned facts (not only backbone retention), paraphrase and composition, and net capacity per byte. [Can a Language Model Learn Facts Continually in Its Weights? (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11020) shows major differences from varying factual restatements, independent of update method. Data diversity is a baseline, not an afterthought. |
| **Test-time learning and fast weights** | [TTT layers (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620) represent recurrent hidden state as a model updated by a learned self-supervised task; [Titans (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663) uses surprise-driven neural memory; [Nested Learning (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695) organizes updates at multiple rates. [TTT-E2E (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675) meta-learns initialization for online next-token updates with sliding-window attention. [Self-Generated Feedback Destabilizes TTT (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076) finds harmful persistent updates on self-generated text in its experiments, and shows that an independent-data commitment check can help. | A long-context result with an episode reset is not lifelong retention. Run frozen, episodic-reset, and persistent variants under equal context and latency. Separate exogenous stream from self-generated feedback using fixed-generator/replay controls; score next segments before learning from them, post-reset, and after delayed recurrence. No update on future tokens, test answers, or future verifier feedback. |

## Benchmarks and protocol

- **Instruction and capability retention:** [TRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06762) measures downstream sequential instruction learning, general capabilities, and instruction following. [CITB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14510) complements it with continual instruction transfer. Include recurring tasks and reordered streams, not just one fixed order.
- **Knowledge updates:** use the [iterative factual-write setting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11020), with held-out aliases/paraphrases, compositional queries, distractors, and unaffected facts. Explicitly distinguish intended replacement of outdated facts from forgetting facts that should remain valid.
- **Persistent TTT:** score unseen independent stream segments before update plus delayed probes; report reset, input-only, self-written, and no-write controls. [Measuring Test-Time Learning Ability in LLMs with Human Comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14448) is a complementary experience-based evaluation, not evidence of persistent weight learning.
- **Metrics:** for score matrix $R_{i,j}$ (task $j$ after stage $i$), report final average accuracy, BWT $\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T}(R_{T,j}-R_{j,j})$, and forgetting $\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T}(\max_{j\le i\le T}R_{i,j}-R_{T,j})$. Include worst-group retention, forward transfer over the frozen base, acquisition/retention Pareto curves, and both reference-token and rollout metrics. Log verifier calls, all train/rollout tokens, FLOPs or measured hours, retained bytes, inference latency, and uncertainty across model seeds and stream orders.
- **Fair comparisons:** fixed per-stage data access, checkpoint-selection rules, and tuning budget; independent development, guard, and test prompts; prompt-distribution audits. Use jointly trained and task-specific models as upper-reference comparisons, not deployable baselines. Compare at matched new-task quality as well as fixed compute. A guard that retains performance by refusing to learn is not a successful continual learner.

## Takeaway

The strongest near-term question is not whether on-policy updates always forget less. It is whether a model can protect **the probabilities of retained behaviors** at matched new-task acquisition, without needlessly freezing all tokens in old generations. Recent on-policy replay, correctness-conditioned KL, and TTT settlement each attack a nearby part of this problem and make naïve “add a retention loss” proposals insufficient. The [proposal](proposal.md) turns that distinction into a test.
