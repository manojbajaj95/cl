# Agent proposer batch 12: one front for rare-event replay (from PR #12)

## Q1. All rare-event replay methods sit on one curve, indexed by the effective gradient weight on the rare class
**Surprise.** In `experiments/rare_event/results.json` (frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B, 896-parameter adapter, two-decision tool simulator), neither the count of rare (jam) examples nor the count of distinct states explains old-behaviour recovery. Direct terminal replay saw ~2,049 jams over 40–43 states and recovered 9.6%. Splitting saw 37–47 jams over 19–26 states and recovered 10.7%. Upsampling saw 4 jams over 4 states in two seeds and got 21.1% vs 2.0%. Re-weighting the *same* MOPD samples toward jams lifted old recovery from 2.5–3.7% to 6.9–11.6%, and lowered new-jam performance in every seed.
**Mechanism.** With 896 parameters, the old and new jam behaviours compete for nearly the same adapter directions. Replay methods then differ only in how much total gradient weight they put on the jam class relative to common states, not in how many jams or which states they show. Prediction: plotted against one number, the effective jam gradient share w = Σ_jam‖g‖ / Σ_all‖g‖ accumulated over training, every arm (no replay, MOPD, weighted MOPD, splitting, terminal replay, upsampling) falls on one old-vs-new Pareto curve. Seed outliers (21.1% vs 2.0%) are explained by w differing across seeds despite equal sample counts, because gradient norms on the few jam samples differ.
**Control that could tie, and why it should fail.** Jam count, distinct-state count, and nominal sampling weight. The raw results already show the first two failing. Nominal weight ignores per-sample gradient norm, which is what separates the two upsampling seeds with identical coverage.
**Pilot.** Re-run PR #12's arms with per-step gradient-norm logging by class (cheap: same model and simulator, 3 seeds). Fit old recovery and new-jam performance as functions of w alone. **Kill** if w explains < 70% of the across-arm variance in old recovery, or does not beat jam count by ≥0.3 R². If it holds, the exploit is a controller that targets w directly to place a run anywhere on the front.
**Ledger.** Rare-event distillation itself failed its gates. This idea explains the failure and gives a single control variable. It is not a new replay method.
