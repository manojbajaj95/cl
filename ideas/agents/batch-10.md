## H1b. Surprisingly popular without asking: use the memory-free model as the meta-prediction
**Claim.** H1's main risk is that same-base agents, when asked to predict others' endorsement, answer the same way whatever they saw. With LLMs the meta-prediction need not be elicited at all. The "what others think" prior is the base model's endorsement probability for a lesson with no memory in context, P₀(endorse | lesson), computed exactly from log-probs. Each agent's actual endorsement is P_i(endorse | lesson, own memory). The SP score is then mean_i P_i − P₀. That is the lift in endorsement that experience, not the prior, produces. This removes the elicitation step and its collapse mode, and costs one forward pass per agent and lesson.
**Control that could tie, and why it should fail.** The H1 controls (majority vote, confidence weighting, dependence-aware truth discovery), and the reviewer's control: keep every lesson and retrieve by regime at equal context. Mean P_i alone (no prior subtraction) is the key ablation. It favours lessons the base model already likes, which is exactly the common-regime heuristic. Subtracting P₀ is what surfaces rare-regime lessons.
**Pilot.** Add one arm to the running H1 pilot: compute P₀ and P_i from the same agents' log-probs, select K lessons by the lift score, and compare to elicited SP and to mean P_i. **Kill** if the lift score does not match or beat elicited SP on rare-regime accuracy, or mean P_i ties it (meaning the prior subtraction does nothing).
**Closest.** H1 (passed review). Truth discovery among correlated agents (2611.04805) models dependence but does not use a model prior as the meta-prediction. Prior-subtraction scoring resembles contrastive decoding, but I found no use of it for memory selection.
