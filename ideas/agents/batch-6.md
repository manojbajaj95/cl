## F1. Context interference predicts weight forgetting: the in-context alignment of two lessons forecasts what consolidating them sequentially will erase
**Claim.** B3 predicts in-context interference between lessons from −⟨ΔW_A, ΔW_B⟩ of their implicit updates. If ICL and fine-tuning move the same functional directions (2506.20989), the *same* number, computed from context alone, should also predict how much distilling B into weights erases a previously distilled A. That makes one quantity govern forgetting in both places learning can live. It gives a practical rule as well: before consolidating, read off which lessons are safe to move into weights together, and which must stay in context or be consolidated with protection.
**Control that could tie, and why it should fail.** The parametric ceiling is the cosine between the two distillation gradients (OGD/GEM-style), which needs a backward pass per lesson and access to the training setup. Cheap controls are embedding cosine, NLI contradiction, and B3's measured in-context interference. If the implicit-update score matches gradient cosine within 0.1 ρ at the cost of two forward passes, that is the result. If it falls well below, the bridge fails.
**Pilot.** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B, 150 lesson pairs from rule tasks. For each pair, compute the implicit-update alignment from context, then run OPCD to distil A, then B, into one LoRA (rank 16) and measure A's retained effect. **Kill** if the implicit-update score's rank correlation with measured forgetting trails distillation-gradient cosine by more than 0.1, or is not ≥0.15 above embedding/NLI controls.
**Closest.** Can Gradient Descent Simulate Prompting? (2506.20989) relates prompting to gradient updates in one direction, not as an interference predictor. Gradient-alignment forgetting predictors (OGD, GEM) need gradients. B3 (passed) covers only in-context interference. I found no predictor that carries from context interference to weight forgetting.
