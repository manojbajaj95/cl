**DTE1. Buy two exits that survive both supplier outages and their own training updates.** Parts: LE4-01 source-disjoint cones, LE4-03 correlated shortages, LE4-04 non-interfering exits, LE4-07 shared circuit, LE3-09 joint outages, DA07 joint uncertainty. *Mechanism:* Check the same learner after useful work from each source. Mark separately which suppliers survive a common outage and which surviving suppliers can still use ordinary updates to advance old-compatible behavior. Pay only for improvements to the worst jointly realizable fallback, not for more vendor names or arbitrary gradient directions. *New:* Supply diversity and the learner's ability to use it could be purchased separately and verified together. *First test:* Cross two supplier dependency graphs with two matched internal bottlenecks, then remove one supplier and execute a finite update from the other.

**DTE2. Set a worst-slice cap on the price of switching to a scarce teacher.** Parts: LE4-02 dominant-source blackout, LE4-06 worst slice, LE4-08 scarce source budget, LE2-01 substitute sample cost, LE3-15 learnable exceptions, RB11 actual fallback. *Mechanism:* Withhold the dominant source and replay the declared switch using the scarce source's normal examples. Release a separate installment for each covered slice only when the learner learns a new exception there without dropping existing ones. Charge any fallback examples and do not average a failed slice away. *New:* A small vendor could be useful as an exit specifically where the incumbent leaves the buyer most exposed. *First test:* Match aggregate switch cost on two rule curricula, vary the rare-slice sample budget and compare average, max-slice and exact joint planning.

**DTE3. Bill a first teacher for repair work it leaves to a different optimizer.** Parts: LE4-10 order swap, LE4-12 exported debt, LE4-13 noisy first entrant, LE4-16 optimizer mismatch, LE3-17 dependence debit, BM4-03 unsettled loss. *Mechanism:* Run both orders on actual checkpoints with fixed source menus and optimizer budgets. Compare each costly second update to an independent open reference under the same first-entrant noise. Keep the second-job backlog and observed old damage separate rather than treating either as paid by strong first-job accuracy. *New:* A release contract could buy handoff states that preserve useful training under other update rules, not just a chain tuned to one optimizer. *First test:* Swap two frozen optimizers across two curriculum orders and run a matched no-first-hop control. Independently vary inherited optimizer state.

**DTE4. Make second-hop gains earn their payout by composing the first-hop skill.** Parts: LE4-14 compositional second use, LE4-15 positive acceleration, LE4-17 blank-capacity control, LE4-18 third-hop cliff, LE3-12 second payment, ER31 shared interpreters, TP20 decoder composition. *Mechanism:* Have an independent second learner build a genuinely new behavior that requires both the incumbent's base and the first entrant's extension. Withhold the composed behavior and match reserved capacity. Predeclare a third-hop holdout to detect whether rapid composition exhausted the next learner's budget. *New:* The contract could distinguish a reusable learned structure from empty capacity or a task split that only looks reusable. *First test:* Compare equal-byte empty adapters, shared interpreter state and disjoint task heads across three synthetic program extensions.

**DTE5. Finish an interrupted lesson without invalidating the old read that comes due next.** Parts: LE4-19 cross-source two-hop, LE4-20 mid-extension outage, LE3-02 prefix exits, LE3-20 forward repair, PX10 causal policy equivalence, VD24 adverse-route transfer, BM4-03 open loss. *Mechanism:* Transfer the minimum public continuation and outstanding old-request witness when source A drops out. Test whether a single source-B policy can continue across interruption points with the same public observation; if it cannot, explicitly budget the required diagnosis or old-compatible repair. *New:* A supplier handoff could serve an unfinished training job rather than relying on a clean checkpoint selected after the fact. *First test:* Interrupt at each prefix in a finite learner with a due read. Compare restarting, prepaying a full shadow learner and independent source-B completion.

**DTE6. Preserve one rare action fiber before learning from its dominant substitute.** Parts: LE4-07 circuit bottleneck, LE4-09 drought audit, LE4-21 diversity-preserving first hop, LE4-24 spurious source coupling, ER2-16 scoped fiber, ER2-20 fiber preservation, BM4-26 separating observation. *Mechanism:* Find a public observation showing whether a rare source still supports its distinct native update after a dominant-only hop. Keep just the cue needed by the rare action when compressing the common representation, and test it with fresh rare-source examples. If repeated drought removes the cue irrecoverably at budget, count that route as closed. *New:* A small action-conditioned state might preserve a rare exit without reserving a full model branch. *First test:* Use four paired toy trajectories with identical endpoint predictions, independently varying rare-source absence and action-cue collision. Match bytes against a static residual and full duplication.

**DTE7. Buy two parallel continuations with an explicit price on reconciling their old obligations.** Parts: LE4-23 merge without retraining, LE4-26 representation versus supply, ER2-27 noncommuting witness, ER32 typed rewrite, RB12 joint certificate, BM4-22 compatible receipts. *Mechanism:* Fork the actual first-hop state. Allow parallel entrants to produce different useful extensions, but pay for mergeability only when one typed composition reproduces both under the original rights and reconciles shared obligations without counting one receipt twice. Check whether reversing the merge order changes old service. *New:* A purchaser could elicit a base that supports non-rival continuation rather than just separate individually successful branches. *First test:* Cross independent supplies with independently mergeable state in a finite two-action learner; compare matched adapters, sequential replay and exact order search.

**DTE8. Finance a new exit with proof that it did not consume the old one.** Parts: LE4-05 exit-forking lesson, LE4-11 natural update non-interference, LE4-22 incumbent rotation control, LE4-25 exact planning control, LE3-21 joint replacement budget, LE2-14 lawful paths, OS19 executable order. *Mechanism:* Offer a productive lesson that adds a source-B path while keeping the source-A path executable after ordinary updates. Price joint feasibility from the actual rights graph and compare to the incumbent's matched feasible rotation, not to a weaker single-source control. Hold back the payout if the claimed fork needs one scarce example in two incompatible slots. *New:* A modest contract could reward creation of a genuinely additional learning route, rather than moving a monopoly into a different lesson order. *First test:* Enumerate supplier menus and finite optimizer steps with fixed rights and joint examples; compare a matched incumbent rotation, a robust planner and the paid fork.

**DTE9. Avoid paying for a new teacher when a cheap diagnostic makes the old handoff sufficient.** Parts: LE4-06 worst slice, LE4-20 interruption, LE4-24 spurious coupling, LE3-23 smallest separator, ER2-14 predictable residual, BM4-14 restricted probe, RB11 actual fallback. *Mechanism:* Precommit the smallest lawful probe that could change the interruption decision. If it identifies an old-compatible source switch at lower full cost, prefer it to a broad dual-source representation purchase. If the incumbent can teach a common safe update instead, pay only the verified difference against that diagnostic path. *New:* The buyer could choose between buying robust internal structure and buying enough evidence to use the structure it already has. *First test:* Use matched checkpoints with similar uncertainty but vary whether the missing source introduces predictable ambiguity or irreducible noise.

**DTE10. A teaching market that pays for two surviving exits after real reuse.** Parts: LE4-01 gradient cones, LE4-02 dominant blackout, LE4-03 correlated shortages, LE4-04 non-interference, LE4-05 exit fork, LE4-06 worst slice, LE4-07 circuit bottleneck, LE4-08 scarce finish, LE4-09 drought audit, LE4-10 order swap, LE4-11 natural update, LE4-12 exported debt, LE4-13 noisy entrant, LE4-14 compositional reuse, LE4-15 positive acceleration, LE4-16 optimizer mismatch, LE4-17 unused capacity, LE4-18 third-hop cliff, LE4-19 cross-source reuse, LE4-20 interrupted completion, LE4-21 preserve exit count, LE4-22 rotation benchmark, LE4-23 parallel merge, LE4-24 spurious coupling, LE4-25 planner control, LE4-26 factorial audit, LE3-02 per-prefix exits, LE3-09 joint outage, LE3-12 second payment, LE3-15 new exceptions, LE3-17 dependence debit, LE3-20 repair, LE3-21 joint budget, LE3-23 public separator, LE2-01 substitute cost, LE2-14 rights graph, DA07 joint uncertainty, RB11 actual fallback, RB12 joint certificate, BM4-03 open loss, BM4-14 restricted report, BM4-22 compatible receipts, BM4-26 separating observation, ER31 interpreter sharing, ER32 typed rewrite, TP20 decoder composition, PX10 causal policy, VD24 adverse witness, ER2-14 predictable residual, ER2-16 scoped fiber, ER2-20 protected cue, ER2-27 noncommutation, OS19 feasible ordering. *Mechanism:* Separate source independence from internal learning compatibility, then test each on the actual state left by live work. Price interruption, old-read debt and scarce-source exceptions separately. Pay for fresh composition and reconcilable parallel work only after occupied-state and optimizer-mismatch controls, retaining action cues where common compression would erase a working exit. *New:* An incumbent might be paid to leave a learner that can switch sources repeatedly rather than a learner that just passes two staged substitutions. *First test:* Enumerate benign synthetic teaching markets with crossed outages, circuit aliasing, supply rights and optimizer mismatch. Compare exact same-information planning, matched replay and translation, and the parent covenant stack. Measure worst-slice second and third-hop costs, outstanding old work and effective exit count.
