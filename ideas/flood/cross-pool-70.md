**NMR1. Keep the veto when the observer retires, but stop renewing it from silence.** Parts: CX06 surviving exclusion, CX20 remove renewal first, BM4-02 observer liveness, BM4-03 unsettled loss, VD24 negative-route transfer. *Mechanism:* Separate the supported effect exclusion from its renewal channel. On replacement, carry open liabilities by caller and version; a liveness receipt cannot settle an outcome. Let the old veto live only for the already supported horizon unless independently invalidated, and require a mapped adverse route before renewing it. *New:* Replacing an observer need not delete a useful skill or imply that an unobserved harm vanished. *First test:* Switch observers during a synthetic stream, with a genuine missing outcome and a known invalidated premise. Compare full revalidation and carrying all receipts unchanged.

**NMR2. Translate a denial by its effect without transferring the old planner.** Parts: CX01 effect mapping, CX21 denial at failover, CX26 fresh replacement, BM4-11 scoped negative receipt, TP20 decoder composition, RB01 partial action, RB06 renamed handoff, RB09 separate effects. *Mechanism:* Give a successor only the supported effect exclusions, timely old routes and observation requirements. Teach a partially defined action contract; let a new caller rebind only supported effects rather than assigning a default to unknown aliases. Verify its local trace mapping. At the first unmapped effect, distinguish a witnessed denial from unknown status; request only a probe that could change the next choice. *New:* A recipient can learn a new implementation without replaying the old expert or adopting its unjustified assumptions. *First test:* Withhold the old trace and change names and branch structure. Compare behavior cloning and raw denial replay against effect contracts, including a same-name action with a new harmful effect.

**NMR3. Rule out the order that blinds the second check, not both deployed skills.** Parts: CX12 relative order, CX18 one causal route, BM4-17 joint exclusion, BM4-18 lost second probe, OS20 necessary cycle. *Mechanism:* Build a joint native event schedule under sealed observation rights. Allow stable configurations when their individual evidence holds, but forbid a transition when the first write masks the only timely probe for another. Enumerate alternate placements before declaring no schedule. *New:* Compatibility can be a property of causal order even when the same eventual pair is safe. *First test:* Compare pairwise compatibility and exact scheduling in a three-write fixture where only some orders retain a negative observation.

**NMR4. Permit a harmless probe without permitting its output to settle a claim.** Parts: CX04 use versus production, CX15 observer mode, BM4-13 separate rights, BM4-14 unsafe report use, DA12 sealed assignment, WS16 seal forecast, WS17 report-only control. *Mechanism:* Allow bounded old-task probes that are legal to execute but label their reports as diagnostic-only while relevant provenance or observability is unknown. Seal both rivals' forecasts before toggling reporting, and verify that the reporting switch itself does not alter treatment or task behavior. Preserve the restriction through derived reports; require independent admissible evidence before promoting a claim. Probe allocation grants no modification rights. *New:* An old diagnostic can remain operational after losing its authority to settle new writes. *First test:* Cross blind assignment with free report reuse; compare local planning value, unsupported confirmations and held-out old reads under delayed independent evidence.

**NMR5. Settle joint damage without inventing a harmless individual write.** Parts: BM4-06 joint release, BM4-22 compatible provenance, BM4-23 possible world sums, CX24 conditional veto exchange, DA07 joint errors. *Mechanism:* Keep compatible joint assignments for all unresolved outcomes. Release only the aggregate proven surplus on supported assignments; preserve individual exclusions when no admissible decomposition supports attributing harmlessness. Refuse to combine marginal witnesses from incompatible versions or cases. *New:* Joint reserve can be reused even when the evidence cannot allocate credit to a specific component. *First test:* Plant correlated missing reports and individually plausible but mutually incompatible settlements; compare exact joint bounds, marginal sums and no release.

**NMR6. Require a substitute meter to expose the same bad deployment.** Parts: CX10 lost contrast, CX17 substitute probe, BM4-09 repair the meter, BM4-21 dominating witness, DA26 paired proxy, WS19 shadow serves during repair, RB22 observation changes lawful rebind. *Mechanism:* Overlap the final lawful old monitor uses with its replacement. Demand that the new test detects the same adverse alternatives by the same old deadline; migrate a pending claim only on the covered slice. Run a matched non-weight alternative while repairing the meter. Carry authorization forward only on the rebinds the paired tests actually settle. Keep unresolved debit live and allow no new spending of that balance until the replacement works. *New:* A meter may be retired without interrupting service or quietly erasing the only decisive negative test. *First test:* Compare agreement on easy cases and matched adverse overlap when a proxy fails specifically under a busy old workload.

**NMR7. Shrink a useful write so the meter can still tell on it.** Parts: CX19 guard versus monitor, CX22 scope actually used, BM4-19 shrink candidate, BM4-20 reserve diagnostic capacity, LW22 disarmed test, RN01 narrowed effect, WS04 residual write, WS10 stacked shadow, WS24 shadow retention. *Mechanism:* Predeclare a restricted candidate effect set and cross guard enforcement with an independently assigned monitor path when lawful. Choose the strongest cost-matched combination of context, retrieval and other non-weight alternatives before exposure, and let it learn from the same receipts. Consider a weight edit only for the verified residual that this combination cannot carry, with the same old-task floor. Narrow the effect set until harmful variants still leave a usable adverse signal and some useful motion remains. Do not infer safety when all feasible probes are blinded. *New:* A narrowed provisional change might keep learning useful without spending the evidence it needs to graduate. *First test:* Compare fixed exposure caps, separate measurement and exact action narrowing with identical positive outputs but different observer coverage.

**NMR8. Release unused backing after an unanswered request only with proof it was never owed.** Parts: BM4-04 no request owed, BM4-07 expiry, BM4-24 fixed loss definition, CX03 enforcement cut, CX11 compatible versions, VD01 upstream intake. *Mechanism:* Track upstream obligations across switching and use a fixed old loss. Distinguish proven absence of an owed request from missed observation of a served one. Expiry ends new exposure but leaves unsettled debit; reclaimed capacity is available only to a genuinely new backed write. *New:* The evidence that frees a reservation can stay separate from whatever evidence originally backed the version. *First test:* Compare downstream receipt counts and fixed expiries on a stream with redirected, cancelled and unobserved requests; exercise actual reuse.

**NMR9. Buy the probe whose least useful outcome can still change a decision.** Parts: BM4-10 binding debit, BM4-12 worst probe outcome, BM4-26 separating observation, CX05 quarantine dispute, CX23 conditional dissent, TP09 affordable query, RB11 cheapest improvement over fallback, RB12 joint certificate, RB14 menu-changing query. *Mechanism:* Choose a permitted public diagnostic that either separates the binding liability or establishes which branches must remain unresolved. Charge a pair of queries against their joint decision effect, comparing it to the actual fallback rather than to doing nothing. Keep a dissenting implementation only if its report could change a backed old decision; otherwise retire it without promoting a rival automatically. *New:* Verification can be allocated by the decisions it protects rather than by expected confidence or implementation diversity alone. *First test:* Use equal-cost queries on indistinguishable benign tasks with opposite safe writes and compare information gain, cheapest probe and worst-case decision planning.
