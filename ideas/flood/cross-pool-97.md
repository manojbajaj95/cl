Focal source: `tree/root-learner-world-coevolution.md` (PR #363, LW#). Raw proposals for synthetic environments and authorized observations.

**CWX1. Separate learning from the learner's effect on what gets measured.** Parts: LW01 world-response operator, LW06 sample provenance, LW07 feedback-sensitive calibration, LW17 mutual adaptation, LW27 self-simplified curriculum, LW29 frozen-world twin, LM12 environment luck, DF03 intervention versus report. *Mechanism:* Cross old and new policies with frozen and responsive toy worlds. Use small randomized policy changes to estimate how exposure and feedback respond, and recalibrate only on supported comparisons. Report unsupported causal splits rather than treating a frozen-world score as the real counterfactual. *New:* A learning gain could be distinguished from the easier data stream produced by the learner's own previous successes. *First test:* Create two equally improving live systems, one that learns and one that changes the mix of incoming tasks. Compare live metrics, frozen snapshots and crossed calibration at the same observation budget.

**CWX2. Rehearse recovery when successful prevention makes the failure rare.** Parts: LW02 prevention-induced amnesia, LW15 easy-task starvation, LW18 partner fallback, LW28 dormant versus superseded regimes, CR29 versioned relearning, D5-18 substitute lesson. *Mechanism:* Use intervention evidence to distinguish a prevented but returning state from a truly obsolete one. Maintain a narrow recovery drill, including a consented human fallback where relevant, and revalidate it against current conditions before reuse. Preserve a substitute lesson only if it exercises the same recovery boundary. *New:* Successful prevention would not be taken as evidence that the learner or its partner can still recover when prevention stops working. *First test:* Use an abstract dispatch simulator with occasional externally induced outages and a skill that has truly become obsolete. Compare uniform replay, deletion after inactivity and scoped recovery drills.

**CWX3. Roll back to a reachable world, not just to yesterday's weights.** Parts: LW03 hysteretic rollback, LW24 ecological corridors, LW30 irreversible commitments, LW31 equilibrium switching, D3-20 observable return, L4-27 scoped rollback. *Mechanism:* Before an edit, test whether an admissible sequence can return the learner and its environment to an observably recoverable state. For a nonreversible edit, require a checked successor recovery route rather than a weight checkpoint. Label old states unreachable if the only proposed corridor relies on lost observations. *New:* Safe rollback would be a property of the coupled system even when neither restoring weights nor restoring an interface alone is sufficient. *First test:* Use a finite world with a delayed adaptation latch and a one-way transition. Compare weight rollback, state reset and a checked recovery corridor.

**CWX4. Slow teaching when the world and its reviewers cannot keep pace.** Parts: LW04 delayed recoil damping, LW05 self-amplification budget, LW22 capability unlocks, LW23 shortcut attractors, LW25 ground self-play, LW26 verification pace, CE4-21 surviving verification route, D5-13 learning beyond reading. *Mechanism:* Allow an acquired capability to open a new curriculum only while useful evidence can still be collected in time. Throttle updates where delayed world responses can amplify them or where high-volume easy feedback crowds out informative cases. Require an independent target-world check before treating synthetic progress as transferable. *New:* Curriculum pacing would preserve verification access rather than optimizing only traversal of the currently rewarding frontier. *First test:* In a synthetic curriculum with delayed reviews, vary lag and task access independently. Compare fixed throttling, difficulty scheduling and bounded closed-loop pacing at equal audit budgets.

**CWX5. Choose whether to learn a behavior or maintain an environment dependency.** Parts: LW08 world-weight allocation, LW09 niche-free core, LW10 versioned interface, LW11 artifact ownership and expiry, LW12 portable niche, LW14 affordance drift, LW32 world lineage, LM32 behavioral contract, DF16 interface migration. *Mechanism:* Compare internal training and a reversible change to an authorized sandbox interface. Declare ownership, expiry and assumed affordances for external state, and retain a native fallback or an explicit gap when the environment patch cannot be exported. Probe both old service and future correction after transplanting only the retained dependencies. *New:* The learner can carry the smallest useful part of its custom environment without mistaking a hidden tool dependency for learned skill. *First test:* Run a toy workflow across two mock tool suites, then expire a helper and alter a valid affordance. Compare full workspace copy, weight-only migration and contract-guided portability.

**CWX6. Preserve independent oversight when learners and users adapt together.** Parts: LW13 independent utility, LW16 supervision collapse, LW19 dialect drift, LW20 defensive anti-cycling, LW21 strategic input shift, D2-04 hidden-context diagnosis, CE4-24 corruption tolerance. *Mechanism:* Audit an independently labeled sample before adopting apparent gains. Keep a small set of benign historical control cases and prompt variants to detect cyclical defensive forgetting and reliance on local dialect. Do not attribute changed measurements to users or their intent without an intervention that separates that claim from model change. *New:* An increase in approval can be distinguished from a loss of independent checking or a change in which inputs reach the learner. *First test:* Use a synthetic scoring game where review rates and input presentations shift independently, with alternating abstract failure regimes. Compare accuracy-only monitoring, static audit and oversight-preserving controls.

**CWX7. Learn in a changing world without erasing the evidence that would correct you.** Parts: LW01 world-response operator, LW06 sample provenance, LW07 feedback-sensitive calibration, LW17 mutual adaptation, LW27 self-simplified curriculum, LW29 frozen-world twin, LM12 environment luck, DF03 intervention versus report, LW02 prevention-induced amnesia, LW15 easy-task starvation, LW18 partner fallback, LW28 dormant versus superseded regimes, CR29 versioned relearning, D5-18 substitute lesson, LW03 hysteretic rollback, LW24 ecological corridors, LW30 irreversible commitments, LW31 equilibrium switching, D3-20 observable return, L4-27 scoped rollback, LW04 delayed recoil damping, LW05 self-amplification budget, LW22 capability unlocks, LW23 shortcut attractors, LW25 ground self-play, LW26 verification pace, CE4-21 surviving verification route, D5-13 learning beyond reading, LW08 world-weight allocation, LW09 niche-free core, LW10 versioned interface, LW11 artifact ownership and expiry, LW12 portable niche, LW14 affordance drift, LW32 world lineage, LM32 behavioral contract, DF16 interface migration, LW13 independent utility, LW16 supervision collapse, LW19 dialect drift, LW20 defensive anti-cycling, LW21 strategic input shift, D2-04 hidden-context diagnosis, CE4-24 corruption tolerance. *Mechanism:* Track which aspects of data, tooling and supervision change because of the learner. Compare live evidence with bounded independent tests, rehearse returning regimes, and admit irreversible or environment-coupled edits only with scoped evidence and a valid recovery route. Treat delayed verification and lost observability as constraints on growth. *New:* A continual learner could improve jointly with its world without taking its own changes to feedback, observability or the workspace as proof of better capability. *First test:* Evaluate finite synthetic coupled systems with lag, hidden state, nonreversible edits, expiring interfaces and independent labels. Compare a frozen-world audit, replay plus rollback and a coupled policy at matched service, audit and recovery costs.
