# Cross-pool merges, round 29

Focal sources: `tree/bounded-burial.md` (PR #142, BB#) and `tree/crossed-evidence.md` (PR #143, XE#). Crosses with recovery witnesses (PR #139, RW#), forgetting cycles (PR #138, FC#), first point of loss (PR #134, FL#), SGD's information-dumping map (PR #133, ID#), memory dilution (PR #103, MD#), Q9 score-vector tomography, Y14 sheaf-consistent lesson stores, Z3 opponent ledgers, scoped authority grants (PR #93, OK#), label-free forgetting detection (PR #126, LF#), and earlier rounds 1–28. These are raw proposals. Count answer-changing request–value crossings, not just distinct values; require a successful internal rebind before calling a procedure a reader. Separate live service, bounded recovery and exact loss. A universal wake word is frozen before held-out scoring, takes no task ID or target feedback, and must restore jointly promised skills in one invocation. Charge burial, wake, probes, and time out of service, and check new-task gains again after wake.

**BCE1. Rent a reader's write protection only after its shortcuts have had a fair chance to fail.** Parts: BB1 constraint rental, BB7 counterfactual write option, BB21 post-wake gain, XE1 fixed-marginal rewires, XE3 rival caches, XE18 future edit cost, RW13 last complete carrier, FER7 write timing. *Mechanism:* Factorially vary answer-changing crossings and randomized release of a write constraint after acquisition. Test whether crossings make it cheaper to protect a return path than to protect live outputs, or instead create a shared reader whose loss strands more tasks. *New:* A curriculum could be valuable for the capacity it makes safe to lend, not merely for the accuracy it installs. *First test:* Rewire a synthetic routing curriculum at fixed counts, hold fact QA fixed, and train B normally after release. Compare tuned always-live, relaxed-retention and exact-store baselines using the same wake budget; score causal rebinds and net B gain after wake.

**BCE2. Take a tomogram of the answers a wake word actually changes.** Parts: BB8 synchronize access, BB14 shared carriers, XE6 contrast geometry, XE9 multi-output fingerprints, Q9 score-vector tomography, RW8 false revival, LF#. *Mechanism:* In evaluation, apply coded combinations of independently verified internal rebinds and public wake probes to shadow copies. Decompose the output vector into shared access shifts, signed changes that follow private bindings, and stale residues; estimate interactions separately rather than assuming the combinations add. The live wake remains a fixed public recipe. *New:* A word that raises average accuracy can be distinguished from one that restores the ability to follow several independent facts. *First test:* Compare coded measurements to one-at-a-time and full factorial measurements on a held-out subset at equal total compute. Include a common bias shift, an arbitrary world-ID cache and opposite-label recipients.

**BCE3. Require local wake promises to agree where their callers overlap.** Parts: BB17 one-shot co-revival, BB19 wake footprints, XE4 narrow cuts, XE15 missing corners, XE16 consumer transfer, Y14 sheaf-consistent lesson stores, FCR8 return tickets. *Mechanism:* For each task region, record which rebinding contrasts and wake words satisfy its service obligations. Check agreement on shared requests, then ask whether a single allowed word works across the whole cover; deliberately sample missing corners where three regions can be jointly inconsistent despite passing pairwise. *New:* The object to combine is a set of locally valid return paths, not a collection of independently high wake accuracies. *First test:* On a route learner with overlapping consumers and an untouched rule, compare the overlap check to pairwise similarity, pairwise joint wake checks and exhaustive menu search. Hold out a new cover and verify any proposed shared word on one recipient in one run.

**BCE4. Keep the evidence for waking a skill separate from the evidence against waking its old world.** Parts: BB15 fail-closed envelope, BB18 private bits across wakes, XE10 stale residue, XE21 revisions without old answers, Z3 opponent ledgers, FC3 item closure, FER9 bad wake paths. *Mechanism:* Record independently whether a frozen word restores correct procedure use at supplied new premises and whether it brings back exact retired derivatives. Track both across repeated wakes and curriculum rounds; an improving aggregate score cannot cancel evidence of stale return. The ledger can quarantine a word without revoking the still-valid skill. *New:* A wake recipe can be credited for recovering execution and still disqualified as a live action. *First test:* Compare a two-channel rule against a single net score and an always-live guard on revision streams with matched wake gain. Include legitimate historical queries outside the current default and test stale responses on new instances.

**BCE5. Let an alarm request a wake without granting it permission to write.** Parts: BB6 request budget, BB15 fail closed, XE13 separating query, XE19 release tests, OK9 causal permission, OK10 scoped grants, OK24 failure triad, LF#, FCR5 diagnostic routing. *Mechanism:* Give a cheap ungraded alarm permission to launch a read-only fork. A separately scoped grant can apply a prevalidated public word to the live learner only under declared cost, collateral and overlap constraints. Keep cases where the witness fails, the grant is absent, and the grant exists but an unrelated obligation blocks execution distinct. Do not select universal words from recipient-specific probes. *New:* A label-free detector can route recovery without silently turning a diagnostic signal into write authority. *First test:* Hide access loss, true verifier drift and budget exhaustion in a rule stream. Compare automatic wake and alarm-only deferral at equal public probes; score unnecessary writes, repair latency and missed service.

**BCE6. Locate the first operation that destroys the ability to follow a replacement fact.** Parts: BB11 idempotent boundary, BB16 shared-word lower bound, XE7 rival editors, XE8 unseen donor, XE12 read before top-1, FL#, ID#, FCR6 operation-level bisection. *Mechanism:* At saved cuts, ask separately whether a direct binding can be moved, whether that move propagates after a fixed wake, and whether the procedure works with supplied values. Find pass-to-fail intervals for each contrast, then replace individual operations in the relevant intervals and check whether the return path and B progress coexist. *New:* A skill can keep serving yesterday's answers long after an operation takes away tomorrow's rebind or wake path. *First test:* Replay small unpartitioned learners through ordinary updates, slot resets and quantization; compare cut localization against endpoint probes. Include an exact finite-state collision as a control, not as the explanation for every bounded miss.

**BCE7. Decide whether to buy sharper contrasts or more carrier precision.** Parts: BB4 lend short-term state, BB7 preserve a write option, XE2 distinct contradictions, XE6 contrast geometry, ID# SGD dump map, MD# memory dilution, RW17 cross-witness support, FI6 archive the readable part. *Mechanism:* Cross dose-matched dilution through training with actual precision-reducing writes, and test whether the same return path fails for redundant crossings or only for rare separating crossings. Try stronger evidence at fixed bytes and more carrier precision at fixed evidence. Audit whether changing the reader restores distinctions before claiming they disappeared. *New:* A retention budget could spend on exposing a weak distinction instead of storing the same weak signal more accurately. *First test:* Use two route families with equal initial margins but different contrast redundancy. Freeze the public wake menu and compare spending on examples, precision and labelled replay under a common byte and compute budget.

**BCE8. Forecast which leg of a forgetting cycle a common wake will not close.** Parts: BB9 ordered universal words, BB20 request-order stress, XE11 intermittently needed facts, XE17 contradiction timing, FC3 same-item recurrence, FC5 forecast an edge, PO#, FCR1 phase refocusing. *Mechanism:* Train on the same multiset with early versus late contradictions, then let a frozen public word act on each cut. Use the exposure history of separating examples to predict the specific losing edge and whether the same items return, before opening the last version or the request trace. *New:* A universal wake may generalize across rules but still be predictably phase-limited across histories of the same rule. *First test:* Hold out whole curricula and independent task panels. Compare recency, marginal flip counts and blind wake-order variants at the same compute; score false edge forecasts as well as closures.

**BCE9. Reuse edit episodes as evidence, never as a cache of yesterday's answers.** Parts: BB5 reteach the writer, BB13 burial interface, XE14 contrasts for large jumps, XE18 edit-cost objective, XE21 revision evidence, FF#, Z1 write escrow, FER1 contrast receipts. *Mechanism:* Turn authorized revisions into new practice requests whose answers are generated from current or synthetic independent bindings. Keep a receipt that a request can separate alternatives; do not replay obsolete answers as targets. Admit dormancy only when a fixed wake still restores held-out rebind behavior and when B keeps its gain. *New:* Revisions can pay for a reusable read interface instead of creating a pile of superseded answer memories. *First test:* Under identical revision schedules and equal examples, compare receipt-selected practice, random counterfactual practice, and direct current-value replay. Hold out larger jumps, arbitrary key permutations and an unseen task family.

**BCE10. Offer burst training capacity against a worst-case request trace, not a checkpoint score.** Parts: BB3 shared intermediate compute, BB6 request budgets, BB17 co-revival, BB20 request order, BB21 keep B gain, XE4 narrow cuts, XE11 conditional witnesses, OK18 grant amortization, SP#, FCR4 cohort clocks. *Mechanism:* Allocate a temporary compute or write lease only when the shared wake envelope covers a forecast request trace and still has slack for a rare separating case. Meter waiting, re-burial and the old and new tasks' recovery windows. Fall back to routing or keep live service when the rare caller's budget cannot be met. *New:* One scarce contrast family can price an entire lease even when endpoint averages and typical wake costs look fine. *First test:* Randomize lease admission on fixed-size learners with bursty public B and independently generated requests. Compare always-live, fixed period leases and an equal-byte portfolio; repeat at matched post-wake B and A/C floors.

**BCE11. A continual learner that earns the right to put skills to sleep.** Parts: BB1 constraint rental, BB3 compute width, BB5 writer repair, BB6 request budgets, BB7 write options, BB8 shared alignment, BB9 ordered words, BB11 idempotence, BB13 burial interface, BB15 fail closed, BB16 shared-word bounds, BB17 co-revival, BB18 private bits, BB19 service footprints, BB20 request pressure, BB21 preserved gain, XE1 degree-preserving rewires, XE2 distinct contradictions, XE3 rival caches, XE4 graph cuts, XE6 contrast geometry, XE7 rival editors, XE9 output fingerprints, XE10 stale residues, XE11 conditional reads, XE13 separating queries, XE15 missing corners, XE17 timing, XE18 future edit cost, XE19 release tests, XE21 revision evidence, RW#, FC#, FL#, ID#, MD#, Q9, Y14, Z3, OK#, LF#, FCR6, FER1. *Mechanism:* Buy answer-changing practice designed to defeat shortcut caches; verify internal rebinds, stale residue and supplied-premise execution separately. Admit a temporary lease only if offline audits find a fixed public word that restores all promised skills together across unseen private histories. Check overlap consistency, keep independent evidence of stale revival, and give a label-free alarm scoped access to request but not authorize a wake. Revoke the lease if its service windows or B gains fail after wake; localize the first lost return path without mistaking menu failure for erasure. *New:* The learner could turn evidence that procedures really read their facts into spendable training capacity, while owing callers a time-bounded return of current, not ancestral, behavior. *First test:* Use an enumerable route world with arbitrary world IDs, sparse answer-changing crossings, repeated revisions, a public B shock and bursts of overlapping callers. Compare exact store plus executor, tuned replay and always-live protection, and equal-cost saved deltas. Measure released capacity, post-wake B progress, causal rebinds, stale derivatives, collateral and request latency separately.
