# Cross-pool merges, round 30

Focal sources: `tree/future-edit-cost.md` (PR #144, EC#) and `tree/rented-constraints.md` (PR #145, RC#). Crosses with the rate–drift law and edit-description lower bound (PR #105, ELB#), minimal edit state (PR #104, MS#), skill markets (PR #124, KM#), thermodynamics of remembering (PR #125, TH#), Y6 plasticity rent and taxes, X1/Y1 override budgets, conservative extension (PR #122, CE#), bounded burial (PR #142, BB#), delayed-release edits (PR #141, DR#), and earlier rounds 1–29. These are raw proposals. Charge data selection, acquisition, training, failed revisions, audit, fallback, storage and serving over the same demand trace. Fix lease directions, radius, term and revocation rules before training B. Keep idempotence, correctness on opposite private obligations and coverage of lease-reachable states separate; measure B again after wake. Labels used to grant a lease are not free evidence.

**ECR1. Charge a lease for the extra edit bits it makes the next writer carry.** Parts: EC7 rate–revision frontier, EC11 query versus value compression, RC3 basin radius, RC5 collateral bits, ELB#, RD1 rate forecast, RD2 separate packets. *Mechanism:* At each candidate lease radius, compare which pending edit intents share a legal realization across the admitted futures and which require different messages after the lease and wake. In a finite host, enumerate the resulting decoder conflicts; use the measured split to choose between more protective constraints, a more durable edit packet, and training on separating examples. *New:* A lease's cost can show up as a higher minimum message length even when both old and new tasks score well right after wake. *First test:* Use a finite route table to calibrate exact message bounds, then a learned reader to test held-out message costs empirically. Compare a fixed-bit codec, a protected lease and equal-byte saved deltas at matched service floors.

**ECR2. Store only the distinctions the next edit and the next wake disagree about.** Parts: EC5 portability across writers, EC12 cheap invalidation key, RC1 protect the wake image, RC2 access versus payload, RC20 saved-delta break-even, MS#, W6 compatible persistent edits, CT5 wake before retirement. *Mechanism:* Compare states that agree on present outputs, then split them only when a supported revision or wake-then-revision sequence requires an incompatible action. Keep a compact signature of the distinctions exposed by those tests. If one sequence makes the quotient invalid, retain an extra state feature or remove that sequence from the lease. *New:* The minimum useful edit state may depend more on the wake and writer together than on what ordinary inference can see. *First test:* Enumerate small transducers with alternate invalidation keys, then test whether the signature predicts held-out joint sequences better than a live-output fingerprint or equally sized saved delta. Report finite-host guarantees separately from learned-host evidence.

**ECR3. Sell joint write windows only when two revisions commute through wake.** Parts: EC13 train for the second edit, EC15 commuting edits, RC7 sub-leases, RC12 commute with B, RC22 shared wake, KM#, KT3 delivery order, FCR10 repair order. *Mechanism:* Before two tenants share a protected subspace, cross their revision orders with wake placement and reserve one frozen joint wake. Distinguish costs caused by contention for the write window from costs removable by training on answer-changing pairs. If co-release fails, allocate sequential windows instead of granting more rank on paper. *New:* Complementary curricula can create a tradable joint write window even when independently safe edits do not compose. *First test:* Compare paired versus marginal practice and bundled versus sequential leases in a two-fact rule learner. Hold counts and total rank fixed; match A/C retention and score the first shared B block after wake.

**ECR4. Tax writers for the repair work they push onto other consumers.** Parts: EC6 indirect evidence, EC18 asymmetric repairs, EC19 counterfactual data swaps, RC6 revocable lease, RC8 fractional rank, RC22 multi-tenant wake, KM#, Y6 plasticity taxes, KT6 irreversible damage. *Mechanism:* Estimate each tenant's added validation, repair and downtime using paired forks with and without its write, including a joint fork to expose shared costs that neither single-tenant audit sees. Settle charges in scarce training steps or wake windows, and let a tenant spend some of that budget upfront on contrasts that lower the supported repair bill. *New:* The tax can fall when a writer teaches the affected consumers to use a real binding, without requiring the model to reserve more parameters. *First test:* Compare rank fees, uniform repair fees and measured marginal charges on a shared rule learner with overlapping consumers. Include cases where separate estimates miss a joint cost; measure realized repair and throughput, not just ledger balances.

**ECR5. Measure the heat of renting the same direction twice.** Parts: EC16 repeated sub-boundary edits, EC17 marginal value before replacement, EC21 delayed stale returns, RC12 commute with B, RC13 re-burial projection, RC14 public drift, TH#, KT1 upkeep rent, Y1 hysteresis. *Mechanism:* Sweep a repeated lease–wake cycle while applying small authorized fact revisions. Separately meter upkeep between leases, the cost of switching back to old service and any accumulated stale response or loss of B after wake. Cooldown is justified only if it changes those trajectories at matched progress, not if it simply delays service. *New:* A direction can be cheap to rent once and expensive to cycle because successive small edits change the future wake basin before top-1 answers move. *First test:* Compare matched total write doses delivered in repeated short leases or one long lease. Use slower schedules, sham wakes and fresh private twins; distinguish transient lag from drift that survives further B.

**ECR6. Make override budgets follow service obligations, not bare step norms.** Parts: EC3 deadline pricing, EC18 unequal repair obligations, RC3 basin radius, RC10 wrong fixed points, RC16 proof-carrying forks, X1 override types, Y1 hysteresis, CE#, CT1 program and learner checks. *Mechanism:* Type a write permit by which binding may change, which callers may temporarily wait, which procedures never lose service, and which public wake is allowed. Estimate a separate write radius per permit on development forks and revoke grants when public reachability checks fail. A low-displacement wake may stop early, but cannot widen a permit by itself. *New:* The same amount of movement can be a valid correction in one contract and an overrun in another, even when both converge to stable outputs. *First test:* Compare fixed norm caps, a global trust region and scoped permits on a rule stream with different urgency and protected exceptions. Include idempotent constant and polarity-flipped wakes as negative controls.

**ECR7. Put a delayed correction in escrow before its lease can close.** Parts: EC3 correction latency, EC20 equalized direct corrections, EC21 delayed stale returns, RC6 revoke while keeping B, RC12 commute with B, DR4 prerequisite stages, DR8 supersession, DR20 bounded certificates, FER4 separate clocks. *Mechanism:* When a correction is due during a lease, separate its already-visible effect from its pending slot payload. Audit both release–wake orders and restart between them. Close the lease only if the remaining payload can be applied later without reviving the retired answer or undoing the gains B already secured; otherwise settle or replace it first. *New:* Reclaiming a protected direction is not the same transaction as discharging a correction still encoded in its optimizer state. *First test:* Interrupt staged toll revisions at several release fractions and compare immediate closure, delayed closure and a same-signal external delta escrow. Score stale derivatives, wake success and B progress after resuming identical public training.

**ECR8. Certify a lease on the distinctions that separate a true wake from a cheap imitation.** Parts: EC2 robust forecasts, EC5 held-out writers, EC8 colliding aliases, RC9 wake retraction, RC17 paired polarity, RC18 adversarial writer, RC19 statistical certificates, BB#, BCE2 wake tomography. *Mechanism:* Use answer-changing paired worlds to choose a small offline audit that predicts which lease-reachable states lead to wrong but stable wakes. Reserve independent private rules and writer paths for evaluation. At deployment, freeze the wake and restrict admission to public checks; if an arm uses labelled audit examples for grants, budget them equally as replay for the rivals. *New:* A cheap certificate can spend its evidence on separating wrong fixed points from valid recovery instead of buying denser samples of ordinary accuracy. *First test:* Fit grants on distinct development worlds and stress with unseen B writers, a linear host and a sham wake. Compare random audits, public contraction-only grants and matched saved deltas.

**ECR9. Insure a bad change forecast with the right to shrink the lease.** Parts: EC2 forecast robustness, EC14 reserve witness budget, EC22 repair queue stress, RC4 drift-based term, RC5 collateral, RC6 revocation, KM#, Y6 credit and insurance, KT5 correlated failures, BB6 request budgets. *Mechanism:* Hold back real repair capacity and a small crossing budget when the current forecast favors long leases. Following a public demand shift, revoke or shorten leases for consumers whose deadlines would otherwise collide, and use the reserve to test the contrast that would change the fallback. Count early revocation, unused reserve and wait time as real costs. *New:* The learner can insure loss of timing slack rather than trying to insure every old model against forgetting. *First test:* Inject correlated revisions and bursts of old-service requests into matched demand traces. Compare uniform short terms, full utilization and reserve-backed leases at equal total bytes and steps, including B progress after emergency wake.

**ECR10. Make cache invalidation and skill dormancy share a failure drill, not a recovery procedure.** Parts: EC4 escape a cache, EC10 cache allocation, EC12 invalidation keys, RC2 lend access, RC15 stopping rules, RC20 break-even, BB8 synchronize access, BB21 preserve B gain, CE#, BCE4 stale-wake ledger. *Mechanism:* On the same drill, change a valid fact, ask an affected consumer to follow it, and wake a dormant independent skill. Score four outcomes: corrected fact, stale cache, restored skill and kept B gain. A cache miss may use an exact current store; a universal wake may not recover an answer from that store and call it retained. Choose the cheapest allocation that passes the independent checks. *New:* Serving can combine cheap invalidation with real bounded burial without confusing retrieval of current facts with recovery of an old private capability. *First test:* Compare exact store plus executor, hybrid cached readers, constrained training and relaxed training plus the same wake on held-out revisions and mixed calls. Use opposite private obligations with identical public wake inputs.

**ECR11. A market that earns future write capacity by making corrections cheaper today.** Parts: EC1 witness bundles, EC2 wrong forecasts, EC3 deadlines, EC5 portable editing, EC7 rate–revision frontier, EC8 aliases, EC11 query compression, EC12 invalidation, EC13 paired edits, EC14 reserves, EC15 commuting edits, EC16 small repeated changes, EC18 asymmetric repair, EC19 attribution, EC21 delayed returns, EC22 queue floods, RC1 wake image, RC2 access leases, RC3 basin radius, RC5 collateral, RC6 revocation, RC7 sub-leases, RC10 wrong fixed points, RC12 wake–B commutation, RC13 re-burial, RC16 proof-carrying grants, RC17 private polarity, RC18 adversarial writer, RC19 statistical bounds, RC20 break-even, RC22 shared wake, ELB#, MS#, KM#, TH#, Y6, X1, Y1, CE#, BB#, DR#, RD1, KT6, FER4, BCE10. *Mechanism:* Buy training contrasts against forecast revision bills, then auction only the protected write windows that a fixed public wake can close at declared service floors. Price each window in real validation, edit-description growth, expected switching work and post-wake opportunity cost. Attach scoped grants, preserve enough state to distinguish legal future edits, and keep a reserve for correlated changes. On expiry or revocation, settle pending edits separately, restore eligible old service, and check that the tenant's B gain survived. *New:* Cheap revision and temporary plasticity become joint products: practice may lower the cost of future corrections while making more write capacity safe to lend. *First test:* Hold model size, initial competence and public demand fixed in an enumerable route world with bridges, exceptions, paired private rules, correlated corrections and restart. Compare uniform curricula plus tuned projection, equal-byte saved deltas plus release, exact-store routing and matched replay. Report training purchases, message bytes, correction delay, lease failures, stale returns, worst old-task service, and B gain after wake separately.
