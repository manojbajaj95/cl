# Cross-pool merges, round 31

Focal sources: `tree/wrong-wake-fixed-points.md` (PR #146, WF#) and `tree/data-only-edit-cost.md` (PR #147, DE#). Crosses with bounded burial (PR #142, BB#), rented constraints (PR #145, RC#), recovery witnesses (PR #139, RW#), future edit cost (PR #144, EC#), the rate–drift law and edit-description lower bound (PR #105, ELB#), Y7/PH19 Hopfield dreaming and attractors, PH10 many-body localization by learning-rate disorder (PR #44), momentum fossils (PR #136, MF#), crossed evidence (PR #143, XE#), and earlier rounds 1–30. These are raw proposals. In a data-only comparison, swap ordinary truthful examples at equal counts and tokens while holding the learner, writer, wake menu and available signals fixed. Test any changed training or wake rule in a separate crossed arm. A public wake settling down is not evidence that it chose the correct private answer. Freeze wake selection before held-out grading, include opposite private obligations, and charge training, audit, anchor bits, fallback, downtime and repairs.

**WED1. Teach withdrawals that leave no attractive road back to the old answer.** Parts: WF8 basin-depth race, WF9 proximal wake, WF13 retirement scars, DE1 withdrawals versus blanks, DE2 reveal a default, Y7 Hopfield dreaming, BCE4 opponent evidence, EC21 delayed stale returns. *Mechanism:* Replace ordinary value-change examples with equally frequent examples that distinguish withdrawal, never-posted status and zero value. Ask whether the same writer and fixed public wake now favor the valid fallback rather than a deep old answer. Run proximity-constrained wakes and attractor suppression only as separate arms, and keep historical requests outside the current default. *New:* A curriculum might reduce stale-basin attraction without an anti-memory write at retirement time. *First test:* Vary the age of a removed toll at matched pre-wake margins. Compare the data swap with equal-example generic crossings and equal-cost replay; score legacy waivers, still-valid historical invoices and exact stale returns separately.

**WED2. Make the wake carry an identity across a rename without carrying the name.** Parts: WF3 path continuation, WF6 burial fingerprints, WF12 era-cue sensitivity, DE3 referent renames, DE15 meaning-preserving placebos, DE21 sacrificial worlds, XE5 nuisance-key swaps, RW6 recipe portability. *Mechanism:* Teach genuine alias changes with the referent held fixed, then compare against surface re-encodings with matched token counts. Use offline wake trajectories to check whether an access path follows the referent through an unfamiliar rename rather than converging on the old token or a newly familiar decoy. The fixed public wake gets no per-recipient alias hint. *New:* A rename can probe whether a wake preserves a skill's referent rather than merely regenerating its customary wording. *First test:* Rename keys and separately split referents in matched synthetic worlds. Hold out alias patterns and writers; compare transcript re-encoding, ordinary crossed evidence and an explicit key store at matched direct-recall efficacy.

**WED3. Spend anchor bits only on the ambiguity the curriculum cannot remove.** Parts: WF16 equivariance, WF19 anchor bits, WF20 co-resident anchors, DE14 competing sources, DE18 shared bits, ELB#, RD7 probe-or-bits receipts, RC5 collateral bits. *Mechanism:* Enumerate which candidate completions are separated by public, truth-preserving evidence and which remain symmetric under every admitted wake. Choose examples that reduce avoidable aliasing, then store the smallest lawful anchor for the remaining private distinctions as a separately priced intervention. Do not claim a data-only reduction in required bits if the public symmetry is unchanged. *New:* The editor can distinguish a training deficiency from a genuine information bill before deciding whether to buy examples or state. *First test:* Enumerate a finite rule family with nuisance source aliases and independent private polarities; validate exact bounds there and compare equal-bit replay, no anchors and full anchors in a learned host.

**WED4. Pin payloads in slow channels without making them expensive to correct.** Parts: WF14 frozen payload, WF15 unique access completion, DE7 masked dependence, DE18 shared bits, PH10 many-body localization, RC1 protect the wake image, BB1 rent the constraint. *Mechanism:* Cross a truthful data swap that exposes masked dependence with a separate arm that assigns fixed random fast and slow learning rates. Ask whether the slow channels keep private distinctions while the fast ones regain access, or simply turn every authorized update into a sticky edit. Compare each data treatment within each fixed optimizer, and test slot and weight interventions rather than assuming the channels follow their assigned roles. *New:* Disorder might localize the payload independently of access recovery, but evidence placement decides whether that separation makes revisions cheaper or harder. *First test:* Compare heterogeneous, uniform and shuffled-rate conditions in a shared route-and-invoice learner. Score safe write cost and post-wake B gain at matched old-service floors, with an equal-byte split adapter as a rival.

**WED5. Use inertial aftereffects to tell a shallow stale wake from a deep one.** Parts: WF2 path bifurcations, WF4 flips along wake, WF8 basin-depth race, DE6 late valid time, DE20 canonical write examples, MF# momentum fossils, DR16 slot aftereffects, FER9 bad wake paths. *Mechanism:* Buy examples that distinguish the date a change was learned from the date it takes effect. Then fork corrected models with matched outputs but crossed exposed and retain-only slots. Log where a fixed wake first chooses the wrong era, and test whether slowing the wake or removing suspect moments makes that choice disappear. *New:* A stale attractor may be hard to reach until optimizer history pushes the learner across its boundary; the training examples may change the boundary without changing the initial answer. *First test:* Vary revision order and age independently in an enumerable invoice world. Compare intact, crossed and norm-matched reset slots with first-update and linearized controls. Score current and explicitly historical queries separately.

**WED6. Audit a wake on the two paths that happened to cancel.** Parts: WF5 independent wakes, WF7 partition invariance, DE10 competing conservation witnesses, DE11 canceling paths, DE16 joint rendering, XE9 multi-output fingerprints, Q9 tomography, BCE2 wake probes. *Mechanism:* Teach equal-final-answer cases using either jointly rendered or separate truthful examples for the two paths. On shadow forks, break the cancellation by one independently verified internal rebind at a time, and compare independent frozen wakes. Stable sums do not certify that the wake restored both components. *New:* An edit can lose one or both dependencies while keeping the advertised answer and the wake's stopping statistic unchanged. *First test:* Construct paired route comparisons in which one cost increase offsets another. Hold final-answer frequencies and competence fixed; score unseen asymmetric edits and compare matched one-at-a-time and coded probes.

**WED7. Teach the wake to preserve the new reason even when the answer never moved.** Parts: WF6 response fingerprints, WF11 revision twins, WF17 ordered fixed points, DE5 fixed invariants, DE8 signed consumers, DE12 same answer, new reason, XE12 read before top-1, EC20 equalized correction. *Mechanism:* Swap in examples where two rule paths yield the same decision but disagree under a later premise change. Audit twins that reach the same post-revision answer for different valid reasons and apply one fixed wake to both. A later separating edit tests whether each twin kept its own reason rather than collapsing onto whichever branch had been trained longer. *New:* A revision can change what the next edit must propagate while requiring no visible output change now. *First test:* Compare exception-first and default-first histories, matching direct recall and current decisions; measure safe second-edit work, unaffected cases and retention after subsequent public updates.

**WED8. Refuse a shared wake when two tenants agree on a number but disagree on its domain.** Parts: WF1 perturbed-restart census, WF10 lease radii, WF18 abstention, WF21 keep B as an anchor, DE4 split shared facts, DE13 interchangeable units, DE17 separable uncertainty, RC22 multi-tenant wakes, BB17 co-revival. *Mechanism:* Use matched examples in which superficially shared facts should split by owner, unit or scope. Audit whether one fixed public continuation restores every promised tenant jointly without flattening distinctions or undoing B. If development evidence finds incompatible outcomes, shrink or partition the lease up front; use public abstention signals only within their tested envelope. *New:* The service footprint of an apparently shared representation may change after an authorized split, even when the current value is the same for both callers. *First test:* Compare one joint wake, sequential lease windows and an equal-byte explicit store on fresh tenants. Test out-of-envelope abstention, held-out conversions, and net B gain after wake.

**WED9. Buy training contrasts against the cheapest wrong wake, not the loudest one.** Parts: WF1 restart census, WF2 bifurcation alarm, WF22 wrong-wake census, DE19 dense collateral audit, DE22 adversarial statistic tests, Y7 Red Queen dreaming, LA7 spurious attractors, RW22 amortized witness search. *Mechanism:* On development worlds, let a challenger look for matched corpora and legal training routes that preserve convergence and benchmark scores while producing distinct wrong fixed points. Use the failures to choose a truthful equal-token swap that targets the missing contrast; do not add a wake-specific loss in the data-only arm. *New:* Hard negative curricula can target the dynamic shortcut that defeats a wake audit, rather than the most visible current prediction error. *First test:* Freeze the selector and writer before seeing new generators; compare uniform crossings, margin matching and equal-cost replay under the same probes. Score silent misses beyond the training protection panel.

**WED10. Price a wake by the expensive edit it makes unnecessary—or the repair it creates.** Parts: WF18 abstaining wake, WF19 anchor bits, WF23 saved-delta break-even, DE9 obsolete winners, DE20 brittle edit examples, DE21 recipe transfer, EC3 deadlines, EC22 flooded repair queues, ECR9 reserve-backed leases. *Mechanism:* Keep a lifecycle ledger for retiring a winner, waking a dormant procedure, and repairing any target that the wake moves back. Purchase training examples using predicted net cost under a fixed set of legal writers and wakes, with a reserve for reconstruction when the policy abstains. Charge fallback and the delays it imposes on other consumers. *New:* Cheap wake-up and cheap editing may substitute in one regime and compound each other's costs in another. *First test:* Run a fixed trace of winner changes and overlapping calls with a held-out demand regime. Compare fixed wake, fixed saved deltas and a predeclared fallback rule at equal bytes and service floors; score stale winner return on novel requests.

**WED11. A curriculum market that purchases safe revisions and dependable returns separately.** Parts: WF1 basin census, WF2 path alarms, WF3 continuation, WF5 independent wakes, WF6 fingerprints, WF8 basin depth, WF9 proximity, WF10 lease radius, WF11 revision twins, WF13 retirement scars, WF14 payload invariance, WF15 uniqueness, WF16 equivariance, WF17 ordered equilibria, WF18 abstention, WF19 anchor bits, WF21 B anchors, WF23 saved-delta break-even, DE1 withdrawals, DE2 defaults, DE3 renames, DE4 splits, DE5 invariants, DE6 valid time, DE7 masked dependence, DE8 signed consumers, DE9 retired winners, DE10 conservation, DE11 cancellation, DE12 reason handoffs, DE13 units, DE14 sources, DE15 re-encoding placebos, DE17 uncertainty, DE18 shared bits, DE19 dense collateral, DE20 canonical writes, DE21 sacrificial worlds, DE22 adversarial statistics, BB#, RC#, RW#, EC#, ELB#, PH10, Y7/PH19, MF#, XE#, BCE4, ECR8. *Mechanism:* Buy ordinary truthful examples that make future writers distinguish revision types, then audit frozen public wakes on lease-reachable states with opposite private obligations and separate current/historical answers. Test whether access returns without overwriting payload, whether stale attractors survive, and which ambiguities require actual bits. Price abstention, fallback and downstream repair against the saved-delta alternative; grant a lease only under its declared service and post-wake B limits. *New:* A learner can get better at editing today's fact without getting any safer at waking yesterday's skill, and the reverse can also be true. A shared lifecycle ledger makes both returns measurable without collapsing them into one accuracy score. *First test:* Use a tiny routing and billing world with late corrections, aliased keys, canceling costs and a burst of old requests. Cross matched curricula with a frozen writer and wake menu before varying either independently. Report revision work, retirements, wrong equilibria, legitimate history, retained private distinctions, downtime and net new-task gain on unseen worlds.
