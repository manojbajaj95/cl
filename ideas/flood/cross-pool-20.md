# Cross-pool merges, round 20

Focal sources: `tree/internal-skill-markets.md` (PR #124, KM#) and `tree/thermodynamics-of-remembering.md` (PR #125, TH#). Crosses with economics (PR #45, E#), physics and mathematics (PR #44, PH#), CL-bench (PR #48, B#), redundant skill populations (PR #116, POP#), and earlier cross-pool rounds. Trades must change real rights to scarce operations. Predictive mismatch, protocol work and repair compute are measured separately; none is assumed to equal physical heat. These are raw and unfiltered.

**KT1. Meter the rent on keeping a skill alive, not the size of its room.** Parts: KM7 reservation maturities, KM10 resource exchange, TH6 housekeeping versus transition cost, E1 parameter rent, Y6 plasticity economy, GP10 role-specific recovery. *Mechanism:* Under a stable recurring stream, randomize small substitutions between rehearsal, protected routing and private rank. Estimate the least ongoing bundle that keeps a held-out service floor; quote it separately from the temporary resources needed to cross into a new traffic regime. Let an incumbent lock in the maintenance bundle while its representative trades its expiring transition claim back. *New:* A scarce capacity slot can have separate enforceable rents for steady service and for changing regimes, even when both permit the same endpoint accuracy. *First test:* Alternate fixed cycles and traffic shifts in a four-skill MLP and compare two-part leases with one-price capacity, static quotas and a same-signal controller. Hold floor, replay steps and byte-time fixed when comparing new-task gains.

**KT2. Make a warranty survive the first lesson after handoff.** Parts: KM13 assignable obligations, KM14 writer bonds, TH19 post-repair rebound, POP3 handoff, FT1 ordinary-history twins (PR #123), CT8. *Mechanism:* A buyer and incumbent fulfill the same service at launch, but release only part of the seller's resources. A randomly selected common future exercises both shadows after the transfer. A larger launch-relative drop in the buyer triggers a pre-funded repair job or route reversion; the launch and future gates remain separately visible. *New:* A skill can transfer ownership without transferring an undetected short-lived liability to the next maintainer. *First test:* Hand off between two matched adapters with different histories; include shocks, non-shock transfers, injected mismatch and optimizer resets. Compare endpoint-only transfer, unconditional overlap and an equally funded central delayed release at matched new-task progress.

**KT3. Trade the order of delivery, not more of the scarce resource.** Parts: KM1 bundle leases, KM7 maturities, TH18 integrated friction, PH4 gap-scheduled switching, GP9 reorder buffers, Y2/Q1. *Mechanism:* Quote swaps between adjacent update windows using estimated relaxation under the intermediate task mixes. Allow two holders to exchange windows without changing their batch allotments or the serving deadline; do not commit a swap until the combined trajectory clears the old floor. *New:* Appointment times can be reassigned to avoid expensive transitions without raising anybody's compute quota or assuming that a gradient snapshot predicts the cost of switching. *First test:* Use the PR #125 ramp toy with two additional protected tasks and fixed minibatches. Compare friction-priced swaps with variance-priced swaps, linear ramps and a same-signal receding-horizon scheduler; charge calibration forks.

**KT4. An auditor earns its next query by changing the action.** Parts: KM12 diagnostic claims, KM24 audit financing, TH7 information feedback, TH13 response/noise diagnostics, Q6 channel attribution, FCU1 reusable experiments. *Mechanism:* Register a cheap default action and a named contingent repair before buying a probe. Randomly withhold some probe results, and use the independently measured savings in failed or unnecessary repairs to renew that probe's budget after charging its forwards and stored state. Buy shared probes via a joint contract if they can change multiple allocation decisions. *New:* A diagnostic can have reusable value even when its score predicts little on average, because it changes which constrained action is chosen. *First test:* Hide routing loss and parameter loss behind identical error rates in a routed bandit. Compare joint probe contracts, prediction-accuracy funding and a centralized value-of-information planner at equal information and repair budgets.

**KT5. Insure failures that travel together, and release cash only on a path that still works.** Parts: KM19 correlated insurance, KM21 independent reinsurance, TH16 rare excursions, PH17 replica overlaps, E10 insurance pools, POP2 failure diversity, BR5. *Mechanism:* Stress alternate holders with paired continuations behind the same trunk and behind genuinely independent routes. Back first losses with pooled replay and simultaneous losses with conditional standby access elsewhere; simulate exercising both rights before counting either as coverage. Price a tail model against empirical joint failures rather than extrapolating from average accuracy or replica count. *New:* Two individually sound policies become unsellable together if they would have to exercise against the same failed pipe or reserve. *First test:* Use a tiny shared MLP with two isolated fallback paths and common-cause shocks at held-out severities. Compare route-aware underwriting with independent premiums and a same-signal centralized reserve, charging standby bytes and false triggers.

**KT6. Charge writers for the damage their return trip cannot reverse.** Parts: KM2 realized interference, KM14 output bonds, TH1 predictive mismatch, TH3 reversible versus irreversible forgetting, PH14 forward/reverse work, E15 externalities, X3 paired learning operators. *Mechanism:* Offer a short repair plan alongside a proposed write; shadow the write, run the declared repair on the shadow, and settle the residual service loss alongside the compute and probe cost of restoration. Compare with a no-write shadow under the same traffic. Never treat an opposite training stream as an exact inverse or use the result to waive a hard preservation floor. *New:* Two edits with identical immediate damage can pay different liabilities because their demonstrated recovery options differ. *First test:* Plant gauge-equivalent moves and matched-mismatch changes with fast or stubborn recovery in a ReLU classifier. Compare settlement on recovery with parameter-norm taxes, post-write KL taxes and cumulative loss constraints.

**KT7. Buy a cooler connection before buying another copy.** Parts: KM3 preemptible rank leases, KM16 portable claims, TH22 hot/cold coupling, PH10 heterogeneous plasticity, POP21 escape lanes, FP2 independent feature barter. *Mechanism:* Let a fragile skill exchange rank for a temporary reduction in coupling to a hot adaptation block, or choose a private route if decoupling also starves it of useful updates. Quote the change using paired coupling perturbations and downstream service under future traffic; restore coupling only after confirming the client still benefits. *New:* A resource right can govern how much learning pressure crosses a shared interface, not just whether a skill has its own slot. *First test:* Train a two-block MLP with a helpful feature that also transmits destructive updates. Compare tradeable coupling limits with fixed fast/slow learning rates, frozen trunk plus rank, and a same-signal coupling controller.

**KT8. Make competing logbooks pay the cost of going silent and returning.** Parts: KM8 shared-feature royalties, KM18 audit appeals, KM22 service-preserving resolution, TH14 bounded recovery tests, PH23 aging, B5 logbook markets, X8 testaments. *Mechanism:* Score active memory programs with untouched queries, but on retirement escrow a capped revival option for any service with no verified substitute. Exercise a randomized fraction of options after later traffic, measure actual recovery cost against never-retired shadows, and fund only portable traces that improve revival; failure to revive within the cap is a bounded result, not proof of fundamental irreversibility. *New:* Selection prices the transition between memory representations rather than letting missing bids erase the cost of an abandoned obligation. *First test:* Use B5's switching-opponent benchmark with rare returns and equal ledger bytes. Compare revival-backed retirement with score-only bankruptcy, static archival and recency plus the same reconstruction budget.

**KT9. Offer someone else the right to cancel a bad learning cycle.** Parts: KM11 joint clearing, KM17 recall rights, TH21 velocity-dependent correction, PH6 order commutators, PH16 asymmetric response, Q1, PO10 (PR #121). *Mechanism:* Test candidate compensating updates in forward and reversed task-block orders at equal counts. A harmed claimant can exercise a pre-sold choice between a jointly priced correction, a block reorder or a deferral. Evaluate the full path: a true cycle correction should suppress order-specific harm without simply slowing the whole stream. *New:* An exposed skill buys the right to change the transition protocol rather than demanding a permanent private gradient or more rehearsal. *First test:* Build a three-task stream with a deliberately asymmetric cycle and an order-insensitive control. Compare leased remedies with PCGrad, ordinary preconditioning and a centralized fixed reorder budget using the same probes.

**KT10. Let multiple clients jointly buy the experiment that separates crowding from forgetting.** Parts: KM5 attention clearing, KM9 rare-service endowments, KM12 diagnosis, TH6 ongoing drive, TH10 budgeted feedback, E20 public funding, Q6, BR7, GP2. *Mechanism:* Give callers a shared claim on a small randomized access window. Measure old service under ordinary arrivals and under restored exposure before spending on either protected ingress or replay. Group funding by distinct demand sources rather than the number of claimants named by the same carrier. *New:* Clients can jointly buy a counterfactual serving condition that neither a gated-off skill nor the router can supply unilaterally. *First test:* In a finite agent queue hide one sound but unreachable old skill and one reachable but degraded skill. Compare collective audit rights with forced exploration, loss-guided replay and a same-information two-stage scheduler, holding call and exposure costs fixed.

**KT11. A reserve can buy a cheaper future instead of waiting for today's loss.** Parts: KM4 curriculum bridges, KM20 post-shock repair, KM23 countercyclical reserves, TH15 retention-constrained transport, TH20 preconditioning, PH3 branch choice, E9 credit lines. *Mechanism:* Before an announced stream jump, let a candidate bridge borrow scarce reserve steps only when a paired forward trial shows it can reach the new service goal sooner without violating the in-flight legacy floor. Pay back using the measured reduction in post-shock restoration demand; cap exposure when several bridges depend on the same reserve. *New:* Borrowing is backed by a tested change in the feasible trajectory rather than a forecast of an abrupt forgetting deadline. *First test:* Use a conflicting task-switch world with alternatives that do and do not need a bridge. Compare fixed pretraining, smooth ramps and identical central reserves at matched total steps, new-task progress and worst old-service excursion.

**KT12. An internal exchange that settles service across the whole learning trajectory.** Parts: KM1 bundles, KM2 liability, KM7 maturities, KM9 endowments, KM11 joint clearing, KM12 diagnosis, KM13 handoff, KM14 warranties, KM15 service chains, KM16 portability, KM18 appeal, KM19 insurance, KM21 independent fallback, KM22 resolution, KM23 reserves, KM24 auditing, TH1 functional mismatch, TH6 housekeeping, TH7 feedback overhead, TH14 bounded recovery, TH15 constrained transport, TH16 tails, TH18 relaxation, TH19 rebound, TH21 protocol correction, TH22 coupling, PH6 commutators, PH14 work, PH17 overlaps, E1 rents, E9 debt, E10 insurance, E20 shared funding, B5 logbook competition, X8 fleet economy, Y6 plasticity economy, Q6 channel attribution, POP1 surviving sets, CT8. *Mechanism:* Clear bundled, expiring claims on rank, route time, updates and independent fallback. Keep separate accounts for continuing service, one-off transitions, audits and post-shock restoration. Make a claim portable only after a demonstrated handoff at launch and after a common training suffix; matched current behavior is not proof of equal stress. Before a large shared write, simulate simultaneous exercise of contingent rights without double-counting capacity. Shadow trials can recommend changes to the schedule or interfaces rather than just quoting a higher price. Settle on measured held-out service and real resource consumption, not on credits or a thermodynamic analogy. *New:* The market's object is a transferable obligation to keep a measurable service alive through future learning, with independent evidence for maintenance, transitions and repairs. *First test:* Couple PR #125's two-task switching world to a rank-limited routed MLP with old shared and independent skills. Alternate stable cycles, shocks and capacity crunches. Compare static quotas, a same-signal controller with all the same actuators but no transferable rights, and a centralized planner allowed to implement the same commitments. Ablate transfer, audit feedback and contingent settlement separately at equal real costs, and report worst-old service through transitions, cascaded defaults and new-task progress.
