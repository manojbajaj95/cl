Raw proposals for isolated synthetic workflows and authorized diagnostic checks.

**GSX1. Forecast the extra recovery work caused by this rollback.** Parts: L3W01 differential suppression, L3W02 incident arrival estimates, L3W03 joint shocks, L3W06 delayed failures, L3W09 fallback contagion, L2W21 rollback shock check, D3-13 compatible histories. *Mechanism:* In an isolated current-world model, compare what each checkpoint still prevents on the same prefixes. Include correlated arrivals, delayed effects and pressure from fallback actions in the recovery workload. If compatible episodes cannot be identified, keep that part of the workload unbounded rather than summing historical marginals. *New:* A rollback certificate can protect shared recovery capacity, not just show that an old checkpoint handles each isolated failure. *First test:* Simulate two recoverable faults that share a queue only on some histories. Compare marginal checks, exhaustive simulation and a screened joint workload bound.

**GSX2. Measure whether a guard works without creating a real incident.** Parts: L3W05 shielded micro-canary, L3W10 benign heartbeat, L3W11 causal guard decay, L3W14 guard exercise, L3W23 guard-presence attribution, L2W07 censoring control, DF03 outcome versus report. *Mechanism:* Use benign exercises on authorized isolated or read-only surfaces to test whether the independent guard still detects and handles a case. Compare matched simulations with and without that guard to distinguish its contribution from prevention or censoring. Date the finding and report unknown when the test only checks receipt. *New:* Zero incidents after rollback would not count as evidence that either the restored checkpoint or the outside guard can still handle them. *First test:* In a toy workflow, let guards decline, disappear independently or acknowledge without acting. Compare passive monitoring, heartbeat-only tests and full staged handling checks.

**GSX3. Treat re-arming as a timed dependency rather than a config toggle.** Parts: L3W13 re-arming handshake, L3W15 re-arming latency, L3W16 transitive guard chains, L3W18 portable guard assumptions, LM26 probe-verifiable handshake, CE4-13 successor opportunity, D5-03 reopening time. *Mechanism:* Record what each independent guard actually does, who can restore it and how long that takes. Verify the chain end to end before replacing the prevention provider, including cases where a downstream safeguard relied on an upstream one. Bind evidence and expiry to the handoff. *New:* A successor can inherit the actual conditions for reversion rather than mistaking a list of external safeguards for independent fallback capacity. *First test:* Build three mock guard providers with duplicated signals and unequal recovery delays. Compare static manifests, local handshakes and timed joint tests.

**GSX4. Retain a verified rollback option instead of keeping only the latest checkpoints.** Parts: L3W04 prevention-preserving partial restore, L3W08 compatible checkpoints, L3W20 temporary shield, L3W22 early hardening, L2W18 scoped revert, L4-27 versioned recovery, D3-20 observable return. *Mechanism:* Rank retained checkpoints by distinct verified restore options, not recency alone. As outside safeguards erode, rehearse partial restore or a temporary independent shield in isolation. Retain a shield only if it preserves present service, covers the missing prevention, and has an explicit exit condition. *New:* A small checkpoint budget can preserve more usable recovery choices than a log of nearby versions. *First test:* Simulate two new skills and an early checkpoint with a uniquely compatible prevention route. Compare latest-only, permanent pinning and a checked compatibility portfolio.

**GSX5. Schedule rollback against demonstrated recovery throughput.** Parts: L3W07 ramped reversion, L3W19 guard-adjusted workload, L3W21 re-arm before reversion, L3W25 independent rollback authorization, L2W26 suppression-aware probation, LW04 delayed recoil, CE4-21 retain verification. *Mechanism:* Require independent evidence of the regression and of a valid recovery path before granting rollback. Use a verified phased schedule only when guarded arrivals remain below recovery capacity over the full lag horizon; suspend the transition if observed lag or backlog exceeds its tested bounds. *New:* A phased restore is an explicit capacity decision rather than an automatic reaction to a monitoring alarm. *First test:* Use an offline queue simulator with bursty faults, delayed service and synthetic false alarms. Compare immediate restore, fixed ramp and evidence-gated reversion.

**GSX6. Release training constraints when independent recovery is proven.** Parts: L3W12 readiness-dependent pinning, L3W17 oversight-preserving policy choice, L3W24 finite plasticity, L3W26 compact joint checkpoint state, LW18 preserve independent fallback, D5-26 state-conditioned sketch, CR24 scoped impossibility. *Mechanism:* Keep a compact dependency record binding each protected preventive effect to checked coverage, a workload bound and time to re-arm. Prefer updates that preserve independent oversight, and relax training constraints only when a substitute has passed the same recovery test. Record the residual uncertainty lost by compressing the record. *New:* A learner can recover plasticity as genuine fallback capacity returns rather than freezing every feature that was once critical. *First test:* Use a finite learner whose guarded features overlap, then add an independently tested replacement. Compare static stiffness, unconditional release and evidence-conditioned pinning.

**GSX7. Make rollback depend on verified recovery, not yesterday's safety net.** Parts: L3W01 differential suppression, L3W02 incident arrival estimates, L3W03 joint shocks, L3W06 delayed failures, L3W09 fallback contagion, L2W21 rollback shock check, D3-13 compatible histories, L3W05 shielded micro-canary, L3W10 benign heartbeat, L3W11 causal guard decay, L3W14 guard exercise, L3W23 guard-presence attribution, L2W07 censoring control, DF03 outcome versus report, L3W13 re-arming handshake, L3W15 re-arming latency, L3W16 transitive guard chains, L3W18 portable guard assumptions, LM26 probe-verifiable handshake, CE4-13 successor opportunity, D5-03 reopening time, L3W04 prevention-preserving partial restore, L3W08 compatible checkpoints, L3W20 temporary shield, L3W22 early hardening, L2W18 scoped revert, L4-27 versioned recovery, D3-20 observable return, L3W07 ramped reversion, L3W19 guard-adjusted workload, L3W21 re-arm before reversion, L3W25 independent rollback authorization, L2W26 suppression-aware probation, LW04 delayed recoil, CE4-21 retain verification, L3W12 readiness-dependent pinning, L3W17 oversight-preserving policy choice, L3W24 finite plasticity, L3W26 compact joint checkpoint state, LW18 preserve independent fallback, D5-26 state-conditioned sketch, CR24 scoped impossibility. *Mechanism:* Track what new prevention suppresses, which outside safeguards still work, how fast they can be restored and whether joint recovery can handle delayed demand. Retain distinct rollback options, verify temporary shields and release protected training constraints only when independent readiness is demonstrated. *New:* Rollback, oversight and future plasticity can share one bounded record without letting reported success stand in for fallback competence. *First test:* Use isolated synthetic workflows with partial checkpoint compatibility, guard-chain decay, contingent bursts and false alarms. Compare checkpoint-only recovery, static pins plus independent audit and the joint policy at matched costs; measure missed hazards, unneeded vetoes and protected service.
