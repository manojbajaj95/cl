# Cross-pool merges, round 23

Focal sources: `tree/cross-generation-inheritance.md` (PR #130, GI#) and `tree/reversible-training.md` (PR #131, RT#). Crosses with Y13's inheritance stack, Y10's Baldwinian assimilation, A2/X8 testaments, PR #37's inherited defect signatures, PR #123's function-matched twins (FT#), PR #111's non-invertible consolidation (NC#), PH11/Y12 spin echo and X2/Y3 weight-space git. These are raw ideas. Exact reversal, useful counterfactual reconstruction, and competence without rewinding are different endpoints. Charge original data, regenerative state, tape, checkpoints, and forward and backward work; score the inherited skill, successor corrections, and untouched old tasks separately.

**IR1. Borrow the ancestor to teach a skill, then uncompute the teacher.** Parts: GI1 world-invariant inheritance, GI7 Baldwinian assimilation, RT1 catalytic learning, RT2 compute–copy–uncompute, Y10, Z5 endosymbiotic consolidation. *Mechanism:* In a scratch branch of the successor, borrow a callable ancestor and fit a portable skill register against paired old/new worlds. Select what to copy by whether it re-derives the skill in the successor without reverting its corrected facts. Then reverse the scratch computation where its inputs and tape permit; the deployable register must work independently of both. *New:* The useful inheritance is the small register that survives removing an adapted teacher, not the ancestor itself or an ability to recover its weights. *First test:* Extend PR #130's overlapping skill and world-correction toy with a reversible scratch learner. Compare with equal-byte distillation, founder replay, and ordinary scratch train-copy-discard; verify what was unwound separately from what the register can do.

**IR2. Ask which parent lesson would have taught the wrong thing in the new world.** Parts: GI2 decision flips, GI3 fossil audits, RT13 training rebase, RT14 single-batch excision, X2 semantic git, Y3 seed log. *Mechanism:* Where a decision has reverted to the old world's answer, replay two small candidate ancestry windows with and without the suspect lesson, then re-execute the same successor handoff. Keep short verified rebase recipes only where removing one window restores the new fact without deleting the inherited skill. Treat ancestry without the needed inputs as unresolved. *New:* The unit of selective rollback can be an episode in the teacher's history rather than a weight coordinate in the student. *First test:* Plant two plausible fossil sources in a three-stage classifier, one entangled with an independent skill. Compare known-checkpoint replay, direct training-data attribution, gated distillation and full exact rebase at matched tape and steps.

**IR3. A testament sells a repair, not just a point in history.** Parts: GI9 train a good ancestor, GI17 inheritance debt, GI22 retirement interview, RT7 replay pebbles, RT17 rewind at inference, A2/X8 testaments, Y13. *Mechanism:* Before deprecation, let the teacher package three competing remedies for each rare old service: a small runnable exercise, a callable source route, or a bounded rewind to recover a training example. Give the successor an independent choice under a first-return latency cap; retire historical storage only after the exercise or live route replaces it. *New:* A testament can name what makes an obligation recoverable and when that recovery is usable in production, rather than merely naming a past implementation. *First test:* Retire an old symbolic agent under a real storage and serving cap. Compare named remedies to static teaching packets, full checkpoints and a centralized budgeted recovery planner; count timeouts and unavailable data as failures.

**IR4. Keep the ancestor's lesson order only where it matters to the successor.** Parts: GI6 developmental order, GI8 plasticity marks, RT8 trajectory transport, RT9 commuting segments, RT10 conflict witnesses, Y2/Q1, GP9 practice reordering. *Mechanism:* Transfer an ordered teaching recipe initially, then test local swaps on the actual successor by reversible forks. Remove precedences only when neither the successor's live old behavior nor its common-future response changes within the declared test scope. Store the surviving edges rather than assuming the ancestor's full curriculum or approximate undo map generalizes. *New:* Successors inherit the demonstrated constraints on order, not fixed weights or an immutable genealogy of practice. *First test:* Use two small architectures and three tasks with the same exposures. Compare predecessor order, random order and measured dependency schedules with exhaustive short permutations and full replay as controls.

**IR5. Promote the successor that keeps the better response to its next lesson.** Parts: GI4 provisional inheritance, GI5 trait versus learnability, GI16 subset selection, RT2 clean retained register, RT19 learned inverse optimizer, FT1/FT4 (PR #123), CT2, KT2. *Mechanism:* Make two successors match current service with different inherited registers. Exercise them with the same legal future lessons and reset or exchange optimizer slots in scratch copies before attributing any divergence. Promote based on protected old and corrected behavior at matched new acquisition; a reversible audition gives a repeatable starting state, not a shortcut around the twin controls. *New:* A lineage can inherit more useful future adaptability without inheriting more of the ancestor's present answers. *First test:* Distill two small ReLU successors with different assimilation histories. Compare endpoint promotion and continuation promotion against injected logit mismatches, linear or fixed-feature controls, full first-step checks, and matched-compute repeated auditions.

**IR6. Quarantine an inherited defect without discarding the successful lineage.** Parts: GI3 triangulation, GI14 oblique transmission, GI18 re-emergence, RT11 dependency DAG, RT12 information-flow tracking, PR #37 inherited signatures, Z4, VI2. *Mechanism:* When peers show the same planted defect signature, fork from their most recent shared teaching event where possible. Re-run its downstream substitutions with the suspect lesson muted and with an independent replacement. Quarantine only routes whose inherited behavior changes in the contrast, and test later updates for resurfacing rather than assuming a silent defect is gone. *New:* Provenance can identify where intervention is worth trying without treating common ancestry as proof of harm or making quarantine a fleet-wide ban. *First test:* In a finite helper-writing relay, plant one inherited signature and one independently recurring mistake. Compare lineage-scoped replacement against node isolation, ordinary retraining and retrospective correlation at equal replay cost.

**IR7. Mark what will become unreachable when a generation deletes its tape.** Parts: GI10 transmission bottleneck, GI17 debt, RT15 garbage attribution, RT16 placement of erasure, NC24 irreducible memory (PR #111), OS1, OS3. *Mechanism:* Before consolidation, compare two histories that the proposed deletion maps to the same retained state. If a permitted future query or teaching job still distinguishes their useful old skills, preserve a small witness or retrain before deleting the supporting history. If they differ only in inaccessible internal details, record the limited scope rather than selling an exact inverse. *New:* An inheritance gate can price an erased distinction by a demonstrated loss of future service, not by the amount of tape reclaimed. *First test:* Enumerate a tiny recurrent learner with known state collisions, then test a matched-byte neural analogue using selective receipts, random tape retention and standard checkpointing.

**IR8. Turn spin echo into a test of what the successor should bother relearning.** Parts: GI12 fragility before the upgrade, GI18 latent inheritance, RT4 approximate inverse dynamics, RT18 reversible thermostat, PH11/Y12 spin echo, TH19 rebound (PR #125). *Mechanism:* In paired shadows, compare an ordinary successor update with a reversible symmetry pulse and a matched sham pulse. Deficit that changes under a pulse with no immediate functional effect nominates a drift-sensitive route for repair; deficit shared across all branches sends the learner to re-grounding rather than more echo steps. Use repeated opposite pulses to see whether the apparent recovery survives a common suffix. *New:* A controlled return protocol can choose between recovering access to a latent old skill and teaching it again. *First test:* In a tiny network with an actual function-preserving symmetry, plant orthogonal drift and overlapping conflict at matched launch deficit. Compare the pulse rule with ordinary replay, reset-and-swap and a constant-rate reverse protocol; do not interpret an empirical echo as an exact inverse.

**IR9. Move the tutor's workshop, then delete the output key.** Parts: GI15 symbolic lore, GI19 niche construction, GI21 objective inference, RT3 gradient-oracle reversal, RT5 counter-based data tape, Y9 oral-tradition codes, VI4 independent stories. *Mechanism:* Ship a compact synthetic practice world in which the successor can re-derive the skill. Split deterministic records that can regenerate declared optimizer inputs from new scenarios that merely approximate the teacher's intent. Permit exact rewind only for the first class; use the second to test whether the successor continues learning where the historical generator never had support. *New:* A single inherited artifact can expose a precise reversible core and an extensible teaching surface without equating synthetic examples with deleted historical data. *First test:* Use deterministic rule worlds followed by a held-out revised law. Compare world transfer with original data/tape, a distilled generator and an ordinary exercise bank at equal storage, checking exactness only where inputs are regenerated.

**IR10. Cross two lineages at the exercise boundary, not at the parameter boundary.** Parts: GI11 cultural ratchet, GI20 recombination, RT11 dependency graphs, RT13 rebase, RT23 removable scaffolds, X2, CT8, FP5. *Mechanism:* Have two ancestors supply verified exercises and declared dependencies for complementary capabilities. Rebase each temporary teaching scaffold onto the actual successor, hold out a third old obligation, and compare either exercise alone with both orders and with the scaffold removed. Keep only the jointly usable succession recipe, including a fallback if one parent carries a world fossil. *New:* Recombination can occur through an audited sequence of teachable actions even when neither parent shares weights or priors with the child. *First test:* Pair two symbolic agents trained on nonoverlapping skills but the same outdated shortcut. Compare joint transfer with best-parent tutoring, pooled distillation, and matched-cost subset search on fresh mixed tasks.

**IR11. A succession system that can graduate skills while retiring their history.** Parts: GI1 invariant skills, GI3 fossil audits, GI4 probation, GI5 learnability, GI6 order, GI7 assimilation, GI9 teachable artifacts, GI10 bottlenecks, GI11 ratchets, GI14 oblique transmission, GI17 debt, GI18 resurfacing, GI19 practice worlds, GI20 recombination, GI22 retirement, RT1 catalytic workspace, RT2 uncompute, RT3 inverse inputs, RT7 pebbles, RT8 trajectory transport, RT9 commutation, RT11 dependencies, RT12 taint, RT13 rebase, RT15 erasure attribution, RT16 placement, RT17 retrieval latency, RT23 scaffolds, Y13, Y10, X8, X2, Y3, Y12, FT1, NC24, OS3, V5, VI2, KT2. *Mechanism:* Let each ancestor offer a callable skill, an exercise, or an executable rebase recipe backed by explicit inputs. A successor uses temporary reversible auditions to learn what can be assimilated without undoing its own corrections. Same-future twins test delayed behavior; localized omission trials diagnose inherited defects. Publish a live-service fallback for unresolved obligations, then retire provisional scaffolds and delete supporting history only where declared future work no longer needs it. Keep exact recovery guarantees separate from bounded evidence of retention. *New:* What passes down the lineage is an evolving set of skills and ways to teach or repair them, while ancestral state can be forgotten deliberately. *First test:* Run several successive small bases in PR #130's corrected-world setting with PR #131-style reversible scratch training. Plant a legacy fossil, a delayed loss and a missing inverse input. Compare plain and gated distillation, checkpoint-plus-replay, and the same-action central planner at matched data, storage and compute. Report skill and correction scores, old service before and after the next lesson, exact-backtrack success, and first-return latency.
