# Cross-pool merges, round 82

Focal source: `tree/root-physical-learning.md` (PR #341, PL#). Older sources: `tree/non-neural-learners-3.md` (PR #340, DS# and FW#; focal in round 81), non-neural learners II (PR #339, FA#), relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#), self-modifying architecture (PR #335, SMA#), sleep and dreaming II (PR #334, SD2-#), evolving representations IV (PR #332, ER4-#), learning economics V (PR #331, LE5-#), brain mechanisms IV (PR #321, BM4-#), and earlier rounds 1–81. Raw proposals for benign synthetic software learners. Freeze lawful observations, old-service deadlines, workloads and full costs.

**LPT1. Sell a reversible regime switch whose return preserves future edit choices.** Parts: PL01 return-point memory, PL02 regime order, DS05 recoverable landing, SD2-09 separating suffix, CR29 version binding. *Mechanism:* Store a compact return state for a nested schedule, but certify resumption with a real correction and an old-reader witness rather than a matching output. A changed rule at the same return point binds only to fresh evidence. *New:* Reversibility could include what the next teacher can do, not just what the model predicts on arrival. *First test:* Run nested shifts in a toy rule stream, then insert an independently revised rule. Compare a regime cache, equal-byte hysteresis and exact return-state checking.

**LPT2. Treat recurring regression as a reason to rehearse, not as evidence that damage has been repaired.** Parts: PL05 cyclic fatigue, PL26 refractory writes, BM4-03 unsettled loss, LE5-14 prefix coverage, FW04 burst tails. *Mechanism:* Schedule a consolidation candidate when protected loss makes repeated excursions. Preserve the actual adverse reads in a separate ledger. During a refractory interval, run a backed old path and confirm the next real native step still advances learning before lifting the hold. *New:* Repeated disruption could trigger a cheaper repair schedule without allowing later recovery to erase old failures. *First test:* Match mean and peak loss across periodic and dispersed toy streams; compare static cadence, a peak trigger and a cycle-sensitive trigger under the same replay budget.

**LPT3. At a moving boundary verify the pending operation before passing write authority.** Parts: PL06 neighbor-gated permission, PL14 switch speed, PL28 pinned ownership, SMA18 unfinished obligations, ER4-21 group cutover, FW07 live transition. *Mechanism:* Use neighboring state to propose a slow or staged ownership change, but require one lawful handoff for all pending readers. Pin the old assignment whenever the new owner cannot finish a due operation. Test prefixes rather than giving a slow schedule credit for endpoint success. *New:* A capacity boundary could move by local evidence while preserving the old service needed during the move. *First test:* Sweep the speed of a toy feature split with two interleaved readers; compare static partitions, global locking and exact staged transitions.

**LPT4. Convert a suspected old-rule error to a paid abstention before regenerating the module.** Parts: PL19 erasure conversion, PL21 common-mode defense, PL22 functional regeneration, CR24 impossibility witness, BM4-26 separating observation. *Mechanism:* Use paired readouts as a software disagreement signal and reserve a small budget of functional queries from intact modules. Reopen normal service only if an independent observation verifies the regenerated read and its next useful correction. When a common-mode change makes both rails wrong together, count that as a failed detector. *New:* A repair pipeline could buy useful bits from other modules only for localized damage rather than replacing a whole predictor. *First test:* Plant independent erasures and correlated errors at equal baseline accuracy. Compare a single head, full replay and budgeted functional regeneration.

**LPT5. Choose where an update lands without hiding who paid for it.** Parts: PL30 local flux, PL32 compensating edit, FW10 paired capacity debit, FA05 local budgets, CR18 unique evidence. *Mechanism:* Impose a local budget on changes to a finite prediction distribution. Report the behavior whose probability mass paid for each new preference, and require the matching debit to fit old floors and timely read obligations along the entire edit. One delivered observation may route work but cannot pay separate obligations twice. *New:* An edit route could carry an explicit account of its side effects even under unchanged initial service. *First test:* Use a small behavior graph with two equally plausible update paths and a protected rare output. Compare unconstrained redistribution, a fixed mask and exact flux budgeting.

**LPT6. Spend novelty space on a new skill only after showing it can survive its first reuse.** Parts: PL08 staged growth, PL13 sparse onset, PL25 skill spacing, DS10 independent destination rows, LE5-24 consumed directions. *Mechanism:* Allocate a local region when ordinary new-task gradients cross a predeclared threshold. Choose extent based on how many independent useful next updates actually survive a first real hop, not solely region distance. If two proposed regions share an indispensable example, count their options jointly. *New:* A growth rule could reserve genuinely distinct future edit options rather than spreading inactive capacity across a model. *First test:* Match active parameters in a toy lattice, varying both physical adjacency and shared example support. Compare distance-based growth, blank reserve and finite route tests.

**LPT7. Keep a rare skill dormant only while its verified recovery remains cheap.** Parts: PL16 rare skill persistence, PL17 minority reserve, PL29 preserve rare variants, LE5-22 independent recovery, CR23 alternative evidence. *Mechanism:* Use a toy collection of learners to compare a dormant reserve and continued low-rate rehearsal. Estimate joint source failures and verify that at least one independent real update revives the skill without relying on a shared hidden cue. If the claimed reserve fails after an ordinary source outage, move the rehearsal budget there. *New:* Rare behavior might be protected by demonstrated revival options rather than by copies all relying on the same dependency. *First test:* Cross rarity and shared outages and compare fixed rehearsal, independent reserves and exact low-budget scheduling.

**LPT8. Move a consolidation boundary only when the actual finite update respects it.** Parts: PL10 active constraints, PL12 overlap radius, PL18 loop redundancy, DS11 uncertain editor, SD2-05 third-edit blocker. *Mechanism:* Use active old constraints to nominate a bounded edit region and encode one small old switch redundantly on that region's boundary. Verify against reachable finite edits and a third intervening update; a correlated change can defeat the redundancy even if parameter distances are small. *New:* A measured local radius could guide allocation without assuming a redundant memory is intrinsically safe. *First test:* Compare parameter-distance screening and exact finite probes on a toy sign loop with isolated and correlated changes.

**LPT9. A continual learner that budgets local transport and verifies each old return.** Parts: PL01 return-point memory, PL02 regime order, DS05 recoverable landing, SD2-09 separating suffix, CR29 version binding, PL05 cyclic fatigue, PL26 refractory writes, BM4-03 unsettled loss, LE5-14 prefix coverage, FW04 burst tails, PL06 neighbor-gated permission, PL14 switch speed, PL28 pinned ownership, SMA18 unfinished obligations, ER4-21 group cutover, FW07 live transition, PL19 erasure conversion, PL21 common-mode defense, PL22 functional regeneration, CR24 impossibility witness, BM4-26 separating observation, PL30 local flux, PL32 compensating edit, FW10 paired capacity debit, FA05 local budgets, CR18 unique evidence, PL08 staged growth, PL13 sparse onset, PL25 skill spacing, DS10 independent destination rows, LE5-24 consumed directions, PL16 rare skill persistence, PL17 minority reserve, PL29 preserve rare variants, LE5-22 independent recovery, CR23 alternative evidence, PL10 active constraints, PL12 overlap radius, PL18 loop redundancy, DS11 uncertain editor, SD2-05 third-edit blocker, PL03 transient memory, PL04 depth penetration, PL07 stacked regimes, PL09 non-equivalent swaps, PL11 replace churned state, PL15 transition order, PL20 nested consolidation, PL23 grid snap-back, PL24 directional errors, PL27 spaced consolidation, PL31 cyclic invariants. *Mechanism:* Use local software budgets to propose staged edits, guarded regime returns and repair; verify actual service and useful future writes rather than trusting a physics analogy. Charge debits, rehearsals and fallbacks, and preserve evidence for cases in which correlated damage breaks redundancy or reversibility. *New:* Pressure to change could be directed toward trainable alternatives without making old losses disappear from the account. *First test:* Compare exact finite planning and matched replay, routing and coding controls on synthetic rule shifts with bursts, rare reads, correlated errors and independent second teachers. Measure old service and real continuation cost.
