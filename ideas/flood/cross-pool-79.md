# Cross-pool merges, round 79

Focal sources: `tree/convergence-relearnability-2.md` (PR #337, IP#) and `tree/root-non-neural-learners.md` (PR #338, NNL#). Crosses with relearnability as memory (`tree/convergence-relearnability.md`, PR #336, CR#), sleep and dreaming II (PR #334, SD2-#), self-modifying architecture (PR #335, SMA#), learning economics III (PR #327, LE3-#), learning economics V (PR #331, LE5-#), evolving representations IV (PR #332, ER4-#), brain mechanisms IV (PR #321, BM4-#), and post-training OOD IV (PR #323, RB#). Raw proposals for benign synthetic continual-learning tasks, built on earlier rounds 1–78. Freeze authorized observations, update budgets, old-service floors and timing.

**FRP1. Buy a finite-response patch whose benefit follows it to a different learner.** Parts: IP01 addressed curvature, IP17 translate the contrast, IP25 causal receipt, CR09 response transplant, LE5-09 preserve update response. *Mechanism:* Translate the difference between patched and unpatched responses rather than either endpoint. Swap the factor between function- and tangent-matched toy learners, testing held-out finite corrections and unrelated old reads before crediting saved examples. *New:* The purchase could be priced on a transportable change in teachability rather than the source learner's history. *First test:* Compare native, transplanted and sham factors on two synthetic regressors matched at launch.

**FRP2. Treat ordered lessons as teaching evidence only if neither example can stand in for the other.** Parts: IP08 ordered read, IP09 distinct observations, IP13 singleton counterexample, CR18 single-origin feedback, SD2-04 aligned draws. *Mechanism:* Require distinct ordinary observations and prove each candidate singleton and pooled step insufficient on the declared fixture. Replay legal orders with matched disturbances and keep an unresolved state when provenance or order is unknown. *New:* An ordered interface might buy the next useful direction without paying twice for a forwarded example. *First test:* Cross repeated, pooled and distinct ordered feedback in a toy nonlinear learner with one duplicated receipt.

**FRP3. Use a transient write only if the old reader is served before the return deadline.** Parts: IP02 blind step size, IP06 timely return, IP07 reachable locality, CR17 reversible retirement, ER4-13 reader deadline. *Mechanism:* Verify finite updates throughout the reachable interval rather than matching launch or final outputs. Give the old reader a paid lawful fallback at intermediate prefixes and allow any reversion only after the useful new update has survived. *New:* A temporary response could buy adaptation without quietly borrowing against a due old read. *First test:* Plant a late return and a correctly timed one at equal endpoints; compare derivative screening, exact prefix checks and an always-on fallback.

**FRP4. Migrate a partly learned correction without carrying the previous optimizer.** Parts: IP12 intervening work, IP18 sparse transport, IP20 optimizer shift, CR10 learning morphism, SMA20 operation translation. *Mechanism:* Carry the partial evidence and signed response through unrelated useful updates and a pruning boundary. Re-encode only the reachable correction effects in the sparse learner. Complete the lesson with fresh evidence and a reset independent optimizer, never copying momentum or a dense teacher. *New:* A useful unfinished correction could survive routine training and representational change. *First test:* Interrupt a two-stage correction at each prefix, comparing checkpoint replay, fixed sparse compression and response transport under matched state bytes.

**FRP5. Admit the third writer only after both prior response claims survive.** Parts: IP04 paired response, IP19 collision ledger, CR15 order locality, SD2-05 third-edit blocker, BM4-22 compatible receipts. *Mechanism:* Rehearse paired and triple prefixes on one compatible history. Keep scoped claims about the actual responses each writer relies on and return the shortest witness if the proposed edit destroys either, even when the separate tests pass. Reuse receipts only across versions they cover. *New:* A modular editor could avoid globally freezing unrelated response directions while detecting shared failures. *First test:* Use a three-writer fixture where two harmless edits consume a redundant cue used by the third; compare pairwise gates and exact joint checks.

**FRP6. Pay for precision only where it changes the outcome of an ordinary correction.** Parts: IP23 equal-byte memory, IP24 precision threshold, CR25 response precision, LE5-24 consumed directions, RB11 actual fallback. *Mechanism:* Allocate bits by whether they change which future updates remain executable after successful first hops. Compare to a matched decoder and a real no-patch teaching fallback, charging extra queries and precision only where they affect a due decision. *New:* A compressed patch could buy a reliable read threshold instead of redundant coefficient accuracy. *First test:* Vary quantization near and far from an unlock threshold under fixed feedback and compute budgets.

**FRP7. Retire a patch only after an independent learner can reacquire its effect.** Parts: IP21 surgical removal, IP27 environment supplies cue, CR23 alternate evidence, ER4-26 dependency erase, SMA14 remove history distinction. *Mechanism:* Remove just the patch factor in a diagnostic copy and require a fresh legal lesson to restore the intended correction before its deadline. Reclaim the factor only after the fallback works following independent intervening edits, and preserve unresolved claims where tested routes fail. *New:* Storage could track scarce teachability rather than response coordinates the stream can now supply on demand. *First test:* Introduce a new public cue while keeping one rare old read ambiguous; compare permanent retention, immediate deletion and scoped reclamation.

**FRP8. Charge the full cost of an installer and test whether its savings survive controls.** Parts: IP22 access frontier, IP25 causal receipt, IP26 matched response negative control, IP28 shared installer, CR32 payment after use, LE5-25 selective loss control. *Mechanism:* Train one response installer on a small family, freeze it and account for its training and storage. Test held-out learners under separately declared observation budgets. Use an observationally matched control to detect undeclared shortcuts and pay only for a selective advantage over equally resourced ordinary teaching. *New:* A reusable writer could lower future teaching cost without shifting the bill to hidden access or installer training. *First test:* Compare per-task fitting, equal-byte spare capacity and a frozen installer across held-out benign tasks, reporting the break-even number of uses.

**FRP9. Wake a dormant correction route only after fresh evidence survives an ordinary update.** Parts: NNL10 cue-triggered dormancy, NNL27 dormant reserve, IP09 distinct observations, IP27 reclaimable response, CR29 versioned relearning, BM4-05 versioned witnesses. *Mechanism:* In a software learner, park the correction interface outside the active update path. Use a candidate return cue to propose reactivation, not to recover a previous answer. Bind it using distinct current-version observations, then require a useful native step and test whether the route can be released once equivalent evidence is routinely available. Keep an old-compatible fallback while the candidate is unconfirmed. *New:* A dormant reserve could save update work without turning a familiar cue into permission to serve stale behavior. *First test:* Alternate synthetic rule settings that share a return cue but change the target. Compare static replay, immediate reactivation and version-gated dormancy at equal storage.

**FRP10. Pass normalized observations from a temporary learner without retaining the learner itself.** Parts: NNL25 disposable scaffolding, NNL35 persistent structure, IP17 contrast transport, IP18 sparse recipient, CR10 learning morphism, SMA20 operation translation. *Mechanism:* Train a temporary software module to expose a stable observation interface for a benign shift. Before retiring it, transport the response contrast to a small recipient and check that ordinary corrections still work in either migration order. Remove and replace the temporary module in a diagnostic copy; charge the remaining interface only for distinctions needed by held-out updates. *New:* A learner could buy useful preprocessing without inheriting the temporary teacher's answers or state. *First test:* Use toy regressors with two input scales and independently held-out targets. Match storage and setup costs against a fixed normalizer, full teacher retention and sparse response transport.

**FRP11. Route a residual to a second learner without billing it as two independent lessons.** Parts: NNL19 coordinated skill group, NNL28 residual clearance, NNL36 in-place capacity, IP19 collision ledger, IP25 causal receipt, CR18 single-origin evidence, RB12 joint certificate. *Mechanism:* In a toy modular learner, hand an unresolved residual to a specialized updater without changing the public read interface. Suppress duplicate correction only after a valid receipt verifies that the recipient actually covers the obligation. Before sharing extra capacity, check the combined writes in one satisfiable history and keep any unresolved scope on the original ledger. *New:* Internal growth could preserve old interfaces and reduce duplicate teaching without hiding unserved work. *First test:* Cross copied residuals, delayed confirmation and incompatible updates on a finite regression task. Compare always training both modules, fixed isolation and confirmed routing at equal capacity.

**FRP12. A market for correction effects that survive the next live edit.** Parts: IP01 addressed curvature, IP02 blind step, IP03 separating derivative, IP04 paired response, IP05 response gauge, IP06 timed return, IP07 path locality, IP08 ordered read, IP09 independent observations, IP10 error tolerance, IP11 stale evidence, IP12 unrelated work, IP13 singleton control, IP14 prefix bound, IP15 net response growth, IP16 response anchor, IP17 contrast transfer, IP18 sparse transport, IP19 collision ledger, IP20 optimizer shift, IP21 removal test, IP22 access frontier, IP23 equal bytes, IP24 threshold precision, IP25 causal receipt, IP26 matched response control, IP27 reclaimable response, IP28 reusable installer, CR09 response transplant, CR10 learning morphism, CR15 order locality, CR17 reversible retirement, CR18 single-origin feedback, CR23 alternative evidence, CR25 precision, CR32 payment on use, SD2-04 aligned draws, SD2-05 third-edit blocker, SMA14 history distinction, SMA20 operation map, LE5-09 preserved response, LE5-24 consumed directions, LE5-25 selective loss, ER4-13 deadline, ER4-26 dependency erase, BM4-22 compatible receipts, RB11 executable fallback, NNL10 cue-triggered dormancy, NNL19 coordinated skills, NNL25 disposable scaffold, NNL27 dormant reserve, NNL28 residual clearance, NNL35 persistent substrate, NNL36 in-place capacity, LE5-06 substitute gradient, LE5-21 step units, LE3-07 second switch, BM4-05 versioned witness, CR29 versioned relearning, RB12 joint certificate. *Mechanism:* Allocate response factors using finite native updates, transport contrasts rather than answers, and reuse an installer only where independent follow-up lessons work. Protect due readers through transient writes and joint collisions. Dormant software reserves, temporary preprocessing and specialized residual learners are worthwhile only if fresh evidence can verify what they still teach after reactivation or replacement. Count evidence by origin, and refill or release memory only after observing a lawful substitute lesson. *New:* A bounded learner could retain genuinely useful ways to relearn while refusing to pay for duplicate evidence, unused precision or inherited optimizer state. *First test:* Enumerate benign finite teaching fixtures with matched outputs, delayed work, missing observations, third-writer conflicts and optimizer changes. Compare exact planning, matched replay, spare rank and ordinary teaching with full storage and compute costs.
