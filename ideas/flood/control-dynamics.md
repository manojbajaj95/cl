Nothing is pre-killed. Every entry is *ambitious but doable* unless marked **Moonshot**, and names the near neighbors I know so the reviewer can rank on novelty alone.

The **frequency-domain group (section A)** is the one I would rank first. Its claim is that forgetting has a transfer function, and the ideas there make fundamental-limit predictions (waterbed, data-rate, resonance) that linear algebra does not settle and a pilot can falsify.

## A. Forgetting in the frequency domain

**CD1. The transfer function of forgetting.** Treat the learner as a plant: the input is the data-mixture signal over time u(t) (fraction of old-task data per step), and the outputs are per-task losses y_k(t). Identify G(z) from chirp or PRBS modulations of u around a nominal schedule. Then predict retention under any schedule by convolution, and design schedules by loop shaping instead of grid search. *Why new:* data-mixing laws (RegMix, DoReMi, 2610.32141) fit static mixture→loss maps, and 2610.09889 identifies a dissipative knowledge-dynamics model under designed excitation. Here the object is a dynamic, frequency-dependent response with poles and zeros, used for synthesis. **Test:** MLP on 2–3 rotated-MNIST tasks, or GPT-2 small on 2 domains. Run 6 sinusoidal mixture modulations at log-spaced periods (5–500 steps), fit a 2-pole/1-zero model, then predict old-task loss under 5 held-out schedules (bursty, decaying, random). **Retention:** old-task accuracy at the end of each held-out schedule versus prediction. **Control:** a static mixing law with an exponential-moving-average input (one time constant). Kill if G(z) is no better than the EMA law (R² gain < 0.1).

**CD2. A waterbed law for continual learning (Bode sensitivity integral).** For a causal learner with bounded update gain, Bode's integral says reducing sensitivity in one band raises it in another. Prediction: define S(ω) as the forgetting response to task switching at frequency ω. Then ∫ log|S(ω)| dω is roughly constant across replay methods at equal update budget. A method that fixes fast alternation must forget more under slow drift, and vice versa. *Why new:* CL theory bounds (2610.14787, 2610.31551, 2604.13460) give per-sequence forgetting, not a conservation law across switching timescales. If true, "no free lunch across timescales" becomes a design rule: you choose where to put the forgetting. **Test:** CD1's rig on 4 methods (plain SGD, ER at 10%, EWC, LwF). Measure |S(ω)| at 6 switching frequencies; check whether the log-area agrees within 15% while the shapes differ. **Retention:** per-frequency old-task accuracy is the measured quantity itself. **Control:** methods ranked by mean forgetting alone. The law is interesting only if the area is conserved while the means differ by more than 2×.

**CD3. Parametric resonance of cyclic curricula (Floquet forgetting).** Alternating A,B,A,B with period T makes training a periodically forced map. Linearize one cycle (the monodromy matrix around the joint solution). Floquet multipliers above 1 at some T mean forgetting *grows* with alternation: a resonance band, as in the Mathieu equation. Prediction: a narrow band of T where interleaving is worse than blocking, at a period set by the old-task curvature and the learning rate (η·λ_old·T ≈ π). *Why new:* anticipatory recovery (2403.09613) observed structure under cyclic training; nobody predicted a resonance band or its location from the Hessian. **Test:** two-task linear regression, then a 2-layer MLP. Sweep T from 1 to 200 at fixed η and compute multipliers from Hessians. **Retention:** both tasks' loss after 50 cycles versus T. **Control:** a smooth monotone fit of loss versus T. Kill if there is no non-monotone band, or if the predicted location is off by more than 2× in T.

**CD4. Input shaping of new-task updates (zero-vibration shaper).** With momentum, the old-task loss responds to a new-task impulse as an underdamped system, so forgetting "rings". The Singer–Seering zero-vibration shaper splits each new-task step into 2–3 impulses with timings and amplitudes chosen from the estimated damping and frequency, cancelling the residual oscillation of the old-task loss. *Why new:* the stability gap (De Lange 2023; continual RL 2611.26252) and optimizer history (2610.03940) describe the transient; shaping the learning-rate pulse train to cancel it is new. **Test:** Adam and SGD-momentum on split-CIFAR-10 with a small CNN. Identify the ringing from one task switch, design a ZV shaper and apply it to the LR schedule at the next switch. **Retention:** stability-gap depth and final old-task accuracy. **Control:** linear LR warmup of equal length. Kill if the shaper does not cut gap depth by at least 30% beyond warmup.

**CD5. Nyquist rate for retention evaluation.** If forgetting and recovery oscillate (CD4), sparse evaluation aliases them. Some reported "no forgetting" results would then be sampling artifacts. Prediction: evaluating at below twice the dominant forgetting frequency gives retention rankings that flip under per-step evaluation. *Why new:* the stability gap was found by dense evaluation. This makes an explicit sampling criterion that an eval protocol must satisfy, and tests whether published method rankings survive it. **Test:** 5 CL methods on split-CIFAR. Rank them at eval every 1, 10, 100 and 1000 steps. **Retention:** old-task accuracy curves at the densest rate. **Control:** final accuracy only. Kill if rankings are stable across sampling rates.

**CD6. Frequency-division plasticity (crossover network).** Like a loudspeaker crossover, split the *gradient stream* into frequency bands with causal filters. The low band goes to slow weights, the high band to fast weights, and a notch removes recurring task-switch frequencies from the slow weights. Skills recurring at a known period get their own band-pass parameter group. *Why new:* multi-timescale and fast weights (Hinton fast weights; continuum memory in Nested Learning/HOPE) split by update period, not by a designed filter bank on the gradient signal. The notch for recurring tasks is new. **Test:** periodic A/B/C stream on an MLP; three parameter groups fed by Butterworth bands versus by update period. **Retention:** accuracy on the task absent for longest. **Control:** EMA slow weights at the same three timescales. Kill if the filter bank does not beat the EMAs by at least 3 pp.

**CD7. Non-minimum-phase skills.** Some skills make old tasks *first worse then better*: an undershoot, the signature of a right-half-plane zero in CD1's G(z). Control theory says such systems cannot be pushed fast without large transient damage, whatever the method. Prediction: CD1's identified zero predicts which new tasks cause transient forgetting even when the final state shows positive backward transfer, and sets a minimum safe learning-rate ramp. *Why new:* backward transfer is measured at the end. This classifies tasks by transient phase behaviour and gives a fundamental speed limit. **Test:** 20 task pairs on an MLP; fit G per pair and classify NMP by sign of the zero. **Retention:** the transient minimum of old-task accuracy. **Control:** gradient cosine between tasks. Kill if the NMP label does not predict undershoot better than the cosine (AUC +0.1).

**CD8. Relay auto-tuning of replay (Åström–Hägglund).** Run a relay experiment: switch bang-bang between old- and new-task data whenever old-task loss crosses a threshold. The limit cycle reveals the learner's critical gain and period. Then set replay ratio and consolidation strength by Ziegler–Nichols-style rules. A two-minute closed-loop experiment replaces a hyperparameter sweep. *Why new:* CL hyperparameters are grid-searched or meta-learned; relay identification is unused. **Test:** split-MNIST and split-CIFAR; relay-tuned ER versus grid-searched ER at 10× the tuning budget. **Retention:** average accuracy across tasks. **Control:** a random-search tuner at the relay's compute. Kill if relay is more than 1 pp below grid search.

## B. Fundamental limits and certificates

**CD9. A data-rate theorem for replay.** Nair–Evans: stabilizing an unstable plant over a channel needs a bit rate at least Σ log|λ_unstable|. Analogy: under new-task training an old skill is "unstable" along directions where new gradients push it uphill, at rates λ_i (from the old-loss Hessian and new-gradient covariance). Prediction: the minimum replay information per step (bits of old-task data or targets) that keeps the skill retained is Σ log(1+η·λ_i). Below it, any replay scheme fails; above it, a coded scheme suffices. *Why new:* replay bounds exist (2608.11690 layer-wise information bounds; 2611.19345 replay-free state migration), but not a rate threshold tied to instability eigenvalues with a sharp phase transition. **Test:** linear teacher-student with 2 tasks. Sweep replay bits per step (quantized targets, sub-sampled examples) and locate the retention transition against the predicted rate. **Retention:** the old-task error transition point itself. **Control:** a "replay fraction" threshold fit on one sweep and transferred to new η. Kill if the predicted rate misses the transition by more than 2× or does not move with η as predicted.

**CD10. IQC certificates for forgetting.** Lessard–Recht–Packard analyze optimizers with integral quadratic constraints solved by small SDPs. Encode "old-task loss" as a performance output and "new-task gradients" as a disturbance in a sector. The SDP then returns a *certified worst-case forgetting bound* for an optimizer plus replay scheme before any training. Sweep the scheme to minimize the certificate. *Why new:* certified CL works on weight balls or held-out anchors (2610.40740). An optimizer-level, data-independent forgetting certificate is new. **Test:** quadratic tasks with known curvature ranges; certify SGD, momentum and EWC. **Retention:** observed worst-case forgetting over 100 random task sequences versus the certificate. **Control:** the naive bound ‖Δθ‖·L. Kill if the IQC bound is not ≥3× tighter, or if it is violated.

**CD11. Learned Lyapunov retention functions as portable skill certificates.** For each old skill, train a small network V_k(θ or LoRA-delta) whose sublevel set {V_k ≤ 1} contains weights where skill k works. Its decrease condition under the replay flow certifies return. Ship V_k with the skill; any later learner adds "V_k ≤ 1" as a constraint. EWC is the special case of a quadratic V. *Why new:* the physics sublevel-set clamp (#28) probes a set; here the certificate is a learned, transferable artifact with a decrease condition, so retention travels with the skill. **Test:** 5 tasks on an MLP; learn V_k from perturbation samples and constrain later training with V_k. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** EWC with an equal-parameter Fisher. Kill if learned V does not beat EWC by ≥2 pp at matched new-task accuracy.

**CD12. Control barrier functions for updates (CBF-QP filter).** h_k(θ) = budget − estimated old-task loss k. Each update is minimally modified so that ḣ_k ≥ −α·h_k: the learner may *approach* the budget at a chosen rate, rather than being blocked (GEM is the α=0 corner). The class-K function α becomes a per-skill knob for "how fast may I spend this skill's margin". For agents, the same filter applies to memory edits, with h measured on probe episodes. *Why new:* GEM/A-GEM and trust regions (2610.40740, 2611.29269) project at a fixed boundary. The CBF rate parameter and its use on discrete agent memory edits are new. **Test:** split-CIFAR with GEM versus CBF at 3 α values. **Retention:** old-task accuracy at a fixed new-task accuracy. **Control:** GEM with a tuned margin. Kill if no α beats tuned GEM.

**CD13. Small-gain composition of agent memory loops from parts.** Measure the incremental gain of each component separately: the writer (how much the memory text changes per unit behaviour change) and the reader (how much behaviour changes per unit memory change). The small-gain theorem predicts loop stability as writer×reader < 1 without running the loop. Components can then be certified and swapped. *Why new:* the killed C2 measured the whole loop's λ. Physics #7 imposes contraction on the whole operator. This is compositional: certify parts, compose guarantees. **Test:** the PR #7 memory-loop rig with 4 writers × 4 readers; measure gains on probes and predict which of the 16 pairs drift. **Retention:** old-skill success after 200 loop steps. **Control:** the 100-step two-order comparator (ledger). Kill if the part-based prediction is below the comparator.

**CD14. Passive adapters that compose by construction.** Constrain every adapter so that its map on the residual stream is passive with respect to a storage function, for example incremental gain ≤ 1 plus a dissipation term. Passive systems stay stable under any feedback or parallel interconnection, so N adapters can be stacked or co-activated without blow-up or interference. *Why new:* LoRA merging and stacking interfere. Lipschitz adapters exist, but passivity as an interconnection-composition law for skills is new. **Test:** GPT-2 small with 8 LoRA skills, passivity-constrained versus free, co-activated 2–8 at a time. **Retention:** each skill's accuracy when co-active versus alone. **Control:** spectral-norm-clipped LoRA at equal rank. Kill if passive does not degrade at least 30% less than clipped as N grows.

**CD15. Dissipation inequality accounting for skills.** Define a supply rate (new-task gradient power injected into old-task directions) and a storage (old-task loss excess). Training satisfies storage(t) ≤ storage(0) + ∫supply − ∫dissipation, with dissipation coming from replay or regularization. Track the three terms online as a "forgetting ledger": a real-time energy account that predicts when a skill's storage will cross the failure budget. *Why new:* forgetting is usually measured after the fact. This is an online energy balance with a falsifiable accounting identity. **Test:** split-MNIST; check that the identity closes within 10% and use it to forecast failure 100 steps ahead. **Retention:** forecast versus actual old-task loss. **Control:** linear extrapolation of old-task loss. Kill if the forecast is no better.

## C. Feedback structure of the learner

**CD16. Internal model principle for recurring tasks.** A regulator tracks a class of references with zero steady-state error only if it contains a model of their generator (Francis–Wonham). For streams where tasks recur (weekly cycles, seasonal domains), add a tiny exosystem module (an oscillator or a learned task-recurrence generator) whose state drives task-conditional weight offsets. Prediction: with the internal model, error on recurring tasks goes asymptotically to zero after the transient; without it, it never does, however much replay. *Why new:* reservoir computing has studied the internal model principle (2610.08987), but not as a CL design rule for recurring tasks. **Test:** periodic A/B/C stream on an MLP; add a 3-state oscillator that gates LoRA offsets, run 20 cycles. **Retention:** error on re-entry into each task per cycle. **Control:** task-ID-conditioned LoRA where the ID must be inferred (no oscillator). Kill if re-entry error does not decay toward 0 faster.

**CD17. Integral action and anti-windup for retention.** Keep an integrator per old skill (accumulated retention error) that raises its replay weight. Prediction from control: when a skill is absent for long, its integrator winds up and, on return, the learner *overshoots* toward the old skill, hurting the new one. Back-calculation anti-windup fixes it. *Why new:* PID gating exists (2610.16129). The windup-overshoot prediction, and anti-windup as the fix, are not in it. **Test:** ER with PI-controlled replay on a stream with long absences. **Retention:** old-task accuracy plus new-task dip at return. **Control:** P-only gating at matched average replay. Kill if no overshoot appears or anti-windup gives under 2 pp.

**CD18. Disturbance-observer updates.** Treat new-task learning as a disturbance on the old-task function. A disturbance observer, a low-pass of the residual between a nominal old-task model's predicted output and measured outputs on unlabeled probes, estimates the disturbance online. A compensating update cancels it inside a bandwidth chosen by the Q-filter. *Why new:* gradient projection removes interference from the current step only. The DOB cancels the *accumulated* disturbance, including nonlinear drift, with a tunable bandwidth. **Test:** split-CIFAR with DOB using 256 unlabeled probes. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** LwF on the same probes at equal compute. Kill if DOB does not beat LwF by ≥2 pp.

**CD19. Model-reference adaptive learning.** The frozen previous model is the reference model. Learn the new task with an adaptation law derived from a Lyapunov function of the tracking error on the old-task output, with σ-modification as principled leakage and projection as bounding. Yields an update rule with guaranteed bounded old-task tracking error. *Why new:* EWC and L2-SP penalize parameter distance. MRAC laws penalize output tracking with a proven adaptation law. **Test:** LoRA on GPT-2 small, two domains. **Retention:** old-domain perplexity. **Control:** LwF with a tuned weight. Kill if MRAC does not beat LwF.

**CD20. Reference governor on labels.** Instead of changing the update, change the *target*. A governor shrinks each new-task target toward the current prediction, just enough that the predicted one-step old-task constraint holds, then relaxes over time as constraints allow. Learning becomes "move the goalpost slowly". *Why new:* label smoothing and LwF-style mixing use fixed weights. The governor computes per-example target mixing from predicted constraint violation. **Test:** split-CIFAR. **Retention:** old-task accuracy at matched new-task accuracy. **Control:** fixed-α target mixing tuned on validation. Kill if the governor does not win.

**CD21. Cascade bandwidth rule for context and weights.** An agent's in-context lessons are the fast inner loop and consolidation into weights is the slow outer loop. Cascade control says the outer loop must be about 5–10× slower in bandwidth or the loops fight. Prediction: the double-dosing oscillation (killed M3, no overshoot found) appears only when consolidation bandwidth approaches lesson-update bandwidth, and M3 may have sat in the safe region. *Why new:* M3 tested one ratio; this predicts a stability boundary in the bandwidth ratio. **Test:** M3's rig with the consolidation period swept from 1× to 20× the lesson-update period. **Retention:** oscillation amplitude of lesson effect across cycles. **Control:** a random-ratio baseline. Kill if no ratio produces growing oscillation.

**CD22. Event-triggered learning.** Write only when a Lyapunov-like trigger fires (the innovation norm exceeds a state-dependent threshold), as event-triggered control does, which guarantees stability with a minimum of transmissions. Fewer writes mean less interference, and the trigger threshold has a closed form. *Why new:* Solowjow–Trimpe "event-triggered learning" targets model updates in control. In deep CL, write gating is heuristic (loss thresholds). **Test:** online stream on an MLP; ET versus a loss-threshold gate at an equal number of writes. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** a random gate at the same write count. Kill if ET does not beat both.

**CD23. Sliding-mode retention.** Define a sliding surface s(θ) = old loss − budget. A switching projection drives the trajectory onto the surface in finite time and keeps it there while new learning proceeds tangentially. Chattering (fast oscillation across the surface) is predicted and removed by a boundary layer. *Why new:* a constrained-optimization analogue with finite-time reaching and a chattering prediction. **Test:** split-MNIST. **Retention:** old-task loss variance and mean. **Control:** A-GEM. Kill if it does not beat A-GEM or chattering is absent.

**CD24. Smith-predictor learning under delayed feedback.** For agents whose outcomes arrive late, learn now on the output of an internal outcome model, and correct by the mismatch when the true outcome arrives. That keeps the loop stable despite delay. *Why new:* the killed delay-compensated gating changed *when to apply*. Gradient IOUs (D2) store promises. The Smith structure learns immediately on a model and corrects later. **Test:** label-delayed split-MNIST (delay 500). **Retention:** old-task accuracy plus learning speed. **Control:** wait-then-train at equal compute. Kill if no speedup at equal retention.

**CD25. Two-degree-of-freedom learning.** Split every skill into a feedforward weight delta, predicted from the task description by a hypernetwork, and a small feedback delta learned from errors. Forgetting hits mainly the feedback part. The feedforward part is recomputable from the description, so retention means "re-run the feedforward". *Why new:* text-to-LoRA hypernetworks generate adapters; this pairs them with a residual feedback adapter and predicts which part carries retention. **Test:** 10 synthetic tasks with descriptions on GPT-2 small. **Retention:** accuracy after 9 later tasks with feedforward recomputed. **Control:** a stored adapter of the same size. Kill if retention is no better per stored byte.

**CD26. Gain-scheduled (LPV) skill families.** Store skills as θ(ρ), affine in a measured scheduling variable ρ (a context embedding), with an LPV stability constraint over the whole ρ range. Interpolating between tasks therefore never passes through a broken region. *Why new:* task-conditioned hypernetworks interpolate without guarantees. This gives a "between-task" guarantee. **Test:** rotated-MNIST with ρ = rotation angle; train at 4 angles. **Retention:** accuracy at trained and intermediate angles. **Control:** linear merging of per-angle models. Kill if LPV is not better at intermediate angles at equal trained-angle accuracy.

**CD27. Tube learning for agent memory.** Plan a nominal memory trajectory (the intended lessons) and keep the realized memory within a tube around it with an ancillary corrector that edits only when the deviation leaves the tube. This bounds drift from noisy writers without freezing memory. *Why new:* trust regions bound a single step; tubes bound the whole trajectory relative to a plan. **Test:** a lesson-store agent with a noisy writer over 200 episodes. **Retention:** old-probe success. **Control:** a fixed per-step edit budget. Kill if the tube does not beat it at equal edits.

## D. Estimation and filtering

**CD28. Kalman smoothing for retroactive consolidation.** Run a Kalman (or Laplace) filter over weights during the stream, then an RTS smoother backward. New evidence revises the *old-task* solution, so the past self gets better: designed backward transfer. Store only filter means and low-rank covariances per task. *Why new:* Kalman filtering of NN weights and VCL exist, but smoothing as a backward consolidation pass is unused. **Test:** linear and last-layer-Bayesian regression with shared latent structure across 5 tasks. **Retention:** old-task error after smoothing versus filtering. **Control:** joint retraining on stored summaries at equal memory. Kill if smoothing does not improve old tasks beyond filtering.

**CD29. Covariance-intersection model merging.** Fusing two fine-tunes of the same base with an unknown cross-correlation: Fisher merging assumes independence, which is overconfident. Covariance intersection gives the consistent fused estimate for any correlation, with weights from a 1-D optimization. *Why new:* merging literature (Fisher, RegMean, TIES) does not treat the unknown correlation between siblings. **Test:** 4 GPT-2 LoRA siblings from one base. **Retention:** each source task's accuracy after the merge. **Control:** Fisher merging. Kill if CI does not beat Fisher on worst-task accuracy.

**CD30. Directional forgetting for OWM/RLS learners.** OWM is RLS-like and loses plasticity as its information matrix grows. Adaptive control fixed the same pathology (covariance windup) with Kulhavý directional forgetting, which forgets only in currently excited directions. *Why new:* known in adaptive control, never imported into OWM, EWC or orthogonal projection. **Test:** 20-task permuted-MNIST with OWM versus OWM plus directional forgetting. **Retention:** first-5-task accuracy. **Plasticity:** last-5-task accuracy. **Control:** a scalar exponential forgetting factor. Kill if directional does not dominate scalar.

**CD31. Persistent-excitation replay.** Retention of a direction requires the data to keep exciting it. Select replay examples to maximize the minimum eigenvalue of the old-task excitation Gramian (in the gradient or feature space of the old task), not loss or diversity. *Why new:* coreset selection maximizes gradient coverage on average (GradMatch) or diversity. The PE criterion targets the weakest excited direction, with a convergence theorem behind it. **Test:** split-CIFAR, buffer of 200. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** herding and random selection at equal buffer. Kill if PE does not beat herding by ≥1 pp.

**CD32. Innovation-whiteness forgetting alarm.** Keep a predictor of the model's own outputs on a fixed unlabeled probe stream. If old-task processing is intact, prediction residuals (innovations) stay white. Forgetting shows as coloured, autocorrelated innovations before accuracy drops. Requires no labels. *Why new:* the killed log-likelihood alarm and physics #2 (critical slowing) use magnitude or recovery time. Whiteness is a distinct statistic. **Test:** split-CIFAR with innovations on 128 probes. **Retention:** lead time of the alarm before a 5 pp old-task drop. **Control:** output KL on the probes. Kill if no lead over KL.

**CD33. Arrival-cost memory for agents (moving-horizon estimation).** The agent re-derives its lessons by optimizing over the last N episodes, plus an *arrival cost* that compresses everything older. That arrival cost is the long-term memory: a text or vector prior with a defined update when the window slides. MHE theory gives stability if the arrival cost under-approximates the true one. *Why new:* agent memories are summaries without a defined role. Here long-term memory has a precise estimation semantics and a rule for how much to trust it. **Test:** a lesson-store agent on a drifting rule world; MHE with a text arrival cost versus a rolling window versus a full log at equal tokens. **Retention:** success on rules from before the window. **Control:** a rolling summary at equal tokens. Kill if no gain.

**CD34. Unknown-input observers for silent task changes.** Model the stream's hidden task switches as an unknown input. An unknown-input observer reconstructs that input from the model's internal state trajectory without labels, so task boundaries and identities can be inferred as "the input that explains the state". *Why new:* task-free CL detects shifts from loss spikes; a UIO estimates the switching signal itself. **Test:** a task-free split-MNIST stream. **Retention:** using the inferred IDs for routing, old-task accuracy. **Control:** loss-spike detection. Kill if boundary F1 is not higher.

**CD35. Closed-loop identification bias in self-training (instrumental variables).** An agent learning from its own trajectories is a system identified in closed loop: its actions correlate with the noise, which biases what it learns (as in OPSD and self-distillation). Inject small known dither into actions and use it as an instrumental variable, so lessons are estimated only from the dither-correlated component. *Why new:* closed-loop identification bias is textbook in control and unaddressed in self-improving agents. **Test:** a bandit or router world where the agent's policy causes a spurious cue. **Retention:** behaviour on old states after learning. **Control:** naive on-policy updates plus random-exploration data at equal samples. Kill if IV does not remove the spurious lesson.

**CD36. SMC adapter populations.** Keep a particle population of LoRA adapters that are reweighted by incoming data likelihood, resampled, and jittered (rejuvenation). Old skills survive as particles whose weights can recover when old data returns. *Why new:* ensembles and population-based training exist; SMC with resampling-as-forgetting, and a principled recovery path, is less explored for LLM adapters. **Test:** GPT-2 small with 16 rank-4 particles, A→B→A stream. **Retention:** speed of A recovery. **Control:** a 16-member ensemble without resampling. Kill if no faster recovery.

## E. Controllability, observability and structure

**CD37. Prompt-reachability retention.** Redefine "retained" as *reachable*: an old skill survives if some short soft prompt still steers the model to it with bounded energy (the controllability of the model with the prompt as input; cf. "What's the magic word?" 2310.04444). Train new tasks with a penalty only on the minimum-energy prompt for old skills, not on the old outputs, so old skills may go dormant but stay one prompt away. *Why new:* CL penalizes output change. Reachability is a weaker, cheaper invariant that allows more plasticity, and it connects to L1 prompt-addressable checkpoints. **Test:** GPT-2 small, 3 tasks; keep a 10-token soft prompt per old task and penalize its loss. **Retention:** old-task accuracy *with* its prompt. **Control:** LwF at equal compute. **Plasticity:** new-task accuracy. Kill if it does not beat LwF on new-task accuracy at equal prompted retention.

**CD38. Kalman decomposition of an update.** Split ΔW into four parts by controllability (does old data excite the direction?) and observability (do old-task outputs see it?). Only the controllable–observable part damages old tasks now; the uncontrollable–observable part damages them *later*, once training excites those directions. Prediction: delayed forgetting comes mostly from the uncontrollable–observable part. *Why new:* null-space methods (Adam-NSCL, OGD) use one subspace from input covariance. The 4-way split predicts *delayed* forgetting. **Test:** 3-task MLP; decompose task-2 updates, then train task 3. **Retention:** task-1 drop attributed per part. **Control:** input-covariance null space. Kill if the split explains no extra delayed forgetting.

**CD39. Balanced truncation of fine-tune deltas.** Compress ΔW with balanced truncation using the input-activation covariance (controllability) and output-gradient covariance (observability). Keep the modes with high Hankel singular values, which carry the skill. *Why new:* ASVD and SVD-LLM use input statistics only; Fisher-weighted SVD uses outputs only. Balanced truncation uses both, with an H∞ error bound. **Test:** compress 10 GPT-2 LoRA skills to rank 2. **Retention:** skill accuracy, plus base-task damage when stacking all 10. **Control:** ASVD at equal rank. Kill if not better.

**CD40. Pinning anchors at driver nodes.** Structural controllability (Liu–Slotine–Barabási) finds the minimum set of driver nodes needed to steer a whole network. Anchor only those parameters (units or heads, chosen from the computation graph's maximum matching) to the old solution and let the rest move. Prediction: anchoring this small set keeps old function better than anchoring the same number of top-Fisher parameters. *Why new:* driver-node selection is used in neural-circuit stimulation (2506.13615), not for choosing which weights to protect. **Test:** MLP, 5 tasks; anchor 5% of units by driver nodes, Fisher or random. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** top-Fisher at equal count. Kill if drivers ≤ Fisher.

**CD41. Geometric disturbance decoupling over many steps.** Wonham's geometric approach finds the largest controlled-invariant subspace inside the kernel of the old outputs. Updates confined to it stay invisible to old tasks *for all future training steps*, not just the next one, because the training dynamics cannot rotate them into view. *Why new:* OGD and null-space methods are one-step. This is multi-step invariance under the linearized training map. **Test:** linearized (NTK) 3-task model; compare one-step null-space with invariant-subspace updates over 500 later steps. **Retention:** task-1 accuracy after task 3. **Control:** OGD. Kill if no gain under the NTK linearization.

**CD42. Feedback-linearizing reparameterization (commuting updates).** Learn an invertible reparameterization φ = T(θ) (a normalizing flow over a LoRA subspace) under which training maps for different tasks become linear and commute, so task updates superpose exactly and order effects vanish. *Why new:* lazy/NTK training is linear by accident and O2 (commutative learning operators) imposes commutativity on the operator. This learns the coordinates in which commutativity holds. **Test:** two-task MLP; learn T so that the A→B and B→A endpoints match. **Retention:** the order gap on both tasks. **Control:** a linearized-network (NTK) training baseline. Kill if T does not cut the order gap by half at equal accuracy.

**CD43. Control allocation for skills (fault-tolerant rerouting).** Implement each skill through redundant actuators (several heads or layers that can each realize it) plus an allocation layer that re-solves, at runtime, which actuators to use as later training damages some. Analytical redundancy detects which actuator "faulted" and reallocates. *Why new:* dropout gives passive redundancy and MoE routes by input. Active reallocation on detected damage is fault-tolerant control. **Test:** GPT-2 small; 1 skill on 3 redundant LoRA heads, damage 2 by later tasks. **Retention:** skill accuracy after reallocation. **Control:** an equal-parameter single adapter plus replay. Kill if no win.

**CD44. Observer-based label-free retention estimate.** Train a Luenberger-style observer on the old model that predicts old-task-relevant internal features from current features. As the model learns, the observer's output estimates the old-task accuracy without old labels, and its correction term says which features drifted. *Why new:* this is CD32's sibling, but it estimates the old *state* rather than raising an alarm. **Test:** split-CIFAR. **Retention:** correlation of the observer estimate with true old accuracy. **Control:** a linear probe trained once on the old model. Kill if r is not ≥0.1 higher.

**CD45. Flat outputs of a network (Moonshot).** A differentially flat system's whole trajectory is an algebraic function of a few flat outputs. Find a small probe set whose outputs algebraically determine the weights (in a LoRA subspace). A skill would then be specified by its flat-output trajectory, and storing a skill means storing a few numbers. *Why new:* this is model stealing turned into a storage primitive. It is a stretch. **Test:** a rank-4 LoRA on a linear layer, where the flat outputs are a minimal probe set. **Retention:** reconstructing the LoRA from stored probe outputs after it was overwritten. **Control:** storing the LoRA itself. Kill if it is not more compact.

## F. Optimal control and planning over learning

**CD46. MPC over training.** Fit a cheap model of the training dynamics (a linearized NTK or CD1's transfer function). At each block, plan the next H blocks of data mix and learning rate to minimize new-task loss under old-task constraints, apply the first, and re-plan. *Why new:* learned data schedulers use RL or bandits. Receding-horizon planning with an explicit learning-dynamics model and hard retention constraints is new. **Test:** 3-task MLP stream. **Retention:** constraint violations and final old-task accuracy. **Control:** a greedy one-step scheduler. Kill if MPC does not beat greedy.

**CD47. The agent's own learning as a control input.** An agent plans "when and what to learn" as actions in its MPC, using a model of its own learning dynamics: how a lesson or fine-tune would change its future success. Learning becomes a planned intervention with a predicted cost to old skills. *Why new:* D3 (hypothetical fine-tuning) gives the simulator; this closes the loop and plans over it. **Test:** a toy agent with 3 skills and an optional "learn" action of known effect. **Retention:** old-skill success over 100 episodes. **Control:** always-learn and learn-on-failure policies. Kill if planning does not beat learn-on-failure.

**CD48. Pontryagin costate replay weights.** Compute the costate of the training trajectory with respect to final multi-task loss (backpropagate through a short window of training) and use it to reweight which past examples to replay. *Why new:* it is close to hypergradient data reweighting (Maclaurin 2015) and meta-replay. The costate as a stored, decaying per-example "importance to the future" is the angle. **Test:** split-MNIST with window 50. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** loss-based replay priority. Kill if no gain.

**CD49. Points of no return in learning (HJ reachability).** Compute the backward reachable set of "irrecoverable" states: checkpoints from which no k-step update sequence on currently available data restores an old skill. Learn a value function that predicts it and refuse updates that enter it. A skill is then lost only when it is *unrecoverable*, not when it dips. *Why new:* the stability gap and recovery literature measures dips. This defines the irreversibility boundary. **Test:** MLP; from 200 checkpoints along A→B, test recovery of A in ≤20 steps of A-only data with a small buffer; fit a classifier. **Retention:** recovery rate of the gated learner. **Control:** a threshold on current A accuracy. Kill if the classifier does not beat it.

**CD50. Viability kernel for plasticity.** Learn the set of learner states from which plasticity can be maintained indefinitely: future tasks remain learnable to a threshold. Filter updates to stay inside it. *Why new:* plasticity-loss work resets or regularizes; the viability kernel is the set-valued object. **Test:** 50-task Slowly-Changing Regression (the ledger rig) with a viability classifier from probe tasks. **Plasticity:** late-task accuracy. **Retention:** early tasks. **Control:** Shrink & Perturb. Note: the plasticity-reserve kill found little spread in this rig, so first check that viability labels vary.

**CD51. Riccati-optimal replay schedules.** In the linear teacher-student CL model, the optimal replay or LR schedule under quadratic retention and new-task costs solves an LQR problem with closed-form Riccati gains. Port the schedule shape to deep networks by matching curvature statistics. *Why new:* CL theory in linear regression (Evron et al., Lin et al.) analyzes fixed schedules; it does not synthesize optimal ones. **Test:** derive the schedule on linear tasks, then apply it to an MLP on split-MNIST. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** a constant replay ratio. Kill if no transfer gain.

**CD52. H∞ learner against worst-case task orderings.** Train against an adversary that reorders or perturbs the incoming stream within a budget, so the learner minimizes the worst-case retention loss (an H∞ filter on the stream). *Why new:* distributionally robust CL focuses on the data distribution, not the *ordering* as a disturbance. **Test:** 5 tasks, with the adversary choosing the order among 10 permutations. **Retention:** worst-order average accuracy. **Control:** a random-order-augmented learner. Kill if worst-case does not improve.

**CD53. Differential game of learner versus forgetter.** Model forgetting as an adversarial player that controls the new-data gradient within a budget. The saddle-point policy is a replay rule guaranteed to hold retention against any stream in the budget class. *Why new:* it is close to CD52 and adversarial CL, but the game value gives a guarantee. **Test:** linear tasks, solved numerically. **Retention:** worst-case stream. **Control:** CD52. Merge candidate.

## G. Attractors, bifurcations and nonlinear dynamics

**CD54. Skills as attractors of a looped block.** In a looped (weight-tied) transformer, store each skill as an attractor of the iterated block. A new skill adds a basin without moving old ones, with basin geometry readable by Jacobian spectra at fixed points. *Why new:* Hopfield and energy transformers store patterns; looped transformers compute. Using looped-block attractors as a continual skill store, with basin-preserving training, is the new part. **Test:** a tiny looped transformer on 4 algorithmic tasks learned sequentially. **Retention:** old-task accuracy and fixed-point stability. **Control:** an unlooped model of equal parameters. Kill if no retention gain.

**CD55. Learning by bifurcation and restoration.** To add a new task, push a scalar knob (temperature, a gate gain) through a pitchfork bifurcation that splits one basin into two. Train the new task into the new branch, then pull the knob back so both basins coexist as a bistable system addressed by the knob. *Why new:* physics #1 (hysteresis) and #3 (homotopy) set up basins or continuation paths; this *creates* capacity by a designed bifurcation. **Test:** a 2-layer MLP with a gain-controlled unit group. **Retention:** old-task accuracy at the restored knob. **Control:** a gate-addressed multi-head. Kill if it is not better per parameter.

**CD56. Heteroclinic skill chains.** Represent multi-step agent skills as heteroclinic channels between saddle states (Rabinovich winnerless competition). Inserting a skill means inserting a saddle into a chain, which preserves order and timing for the other chains. *Why new:* sequence memory in RNNs is usually trained end to end; saddle-chain insertion is a structural, local write. **Test:** a small RNN storing 4 sequences, then inserting a 5th. **Retention:** recall of the first 4. **Control:** fine-tuning on the 5th sequence with replay. Kill if no gain.

**CD57. OGY chaos control of task alternation.** With large learning rates, alternating A/B training is chaotic, and the chaotic set contains unstable periodic orbits that pass through regions good for both tasks. Tiny, well-timed LR nudges (OGY) stabilize such an orbit, giving retention through timed micro-interventions instead of replay. *Why new:* edge-of-chaos tuning was killed (E2), but that tuned a global λ. OGY stabilizes a *specific* orbit with local feedback. **Test:** two-task MLP at a high LR; find UPOs via close returns and apply OGY nudges. **Retention:** both tasks along the orbit. **Control:** a matched-variance random LR jitter. Kill if no stabilized good orbit.

**CD58. Kapitza vibrational stabilization of a joint solution.** A fast zero-mean oscillation can stabilize an equilibrium that is unstable without it (Kapitza's inverted pendulum). Prediction: rapid A/B alternation, or high-frequency weight dithering along old-task directions, creates an effective potential with a minimum at a joint solution that is a saddle under either task alone. There is a sharp frequency threshold, and the Kapitza effective potential predicts which solution. *Why new:* interleaving is known to help; a threshold frequency predicted from an averaged effective potential is not. **Test:** two-task linear and MLP; sweep the dither frequency. **Retention:** joint loss versus frequency, compared with the threshold predicted from the averaged Hessian. **Control:** a monotone fit. Kill if there is no sharp transition at the predicted frequency.

**CD59. Phase-response-timed replay.** In a cyclic curriculum, retention oscillates (CD3). Measure the phase-response curve, meaning how much one replay pulse shifts or damps the oscillation depending on the cycle phase at which it lands, and fire replay only at high-gain phases. *Why new:* replay schedules are uniform or loss-triggered; PRC timing is new. **Test:** periodic A/B on an MLP with 1 replay pulse per cycle at 8 phases. **Retention:** A accuracy. **Control:** uniform replay at equal count. Kill if the best phase is not ≥2 pp above uniform.

**CD60. Kramers clock for forgetting.** Forgetting as noise-driven escape from the old basin predicts an Arrhenius law: rate ∝ exp(−ΔU / T_eff), with T_eff ∝ η/B. Measure barrier height ΔU by nudged elastic band between old and new minima, and predict forgetting time across learning rates and batch sizes from one number. *Why new:* the physics flood's glassy clocks (#23) track parameter age; this is a barrier-height law with a testable exponent. **Test:** 2-task MLP on a grid of η×B. **Retention:** time until old accuracy falls 10 pp. **Control:** power-law fits in η alone. Kill if Arrhenius does not fit better across B.

**CD61. Contraction metrics for weights.** Learn a Riemannian metric M(θ) in which old-task training dynamics are contracting toward the old solution (Lohmiller–Slotine). Apply new-task updates M-orthogonally, so any perturbation from them decays back under the contraction. *Why new:* physics #7 contracts agent memory operators; this learns a metric for weights. **Test:** 3-task MLP with a diagonal-plus-low-rank M. **Retention:** old-task accuracy. **Control:** Fisher-orthogonal updates. Kill if no gain.

**CD62. Port-Hamiltonian skill exchange.** Build the learner so updates are energy-conserving exchanges between skill "ports", with a Hamiltonian that sums skill energies plus a dissipative port for forgetting. Forgetting then must show up as measurable dissipation or as energy transfer to another skill. *Why new:* the Noether/balancedness kill found conserved quantities erased by finite steps and momentum. Here the structure is enforced by a symplectic integrator, which addresses that cause. **Test:** a toy 2-skill network with symplectic updates. **Retention:** skill energy balance versus accuracy. **Control:** a non-symplectic version. High risk; a reviewer should weigh the Noether kill.

## H. Data-driven and fleet ideas

**CD63. Willems memory (fundamental-lemma replay).** By Willems' fundamental lemma, any trajectory of a linear system is a linear combination of columns of a Hankel matrix built from one persistently exciting trajectory. Store an old skill as a Hankel block of its activation trajectories (for an agent: state–action sequences). Regenerate any old-task trajectory, or a DeePC-style plan, by solving a least-squares problem instead of storing examples. *Why new:* DeePC is used in control; as a CL memory with a PE condition for completeness it is new. **Test:** a linearizable toy agent (an LQR task) with 3 old tasks stored as Hankel blocks. **Retention:** old-task cost using Hankel-regenerated trajectories for replay. **Control:** an equal-byte example buffer. Kill if not better per byte.

**CD64. Takens rollback from a scalar trace (Moonshot).** Training is a deterministic map given data and seeds, so a delay embedding of one scalar observable (a random projection of the weights, logged every step) contains the full state up to embedding conditions. Log one float per step, and reconstruct earlier full weight states with a learned decoder. *Why new:* the SM1 seed log stores seeds and scales and regenerates exactly. This stores only an observable, and relies on embedding rather than replay. **Test:** a 1k-parameter MLP; reconstruct the state at step t from a delay vector. **Retention:** old-task accuracy of the reconstructed state. **Control:** storing k random checkpoints at equal bytes. Kill if reconstruction error is large.

**CD65. Synchronization-based skill transfer through a 1-D channel.** Couple two models (teacher and student, same architecture) through a single scalar coupling signal, as chaotic master–slave synchronization does. The student synchronizes to the teacher's skill without data. Fleet members can sync through low-bandwidth channels and drop the coupling once in sync. *Why new:* distillation needs outputs on data. Synchronization transfers state through a minimal coupling. **Test:** two small RNNs where the teacher has an extra task; couple via 1 scalar per step. **Retention:** student old-task accuracy. **Control:** distillation with an equal number of transmitted scalars. Kill if sync does not transfer more per scalar.

**CD66. Formation control in weight space for fleets.** Keep a fleet of models at fixed offsets from each other in a task-relevant subspace, covering different skills, with a formation-keeping controller. When one member forgets, its neighbors pull it back to its slot, and the formation as a whole stores the skill set. *Why new:* federated averaging collapses diversity, and population-based training does not keep a formation. **Test:** 4 MLPs on a 4-task stream, with formation consensus versus FedAvg versus independent training. **Retention:** per-slot old-task accuracy. **Control:** independent members with equal communication. Kill if no gain.
