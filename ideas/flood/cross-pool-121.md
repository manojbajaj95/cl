# Cross-pool merges, round 121

Focal source: `tree/root-never-ending-pretraining.md` (PR #389, NP#). Older sources: `tree/open-ended-learners-5.md` (PR #388, OE5-#; focal in round 120), open-ended learners 3 (PR #386, OE3-#) and root (PR #384, OE#), population learners V (PR #383, PL5-#) and root (PR #379, PL#), test-time learners root (PR #373, TT#), learner–world co-evolution IX (PR #372, L9W#) and VIII (PR #371, L8W#), deployment feedback 5 (PR #357, D5-#) and 4 (PR #355, D4-#), relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#), and sleep and dreaming II (PR #334, SD2-#). Raw proposals for one continuously pretrained lineage in harmless synthetic streams; branches are experimental controls only.

**NPX1. Trade replay only when the substitute preserves the next lesson.** Parts: NP01 exposure-null twins, NP02 signed revision boundary, NP03 complementary batches, NP04 residual evidence, NP05 replay exchange witnesses, NP06 learning-equivalent deduplication, PL5-18 independent observation histories, OE3-03 signed interactions, D5-18 lawful teaching substitutes, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Use matched streams that remove direct and paraphrased exposure without changing the chronology. Test replay pairs and compressed revisions against the original batches, then give both branches the same new lesson. Accept a substitution only for the behaviors, revision boundaries and follow-on lessons that it preserves. *New:* A fragment may replace another for static retention but not for recognizing an older claim's valid interval or learning the next revision. *First test:* On synthetic histories with randomized facts and near duplicates, compare frequency deduplication, residual replay and tested substitutions at equal raw-byte exposure and compute.

**NPX2. Move the tokenizer without silently changing what each byte can teach.** Parts: NP07 byte-prefix update conjugacy, NP08 segmentation disagreement, NP09 boundary-conditioned moments, NP10 occurrence credit, NP11 future-learning witness, NP12 split–merge pulse, NP13 budget-neutral vocabulary swap, OE5-09 order checks, TT10 avoid double dosing, L8W22 scratch round trip. *Mechanism:* Compare train-then-migrate with migrate-then-train on identical raw spans. Conserve each span's replay credit, transport moments only from the relevant boundary roles, and keep dual parses only where their responses still disagree. End both branches at the same tokenizer and subtract the effect of conversion without data. *New:* A tokenizer can preserve predictions while changing how the same evidence will affect the next update. Budget-neutral swaps can be judged on that change instead of token counts. *First test:* Use a fixed-row byte-level learner with ambiguous repeated prefixes. Compare row averaging, role-conditioned transport and dual-parse repair on held-out byte events and later lessons.

**NPX3. Give a model temporary capacity only when a same-shape successor keeps the learning gain.** Parts: NP14 two-shock witness, NP15 symmetry-safe moments, NP16 clone disagreement, NP17 tangent-rank bound, NP18 temporary width, NP19 backward traffic split, NP20 vocabulary–width interaction, PL03 successor stress test, D4-24 future teaching response, L8W17 update-matched plasticity. *Mechanism:* Use a local response-rank estimate to propose where to add capacity, then check ordinary batch pairs in both orders on shadow branches. Compare width and vocabulary edits in both orders without intervening training; verify any gain after returning to the original shape and testing another lesson. *New:* Extra width may be useful for acquiring a dependency even if it is unnecessary to express it; preserving initial predictions is not enough to demonstrate that benefit. *First test:* Create toy polysemy with one shared and two contrasting meanings. Compare permanent growth, an equal-compute fixed-width learner and a temporary loan followed by compression, on held-out batch pairs.

**NPX4. Budget retention using the response to controlled training pulses.** Parts: NP21 signed forgetting impulses, NP22 joint survival, NP23 effective optimizer age, NP24 retention hysteresis, NP25 rewarm shock spectrum, NP26 drift versus diffusion, NP27 recurring upkeep, OE5-26 recovery after pretraining, TT28 frozen shadow, D5-26 lesson survival after compression. *Mechanism:* From identical checkpoints, vary pulse duration, mixture order and gradient variability while holding mean movement approximately fixed. Fit signed cohort responses and test whether they compose on held-out streams. Budget replay against whole requirement bundles, reporting optimizer age only when the projected probe identifies it. *New:* A replay budget can fail even if most facts survive: correlated losses, moment state and order can make a whole dependency bundle unusable. *First test:* Run harmless synthetic A–B–A streams with correlated prerequisite families. Compare cost predictions from token counts, independent forgetting rates and calibrated pulse responses at matched new-domain gain.

**NPX5. Track the newest coherent answer rather than the newest fact seen.** Parts: NP28 bitemporal correction, NP29 randomized reveals, NP30 coherent join cutoff, NP31 incomparable snapshots, NP32 scoped omissions, NP33 calendar translation, NP34 propagation lag, OE25 independent verifier, L9W14 observable predicates, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation. *Mechanism:* Generate independent valid-at and knowable-by witnesses with randomized reveal times. Evaluate operands and derived joins at a shared date; score omissions only in declared complete versions and use paired calendar shifts with explicit exceptions. Compare checkpoints by verified cells without forcing a total order. *New:* A lineage may gain a newer fact while losing the ability to form a coherent old join; reveal timing and derived adoption should be measured separately. *First test:* Build exogenous synthetic histories with late corrections, incomplete lists and lagging consequences. Compare a single cutoff, fact-level dating and independently checked bitemporal join frontiers.

**NPX6. Keep a byte-level audit of what one pretraining lineage still knows and can still learn.** Parts: NP01 exposure-null twins, NP02 signed revision boundary, NP03 complementary batches, NP04 residual evidence, NP05 replay exchange witnesses, NP06 learning-equivalent deduplication, PL5-18 independent observation histories, OE3-03 signed interactions, D5-18 lawful teaching substitutes, CR29 versioned relearning, NP07 byte-prefix update conjugacy, NP08 segmentation disagreement, NP09 boundary-conditioned moments, NP10 occurrence credit, NP11 future-learning witness, NP12 split–merge pulse, NP13 budget-neutral vocabulary swap, OE5-09 order checks, TT10 avoid double dosing, L8W22 scratch round trip, NP14 two-shock witness, NP15 symmetry-safe moments, NP16 clone disagreement, NP17 tangent-rank bound, NP18 temporary width, NP19 backward traffic split, NP20 vocabulary–width interaction, PL03 successor stress test, D4-24 future teaching response, L8W17 update-matched plasticity, NP21 signed forgetting impulses, NP22 joint survival, NP23 effective optimizer age, NP24 retention hysteresis, NP25 rewarm shock spectrum, NP26 drift versus diffusion, NP27 recurring upkeep, OE5-26 recovery after pretraining, TT28 frozen shadow, D5-26 lesson survival after compression, NP28 bitemporal correction, NP29 randomized reveals, NP30 coherent join cutoff, NP31 incomparable snapshots, NP32 scoped omissions, NP33 calendar translation, NP34 propagation lag, OE25 independent verifier, L9W14 observable predicates, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation. *Mechanism:* Use matched raw-stream controls to price replay, compare training responses before and after tokenizer or shape changes, and measure when an update makes a dated dependency usable. Score saved capacity only when it leaves both the old behaviors and the next lesson intact. *New:* One audit can tell apart updated knowledge, exposure that merely refreshed it, and interface changes that altered how it can be learned again. *First test:* Run a harmless synthetic next-token stream with randomized facts, paired revisions, tokenizer swaps and growth loans. Compare ordinary replay and prediction-preserving migration with the combined audit at equal compute and raw-byte exposure.
