# Cross-pool merges, round 14

Focal source: `tree/replay-deadlines.md` (PR #113, RP#). Crosses: the three clocks and survival policies of round 13 (SV#, PR #112 SP#), the rate–drift law (round 11 RD#, PRs #6 and #105), augmented-state memory and mergers (round 12 OS#, PRs #109–111), and B27/Q8 (PR #48 and round 4). PR #113 separates reading a still-decodable target before a shock from writing it back after. Exact full-state mergers constrain target-blind recovery, not replay carrying an independent discriminator; smooth contraction only gives an operational threshold. The escrow result is from the announced-window linear host and carries temporary storage costs. These are raw and unfiltered.

**RE1. Put a fourth clock on the escrow itself.** Parts: RP1 record-before/replay-after, RP2 byte-time, SV1/SV11, RD9, OS1, B27, Q8. *Mechanism:* Track world validity, decoder validity, and legal repair access separately. Once a self-target has been captured, track its own validity: retain it across decoder drift and recode its residual if a checkpoint changes the surviving side information, but discard or revalidate it when the world rule changes. Release it after the live learner passes a disjoint post-settlement check. *New:* A frozen label can outlast its teacher without outliving the claim it represents; its retention cost ends with verified settlement, not simply with the end of training. *First test:* Extend the B27 transmitter world with independent rule changes and learner shocks. Compare wrong restorations, service, and byte-time against one-clock eviction, fixed escrow and insertion-time logits.

**RE2. Escrow the disagreement rather than the teacher.** Parts: RP3 flip-imminent capture, RP11 disagreement replay, RD1/RD2, OS1, SV3. *Mechanism:* Before the shock, encode just the labels or contrasts needed to separate the intents that the next decoder is predicted to confuse. Use a short check to identify when a predicted surviving side channel actually failed; use a priced full-target fallback instead of decoding a guessed residual. *New:* The pulse of temporary memory is sized by the future confusion graph rather than the number of old examples. *First test:* In the PR #113 linear host, plant two equally large drift directions that differ in which targets they confuse. Compare bytes, byte-time and wrong settlements against uniform self-label escrow, random target subsets and fixed low-rank logits.

**RE3. Make a cheap read broad and the live write narrow.** Parts: RP1 split read and write, RP6 self/label value, SP19 decision compression (PR #112), SV2, RD7, OS4. *Mechanism:* Record a compact pre-shock sketch of the teacher on regenerable inputs; use the post-shock model to select which old obligations need settlement. Replay a decoded target only if the sketch still singles out an admissible repair, and abstain on undecidable cases. *New:* Capture is planned for the range of future needs, but expensive replay is charged only where the realized shock calls for it. *First test:* Use a finite two-obligation host with one announced window type and multiple severities. Compare replay calls and residual bits against replaying the whole captured pool, a post-shock-only selector, and matched-budget DER.

**RE4. Treat rounded collisions and fading margins as different markets.** Parts: RP7 tangent horizon, RP8 precision clock, OS8/OS11, SV7, RD1. *Mechanism:* Before a checkpoint, classify distinctions into exact collisions under the actual rounded transition and merely unreliable reads under bounded noise. Pay for exact discriminators in the first group and choose extra measurement, soft targets or abstention for the second. No claim of exact merger from a quantized observation of a higher-precision state. *New:* The system can switch between zero-error escrow and uncertainty-priced recovery within one stream. *First test:* Pair an injective scalar SGD stream with a quantized variant having the same initial accuracies and similar visible decay. Compare targeted capture to a single global margin clock and full-state snapshots.

**RE5. Read once, settle twice, and do not let copied errors compound.** Parts: RP14 cross-shock transfer, RP22 deadline eviction, RD4/RD6, OS9, SV6, Q8. *Mechanism:* Keep a small immutable anchor alongside refreshed self-targets only where the refreshed target cannot be checked independently. After the first shock, let confirmed settlement generate the next teacher; if the two disagree on a live rule, branch the evidence rather than silently overwriting the anchor. *New:* Successive escrows form an auditable chain without retaining every snapshot or assuming each generation is equally trustworthy. *First test:* Alternate two modest shocks that erase different parts of a teacher. Plant one first-generation label error and compare propagated errors, byte-time and retention to fixed anchors, blind rolling escrow and true-label replay.

**RE6. Buy protection only when settlement would arrive too late.** Parts: RP10 post-shock settlement, RP24 prevention split, SV5/SV9, OS5/OS6, RD3. *Mechanism:* Separate an obligation with a hard service floor from one that allows a bounded outage. For a forecast shock, simulate whether escrow-led repair can complete within the allowed interval; protect only the hard or unreachable cases with an actual update constraint. A stored answer does not waive latency or admissibility requirements. *New:* The same target can merit prevention in one service contract and cheap delayed settlement in another; the policy is not just a prediction of forgetting magnitude. *First test:* Give two obligations identical shock loss but different allowed repair lags in the linear host. Compare worst-window breaches and matched-progress cost against all-projection, all-settlement and a maximum-loss scheduler.

**RE7. Run an auction between dormant payloads and a live teacher.** Parts: RP2 byte-time, RP4 snapshot ladder, B27, Q8, SV6, RD9, OS2. *Mechanism:* Let a dormant obligation claim shared temporary storage when its next plausible return makes a pre-shock capture cheaper than reviving its teacher later. Store a raw residual for unreachable but still-live obligations; garbage-collect a snapshot only when no dependent obligation would lose a usable read or fallback. *New:* Retention can be priced as dependency-weighted holding cost rather than treating frequent access as proof of value. *First test:* Combine long-silent transmitter classes with short public drift windows. Compare bytes-times-steps, rare-return accuracy, and peak memory against age eviction, shared frozen teachers and equal-byte-time label buffers.

**RE8. Use a public shock notice to negotiate an edit patch, not a weight snapshot.** Parts: RP20 federated escrow, RP21 agent-note escrow, W8 (round 10), RD5, OS7, SV8. *Mechanism:* Compile pre-window note implications into a compact witness that each receiving learner can check against its own surviving state. Send only the residual needed to make the patch identifiable at that host; withhold a shared patch if independent checks show the hosts still interpret it differently. Charge network delay, retransmission and local writes. *New:* Cross-host escrow transfers what would become ambiguous, not a teacher tied to any one optimizer or model version. *First test:* Run two tiny note readers whose models drift in different directions. Compare local and shared residuals to identical snapshot distillation and full-intent messaging under the same delivery deadlines.

**RE9. Test whether public notices are worth acting on at all.** Parts: RP5 canary clock, RP6 self/label value, RP23 stopping boundary, SV2/SV8, RD7, OS10. *Mechanism:* Use a small labeled canary to validate whether a public notice changes the forecast enough to justify capturing targets. Optimize a stop-or-defer decision under both false alarms and unannounced shocks; if the notice cannot outperform a fixed capture budget after charging the canary, keep the fixed policy. *New:* Forecasting earns its keep only by improving a real capture decision, rather than by beating a hindsight-adjusted loss predictor. *First test:* Corrupt notices in the PR #113 window stream and hold out a shock family. Compare notice-gated capture to fixed capture, canary-only triggering and a hindsight schedule, charging labeled canaries and target holding.

**RE10. Checkpoint only after putting endangered semantics somewhere readable.** Parts: RP1 escrow, RP15 long-horizon installs, NC17 optimizer kernel reset (PR #111), OS12, RD5, SV11. *Mechanism:* Before deliberate non-invertible consolidation, identify which incompatible live contracts would collide in the full learning state. Record a self-target if it is still independently decodable; otherwise require explicit evidence or refuse the consolidation. After the reset, settle the captured target and delete it only when an untouched continuation confirms that doing so leaves the contract recoverable. *New:* One lossy commit can retire optimizer history without discarding live intent, and the escrow is charged to the decision that actually causes the collision. *First test:* In a finite momentum editor, compare pre-reset capture and selective reset against a fixed replay buffer, a weight-only reset and a no-reset baseline. Measure distinct histories retired, old service and target byte-time.

**RE11. A replay system that rents an answer only while the learner cannot supply it.** Parts: RP1 split capture/settlement, RP2 byte-time, RP3 selective capture, RP5 canaries, RP6 self/label ratio, RP7 smooth contraction, RP8 exact rounding, RP10 post-shock repair, RP11 disagreement replay, RP14 relay, RP15 installation horizon, RP20 host transfer, RP22 eviction, RP24 prevention split, SV1, SV3, SV5, SV6, SV8, SV9, SV11, RD1, RD2, RD4, RD5, RD7, RD9, RD11, OS1, OS4, OS6, OS11, OS12, B27, Q8. *Mechanism:* For each live rule, keep separate clocks for truth, decoding, and legal repair. Capture only the information that later decoding is predicted to lose and charge it while held. Let a public shock or optional lossy commit pass before settling where the service contract permits; retain protected bits or prevent damage where waiting would foreclose repair. Invalidate stale targets independently of forecasts about them. *New:* Combines a rate estimate, a transient storage bill, and a service constraint into one editor that explicitly chooses when to read, which distinctions to rent, and when to write. *First test:* Start with the PR #113 announced-window host plus an actual quantized reset, a silent recurring task and one world-rule change. Compare equal-byte-time DER, equal-byte true-label replay, hindsight ordinary self-replay, and prevention-only at matched update and replay budgets including decay; report peak bytes, byte-time, per-window retention and new-task learning.
