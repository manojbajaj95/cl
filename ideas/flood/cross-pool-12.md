# Cross-pool merges, round 12

Focal sources: `tree/optimizer-state-memory.md` (PR #109, OM#), `tree/merger-certificates.md` (PR #110, MC#), and `tree/non-invertible-consolidation.md` (PR #111, NC#). Crosses with the rate–drift law (reviewer top-10 #4, PR #6; PR #105 and round 11 RD#) and earlier tree nodes. Optimizer state is part of the decoder only insofar as legal future actions can actually read it or turn it into behavior. A collision of weights alone is not a merger of full learning states. These are raw and unfiltered.

**OS1. Recode before the checkpoint forgets how to read.** Parts: OM19 optimizer portability, OM23 precision collision, MC2 deadline parity, MC19 residual handoff, RD1/RD4. *Mechanism:* At each scheduled optimizer or checkpoint transition, enumerate which target distinctions the next decoder can still infer from the *actual* state. Recode only distinctions about to disappear, allowing one packet to cover mutually exclusive erasures if the public transition identifies which side survived. *New:* The byte cost of switching optimizers is a prospective code for the new reader, not a dump of all old buffers. *First test:* Use a two-register quantized momentum learner whose reset or rounding erases one of two target bits. Enumerate the rate before and after transition and compare charged packets with storing both bits, saving all buffers and refreshing all edits.

**OS2. Turn a pending moment into an ordinary prediction before its replay option expires.** Parts: OM4 moment impulse response, OM6 discharge into weights, MC7 last replay exercise, MC8 two-part repair, RD9. *Mechanism:* Schedule one target-blind batch that opens a writable direction and discharge a target-dependent moment into the predictor before an upcoming reset. After discharge, delete the moment and check the old task directly. If no qualifying batch is guaranteed before the deadline, store the distinguishing residual instead. *New:* The system chooses between spending a finite-time write opportunity and spending durable bits; a successful recovery from a saved optimizer checkpoint is not counted as ordinary retention. *First test:* Fork a two-feature momentum learner with matching initial predictions and differing pending moments. Vary the arrival time of the same ambiguous batch; compare discharge to warm-start moments, periodic replay and a one-bit explicit repair.

**OS3. Choose which pasts to make identical and which futures to keep apart.** Parts: OM17 protected coarse state, MC4 incompatibility hyperedges, MC21 disposable history, NC1 edit-closed annihilator, SC1 annihilator compiler (PR #108), RD5. *Mechanism:* Compile a single legal consolidation word that contracts within compatible history cohorts. Protect a residual for any group of live targets without a common acceptable answer, even if every pair overlaps. Pick the remaining quotient and a code for the residual jointly. *New:* Consolidation and protection no longer use the same notion of similarity: one deliberately forgets training history, the other maintains enough options to satisfy live contracts. Check equality on the augmented state or exact equality of allowed continuation traces; small output gaps do not certify either. *First test:* Extend the PR #108 register toy with three set-valued targets that overlap pairwise but not jointly. Enumerate safe words and messages, comparing all-pairs routing, state averaging and exact subset synchronization.

**OS4. Split the optimizer's failure bill into unreadable, unwritable and erased.** Parts: OM5 mount-point swaps, OM22 recovery from ambiguous support, MC18 hidden optimizer channel, MC24 replay impossibility, FC19 inaccessible read (PR #107), RD7. *Mechanism:* Use paired forks with identical predictions and target-blind support. Swap optimizer and schedule state, then allow the same recovery actions. Test in order whether a target discriminator still exists, whether anything legal can read it in time, and whether the desired behavior is writable under the retention guard. *New:* A single apparent forgetting event can justify a cheap new decoder, actual memory, or a new write interface; optimizer state counts only where the intervention supports it. *First test:* Plant separate controls for velocity-only information, exact full-state merger and intact target labels behind a blocked write. Compare intervention diagnosis to recovery time and old margin alone.

**OS5. Make the learner bid examples against bits.** Parts: OM7 finite-step protection, OM21 sample overhead, MC1 cut-or-carry, MC10 replay covers witnesses, RD3. *Mechanism:* When an update is about to collapse target distinctions, compare a retention-safe substitute update or replay batch against the smallest carried discriminator. Select one intervention for multiple edges when it keeps the same pairs apart, and price it in examples to matched progress rather than size of the weight correction. *New:* A learner can buy a lower description rate with extra training, but only for specific collision edges it demonstrably removes. *First test:* Enumerate actual updates for a finite two-task learner, then train a tiny linear host with planted collisions. Map bits versus samples at matched new-task accuracy and compare with fixed projection, MIR-like replay and full intent.

**OS6. Write for the worst moment, not the checkpoint.** Parts: OM14 harmonic probability, OM16 first-passage failures, MC16 collision risk versus latency, MC17 revocable scope, RD8. *Mechanism:* Assign each obligation either exact protection or an explicit first-failure probability and a priced fallback. Use an enumerable training kernel to estimate the first failure horizon and schedule residual storage before the risk ceiling, revoking the estimate when the allowed kernel changes. Never interpret an unchanged expected harmonic statistic as an invariant along individual trajectories. *New:* A memory-rate curve can be keyed to uninterrupted service rather than mean forgetting measured at the last checkpoint. *First test:* Create two noisy update sequences whose terminal error rates match but whose transient crossings differ. Compare calibrated failures, latency and memory against final-accuracy and old-margin policies.

**OS7. Trade a little local recovery latency for a fleet-wide rate cut.** Parts: OM8 protected overlap, OM12 common finite-step accounting, MC20 federated split, SC21 cross-backend reset (PR #108), RD5/RD6. *Mechanism:* Give hosts a common observable retention contract, compile different local optimizer moves, and carry discriminators only at sites that can deliver them before a certified local collision. Charge the messages, delay and protected capacity; use an explicit local copy whenever delivery is not guaranteed. *New:* Portability is rated by which distinctions survive or can arrive in time, not by whether replicas share parameter coordinates or optimizer buffers. *First test:* Use two tiny editors with different momentum rules and one planted communication delay. Compare shared residuals with independent full-intent copies and weight alignment, holding service requirements fixed.

**OS8. Learn a checkpoint codec that spends precision where bits are about to disappear.** Parts: OM2 gradient covariance writes, OM23 precision threshold, MC6 code over lifts, NC3 kernel quantization, FC9 precision versus coverage (PR #107), RD2. *Mechanism:* Assign precision to behaviorally meaningful optimizer coordinates only if their quantized codewords remain distinguishable after the next admitted update. Jointly choose the legal write lift and rounding grid; move any remaining discriminator into a separately priced packet, then coarsen the certified nuisance complement to canonicalize obsolete histories. *New:* Precision is purchased for downstream semantic separation, not numerical reconstruction of the current trained state. *First test:* Use a tiny quantized Adam-like learner with matched prediction and effective learning rates at installation. Sweep precision, step and restart, and compare retained contracts with magnitude-based mixed precision and equal-byte explicit intent.

**OS9. When two cheap refreshes fight, price the incoming obligations as a bundle.** Parts: OM9 debit a drift budget, OM10 signed debt, MC22 nested replay, MC5 multi-query toll, RD4. *Mechanism:* Before honoring a scheduled discharge or replay, enumerate what it can erase for other live writes. Form an obligation bundle whenever repairing either member on its own transfers the same deficit back to the other; pay for a jointly legal update or carry a separating code for the bundle. *New:* A repayment cascade can arise from the chosen repair, not from the original drift. A bit ledger becomes a planner for interference between repairs. *First test:* In a two-output affine learner, plant repair orders that oscillate and one joint legal correction. Compare singleton deadlines, equal-call joint replay and the bundle plan on untouched obligations, additional bits and repair calls.

**OS10. Make a learner forget its curriculum without forgetting which optimizer it can safely become.** Parts: OM15 sticky decision regions, OM19 optimizer portability, MC13 branch on a counterexample, NC17 selective moment reset, SC23 obstruction certificate (PR #108), RD5/RD7. *Mechanism:* Find a common reset for histories with the same live contract, then certify it against the *next* optimizer as well as the current one. A distinguishing continuation under the successor optimizer either forces a protected handoff bit or rules out the reset. Compile for the smallest agreed continuation alphabet that still permits useful next edits. *New:* Turn checkpoint portability into a test of whether the future optimizer can still read the intended distinctions, not whether a transfer reproduces current outputs. *First test:* Give matched-prediction forks different pending velocities and two legal successors, one that can promote a nuisance moment into a future read. Compare reset plus optional residual with cold start, full-state handoff and a reset selected only under the current optimizer.

**OS11. A loss ledger that distinguishes what was destroyed from what became cheaper to decode.** Parts: NC9 history bits, NC10 lossy commits, NC17 optimizer kernel reset, OM23 precision collision, MC15 bounds, MC21 disposable history, RD5. *Mechanism:* Within each declared live contract, choose a common lossy commit that merges augmented-state histories, then keep a separate ledger for candidate histories erased and protected message bits still needed across contracts. Run the same accounting against an injective contraction that can shrink output gaps but cannot by itself collapse distinct full states. *New:* A reset can reduce history distinctions yet increase the rate needed to retain intent; the two quantities should not be treated as a single conservation law. *First test:* Enumerate a finite optimizer with one nuisance register, one live bit and one pending moment. Compare exact transition classes, future edit tests retired, and protected rate against a contraction, a weight-only projection and moment reset without a residual.

**OS12. A lossy checkpoint with a receipt for every distinction it throws away.** Parts: OM4 moment pulse, OM5 swap audit, OM6 discharge, OM7 finite-step rule, OM16 first-passage risk, OM19 optimizer swap, OM21 price in samples, OM23 actual precision, OM24 failure-driven memory, MC1 cut-or-carry, MC2 parity, MC4 hyperedges, MC7 replay deadline, MC8 shared repair carrier, MC10 witness-covering batch, MC13 counterexample planning, MC17 revocable scope, MC18 hidden optimizer channel, MC19 residual handoff, MC24 blocked repair, NC1 edit-closed kernel, NC3 quantize the complement, NC9 count erased histories, NC10 lossy commit, NC17 reset moments selectively, NC18 separating suffix, SC1 annihilator (PR #108), SC23 obstruction (PR #108), RD1, RD4, RD5, RD7, RD9. *Mechanism:* At a proposed checkpoint, test candidate singular updates against the continuation-visible reads of the full optimizer state. First discharge moments that can safely become predictions. Pay to prevent the remaining live-contract collisions or store a discriminator with a checked repair path and deadline. Commit only after separating exact history merges from approximate agreement and logging which later edits can invalidate the receipt. *New:* The cost of a checkpoint is split into what the learner can forget now, what it can relearn from target-blind data, and what must survive as evidence; rate–drift becomes a bill the workload can change. *First test:* Use a finite momentum/register editor with two histories, mutually exclusive erasures, one blocked repair, and a stronger continuation added midstream. Compare bits, sample cost, failures before deadlines and tests safely retired against full-state saving, equal-budget replay, and a protected weight projection with ordinary optimizer reset.
