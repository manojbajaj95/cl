Raw proposals; charge rehearsal, migration, validation and steady-state service work separately.

**URX1. Stress-test inheritance on partly competent successors, not blank slates.** Parts: L3-01 hard absorption masks, L3-04 rare exceptions, L3-05 entrenched priors, L3-06 architecture by knowledge grid, L2-25 mocked upgrade, LM07 capacity controls. *Mechanism:* Vary successor architecture, existing knowledge and plasticity separately. Search for costly absorption masks only within compatible states and hold back some successor types for validation. Train for missing tail behavior without overwriting the better-known bulk. *New:* A packet that transfers to an empty proxy can still fail when an otherwise stronger successor knows only part of a skill. *First test:* Compare uniform rehearsal, stress rehearsal and matched full retraining on synthetic successors with the same overall scores but different rare gaps.

**URX2. Spend exact rehearsal on cases that could invalidate the handoff.** Parts: L3-08 tangent approximation, L3-09 interference trigger, L3-10 fresh amendment, L3-25 hub versus leaf margin, D5-14 cheap audit triage, D4-24 actual next gradient. *Mechanism:* Use preliminary overlap and tangent estimates to allocate exact rehearsal, but check held-out low-overlap cases and widen the audit when estimates miss relevant failures. Give high-dependency slices tighter bounds, and require a real subsequent teaching step before certifying their editability. *New:* Approximate rehearsal may save work while keeping the future learning check tied to the successor's native optimizer. *First test:* Build a small upgrade toy with equal present scores and a saturating unit invisible to the linear proxy. Compare exact rehearsal, fixed trigger and calibrated triage at equal work.

**URX3. Diagnose the seam before choosing a bridge or a rebuild.** Parts: L3-03 alternate valid convention, L3-11 convention splice, L3-12 minimal bridge, L3-13 regrounded packet, L3-20 two-way seam diagnosis, LM16 detachable scaffold, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing. *Mechanism:* Separately replay the native upstream output into the old downstream consumer and test the migrated consumer end to end. If the former exposes a format mismatch, compare a bounded bridge with rebuilding downstream on native examples. If only end-to-end fails, test representation interference before reinstalling anything. *New:* The outcome can keep an upstream improvement while selecting a repair for the actual fault instead of restoring the entire predecessor. *First test:* Cross a valid relabeling with a shared-feature collision. Compare symmetric weight migration, unconditional bridge and diagnosis-guided repair under equal old service.

**URX4. Check chains and triple interactions before dropping an apparently absorbed skill.** Parts: L3-14 transitive dependency closure, L3-15 overlap screening, L3-17 higher-order checks, L3-19 targeted shadow probe, L3-22 write-time dependency edges, L2-17 drop-migrate coupling, D3-12 dependency frame, SD2-05 third-edit witness. *Mechanism:* Keep a sparse directed dependency record with positive and negative examples. Use overlap and shadow signals to prioritize tests, not to assert that absent edges are safe. Recheck relevant chains, run covering probes over reachable triples, and retain an explicit unknown where no test or checked frame covers the case. *New:* A cheap screen can narrow expensive multi-skill checking without making pairwise success a guarantee of safe excision. *First test:* Expose a three-stage symbolic skill chain with one triple-only failure and one unreachable combination. Compare pair checks, exhaustive compatible histories and screened chain plus triple checks.

**URX5. Preserve the successor's independent fix while migrating private behavior.** Parts: L3-02 contradicted workaround, L3-16 retire-migrate coupling, L3-18 protect absorbed features, L3-26 oracle bug-fix rehearsal, D3-13 compatible histories, L4-17 preserve unrelated effect. *Mechanism:* Inject an independently verified fix into a rehearsal successor, then test the candidate downstream update against it and the private behavior it must carry. Use projection only as a candidate repair and verify it with real readouts; if the legacy compensation cannot be cleanly separated, leave that slice pending. *New:* A handoff could distinguish a private rule worth inheriting from a later rule whose only purpose was compensating for a retired bug. *First test:* Use a base with a unit conversion bug and a downstream compensating rule; add a real private exception. Compare porting all weights, projection alone and verified fixed-base rehearsal.

**URX6. Teach across implementation swaps, or keep one genuine seam witness.** Parts: L3-21 convention jitter, L3-23 independent implementation swaps, L3-24 seam co-packing, D3-19 reusable peer abstraction, D5-18 equivalent lesson, CE4-21 survive unrelated write. *Mechanism:* Rehearse with alternate valid conventions and independently trained upstream implementations while keeping the downstream obligation fixed. Penalize dependency only when it can be removed without undue predecessor cost. Otherwise store a composite witness, its scope, and a test for a lawful substitute. *New:* A packet can make an unavoidable dependency explicit rather than masking it as a supposedly modular skill. *First test:* Swap two equivalent implementations in a finite grammar and vary whether a downstream treaty depends on explicit formatting. Compare separate packets, full joint storage and scoped seam co-packing.

**URX7. Make a real upgrade failure improve the next rehearsal without becoming a universal veto.** Parts: L3-07 nested upgrades, L3-27 feedback into simulator, L2-20 multihop dispositions, L2-28 localized re-admission, LM32 lineage contract, D4-26 counterexample against veto. *Mechanism:* Encode each validated failure as a bounded perturbation with a matched case that survives it. Rehearse both on the next generation and retain the minimum packet that can reopen a wrongly dropped slice. Expire perturbations when their premises no longer hold. *New:* A successful handoff can get cheaper over time without assuming either monotonic base capability or permanently incompatible interfaces. *First test:* Simulate three generations with a newly absorbed skill that later regresses and a one-off convention shift. Compare accumulating replay, permanent blockers and scoped simulator feedback.

**URX8. Rehearse the next upgrade as a set of compositional changes, not independent skill scores.** Parts: L3-01 hard absorption masks, L3-04 rare exceptions, L3-05 entrenched priors, L3-06 architecture by knowledge grid, L2-25 mocked upgrade, LM07 capacity controls, L3-08 tangent approximation, L3-09 interference trigger, L3-10 fresh amendment, L3-25 hub versus leaf margin, D5-14 cheap audit triage, D4-24 actual next gradient, L3-03 alternate valid convention, L3-11 convention splice, L3-12 minimal bridge, L3-13 regrounded packet, L3-20 two-way seam diagnosis, LM16 detachable scaffold, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing, L3-14 transitive dependency closure, L3-15 overlap screening, L3-17 higher-order checks, L3-19 targeted shadow probe, L3-22 write-time dependency edges, L2-17 drop-migrate coupling, D3-12 dependency frame, SD2-05 third-edit witness, L3-02 contradicted workaround, L3-16 retire-migrate coupling, L3-18 protect absorbed features, L3-26 oracle bug-fix rehearsal, D3-13 compatible histories, L4-17 preserve unrelated effect, L3-21 convention jitter, L3-23 independent implementation swaps, L3-24 seam co-packing, D3-19 reusable peer abstraction, D5-18 equivalent lesson, CE4-21 survive unrelated write, L3-07 nested upgrades, L3-27 feedback into simulator, L2-20 multihop dispositions, L2-28 localized re-admission, LM32 lineage contract, D4-26 counterexample against veto. *Mechanism:* Keep a scoped teaching packet with a list of what it relies on and what later edits must remain feasible. Use varied partly competent successors to predict seams and verify them through exact interventions where risk warrants. Keep independent improvements, migrate only necessary effects, and use real failed handoffs to refine the next rehearsal. *New:* A succession policy could retain the ability to update rare behavior while preserving improved base skills even when their implementations change the behavior of downstream compositions. *First test:* Use synthetic multi-generation upgrades with alternating valid conventions, fixed bugs, rare tails, hidden triple dependencies and a later amendment. Compare full transport, isolated triage with a fixed simulator and composition-aware rehearsal at equal byte and training budgets.
