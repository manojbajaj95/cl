Raw proposals for harmless synthetic environments.

**SCX1. Archive a task as a versioned, amendable teaching environment.** Parts: OE01 regenerative keys, OE02 reward programs, OE03 worlds as persistent state, OE04 experience compiler, OE05 archive description length, L9W14 observable interfaces, L8W22 scratch round trip, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Package a generated toy task with its generator version, observable checker and an approved amendment fixture. Admit a compressed archive representation only after a scratch learner can rebuild the skill and learn the amendment. Keep the previous version where a compressed checker admits a different behavior. *New:* An archive can preserve ways to learn and correct a skill, not merely regenerate familiar examples. *First test:* Compare byte-matched replay, seed-only regeneration and checked executable archives across harmless grammar tasks whose rules later change.

**SCX2. Choose curricula by retained frontier growth rather than repeated rediscovery.** Parts: OE06 forgetting-horizon novelty, OE07 interestingness retention, OE08 frontier expansion, OE11 learning-dynamics forecast, OE23 abstraction progress, OE27 learnability promises, OE32 growth–retention frontier, TT28 no-update shadow, PL5-10 held-out effect family. *Mechanism:* Value a proposed lesson by the held-out tasks it unlocks minus the old skills it damages, using an equal-work no-update or alternative-lesson arm. Distinguish forgotten skills coming back from genuinely new capabilities, and check forecasts on an effect family not used to train the proposer. *New:* A curriculum can be credited for what it makes learnable next without treating every repeated invention as frontier growth. *First test:* Construct toy families with cyclical rediscovery and rare transferable tasks. Compare novelty-only, learning-progress and verified frontier scoring under the same trial budget.

**SCX3. Treat future teachability as a capacity constraint on invented tasks.** Parts: OE10 gradient novelty, OE12 plasticity proposer, OE21 scratch-lesion recovery, OE22 interference rehearsal, OE24 learned retention rule, OE26 voluntary forgetting, OE31 niche allocation, L8W17 matched plasticity audit, D4-24 future lesson, PL27 divergent seed. *Mechanism:* Use cheap scratch trials to propose new task families, then test whether the accepted write leaves old behavior recoverable and unrelated later lessons teachable. Compare alternate budgets and mask only scratch copies of a harmless learner. Retain a low-use niche if it offers an independently verified future learning route. *New:* The mechanism rewards genuinely reusable capacity instead of high effective rank or more forgotten tasks. *First test:* On synthetic streams with one rare but necessary abstraction, compare gradient novelty, ordinary rehearsal and teachability-gated capacity allocation after matched work.

**SCX4. Keep invented goals and commitments stable when representations change.** Parts: OE09 checkpoint disagreement, OE13 goal re-grounding, OE14 observation growth, OE15 intention retention, OE16 autobiographical tasks, L6W12 representation drift, D3-09 witness identity, TT33 checkpointed streams. *Mechanism:* Carry each goal with a behavioral witness and an observable completion condition. After a representation update, compare checkpoint disagreement against that condition before calling it a new skill or a lost intention. New observations can distinguish goals, but cannot silently change what an old commitment means. *New:* A continual curriculum can audit drift in the objectives it pursues independently of how well it solves them. *First test:* Give a toy learner one ongoing obligation, one intentionally renamed goal and one representation rotation. Compare latent-only tracking, fixed IDs and witness-grounded reidentification.

**SCX5. Rehearse an abstraction through the smallest task that distinguishes its role.** Parts: OE17 minimal-pair invention, OE18 curriculum bridges, OE19 compositional rehearsal, OE20 boundary mutations, OE29 broken invariance, OE30 diff-conditioned tasks, D5-18 replacement lesson, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation. *Mechanism:* Generate matched benign continuations that vary one suspected assumption and reuse older abstractions. Prefer a bridge task if it both advances the new capability and still teaches the old one; substitute an approved fixture if the original no longer distinguishes them. Compare teaching on these tasks with simply retaining them as test inputs. *New:* Task invention can localize what went wrong and provide rehearsal without assuming every composition exercises its stated prerequisites. *First test:* Use toy compositional rules where a shortcut passes ordinary rehearsal but fails on a later continuation. Compare exact replay, unconstrained generation and matched bridge tasks.

**SCX6. Verify the checker separately from the learner whenever a world law changes.** Parts: OE25 verifier retention, OE28 world-law dependencies, PL5-18 independently collected observations, D3-13 compatible histories, D5-26 teaching-response retention. *Mechanism:* Keep independent reference cases for the generator and checker, including false positives and obsolete cases. After a synthetic law amendment, retest declared dependencies and sample outside them. Do not count a task as mastered when only the learner-generated checker now agrees with the learner. *New:* The generator, judge and solver can fail independently, so a changing task archive needs checks that cannot all drift together. *First test:* Plant a changed world invariant and a checker that silently accepts a shortcut. Compare joint learner–checker progress, full retesting and dependency-guided retesting with independent controls.

**SCX7. Build a self-curriculum whose worlds can reteach, challenge and correct the learner.** Parts: OE01 regenerative keys, OE02 reward programs, OE03 worlds as persistent state, OE04 experience compiler, OE05 archive description length, L9W14 observable interfaces, L8W22 scratch round trip, CR29 versioned relearning, OE06 forgetting-horizon novelty, OE07 interestingness retention, OE08 frontier expansion, OE11 learning-dynamics forecast, OE23 abstraction progress, OE27 learnability promises, OE32 growth–retention frontier, TT28 no-update shadow, PL5-10 held-out effect family, OE10 gradient novelty, OE12 plasticity proposer, OE21 scratch-lesion recovery, OE22 interference rehearsal, OE24 learned retention rule, OE26 voluntary forgetting, OE31 niche allocation, L8W17 matched plasticity audit, D4-24 future lesson, PL27 divergent seed, OE09 checkpoint disagreement, OE13 goal re-grounding, OE14 observation growth, OE15 intention retention, OE16 autobiographical tasks, L6W12 representation drift, D3-09 witness identity, TT33 checkpointed streams, OE17 minimal-pair invention, OE18 curriculum bridges, OE19 compositional rehearsal, OE20 boundary mutations, OE29 broken invariance, OE30 diff-conditioned tasks, D5-18 replacement lesson, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation, OE25 verifier retention, OE28 world-law dependencies, PL5-18 independently collected observations, D3-13 compatible histories, D5-26 teaching-response retention. *Mechanism:* Treat generated tasks and checkers as versioned teaching assets. Select lessons by what they unlock and what they leave teachable, repair goals against observable witnesses, and test changed world laws with independent examples before trusting a new frontier. Keep separate measurements for solver, judge and curriculum retention. *New:* Open-ended progress can mean acquiring genuinely new capabilities while keeping the ability to recover old ones and amend them later. *First test:* Run harmless synthetic curriculum streams with nested rules, changing validators and rare reusable abstractions. Compare raw replay, novelty-only generation and verified frontier selection with versioned teaching environments at matched work and storage.
