# Cross-pool merges, round 35

Focal sources: `tree/forbidden-combinations.md` (PR #154, FC#), `tree/disjunctive-protection.md` (PR #155, DP#), and `tree/mergeable-writes.md` (PR #156, MW#). Crosses with finite safe regions (PR #152, FS#), slack erosion (PR #153, SL#), unregistered dependence (PR #150, UD#), stored slack (PR #151, SS#), merger certificates (PR #110, MC#), redundant skill populations (PR #116, POP#), adapter contracts (PR #117, AC#), X1/Y1 override budgets, and earlier rounds 1–34. These are raw proposals. A backup is a complete producer–consumer route on a declared request slice, not a surviving slot. Bind certificates to the base, deployed writer, old domain and arithmetic. Distinguish an atomic endpoint, a checked serving order and all permitted orders; measure B utility after the actual merge. Track slack in fact units separately from writer geometry, and charge menus, verification, repairs and fallback.

**DWM1. Merge menus by handing off the last live route before spending it.** Parts: FC4 rotating custody, DP2 make-before-break, MW02 compensation prerequisites, FS03 safe paths, UD1 producer–consumer pairs, POP# redundant holders. *Mechanism:* Each writer offers alternatives meeting its own B floor. Annotate options that install a complete replacement route and options that consume an existing one. Return a checked order in which replacements enter service before the current route is altered; permit an endpoint-only handoff only when intermediate states cannot serve. *New:* The merge exports a reusable handoff plan, not a final checkpoint that leaves the next writer to rediscover who protects the old task. *First test:* Enumerate a two-route ReLU learner with competing writers. Compare greedy merge, freeze-one-route and exact menu selection; score last-route failures and accepted B work.

**DWM2. Price a coalition by the rare route boundary it consumes.** Parts: FC2 cardinality budgets, FC20 common-mode backups, DP10 failure domains, MW19 higher-order conflicts, SS4 joint slack, SL9 rare exceptions, FP4 shared-risk reserves. *Mechanism:* Identify request slices where distinct backups share a consumer boundary. Use independently verified fact perturbations to measure its reserve; use realized finite writes to check whether the coalition exhausts it. Record a forbidden coalition only where it fails the scoped contract, not merely because the writers share parameters. *New:* The same three menu choices can be safe for common requests and jointly wipe out the last route on a rare exception even though every pair passes. *First test:* Plant overlapping failure domains and then test unplanted ones in a learned toll model. Compare pairwise checks, holder counts and coalition-aware menus at equal verifier budget.

**DWM3. Offer a smaller write only if it still deserves a place on the menu.** Parts: FC13 writer-relative clauses, DP6 future flexibility, DP20 marginal protection price, MW01 useful alternatives, MW07 repair a menu, FS15 future options, X1 override budgets. *Mechanism:* Ask each writer for substitutes that meet a fixed task floor and use different surviving routes. Rank choices by the future edits they keep available on a past-only menu. If all useful alternatives consume the same last route, export a conflict or abstain rather than selling a trivial step as a merged task. *New:* A merge can pay for preserving choice on tomorrow's writer without taking credit for barely moving today's one. *First test:* Give each tenant identical proposal compute and compare same-delta scaling, diverse alternatives and a fixed projection; score follow-on B gains and worst old-service prefixes.

**DWM4. Separate a writer's override budget from the budget it inherits from neighbors.** Parts: FC3 required co-changes, DP4 reference-anchored slack, MW05 commuting transitions, MW09 selective coordination, UD14 anchored residuals, SS20 multi-hop slack, Y1 hysteresis, USR6 dual budgets. *Mechanism:* Debit each allowed prefix against acquisition-anchored constraints; do not reset them when a new menu is appended. Allow independent commits only when combined traces preserve the required route assumptions. Otherwise bind the compensation or order explicitly and keep fact tolerance distinct from permitted rule distortion. *New:* An update may spend less than its individual budget yet need another writer's cooperation to be legal at a shared consumer. *First test:* Compare rolling budgets, independent quotas and ordered contracts on alternating revisions that move shared gates. Include disjoint-coordinate writes with overlapping downstream effects.

**DWM5. Transport the proof only where the new adapter has not changed the meaning of a backup.** Parts: FC6 clauses over subspaces, DP9 route identity after rebasing, MW13 exact factorization, MW14 mixed remainders, MW18 moved consumers, AC#, FP7 caller-side checks, FS10 writer transport. *Mechanism:* Transport live-route witnesses through the new writer map only on domains proved to land inside the old certified domain. Recheck mixed adapter directions and the downstream writer–consumer pair; keep valid unaffected branches, but require a new certificate before a moved consumer counts as backup. *New:* Equal writer spans can leave different alternatives admissible, even at identical current answers. *First test:* Cross exact rebases, rotations of bounded domains and same-output changed consumers. Compare full rechecking, cached premise reuse and span-only transport on held-out writes.

**DWM6. Learn conflicts from failed merges, and retire the ones a real repair makes obsolete.** Parts: FC7 conflict-driven learning, FC9 group testing, DP8 ablation-to-write transfer, DP19 clause compaction, MW15 unaffected premises, MW22 impossibility versus abstention, UD12 independent audits, MC#. *Mechanism:* On a failed shadow trace, minimize the failing combination under the fixed writer and request slice. Cache the clause with its premises; after a migration or route repair, test whether those premises still hold. Compress clauses only when implication covers the promised menu; absence from the registry is not permission. *New:* A negative result from one merge can save later search without becoming a permanent ban on a write that has since gained a replacement route. *First test:* Alternate recurring conflicts and route repairs. Compare endpoint retries, static clauses and premise-scoped reuse with independent audits and a finite trace oracle.

**DWM7. Spend replay on a new route only if it widens the merge contract.** Parts: FC18 replay when one backup remains, FC21 survivor-led repair, DP12 last-route trigger, DP15 replay for a second route, MW08 certified dropout fallback, MW21 unknown next merger, POP#, SL19 weakest tolerance. *Mechanism:* When the last independent route's reserve erodes, use the same replay budget either to reinforce it or to teach a separate producer–consumer path. Admit extra menu choices only after realized writes demonstrate that the new route survives a different failure domain. Distinguish a live fallback from a route that needs a priced recovery. *New:* Replay can buy more future composability even when it buys no immediate accuracy. *First test:* Match pre-merge A/C service and replay cost, then expose both learners to previously unseen writer combinations. Compare route replacement, single-route reinforcement and fixed redundancy.

**DWM8. Merge two groups without carrying their entire proof histories.** Parts: FC19 series–parallel clauses, DP17 treewidth budget, DP19 compaction receipts, DP22 global survivor after merge, MW04 merge already-merged groups, MW11 multiple custodians, MC#, FS13 sufficient summaries. *Mechanism:* Export a summary of complete route obligations, shared assumptions and allowed orderings. Compose summaries at group boundaries, rechecking only the cross-group interactions for which the old premises do not suffice. If compression drops a route or a prerequisite, report which future traces are lost rather than silently widening the contract. *New:* A merge summary can be judged by how much future joint work it admits, not just by the number of saved proof bytes. *First test:* Form groups with separable and entangled routes; compare full re-verification, summary composition and flattened masks over repeated merges. Score proof cost, soundness and missed useful traces.

**DWM9. Rebase a stale menu by asking which route will hit its boundary first.** Parts: FC8 finite-write gates, FC22 re-replicate before depletion, DP14 route-to-zero hazard, DP21 shared backup leases, MW10 precondition-based rebase, MW20 repeated migrations, SL7 distinct failure clocks, SS13 stale slack, FSR4 proof invalidation. *Mechanism:* Before applying an old menu to a new base, test stale premises on independent slices. A fact-only drift can require new tolerance evidence, while B-only drift can require a new survivor certificate even at unchanged answers. Replace only the affected alternatives; if no bounded check distinguishes the causes, withhold the stale menu. *New:* Two menus with identical accepted choices today can have different useful lifetimes depending on which route their proofs silently share. *First test:* Hold current accuracy fixed and independently vary fact movement and B training between submission and commit. Compare age expiry, full re-verification and selective rebase at the same old-service floor.

**DWM10. Let a needed co-change travel as a verified alternative, not as hidden coordination.** Parts: FC3 anti-clauses, FC11 compile known structure, DP11 direct service versus recovery witness, DP18 fallback on timeout, MW02 explicit compensation, MW06 certified fallback ranges, MW17 adapter swap check, UD16 noncomposition, AC#. *Mechanism:* If a new writer moves an interface and its consumer must move with it, offer the coordinated change as one menu operation with an explicit publish mode. Provide a cheaper certified fallback for bounded search, or abstain when the co-change cannot be verified; a recovery-only option cannot satisfy a continuous-serving promise. *New:* The product of a merge can be a negotiable obligation to move together, not simply the intersection of independently allowed writes. *First test:* Use a base upgrade whose old consumer fails on unchanged activations. Compare simultaneous checked migration, either serial order and an equal-budget preserved old route, with a timeout and restart.

**DWM11. A federation of learners that can trade write rights without losing the last working route.** Parts: FC1 hitting sets, FC2 cardinality, FC3 co-changes, FC4 rotation, FC6 learned subspaces, FC7 learned conflicts, FC8 finite gates, FC9 group tests, FC13 writer scope, FC18 replay triggers, FC19 series–parallel structure, FC20 common-mode failure, FC21 survivor repair, FC22 re-replication, DP2 make-before-break, DP4 anchored slack, DP6 future flexibility, DP8 transfer, DP9 route identity, DP10 failure domains, DP11 recovery witnesses, DP12 last-route triggers, DP14 hazard, DP15 second-route replay, DP17 treewidth, DP18 timeout fallback, DP19 compaction, DP21 shared leases, DP22 global survivors, MW01 useful options, MW02 prerequisites, MW04 repeated merge, MW05 commute, MW06 fallback ranges, MW07 menu repair, MW08 dropout, MW09 coordination, MW10 rebase, MW11 custodians, MW13 factorization, MW14 mixed remainder, MW15 premise reuse, MW17 swap checks, MW18 moved consumers, MW19 higher-order coalitions, MW20 lifecycle cost, MW21 unknown writers, MW22 scoped impossibility, FS#, SL#, UD#, SS#, MC#, POP#, AC#, X1, Y1, USR4, FSR5. *Mechanism:* Each learner offers several useful writes plus skip. Construct a shared contract over complete live routes, ordered compensation and finite conflict clauses; return a checked serving trace or an atomic publish, not a bare union of certificates. As facts, bases and tenants change, recheck only justified portions, spend replay on fading independent backups, and hand the next merger a summary that states what was not verified. *New:* Independent learners could lend each other plasticity by transferring custody of old obligations, without each freezing every alternative route or keeping every original training example. *First test:* Use an enumerable ReLU routing world with rare exceptions, several writers and successive adapter migrations. Compare tuned projection and replay, full trace verification, and incremental repair with the same menu generator. Measure attained B utility, surviving independent routes, worst prefixes, proof and coordination work, and reuse on unseen mergers.
