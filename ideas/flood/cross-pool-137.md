## Merge proposals

**ACE1. Measure what a later learner can still read after a toy shortcut correction.** Parts: AL3-01 future-read map, AL3-02 accessible source distinctions, AL3-03 label-cost curves, AL3-04 bounded raw tap, AL3-05 low-variance contrasts, AL3-06 effect-based alignment, AL3-07 hidden future labels, AL3-08 channel ablation, AL3-10 separate old labels from source, AL3-13 worst-reader cost, AL3-15 source versus proxy, AL2-11 future consumer, NP4-13 response to future evidence, OE3-12 relearnability. *Mechanism:* In a toy circuit with a deliberately planted misleading route, match the edits on immediate correction and legitimate use. Fit a small map on unlabeled paired views and freeze it before an independent evaluator draws future consumer tasks. Restrict the comparison to views those tasks can actually access. *New:* Two edits with identical current scores can leave different label costs for tomorrow's learner. The map adds value only if it does more than exposing the raw source or reconstructing a proxy for the old task. *First test:* Compare mapped and raw taps, ordinary alignment and direct teaching at matched state and label budgets. Reverse a spurious proxy and report labels to target including noncompletion.

**ACE2. Preserve a newly taught reader when a previous correction is withdrawn.** Parts: AL3-09 map composition, AL3-11 counter retraction, AL3-12 missing-evidence diagnostic, AL3-14 total map cost, L3-03 transport by effect, NP4-19 state equivalence under future operations, NP5-01 interacting lessons. *Mechanism:* Fit future-read maps before a new toy consumer is revealed. After two edits, compare a chain of maps with one direct alignment. When an independent witness invalidates the first edit, retest the new consumer and request evidence for missing distinctions only if the mapped read fails. *New:* Rolling back a correction is incomplete if it restores the old decision but forces a newly learned consumer to relearn its input. *First test:* Teach a later consumer, then withdraw the toy correction. Compare mapped restoration, replay, direct alignment and raw restoration at equal evidence; charge collection of paired views, storage and runtime reads.

**ACE3. Verify a first choice on clean replays before teaching it.** Parts: AL3-16 continuation contract, AL3-17 first preventable window, AL3-18 nomination without labels, AL3-19 clean executability, AL3-20 paired outcomes, AL3-21 commitment-local write, AL3-22 goal-dependent collision, AL3-27 equivalent decompositions, AL3-29 smallest supported update, AL2-20 teach the verified fork, AL2-23 independent verification, PL5-10 held-out effect families. *Mechanism:* Use a temporary change to a planted toy heuristic only to nominate two choices. Remove it and replay both choices from the same clean prefix under independently chosen continuation policies. Teach a local preference only when both choices are executable and the verified advantage is supported under the declared goal; otherwise record uncertainty. *New:* Finding a better-looking trace does not establish that its first choice caused the gain. The clean comparison must distinguish a true preference from solver limitations, equivalent solutions and incidental formatting. *First test:* On synthetic table tasks with matched early choices, compare clean branching and equal-budget best-of-N against assisted nominations. Evaluate the resulting learner on unseen tasks with no intervention, charging both branches and verifier calls.

**ACE4. Revalidate only the choice comparisons a solver change could overturn.** Parts: AL3-23 changed verifier tolerance, AL3-24 targeted suffix recheck, AL3-25 preserve disagreement, AL3-26 action-effect transfer, AL3-28 later recovery, AL3-30 longitudinal cost, AL31 conditions for reopening, NP5-26 future-revealed effects. *Mechanism:* Record both clean branch summaries and the continuation conditions in which their ordering holds. When a benign solver or tolerance changes, retest the close or conflicting cases and a random sample of stable cases. Reopen rather than teach when the comparison is unresolved; compare equivalent choices by their observable effects rather than wording. *New:* A stored preference is reusable only if the limits of its evidence tell the next learner what to recheck and what it can keep. *First test:* Vary continuation competence and permitted recovery on harmless toy tasks. Compare targeted rechecks with fresh collection and pooled preferences at matched cost; measure missed reversals, unnecessary rechecks and retention.

**ACE5. Leave evidence that the next learner can use after both an edit and a solver change.** Parts: AL3-01 future-read map, AL3-02 accessible source distinctions, AL3-03 label-cost curves, AL3-04 bounded raw tap, AL3-05 low-variance contrasts, AL3-06 effect-based alignment, AL3-07 hidden future labels, AL3-08 channel ablation, AL3-10 separate old labels from source, AL3-13 worst-reader cost, AL3-15 source versus proxy, AL2-11 future consumer, NP4-13 response to future evidence, OE3-12 relearnability, AL3-09 map composition, AL3-11 counter retraction, AL3-12 missing-evidence diagnostic, AL3-14 total map cost, L3-03 transport by effect, NP4-19 state equivalence under future operations, NP5-01 interacting lessons, AL3-16 continuation contract, AL3-17 first preventable window, AL3-18 nomination without labels, AL3-19 clean executability, AL3-20 paired outcomes, AL3-21 commitment-local write, AL3-22 goal-dependent collision, AL3-27 equivalent decompositions, AL3-29 smallest supported update, AL2-20 teach the verified fork, AL2-23 independent verification, PL5-10 held-out effect families, AL3-23 changed verifier tolerance, AL3-24 targeted suffix recheck, AL3-25 preserve disagreement, AL3-26 action-effect transfer, AL3-28 later recovery, AL3-30 longitudinal cost, AL31 conditions for reopening, NP5-26 future-revealed effects. *Mechanism:* Keep a map for accessing preserved toy source distinctions and scoped evidence for each verified choice. Freeze maps before future tasks are drawn; discover candidate choices separately from labeling them on clean replays. Withdraw unverified claims and keep the relevant evidence needed to reopen them. *New:* Continual benefit means later learning is cheaper even when the first correction is revoked or the continuation used to verify a choice changes. *First test:* Run a synthetic stream with two local edits, a new consumer and a change of solver. Compare independent map and fork components and their combination against raw reads, replay and fresh clean branching at matched current performance, labels, state and verifier calls.
