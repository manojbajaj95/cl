# Cross-pool merges, round 118

Focal source: `tree/open-ended-learners-3.md` (PR #386, OE3-#). Older sources: `tree/open-ended-learners-2.md` (PR #385, OE2-#; focal in round 117), `tree/root-open-ended-learners.md` (PR #384, OE#), population learners V (PR #383, PL5-#), test-time learners root (PR #373, TT#), learner–world co-evolution VIII (PR #371, L8W#), deployment feedback 5 (PR #357, D5-#) and 4 (PR #355, D4-#), relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#), and sleep and dreaming II (PR #334, SD2-#). Raw proposals for harmless synthetic environments.

**RVX1. Buy a revival basis that works as a sequence, not a list of promising skills.** Parts: OE3-01 minimal basis, OE3-02 revival modes, OE3-03 signed revival, OE3-04 revival algebra, OE3-07 step-size window, OE3-14 curriculum ordering, OE2-03 directed revival, TT28 no-update shadow. *Mechanism:* Estimate the effect of bounded lessons from matched checkpoints, then validate candidate mixtures and orders on scratch learners. Count collateral losses as well as recoveries. Treat the signed graph as a source of candidates, not a proof that effects are transitive or that a negative cycle makes recovery impossible. *New:* A small set of lessons is valuable only if the actual sequence restores the repertoire at the promised budget. *First test:* Plant two hubs whose lessons pass separately but interfere in one order. Compare greedy out-degree, mode mixtures and jointly checked schedules at equal update work.

**RVX2. Refresh a recovery route before relying on it for a reset.** Parts: OE3-06 edge lifetimes, OE3-08 forgetting fingerprints, OE3-09 portability, OE3-10 synthetic keys, OE3-11 trained hubs, OE3-17 reset insurance, L8W22 scratch round trip, PL5-10 held-out effect family. *Mechanism:* Distinguish loss of a skill from loss of the lesson that used to revive it. Before a planned reset, test the stored keys on scratch copies across checkpoint age, perturbation and model changes, with independently checked held-out families. Keep a fallback for any route that fails. *New:* The insurance budget is set by measured recovery after the proposed reset, not by the number of working edges before it. *First test:* Compare storage of every toy skill, a static basis and a checked refresh policy across drift, partial reset and backbone change.

**RVX3. Protect the ability to relearn even when old outputs are deliberately allowed to fade.** Parts: OE3-05 revival equivalence, OE3-12 protect paths, OE3-13 shed recoverable leaves, OE3-15 context versus weights, OE3-16 bounded path closure, D4-24 future native lesson, CR24 bounded recovery claim, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation. *Mechanism:* Measure hint-only recovery separately from a weight update and test the recovered learner on a continuation that distinguishes similar skills. Allow old outputs to fade only when the approved recovery path still works alongside later updates. For removed toy skills, report no recovery found only for the tested lessons and budget. *New:* Shared revival is evidence of a common route, not proof of behavioral identity or permanent forgetting. *First test:* Use harmless role-free toy rule streams where two skills share a hint but diverge on continuations, and a third recovers only after a weight update. Compare output retention with checked recovery targets.

**RVX4. Store stage-specific objectives only when the probe and lesson agree with an independent judge.** Parts: OE3-18 probe–lesson mismatch, OE3-20 objective versus data revival, OE3-21 cluster objectives, OE3-22 stage-specific reward machines, OE3-23 probe and reward consistency, OE3-25 checker-entry assay, OE2-22 reward entries, OE25 verifier retention, D5-26 teaching after compression. *Mechanism:* Store an objective and a diagnostic for the stage that lapsed. When they disagree, compare each with independent acceptance fixtures before rewriting either. Compare objective optimization, context-only use and trajectory rehearsal from matched starts; count checker and generator storage as well as the objective. *New:* Agreement is useful only if it identifies the stage that needs repair and does not let the same shortcut pass both the probe and the reward. *First test:* Build sequential synthetic puzzles with one misleading intermediate reward and a shortcut checker. Compare flat rewards, separate replay and independently checked stage-specific rehearsal.

**RVX5. Forecast retention from revival effects rather than objective length alone.** Parts: OE3-19 revival descriptors, OE3-24 length and lifetime, OE3-26 archive forecasts, OE08 frontier expansion, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Describe each lesson by measured gains and losses on other skills, alongside its length, cost and version. Forecast the next write on held-out checkpoints, remeasuring only affected claims when the world changes. If length predicts retention only within one task family, do not use it as a global rule. *New:* The archive can test whether recovery effects predict future retention beyond simpler proxies such as reward size, recency and current score. *First test:* Sweep short and long objectives with matched difficulty in harmless toy worlds; compare forecasts based on length, recency and signed revival against later observations.

**RVX6. Schedule rehearsal around verified recovery routes instead of old accuracy alone.** Parts: OE3-01 minimal basis, OE3-02 revival modes, OE3-03 signed revival, OE3-04 revival algebra, OE3-07 step-size window, OE3-14 curriculum ordering, OE2-03 directed revival, TT28 no-update shadow, OE3-06 edge lifetimes, OE3-08 forgetting fingerprints, OE3-09 portability, OE3-10 synthetic keys, OE3-11 trained hubs, OE3-17 reset insurance, L8W22 scratch round trip, PL5-10 held-out effect family, OE3-05 revival equivalence, OE3-12 protect paths, OE3-13 shed recoverable leaves, OE3-15 context versus weights, OE3-16 bounded path closure, D4-24 future native lesson, CR24 bounded recovery claim, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation, OE3-18 probe–lesson mismatch, OE3-20 objective versus data revival, OE3-21 cluster objectives, OE3-22 stage-specific reward machines, OE3-23 probe and reward consistency, OE3-25 checker-entry assay, OE2-22 reward entries, OE25 verifier retention, D5-26 teaching after compression, OE3-19 revival descriptors, OE3-24 length and lifetime, OE3-26 archive forecasts, OE08 frontier expansion, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Measure context and weight recovery separately, track how recovery routes change after new writes, and validate small rehearsal bases on scratch learners. Pair stage-specific objectives with independent checks and keep fallback lessons where the archive cannot still recover a skill. *New:* A recovery-aware archive can distinguish skills that have faded, routes that have broken, and checks that no longer describe the intended behavior. *First test:* Use synthetic continua with order-dependent interference, fading routes, misleading checkers and controlled resets. Compare uniform replay, out-degree scheduling and checked basis rehearsal at matched storage and work.
