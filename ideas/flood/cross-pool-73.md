**LKH1. Make the next training job a paid handoff instead of a shadow audit.** Parts: LE3-01 live scheduled takeover, LE3-07 second independent switch, LE3-12 second-use payment, LE2-01 substitute cost, ER25 explicit provider, TP25 replace source. *Mechanism:* At a frozen boundary give a real entrant the scheduled job, with any lawful supplier dependency made an explicit argument. Independently draw a second entrant to train the actual state left by the first; pay for a drop in its sample cost only if both jobs and old exceptions survive. *New:* A teachable handoff could be a product bought through useful work rather than a one-step substitute demonstration. *First test:* Use three synthetic grammar extensions and two incompatible supplier conventions. Match both entrants' budgets and compare shadow audits, fixed alternation and the two-live-job contract.

**LKH2. Fund a witness for the detour even when the failure modes are shared.** Parts: LE3-02 prefix exits, LE3-03 correlated shortages, LE3-09 joint outages, LE2-24 bounded reachability, PX10 causal policy equivalence, DA07 joint uncertainty. *Mechanism:* Require one public replacement policy over interruptions and plausible joint supply outages. Share verification only between histories with the same executable exit policy; never assemble an exit from supplies that coexist only in mutually exclusive scenarios. *New:* A covenant could permit a productive detour while screening out redundant routes that fail together. *First test:* Hold endpoint sample cost constant and plant either a common supplier outage or independent shortages. Compare endpoint-only checks, independent-route counting and exact joint interruption planning.

**LKH3. Buy one ordinary example that leaves two incompatible trainers able to advance.** Parts: LE3-04 public gradients, LE3-06 incompatible policies, LE3-15 learnable exceptions, LE2-23 spent plasticity, ER11 action effects, BM3-22 observe versus write. *Mechanism:* Pay only when the earlier lesson leaves both frozen public entrant policies a useful native update on their own examples, including one new old-rule exception. A probe can identify a candidate state but cannot stand in for the finite write or transfer a write right. *New:* A lesson could preserve multiple executable directions for adapting a rule, rather than merely retaining a matching prediction interface. *First test:* Match present outputs in two factorized learners. Test both fixed public update rules on held-out exceptions against projected updates, replay and a translation-based control.

**LKH4. Pay to retire a public diagnostic only after an incumbent makes it unnecessary.** Parts: LE3-08 entrant blindness, LE3-14 removable hint, LE3-23 smallest public separator, BM4-26 separating observation, RB11 actual fallback, HW20 independent reconstructions. *Mechanism:* Register the cheapest useful diagnostic that resolves which first entrant write is safe. Invite suppliers to change the learner so a single ordinary next write is safe across both previously confusable states. Verify the common action without revealing the distinguishing hint; share verified savings against the executable diagnostic baseline. *New:* Procurement could pay for eliminating a recurrent information need rather than buying a new private translator. *First test:* Use two equal-output states with initially disjoint safe action sets. Compare a public diagnosis policy, shadow probes, static hints and a same-budget upstream lesson.

**LKH5. Reserve one due repair for a failed takeover without erasing the failure.** Parts: LE3-19 separate safety and payout, LE3-20 productive repair, LE2-13 round-trip cost, BM4-03 unsettled loss, CX21 failover denial, VD24 carry adverse route. *Mechanism:* Precommit a different entrant and a lawful already-due corrective job. A missed takeover keeps its own adverse record, scope and cost even if repaired. Release a separate continuation installment only after a later entrant can train the repaired state without borrowing the incumbent's failed test examples. *New:* A credible forward repair could improve the incumbent's initial teaching incentives without paying it for a failure it created. *First test:* Compare immediate settlement, unconditional equal-cost repair and a conditional reserve in a finite learner with one irreversible public feature loss.

**LKH6. Debit closed prefixes even if the seller later sells a useful rescue.** Parts: LE3-10 incumbent-quality control, LE3-17 dependence debit, LE3-24 restrictive covenant, LE3-26 participation constraint, LE2-21 buyout edge, WS04 residual edit. *Mechanism:* Fix an equally resourced open reference before the incumbent acts. Carry incremental excess replacement cost across exposed prefixes; separate verified endpoint value from those debits. Enumerate what suppliers would actually accept before treating any resulting better route as attainable. *New:* A contract might keep good rescue lessons available without rewarding an avoidable lock-in-and-rescue cycle. *First test:* Use two supplier types and two prefixes with matched endpoint quality. Compare a hard veto, endpoint pricing and carried debits with exact same-information participation and continuation planning.

**LKH7. Buy a single ordinary update for two obligations only when both receive it.** Parts: LE3-16 useful production audit, LE3-21 joint replacement budget, LE2-14 rights graph, OS19 executable order, RB12 joint certificate, SK05 fixed common key. *Mechanism:* Commit the next required old update and the new task together, and check one common allowed example stream and order. Use a fixed shared key only when both sites can derive their lawful local inputs from it. Record one consumed example once; demand direct evidence that each promised service was actually delivered. *New:* A replacement price could reward genuinely shared work instead of counting two separately short curricula that cannot run together. *First test:* Construct a finite learner with equal per-job distances and different joint schedulability. Compare merged budgets, independent shortest paths and exact joint planning.

**LKH8. Make a retained feature pay rent by helping remove the incumbent's convention.** Parts: LE3-05 withdraw privileges, LE3-22 new buyer needs, LE3-25 productive feature, ER16 merge with residual, SK05 fixed common key, TP20 composable decoder. *Mechanism:* Store a bounded bridge feature only if ordinary public updates use it to replace a private convention while serving scheduled tasks. Remove teacher-only help one privilege at a time and test an unseen caller using only lawful local reads. Charge storage to the bridge until it actually ceases to be needed. *New:* A small retained representation could be worth buying for its ability to make one supplier dispensable, not for matching that supplier indefinitely. *First test:* Compare retained translation, public replay and a learned bridge at equal memory and update cost. Remove the private view and test two future subskills.

**LKH9. Sell a narrow refusal when full portability is infeasible.** Parts: LE3-13 cross audit provenance, LE3-18 nonportable family, LE2-22 retaining switch, BM4-11 negative scope receipt, CX21 failover denial, EL21 scoped renewal. *Mechanism:* If independent schedulers cannot support a lesson family inside its real rights and old-service limits, return a scoped negative witness and an executable fallback for the remaining families. Cross lesson procedures with independent outcome fixtures so the denial applies to the failing family rather than the entire supplier; renew only checked claims. *New:* A public, scoped refusal could stop a market from interpreting missing portability evidence as either a universal guarantee or a universal ban. *First test:* Plant one nonportable family and one unnoticed correlated test generator. Compare all-or-none certification, naive per-family scores and preregistered crossed tests.

**LKH10. Sell the right to retire a token only if a substitute still has a useful update.** Parts: ER2-01 future training equivalence, ER2-02 bounded witness, ER2-06 provenance bit, LE3-04 public gradients, LE2-15 continuation witness, BM4-26 separating observation. *Mechanism:* Verify byte continuations and the native finite first update from lawful entrant examples before deleting the token. If output equivalence holds but a public update still separates the parses, keep a billed training-only residual or provenance bit under an explicit horizon. Lift the restriction only when a new public witness covers the retired case; do not treat silence as evidence. *New:* A representation rewrite could buy cheaper inference without making the entrant pay to recover a missing learning direction. *First test:* Start two parses with matched byte outputs and different lawful finite-step effects. Compare output-only retirement, full retention and an equally priced residual on the next entrant lesson.

**LKH11. Insure rare old actions during live alphabet contraction.** Parts: ER2-04 live cache migration, ER2-09 fiber transport, ER2-16 scoped fiber, ER2-19 dual clock, ER2-20 fiber preservation, LE3-02 interruption exit, VD24 adverse-route transfer, CX21 failover denial. *Mechanism:* Run action-specific splits on the fast clock and check their transported cues before each slow alphabet contraction. Move live cache state and pending old-service evidence together. If the new cache cannot sustain a declared rare action during interruption, fund a real old-compatible fallback rather than dropping the request or certifying from easy byte traffic. *New:* A buyer might pay for representation savings only when old live requests and their evidence remain serviceable throughout migration. *First test:* Schedule rare old actions and a supplier outage during a toy sequence model migration; compare cache rebuilding, a compatibility wrapper and exact transport at matched memory and work.

**LKH12. Auction the order of two structural rewrites rather than pricing them separately.** Parts: ER2-14 predictable residual, ER2-18 paid split budget, ER2-21 bit-cost comparison, ER2-27 noncommuting witness, LE3-21 joint replacement budget, LE2-13 return cost, OS19 executable order. *Mechanism:* Ask suppliers for a joint quote for token retirement and action-conditioned repair, including intermediate old service and the future entrant's usable action space. Allocate the fiber only for a predictable held-out residual, not for noise; compare both orders at the same retained bytes and count each required corrective example once. *New:* Noncommuting edits could warrant a different purchase order even when both isolated rewrites look cheap. *First test:* Use the six-state noncommutation fixture, add an independent stochastic transition as a null, and compare separate quotes, a fixed ordering and exact joint planning.

**LKH13. Preserve each covered old service through a finite write and keep unsettled loss visible across suppliers. On each slow structural rewrite, preserve live cache behavior and the action cues already relied on by fast repairs. Pay for token retirement only with a covered continuation or a priced executable fallback. Value real reductions in future diagnostic and training cost against a fixed open reference. Separate prefix safety from payout, including a forward repair that cannot erase old damage. *New:* A learner might gain credible options to change teachers repeatedly without sacrificing the useful detours and compact representations those teachers can supply. *First test:* Enumerate benign teaching markets with matched lawful information, example menus, retained bytes and costs. Cross interruption timing, supplier concentration, private conventions and rare exceptions, and noncommuting merge-split orders. Compare exact buyer planning, the parent covenant and payout stack, and matched replay plus translation; check first and second handoff costs and each old obligation.
