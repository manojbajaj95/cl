# Cross-pool merges, round 37

Focal sources: `tree/declared-footprints.md` (PR #159, DF#) and `tree/room-making-writes.md` (PR #160, RM#). Crosses with make-before-break (PR #157, MB#), precondition writes (PR #158, PW#), forbidden combinations (PR #154, FC#), disjunctive protection (PR #155, DP#), mergeable writes (PR #156, MW#), behavior footprints (PR #102, BF#), footprint counterfeits (PR #107, CF#, called FC# in round 11), unregistered dependence (PR #150, UD#), and earlier rounds 1–36. These are raw proposals. A declared mutation footprint is a restriction on a whole step, not evidence of behavioral independence. A useful provider must establish a guard that was false without sacrificing its own acquisition or old obligations; fulfillment is not admission. Charge discovery, scratch state, rejected steps and independent checks.

**RFW1. Convert a step veto into a request another learner can fulfill.** Parts: DF1 phase-specific offers, DF20 footprint repair, RM08 executable requests, RM09 alternative fixes, PW16 negotiated entry, MW07 menu repair. *Mechanism:* When the confined builder hits a forbidden effect, report which read obligation blocks the effect. Offer two repair paths where available: an equivalent local effect with no shared mutation, or a behavior-level postcondition that a useful provider can establish to release the blocked right. Hold the original payload fixed and recheck both its footprint authority and its entry guard at publication. *New:* A denied step can recruit a provider without quietly promoting an unauthorized write into the next menu. *First test:* Plant a running-stat veto and give a separate learner two equally useful acquisition choices, only one of which moves the reader. Compare freeze, local refit and request-driven repair at equal total work.

**RFW2. Make room for a future optimizer step, not just a future inference.** Parts: DF2 delayed optimizer readers, DF14 learner readers, RM01 frozen consumer, RM17 replenishment, MB4 drain, PW13 optimizer continuation, UD1 producer–consumer pairs. *Mechanism:* After inference drains, retain read obligations for pending optimizer continuations and teachers. Learn a provider that relocates those reads or establishes an equivalent scoped continuation before authorizing reuse of their old storage. Once the consumer spends the room, ask the next provider for a replenishment that preserves the consumer's newly acquired task. *New:* A teacher or optimizer bank can remain occupied by future learning even after its last serving request has finished. *First test:* Make two models match current outputs but depend on different momentum banks a step later. Compare inference-only release, blanket retention and continuation-aware release on later B gains and A/C retention.

**RFW3. Match a semantic request in a basis you can afford to distrust.** Parts: DF6 confined probes, DF13 shadow independence, RM13 behavior-only negotiation, RM20 information floor, BF2 core versus envelope, CF8 missing boundary distinctions, UD12 independent audits. *Mechanism:* Let a requester publish a small behavioral basis and a scoped implication to its guard rather than name the provider's coordinates. Challenge the implication with held-out suffixes and shadow interventions that remove the old carrier; include probe effects in the declared footprint. If two indistinguishable providers require different remedies, buy a distinguishing observation instead of authorizing either from proxy agreement. *New:* A negotiation can transfer a behavioral postcondition without assuming its cheapest visible witness is a unique realization. *First test:* Train two providers that match every public probe but diverge after a legal update near a boundary. Compare direct coordinate exchange, fixed probes and selectively expanded behavior requests on bits, useful admissions and false release.

**RFW4. Reclaim shared state by covering readers, not by adding up freed bytes.** Parts: DF8 union receipts, DF9 first slice funds the second, DF16 common-state targeting, RM07 complementary suppliers, RM04 transfer room, DP22 global route protection, FC20 common-mode backups, MB13 sliced handover. *Mechanism:* Give each provider a useful acquisition that can move one reader slice. Build the first replacement with scratch, spend only its private release on the second, then join the reader-indexed receipts to authorize a frozen consumer that needs the shared bank. Keep rare or fallback readers as blockers even when their traffic counter is zero. *New:* Two providers can jointly create write rights neither could buy by clearing the same number of private bytes alone. *First test:* Use two old skills with a shared normalization bank and one spare cell. Compare byte-count auctions, a full clone and reader-coverage scheduling on which useful writes can actually execute.

**RFW5. Reject a circular promise unless there is a publishable first step.** Parts: DF15 version-bound phases, DF17 copy the last blocker, RM16 reciprocal room, RM11 scoped infeasibility, FC3 required co-changes, PW02 necessary versus sufficient order, MW02 compensation order, MPC5 overlap scheduling. *Mechanism:* For two pending learners promising to free each other's state, derive which phase can run with current rights and old readers. Try a small independent provider or temporary copy to break the cycle. Admit a joint endpoint only if publication is truly atomic and handles preexisting requests; otherwise return a scoped deadlock or an unknown, depending on what was checked. *New:* A mutually useful pair can have two valid final guards and no admissible way to start under the current overlap budget. *First test:* Enumerate reciprocal writes with and without a spare cell and with held requests. Compare joint endpoint checks, static dependency edges and phase-aware scheduling on false accepts and foregone utility.

**RFW6. Protect the path to the next admission, not only today's acquired skills.** Parts: DF19 next-writer budget, DF4 clocks and teachers, RM06 no displaced client, RM02 survive rivals, PW04 event expiry, MW19 coalition conflicts, UD21 shared cuts, DWM4 shared budgets. *Mechanism:* For known pending clients, score a provider on which reachable guards it establishes and which it destroys, not just one client's immediate admission. Register destructive combinations over clocks, teacher state and route effects; keep a certificate for at least one feasible continuation per promised client and invalidate only the affected traces when a rival actually intervenes. *New:* A provider can leave old accuracy unchanged while taking away someone else's only affordable way to learn. *First test:* Give three learners distinct menus with one pairwise-invisible interference through a shared teacher. Measure future realized B utility and worst-prefix A/C for guard-first, margin-first and continuation-preserving selection.

**RFW7. Reissue a room receipt when a routing boundary moves, even if no weights do.** Parts: DF11 fallback registration, DF12 boundary repair, RM05 routing-only provider, RM12 validity window, MB9 latency demotion, DP9 route identity, BF20 operating regions, MPC3 dispatcher retention. *Mechanism:* Let a routing provider make a frozen write admissible by moving live readers off its target. Bind the proof to the dispatch regions and deadline; partition a later routing revision into unchanged and changed regions. Revoke unused rights on affected regions until new reader coverage and timely independence are shown; do not reopen the entire search for unchanged slices. *New:* A learned opportunity to write can disappear at constant weights and constant accuracy if a fallback becomes obligated to the old bank again. *First test:* Shift routing under matched outputs and variable load with an infrequent held request. Compare unscoped receipts, full re-verification and domain-difference repair on missed deadlines and recoverable write rights.

**RFW8. Sell choices at several footprint sizes rather than a single minimum mask.** Parts: DF3 normalization isolation, DF5 physical alias closure, DF7 granularity search, RM15 lower establishment cost, RM18 help weaker editors, MW01 useful options, PW14 smaller guards, BF5 timed access. *Mechanism:* Train equally budgeted offers at different isolation granularities: private statistics, a partially shared group, or a larger block with a stronger release proof. Include actual aliases and the cost of getting ready in each offer. Let the merger pick the useful option that enables other writers without crossing their service or peak-memory limits. *New:* The smallest syntactic footprint can be the most expensive room to establish if every tiny cell has a different reader or proof obligation. *First test:* Vary aliasing and old-reader density with fixed B data and optimizer steps. Compare weight masks, coarse cloning and footprint menus on total establishment cost and realized follow-on admissions.

**RFW9. Compile a learned room-making recipe without caching its right to execute.** Parts: DF18 reusable release patterns, DF15 exact receipts, RM14 reply policy, RM19 teach a newcomer, RM22 closed-loop planner, PW20 admission bits, CF7 validity leases, MW04 repeated merges. *Mechanism:* Learn a reply policy that proposes a provider and a release schedule from previous repairs. Reuse the schedule as a template only; check fresh aliases, current obligations and frozen-consumer applicability against a new instance before publication. Count what has to be remembered besides successful schedules to distinguish a real repeat from a look-alike. *New:* What gets inherited can be how to create room, while the proof that there is room today stays local. *First test:* Hold out task orders and alias layouts, including identical public menus with different old readers. Compare an equal-bit schedule cache, from-scratch planning and learned templates with the same live checker.

**RFW10. Diagnose why nobody can make room without broadening the contract.** Parts: DF21 coarse isolation, DF22 unregistrable readers, RM10 missing versus false facts, RM11 scoped impossibility, RM21 non-margin comparison, UD8 reachable routes, MB22 budget conflict, CF17 strength ladders. *Mechanism:* Separate an unauthorized attempted mutation, an unaccounted reader, a disproved behavioral guard and lack of search evidence. Discharge each with different work: a narrower offer, dependency discovery, a useful provider, or more checking. Compare intermediate evidence on a ladder of stronger but still permitted attempts; if coarse isolation already reaches the same utility frontier, report that rather than claim a fine-grain benefit. *New:* The same refusal can identify different resources to buy next, without treating optimizer uncertainty as an absent reader or a certificate of impossibility. *First test:* Plant each failure in a finite world with an exact oracle, then mix them in a small learned host. Compare one-error retries, coarse isolation and scoped diagnosis on correct remedies per verifier step.

**RFW11. A continual learner that trades room to learn without trading away old service.** Parts: DF1 phased offers, DF2 optimizer slots, DF3 normalization, DF4 clocks and teachers, DF5 aliases, DF6 probe containment, DF7 granularity, DF8 reader receipts, DF9 staged funding, DF10 split last consumers, DF11 session pinning, DF12 boundary repair, DF13 shadow independence, DF14 training readers, DF15 receipt versions, DF16 common state, DF17 targeted copies, DF18 compiled patterns, DF19 future families, DF20 veto repair, DF21 coarse isolation, DF22 failure scope, RM01 frozen consumers, RM02 rivals, RM03 unannounced consumers, RM04 room transfer, RM05 routing effects, RM06 other clients, RM07 complementary providers, RM08 executable requests, RM09 alternate fixes, RM10 missing facts, RM11 infeasibility, RM12 validity windows, RM13 behavioral language, RM14 reply policy, RM15 cheaper establishment, RM16 reciprocal room, RM17 replenishment, RM18 weaker editors, RM19 newcomer teaching, RM20 information floor, RM21 margin controls, RM22 closed-loop planning, MB#, PW#, FC#, DP#, MW#, BF#, CF#, UD#, MPC1, DWM6, RD10. *Mechanism:* Writers publish useful phased offers with complete mutation footprints; blocked frozen consumers publish checked, behavior-level requests for room. Providers learn their own task while moving reader obligations or otherwise establishing those requests. Join receipts over all live, in-flight and training readers; authorize only the covered resource versions, then recheck fulfilled guards and newly acquired tasks when the consumer publishes. Keep unverified dependencies and scope-limited conflicts explicit, and let past successes propose the next repair without granting write rights. *New:* Independent learners could teach one another how to enable useful writes, then trade the resulting capacity repeatedly without sharing their training histories or relying on a master allocation of permanent masks. *First test:* Build an enumerable stateful learner with a shared teacher, aliased scratch, rare fallback and delayed consumers. Compare coarse isolation with safe handoff, closed-loop planning with the same offers, and learned repair templates with live checks. Measure old-service prefixes, actual guard establishment and admission separately, acquired utility, verified capacity reuse, peak state and total checking work.
