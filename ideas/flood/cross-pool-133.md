## Merge proposals

**ACA1. Wait for evidence that distinguishes two explanations of the same error.** Parts: AL01 rolling contrasts, AL02 balanced shortcut cells, AL03 contrast swaps, AL04 independent witnesses, AL05 subtraction and addition factorial, AL06 verifier-scored twins, AL17 discriminating queries, AL31 no-write certificates, PL5-10 held-out effects, HC26 independent tests. *Mechanism:* On a synthetic task, one input field supplies the rule and another is a sometimes-correlated decoy. Use paired examples to nominate explanations, then query cases on which they disagree. Keep a record of what evidence would justify a write if the witnesses do not resolve the disagreement. *New:* The stored object is an unresolved choice with a useful next query, not a tentative weight edit that may already damage an old rule. *First test:* Switch the decoy correlation and measure accepted wrong changes and examples to recovery versus recency-matched in-context learning, raw contrast steering and random queries.

**ACA2. Protect legitimate uses of a cue when correcting a single decision.** Parts: AL07 bounded shortcut write, AL08 selector-controlled exclusion, AL09 scoped rescue, AL10 sign reversal, AL11 dependency-specific correction, AL20 local interference repair, AL22 shared exclusions, AL26 relational scope, HC13 concentration assay, TT03 protected updates. *Mechanism:* On a toy two-output circuit, test whether a misleading cue causes errors in one output while still being useful to the other. Compare a correction confined to the failed decision with suppressing the cue everywhere. Share corrections only where independent tests show the same effect; abstain when useful and misleading cases cannot be separated. *New:* A reusable correction specifies a bad dependency while preserving useful readings of the same cue elsewhere. *First test:* Cross a real selector with a nuisance field and vary their overlap. Compare a decision-specific correction, matched-rank LoRA and whole-feature suppression on held-out cue combinations and on the old output.

**ACA3. Rebuild a correction when its evidence is withdrawn, not just when its weights change.** Parts: AL12 reversible dose, AL13 evidence retraction, AL14 frozen and current contrasts, AL15 disagreement memory, AL16 separate regime transitions, AL18 record of incompatibility, AL19 order assay, AL21 cancellation versus forgetting, AL23 retirement versus prohibition, AL24 preserve future distinctions, NP3-22 source withdrawal, NP5-01 lesson interaction, NP5-26 protected future reads, OE3-12 relearnability. *Mechanism:* Retain which verified witnesses supported each benign correction and keep positive lessons distinct from scoped exclusions. When a label is withdrawn, rebuild from the remaining evidence, check whether later lessons depend on that evidence and retest past rules after temporarily removing a compensating edit. *New:* Undoing an old numeric patch is different from retracting the evidence that justified it; either can leave recent behavior unchanged while causing a later exception to fail. *First test:* Give a toy learner two overlapping rule updates and withdraw one verified witness. Compare inverse rollback, replay from the surviving evidence and selective recomputation; measure old-rule recovery and the effect of a withheld exception.

**ACA4. Use temporary intervention to gather better examples, not as the learned artifact.** Parts: AL25 rule binding, AL27 separate skill and inhibition, AL28 temporary exploration, AL29 distill a teaching pulse, AL30 exception-versus-geometry budget, AL32 next-write headroom, AL33 frozen benign edit prior, AL34 independent behavior reviewer, NP4-13 response to future evidence, HC20 tested exceptions. *Mechanism:* For harmless toy table transformations, compare an unmodified sampler with a temporary reduction of a deliberately planted task decoy during candidate generation. Remove the intervention before evaluation and retain only externally verified examples to learn a reusable rule. Keep the independent old-task check fixed. *New:* Improved exploration and actual teaching are separable: a correction that only helps while the temporary intervention runs has not taught the new rule. *First test:* Withhold a rule family and compare verified examples from both samplers at equal calls with lookup and low-rank learning at equal storage; test on fresh values after the intervention is gone.

**ACA5. Keep the evidence, the positive rule and the scoped correction separately.** Parts: AL01 rolling contrasts, AL02 balanced shortcut cells, AL03 contrast swaps, AL04 independent witnesses, AL05 subtraction and addition factorial, AL06 verifier-scored twins, AL17 discriminating queries, AL31 no-write certificates, PL5-10 held-out effects, HC26 independent tests, AL07 bounded shortcut write, AL08 selector-controlled exclusion, AL09 scoped rescue, AL10 sign reversal, AL11 dependency-specific correction, AL20 local interference repair, AL22 shared exclusions, AL26 relational scope, HC13 concentration assay, TT03 protected updates, AL12 reversible dose, AL13 evidence retraction, AL14 frozen and current contrasts, AL15 disagreement memory, AL16 separate regime transitions, AL18 record of incompatibility, AL19 order assay, AL21 cancellation versus forgetting, AL23 retirement versus prohibition, AL24 preserve future distinctions, NP3-22 source withdrawal, NP5-01 lesson interaction, NP5-26 protected future reads, OE3-12 relearnability, AL25 rule binding, AL27 separate skill and inhibition, AL28 temporary exploration, AL29 distill a teaching pulse, AL30 exception-versus-geometry budget, AL32 next-write headroom, AL33 frozen benign edit prior, AL34 independent behavior reviewer, NP4-13 response to future evidence, HC20 tested exceptions. *Mechanism:* Separate verified evidence, learned benign rules and corrections to specific task dependencies. Admit changes only with an independent retention check, preserve disputed cases for a future query and reopen shared exclusions when evidence changes. Keep all interventions inside synthetic task fixtures, not model safety behavior. *New:* A continual learner can preserve what made a correction warranted and reuse that knowledge when a rule returns, instead of trying to infer its intent from a merged delta. *First test:* Run a synthetic stream with a misleading cue, a genuinely new rule, a retraced regime and a withdrawn witness. Compare joint and separated memory against replay, lookup and matched-rank LoRA at equal state and examples; report new-rule transfer, old-rule retention, reversibility and cost.
