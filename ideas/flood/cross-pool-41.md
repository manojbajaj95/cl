# Cross-pool merges, round 41

Focal sources: `tree/accommodation-games.md` (PR #167, AG#) and `tree/return-corridors.md` (PR #168, RC#). Crosses with self-closing witnesses (PR #165, SW#), rejected-edit witnesses (PR #166, RE#), witness examples (PR #163, WE#), finite edit guards (PR #164, FG#), finite safe regions (PR #152, FS#), make-before-break (PR #157, MB#), skill markets (PR #124, KM#), and earlier rounds 1–40. These are raw proposals. A winning strategy must answer every declared move, not just the ones that make its residual menu look good. Charge transient cover before corridor entry as well as the frozen buffer after entry. Hold the editor bank, truthful example quota and accommodation menu fixed across comparisons; distinguish safe endpoints, served prefixes and legitimate refusal.

**AGR1. Teach different branches to return to the same place with a move still left.** Parts: AG01 winning kernels, AG02 reactive policies, RC1 entry funnels, RC3 obligation-specific contraction, SW4 paired relations, FG14 bounded strategies, FS# finite safe regions. *Mechanism:* Select today's truthful examples so every declared training branch enters a shared witness region by a deadline. Require entry into the part of that region with a verified answer to every allowed next edit, not just into a region that preserves today's labels. Check all prefixes and protect attained new-task utility along the way. *New:* Branches can converge in what they leave the learner able to do next without converging in their full weights. *First test:* Enumerate a two-move ReLU game with equal-accuracy branches that differ in future moves. Compare natural convergence, one-step guards and selection for entry into the winning set.

**AGR2. Sell a handoff only when every path can reach the replacement before the lease expires.** Parts: AG04 live versus atomic, AG09 residual frontiers, RC7 transient cover, RC16 declared exits, MB4 draining requests, KM7 reservation maturities, MPC5 overlap scheduling. *Mechanism:* Keep the source serving and reserve transient witness capacity until the slowest admitted branch can use the common buffer. Do not release at average entry or while old requests are pinned. Offer fast-entry and slow-entry variants with different checked residual menus; an exit requiring the source must retain the lease or be refused before it takes effect. *New:* A permanent byte saving may be unusable because the adaptation game needs a short-lived capacity guarantee first. *First test:* Vary burst load, peak bytes and late branch entry. Compare immediate atomic swap, fixed overlap and a checked handoff schedule at the same admitted edits and service floors.

**AGR3. Combine a public entry port with a proof of what it lets a later writer do.** Parts: AG12 residual factorization, AG13 expiry, RC14 public entry ports, RC23 changed future laws, SW11 mixed regions, FG15 effect inclusion, WFG7 scoped transport. *Mechanism:* Export a corridor's verified buffer and the residual moves it supports. An independent writer with a frozen procedure proves entry under its actual optimizer and that its permitted effects preserve the port's premises. Compose writers only where joint effects and intermediate states are checked; otherwise retain the valid slice and withhold the rest. *New:* One learner can reuse another's proof without sharing training examples or assuming that similar writer names imply similar dynamics. *First test:* Give held-out adapters equal passive outputs but different bounded effects; compare full rechecking, endpoint-only reuse and scoped entry contracts.

**AGR4. Teach a common reply to edits the learner cannot tell apart.** Parts: AG07 partial observation, AG21 residual bits, RC2 obligation quotients, RC11 symmetries, WE4 counterfactual responses, SW12 source summaries, SRW4 targeted questions. *Mechanism:* Group possible edit effects by their observed probe transcript. Choose examples that make all states in a group enter a verified region admitting one common reply, even if a full-information policy would choose different moves. If the group has no shared reply, buy a distinguishing probe or refuse. *New:* A teaching example can replace a future measurement by making an otherwise important uncertainty irrelevant to the accommodation. *First test:* Blind two equally plausible adapters that differ in a hidden gate. Compare extra probes, exact full-information planning and teaching for a common reply at fixed evidence cost.

**AGR5. Teach from a losing game tree, not just its last failed edit.** Parts: AG16 counter-strategy trees, AG22 horizon exhaustion, RC17 worst-branch corridors, RC9 verifier work, RE05 causal prefixes, RE17 displaced refusals, SRW2 correction classes. *Mechanism:* When every reply to a fixed sequence eventually loses, extract a small branching counter-strategy over the declared reply menu. Find truthful examples that stop the critical branches from splitting into incompatible witness regions. Retain the original demand and check all replies after refitting; a budgeted search that finds no winning reply is not an impossibility proof. *New:* The useful lesson may prevent a safe first move from spending the only answer two rounds later. *First test:* Plant first-round ties that conceal different exhaustion traps. Compare terminal-error replay, single-refusal repair and counter-strategy teaching on a blinded population of three-round games.

**AGR6. Trade future replies as a scarce resource, not the size of today's update.** Parts: AG09 residual frontiers, AG11 residual cost, RC13 proof exchange rates, RC20 certificate debt, KM2 realized interference, FS# finite paths, SRW6 opportunity costs. *Mechanism:* Offer equal-utility write variants and quote their checked residual options against a fixed demand bank. Price not only consumed moves but the extra witness bytes and verification work needed to return after the chosen edit. Recompute prices after acquisitions become protected; a concentrated menu loss cannot be canceled by irrelevant moves elsewhere. *New:* A tiny edit can be expensive because it breaks a cheap shared return proof, while a larger edit leaves the future reply menu intact. *First test:* Compare norm pricing, raw residual counts and demand-weighted certified costs on withheld editor sequences and actual retained utility.

**AGR7. Replenish a reply menu without undoing the skill that just spent it.** Parts: AG06 replenishment, AG17 growing obligations, RC15 nested corridors, RC19 full optimizer state, SW20 invariant tails, FG13 residual guards, WE9 compatible refresh. *Mechanism:* Use a costed reshaping move from the common menu to enter a fresh corridor whose warranty includes every installed task. Verify momentum and clock state so a later refresh does not revive the old vulnerability. Publish only a finite renewed horizon unless an inductive strategy also covers replenishment cost and the declared obligation growth. *New:* Room can be renewable without treating yesterday's checkpoint, with fewer learned tasks, as today's safe destination. *First test:* Alternate frozen edits and replenishment under bounded memory. Compare resets, one-use allowances and scoped renewal on acquired-task retention and time to honest exhaustion.

**AGR8. Compress the game state only when the next training job cannot tell the difference.** Parts: AG08 residual menus, AG10 future-game equivalence, AG18 optimizer-state moves, RC10 affine training regions, RC12 buffer grammar, SW10 optimizer clocks, WE3 delayed effects. *Mechanism:* Propose a shared witness and policy for post-edit states with identical remaining games. Check that permitted training steps and edit/reply transitions preserve the equivalence, including delayed slot reads; fall back to separate regions where they diverge. Export a compact class rather than raw weights when the class is independently checkable. *New:* A return corridor could compress both the memory needed to continue training and the strategy needed to negotiate future edits. *First test:* Match weights but vary momentum at the cut. Compare weight bins, parameter envelopes and verified game classes for bytes, checker work and false merges.

**AGR9. Let uncertainty choose a conservative reply without letting it curate the demand.** Parts: AG05 adaptive editors, AG14 conservative predictors, RC8 gate-stable tubes, RC18 probabilistic entry, SW6 rare-rollout coverage, RE21 fixed demand, MB18 expiring readiness. *Mechanism:* A learned predictor proposes low-cost replies and predicts return time, but a live checker verifies the actual prefix trajectory and fallback. Keep rare branches under deterministic floors even if common training branches have a separate probabilistic entry contract. A predicted long return invalidates no obligation and licenses no out-of-scope write. *New:* A corridor can make reply search cheaper without letting a confidence score silently restrict the game. *First test:* Let an editor adapt after seeing previous replies and target rare gates. Compare exhaustive planning, score-only replies and checked proposals on the same menu, including search time and abstention.

**AGR10. Find out whether the winning corridor was free all along.** Parts: AG15 reshape versus react, AG19 contingent-planning control, AG20 fixed menus, RC6 natural entry, RC21 non-rigidity, RC22 meta-learning control, WE22 substitution break-even, FG21 fixed editor bank. *Mechanism:* Compare ordinary training that waits for natural branch convergence, truthful selection aimed at the winning corridor, and budget-matched reactive repair. Give each the same moves, demand and verification access. Separate reductions in witness size from gains that come only from stalled later learning, easier verification or a longer overlap. *New:* A learner could pay only for the part of branch alignment that expands useful certified play beyond what time and ordinary planning already buy. *First test:* Sweep naturally convergent and persistently branching hosts with equal acquisition floors, measuring net utility, verified bytes and total work.

**AGR11. A continual learner that teaches itself where to return after each useful edit.** Parts: AG01 winning kernels, AG02 reactive play, AG03 compiled policies, AG04 visible prefixes, AG05 adaptive demand, AG06 renewal, AG07 partial observation, AG08 residual menus, AG09 Pareto choices, AG10 game equivalence, AG11 opportunity prices, AG12 joint contracts, AG13 expiry, AG14 checked predictors, AG15 proactive shaping, AG16 counter-strategies, AG17 new obligations, AG18 optimizer moves, AG19 planner controls, AG20 equal menus, AG21 residual bits, AG22 exhaustion, RC1 entry funnels, RC2 obligation quotients, RC3 targeted contraction, RC4 synchronizing data, RC5 shared bottlenecks, RC6 natural entry, RC7 transient cover, RC8 gate stability, RC9 verifier work, RC10 affine regions, RC11 symmetry, RC12 buffer grammar, RC13 proof exchange, RC14 public ports, RC15 nested corridors, RC16 exits, RC17 worst branches, RC18 stochastic entry, RC19 full optimizer state, RC20 certificate debt, RC21 non-rigidity, RC22 meta-learning controls, RC23 changed laws, SW#, RE#, WE#, FG#, FS#, MB#, KM#, SRW3, SRW6, MPC5. *Mechanism:* Select truthful current examples that guide declared future learning branches into a shared, independently checked witness region with a verified accommodation policy. Cover transients, switch and drain before retiring the source, and publish a scoped residual menu after each admitted edit. Use losing game trees to choose later examples or issue a checked fallback or refusal; never drop acquired tasks to make the game winnable. *New:* The learner can pay in teaching examples to reduce both the memory it must carry and the future repair choices it must keep open. *First test:* Use a bounded stateful ReLU world with delayed optimizer effects, open requests, branch-dependent teaching and a blind editor bank. Compare ordinary convergence plus incremental verification, same-menu contingent planning and matched learned trajectory shaping. Report old-service prefixes, new-task utility, residual admissions, evidence bytes, peak overlap and verification work separately.
