Raw proposals for one continuously pretrained lineage in harmless synthetic streams.

**LWX1. Move event probabilities without rewriting their internal structure.** Parts: NP3-01 mass transport, NP3-02 fixed marginals, NP3-04 stop hazard, NP3-06 language reweighting, NP3-08 shared joint, NP2-01 byte effects, NP2-11 protected reads, NP07 update conjugacy. *Mechanism:* Declare the conserved quantity before writing: within-slice odds for a language reweighting, marginals for a dependence update, and conditional continuations for a stopping edit. Choose the operator that matches the lesson and check the full finite distribution after migration rather than a shortlist of logits. *New:* Two edits can give the same answer on a probe yet disagree on which probabilities should move elsewhere. *First test:* Use enumerable synthetic grammars with overlapping slices and ambiguous segmentations. Compare constrained edits with ordinary fine-tuning at equal target fit; check conservation and reversibility.

**LWX2. Write a constraint once and keep it meaningful on unseen representations.** Parts: NP3-03 bound roles, NP3-05 finite symmetry, NP3-07 feasible intervals, NP3-09 partial orders, NP3-10 equivalence classes, NP3-11 interior credit, NP3-12 conservative refinement, NP2-26 cross-alphabet correction, PL5-10 held-out effects, D5-26 future teaching responses. *Mechanism:* Specify protected constraints in observable byte coordinates. Test that binding and invariance generalize across names and execution paths, and let later writes choose values within bounds or divide a conserved total. Where endpoint supervision agrees, test whether reallocating intermediate credit still changes what a subsequent lesson can teach. *New:* The writable object need not be a fixed answer: it can be a set of permitted future updates with a stable meaning after the representation changes. *First test:* Vary synthetic names, token boundaries and event partitions. Compare point edits, augmentation and scoped constraints on unseen permutations and subsequent refinements.

**LWX3. Withdraw only the evidence channel that supplied a conclusion.** Parts: NP3-13 inherited evidence, NP3-14 revocable calibration, NP3-16 coverage of silence, NP3-18 common experiment units, NP3-20 rule versus instance, NP2-21 independent roots, NP32 scoped omissions, PL32 independent support. *Mechanism:* Separate shared experimental units, inherited statistics and new observations from the rules that interpret them. Removing a unit, calibration or failed search's coverage triggers a different recomputation while leaving independent measurements and directly taught instances intact. *New:* Two downstream claims can share a source but disagree on what survives revocation because one used its measurement and the other used its interpretation or coverage. *First test:* Use toy instruments with duplicated reports and partial independent confirmation. Compare root-level deletion and channel-specific revocation against a reference reconstruction.

**LWX4. Preserve coarse conclusions without promoting uncertain details.** Parts: NP3-15 defeaters, NP3-17 uncertain partitions, NP3-23 admission proofs, NP3-24 precision guarantees, NP3-25 expertise calibration, NP3-26 coarse observability, NP2-22 admissible sets, NP30 coherent joins, OE25 independently retained verifier. *Mechanism:* Maintain an estimate, its precision claim and its grounds separately. Use admissible source partitions to bound dependence, and allow an observation to support only the distinctions it can resolve. After a proof or defeater is removed, check which conclusions become possible under independently retained evidence. *New:* A revocation can widen uncertainty or restore a blocked inference without changing a point estimate or making a fine-grained conclusion credible. *First test:* Use synthetic reports with shared totals and incompatible allocations. Revoke selected credentials or calibration evidence and score bounds, coarse answers and dated joins against a reference.

**LWX5. Change the basis of a belief and test what the next lesson sees.** Parts: NP3-19 support transfer, NP3-21 optimizer calibration withdrawal, NP3-22 source-tagged distillation, PL4-17 factorized moments, PL03 successor stress tests, TT28 no-update shadow, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Hold current answers fixed while exchanging evidential support or removing a source from the preconditioner. Track source-dependent distillation separately. Use matched successor branches to distinguish what changes on later withdrawal, later learning or later restoration. *New:* A write with no immediate output effect can change which future correction is allowed and which gradients carry it. *First test:* Train toy learners with matched answers but different provenance and moment state. Compare answer-only edits, source-aware weights and separately revocable optimizer state under a common continuation.

**LWX6. Make one weight-write ledger that records what each operation must conserve.** Parts: NP3-01 mass transport, NP3-02 fixed marginals, NP3-04 stop hazard, NP3-06 language reweighting, NP3-08 shared joint, NP2-01 byte effects, NP2-11 protected reads, NP07 update conjugacy, NP3-03 bound roles, NP3-05 finite symmetry, NP3-07 feasible intervals, NP3-09 partial orders, NP3-10 equivalence classes, NP3-11 interior credit, NP3-12 conservative refinement, NP2-26 cross-alphabet correction, PL5-10 held-out effects, D5-26 future teaching responses, NP3-13 inherited evidence, NP3-14 revocable calibration, NP3-16 coverage of silence, NP3-18 common experiment units, NP3-20 rule versus instance, NP2-21 independent roots, NP32 scoped omissions, PL32 independent support, NP3-15 defeaters, NP3-17 uncertain partitions, NP3-23 admission proofs, NP3-24 precision guarantees, NP3-25 expertise calibration, NP3-26 coarse observability, NP2-22 admissible sets, NP30 coherent joins, OE25 independently retained verifier, NP3-19 support transfer, NP3-21 optimizer calibration withdrawal, NP3-22 source-tagged distillation, PL4-17 factorized moments, PL03 successor stress tests, TT28 no-update shadow, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Define byte-level conservation laws for distribution edits and keep evidence, interpretation, authority and optimizer effects separately revocable. Check later lessons after tokenizer changes and retain only conclusions supported by surviving observations. *New:* A lineage can keep the same answer while changing why it is justified or how the next update can move it. *First test:* Run a harmless synthetic next-token stream with overlapping events, dependent observations and changing vocabularies. Compare point edits and whole-source deletion with the scoped ledger against a finite oracle.
