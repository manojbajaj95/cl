# Cross-pool merges, round 24

Focal sources: `tree/fossil-beliefs.md` (PR #132, FB#) and `tree/information-dumping-map.md` (PR #133, ID#). Crosses with Y8 Lindy/Chesterton retirement, X4 Popperian contracts, survival memory (PR #112, SP#), the diffuse-damage result for fixed MEMIT keys (PR #24), non-invertible consolidation (PR #111, NC#), optimizer-state memory (PR #109, OM#), memory dilution (PR #103, MD#), inheritance (PR #130, GI#), and earlier rounds. These are raw proposals. Score the inherited rule, the current premise, authorized policy exceptions, historical queries and untouched old tasks separately. A weak reader failing to recover an old distinction is not proof it was erased.

**FI1. When a premise expires, discard the binding rather than the procedure.** Parts: FB6 expiring fact slots, FB7 role-equivariant operators, ID7 friction valves, ID10 write exchanges, MD3 authority versus binding, GI1 invariant skills, CT4. *Mechanism:* Give otherwise identical successors the same rule and swapped world facts. Estimate which update directions change premise-dependent answers but spare the procedure under held-out premises. Route a fixed damping budget toward the stale binding and retain a separate public policy port; classify the damping as suppression, not deletion. *New:* Premise expiration can determine where the optimizer spends its forgetting budget without asking the learner to abandon the skill. *First test:* Use a tiny route ranker with bridge closures and an owner vehicle restriction. Compare scoped damping with explicit new-fact replay, lookup-only slots and an equally tuned generic projector; report procedure score at matched fossil rate and new-task acquisition.

**FI2. Put a sunset clause on the right to throw an old distinction away.** Parts: FB19 correction provenance, FB20 correction leases, ID1 admissible collisions, ID11 tomorrow's readout, Y8, X4, SP13 repair windows, NC24 irreducible memory. *Mechanism:* Before a quantization or reset, propose scoped preimages that collapse to the same post-state. A retirement receipt must state which old or authorized queries can no longer distinguish those histories and what would revoke permission. Probe likely successor reads, not only current ones; keep a minimal recovery witness when the premises for deletion are likely to change. *New:* The authorization to erase a distinction travels with explicit validity conditions, separate from whether that distinction was inexpensive to retain. *First test:* Enumerate a small fixed-point learner with archived facts and owner exceptions. Compare audited expiry with age-only, recency and equal-byte checkpointing under both genuine and incorrectly reported corrections.

**FI3. Catch a fossil that first returns in the velocity register.** Parts: FB11 correction versus inheritance order, FB16 paired students, ID3 first-loss cuts, ID9 momentum shadow, OM5 mount points, OM7 actual updates, FT4 (PR #123), IR5. *Mechanism:* Match corrected outputs in two heirs trained with different orderings, then cross their weight and optimizer states in scratch copies. Run the same next minibatches, calibrating step sizes, and ask whether old-world answers follow the slots only after the write. Reset narrowly or rehearse only the implicated learning response if the slots carry the effect. *New:* A correction can be live at launch yet unusually easy to reverse; the release test can identify which state needs repair. *First test:* Use a two-layer classifier with momentum, counterbalanced order, dose-matched launch mismatch, first-step checks and a fixed-feature control. Compare localized slot intervention with full reset, additional correction replay and output gating at matched acquisition.

**FI4. Build a fossil alarm that does not require the damage to be sparse.** Parts: FB2 negative-control ancestors, FB22 consequence-weighted audits, ID5 transported output footprint, ID19 equivalence classes, PR #24 diffuse MEMIT-key damage, MD1 retention debits, Q9 tomography. *Mechanism:* Compare aggregate fossil and retained-skill effects on independent, stratified probe panels over multiple update cuts. Sample broad combinations before spending more calls on any localized repair; allocate by expected decision impact and uncertainty, not by a presumption that the largest few damaged keys tell the whole story. *New:* An inherited-fact detector can recommend a broad repair or abstain from localization instead of treating an unstable ranked list of keys as a diagnosis. *First test:* Inject diffuse and localized fossil leakage into separate tiny model transfers at matched initial loss. Compare grouped causal probes with top-key syndromes, independent probes and broad equal-cost adaptation; grade the selected repair separately from the alarm.

**FI5. Keep an archived answer in a reader that cannot silently become the current default.** Parts: FB9 scope restrictions, FB17 historical namespace, ID4 weights/slots/tape matrix, ID6 conditional tape reads, NC1 edit-closed resets, SV1 three expiry clocks, IR7. *Mechanism:* Move an obsolete fact into a dated archive or teaching exercise, then test whether the live default can recover it when the archive is not authorized. Use paired archive ablations and later update forks to find leaks through ordinary retrieval or optimizer slots. Keep permitted historical reads callable rather than equating noninterference with total erasure. *New:* Forgetting the default and remembering a dated claim can have separate storage paths and measurable leakage boundaries. *First test:* In a small time-indexed rules learner switch defaults twice, with and without an explicit history field. Compare isolated archives with prompt tags, gated distillation and full checkpoint rewind under a fixed serving budget.

**FI6. Archive what the future reader needs, not what the backward pass happens to shed.** Parts: FB5 correction-robust donor evidence, FB18 cheap reactivation audits, ID2 amplification knockoffs, ID14 residue-to-repair compilation, SP3 soft read parity, RT2 uncompute (PR #131), GI22 retirement interview. *Mechanism:* Rank candidate retained summaries by incremental old-service recovery when added to the terminal state and identical allowed batch inputs, not by how much an inverse run destabilizes when damaged. Compile the best residue, correction evidence or rehearsal sketch into a deployable reader before discarding the temporary diagnostic branch. *New:* Rollback instrumentation can identify a tiny repair payload even where keeping an exact inverse is too expensive or inappropriate. *First test:* Use the fixed-point host with zero-damage, matched amplification and no-tape controls. Compare selected packets to random residues, ordinary compressed replay and partial checkpoints at equal bytes and live repair steps.

**FI7. Make a sink map expire when its premise does.** Parts: FB14 correlated facts, FB20 correction leases, ID21 map expiration, ID23 cross-optimizer translation, OM12 cross-optimizer protection, SP7 curriculum sets, CT9. *Mechanism:* Attach each direction-sensitive intervention to the premise combinations, optimizer and serving port on which its forward behavior was tested. Move to a changed regime using a short paired direction test and held-out premise combinations. Refresh only directions whose intervention effects changed, or fall back to rehearsal when the old map no longer transports. *New:* The bounds on a forgetting map become an active renewal signal, not a reason to freeze a learning rule after one calibration. *First test:* Change the correlation between premises and switch momentum to plain SGD in separate streams. Compare targeted renewal with cached directions, periodic remapping and a full same-budget re-estimator.

**FI8. Give high-consequence premise failures a way to challenge deliberate forgetting.** Parts: FB1 separating world queries, FB10 query budgeting, FB22 consequence-weighted audits, ID8 fair discards, ID24 unavoidable loss, SP9 decision-changing probes, KT4, X4. *Mechanism:* When a proposed resource limit would collapse two still-plausible premise/skill pairs, buy the cheapest observation that changes which pair must stay distinct. If observation cannot resolve the conflict, compare keeping an executable exception with declaring the old service infeasible under the current capacity rather than distributing harm under an average floor. *New:* A learner can spend scarce evidence to decide which distinctions require capacity before it allocates inevitable loss. *First test:* Use a symbolic planner with a real memory cap, cheap ambiguous signals and rare expensive failures. Compare consequence-driven probes to confidence sampling, worst-loss replay and a same-signal planner; audit first-return safety on the entire old panel.

**FI9. Diagnose whether two safe inherits share a hidden sink.** Parts: FB12 protect future corrections, FB21 commuting inherits, ID13 parity across exits, ID22 joint sinks, NC21 commuting projections, IR10, BI7 growth probation. *Mechanism:* Admit two new teaching packages provisionally, then run both orders and a common future on matched copies. Cross the erasure regimes: each package alone may spare the same old distinction only because it leaves a different fallback path untouched. If the combination exposes a collision or slow loss, reserve an independent path, order the updates, or scope one correction. *New:* A pair of succession packages can exhaust functional redundancy without breaking either package's own test. *First test:* Plant two individually benign writes with a joint slot/feature interaction in a small compatible multitask model. Compare sequential promotion, equal-cost joint replay, joint sink testing and full shared suffix rechecking.

**FI10. Separate skill drift from an ancestor that was confidently wrong.** Parts: FB3 rule queries, FB13 separate marginals, FB15 grounded credit, ID16 borrow-back, ID18 overwrite recovery, MD8 derived clauses, GI3 triangulation, LG6. *Mechanism:* Use a temporary rule probe and an independent current-world read. Compare what the teacher recommends under the original and supplied premises, then compare what the successor can do with a borrowed rule readout versus after repair. If restoring the readout revives only the stale answer, revise the target instead of preserving its optimizer trace. *New:* The same measured old-output loss can warrant three different actions: restore access, relearn a procedure, or leave an obsolete output forgotten. *First test:* In a gate-and-load simulator cross an obsolete premise, an incorrect successor correction and a genuinely lost routine. Compare with a world-conditioned ancestor, two-teacher patching and same-cost old/new replay.

**FI11. A lineage that decides what to forget by asking what still needs to be true.** Parts: FB1 separating queries, FB2 negative controls, FB5 correction-robust evidence, FB6 fact slots, FB7 operators, FB9 scopes, FB11 order, FB12 future corrections, FB16 paired students, FB17 historical reads, FB18 recovery, FB19 provenance, FB20 leases, FB21 commuting inherits, FB22 audit allocation, ID1 collisions, ID2 inverse controls, ID3 first cuts, ID4 state substitution, ID5 transported footprints, ID6 tape reads, ID7 friction, ID8 discard budgets, ID9 slots, ID11 deferred reads, ID13 parity, ID14 residue repair, ID19 equivalence, ID21 map expiration, ID22 joint sinks, ID24 limits, Y8, X4, SP13, Q9, NC24, OM5, MD1, GI3, IR7. *Mechanism:* Store an inherited procedure with scoped fact and policy queries. Before claiming a correction or retiring a memory, separate plausible rules on fresh world interventions. Follow the useful distinctions through optimizer cuts into weights, slots and permitted side state; choose a retention packet or first-loss intervention only when a forward trial improves live service. Where facts genuinely expire, keep historical reads separate and verify later learning does not resurrect the obsolete default. Suspend further deletions and recheck routing if the contracts they relied on change. *New:* Semantic retirement and optimizer-level disposal become separate, testable decisions joined by the same deployment obligation. *First test:* Extend PR #130's corrected-world toy with PR #133's fixed-point update cuts and a compatible old-task cohort. Plant a bad teacher premise, an admissible arithmetic collision, a recoverable masking event and diffuse interference. Compare tuned disagreement gating, correction replay, conventional optimizer resets and matched-byte checkpointing. Report live old service and current corrections at matched acquisition, access under declared readers, first-return repair cost and residual fossil behavior.
