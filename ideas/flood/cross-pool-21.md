# Cross-pool merges, round 21

Focal sources: `tree/label-free-forgetting-detection.md` (PR #126, LF#) and `tree/developmental-growth.md` (PR #127, DG#). Crosses with MX3/MY3 correctability, Q9 tomography, B13 dual-written alarms (PR #48), the detection-versus-recovery result (PR #101), neuro-bio (PR #46, NB#), and tokenizer/interface growth (PR #115, BI#). All are raw proposals for software learners. Every ungraded arm keeps fresh old-task labels sealed and is compared with a strong affordable stratified labelled canary; count retained references, shadow and probe calls, and development/repair costs. Detecting a change, locating a fault, and restoring service are separate outcomes.

**LG1. Use the new branch's first breaths to catch readout spillover.** Parts: LF2 signed responses, LF12 crossed transplants, DG8 receptor gating, DG13 scaling, Q9 tomography, BR2. *Mechanism:* While a branch is on probation, give its outbound path small positive and negative gain changes on matched old inputs. Cross these with an old/current shared readout swap. Compare against the healthy response on common seeds and decode branch interactions explicitly rather than presuming linear additivity. If bypassing the branch restores the old pattern, stage an isolation repair before considering more growth. *New:* A growth event supplies a named intervention that can separate harmful spillover from a change in the decoder's sensitivity, even with unchanged incumbent weights. *First test:* In the PR #127 widening world, plant branch-only, readout-only and joint faults plus harmless rescaling. Compare against old-logit KL and one-at-a-time ablations, then grade localization and actual repair separately on fresh old inputs.

**LG2. Reopen a critical period for the route that lost the ability to learn.** Parts: LF4 matched immediate answers, LF13 restoration curves, DG7 reversible commitment, DG22 critical-period reopening, MX3, MY3, Z6. *Mechanism:* Measure a family of paired instruction-override responses, cross cue strength with a reversible local unmask, and compare shadow cohorts with matched launch decisions and margins. Use the contrast to nominate a cohort for temporary plasticity, not as a direct estimate of accuracy or a universal scalar. Judge the nomination by later learning and retention under an identical practice stream. *New:* The meter would choose where to reopen plasticity, rather than compress all skills to one global readiness score. *First test:* In a routed symbolic learner, plant equally accurate cohorts with distinct gate locks and compare local reopening to LR tuning, age-only reopening and a margin-matched correctability selector.

**LG3. Grow a small reference from two independent ways of remembering.** Parts: LF7 independent realizations, LF20 historical veto, DG2 shadow decoders, B13 dual-written procedures, POP2 failure directions (PR #116), GP6. *Mechanism:* Keep a text procedure and an independently reconstructed compact executable path on the same ungraded requests. Raise a specific divergence alert only in regions where the paths agreed during healthy operation and where their update dependencies differ. If both use the same newly changed primitive, mark the alarm as unable to diagnose common failure. *New:* Choosing what to duplicate for independent observation can differ from choosing what to duplicate to serve traffic. *First test:* Use a tiny rules agent with note-conditioned and learned routes; plant a shared primitive failure, independent corruption and a valid improvement. Compare with one old checkpoint, raw disagreement and both routes updated together at equal bytes.

**LG4. Move a sentinel only after its replacement inherits what it can distinguish.** Parts: LF18 sentinel rotation, LF21 staggered cohorts, LF22 coverage/abstention, DG14 transient scaffold, DG21 variable maturation, NB25, Z6, KT2. *Mechanism:* Let short-lived probe circuits mature in cohorts, but require an overlap window in which the incoming circuit responds to the same known sandbox interventions on fixed probe families. Stagger replacements and use a holdback cohort to subtract shared wrapper drift. Retain only the overlap needed to cover still-distinguishable failure stories; explicitly mark common blind spots. *New:* A monitor can outlive its own implementation without resetting its monitoring baseline every time the model grows. *First test:* Replay workload and wrapper shifts while rotating probes in a tiny route mesh. Compare against synchronously rotated, fixed and count-based panels at equal lifetime storage and calls.

**LG5. Let a missing consumer appeal before a dormant circuit is pruned.** Parts: LF15 fixed-prefix reachability, LF16 route enrichment, DG11 consumer-verified pruning, DG12 competitive capacity, NB28 ghost conduits, GP2, KT10. *Mechanism:* When a branch appears unused, give its registered downstream consumers a short controlled access window before allocating its space elsewhere. Run matched calls just before and after the suspect gateway without exposing the desired outcome. Retain a compact reactivation trace when downstream behavior still depends on the branch; flag inconclusive cases rather than treating silence as consent. *New:* Pruning distinguishes lack of demand from inability to reach a service, including stale consumers the ordinary router no longer represents. *First test:* In a multi-stage agent queue, hide a rare but usable branch and an equally quiet broken one. Compare degree/activation pruning, fixed timeouts and ordinary forced routing, measuring old-task return and capacity freed.

**LG6. Diagnose the broken seam before letting neighboring compartments regrow it.** Parts: LF12 crossed transplants, LF17 challenge trees, DG15 boundary-anchored regeneration, DG16 localized rebuild, NB21 planarian regrowth, FP5, V10 (PR #101). *Mechanism:* Hold the same external environment fixed and cross restored upstream/downstream boundary signals to distinguish severed transport, changed consumer and missing internal competence. Use the diagnosis to choose a scoped regrowth, port repair or rollback in a shadow; report a detection even if no proposed repair works. *New:* A regenerating compartment uses its boundary not only as a reconstruction target but as an intervention surface to decide whether it should regrow at all. *First test:* Plant seam-straddling lesions, API migrations and faults with indistinguishable initial traces in a tiny workflow. Hold the alarm and repair budget fixed; compare stable recovery and new-skill collateral loss to unconditional regeneration and rollback.

**LG7. Make the rebuild remember what the live branch learned yesterday.** Parts: LF4 relaxation twins, LF13 restoration curves, DG5 assimilation, DG17 regeneration-gated plasticity, NB21, CT5, KT2. *Mechanism:* Before an in-place regeneration, materialize two short-lived candidates, one from the standing blueprint and one carrying a compact recent-learning trace. Probe each on historical old inputs and on ungraded recent response neighborhoods, then run the same short continuation. Accept neither automatically if it restores the old trace by deleting recent behavior; leave the live compartment alone or delegate to a fallback. *New:* Regenerability becomes a test of both old service and the ability to carry recent learning through a repair, not just reconstruction of the original weights. *First test:* Add post-growth tasks to PR #127's lesion toy. Compare staged assimilation with frozen blueprint repair, periodic assimilation and checkpoint restore at equal bytes; score all skill generations before and after the next update.

**LG8. Give a tokenizer's daughter branches separate probation histories.** Parts: LF8 balanced semantic squares, LF11 monitor invariance, LF19 disagreement boundaries, DG3 duplication, DG7 reversible fate, BI4 token splits, BI5 tombstones, BR1/BR3. *Mechanism:* Create daughter tokens with an initially reversible alias back to a byte-level reference. Probe matched strings that cross segmentation boundaries and allowed context changes. Use interactions to nominate the split or previously existing consumer as a source of spillover; where the probe relation itself fixes the answer, count it as supervision rather than claiming a label-free win. *New:* A tokenizer can carry a temporary reference path built into each split, so architecture changes are testable before the old segmentation is retired. *First test:* Use a small byte-language model with a merge that helps one context and breaks another. Compare probation and selective alias reversion against static merges, raw perplexity drift and frozen old embeddings plus new tokens.

**LG9. Mutate the repair blueprint on its own shadow before exposing all its descendants.** Parts: LF3 hysteresis, LF9 withheld composition paths, DG1 compartment switches, DG2 shadow enhancers, DG20 canalization, Q9, CT9. *Mechanism:* Toggle the proposed blueprint update in separate small shadows and apply the same ungraded compositional inputs under identical readout conditions. Track both the direct response and what persists after reversing the toggle. If individual changes look benign but the joint shadow diverges on previously stable compositions, keep the old blueprint for those compartments and retry only the implicated combination. *New:* A shared growth rule gets a staged dependency test over actual descendant behavior rather than a global mutation budget based on genotype size. *First test:* Create two local enhancer changes with a shared downstream interaction in a small generated network. Compare joint toggles to independent tests, parameter-distance gates and full blueprint freeze under benign and harmful growth.

**LG10. Let blind spots compete for a new observation port, not a new predictor.** Parts: LF5 tagged responses, LF17 adaptive challenges, LF22 explicit abstention, DG6 lateral inhibition, DG10 guided wiring, KT4, FCU1. *Mechanism:* When two plausible failure stories imply different repairs but have identical signatures at current ports, spend probationary capacity on rival sandbox readouts rather than another confidence head. Drop redundant ports that separate the same stories and reserve a rotation slot for uncovered families. If no legal port resolves the ambiguity, abstain or buy an independently labelled audit outside the ungraded arm. *New:* The monitor can grow better interventions into the learner rather than scoring the same observations harder. *First test:* Build a small routed learner with pairs of observationally identical failures, some separable by a non-answer-bearing toggle. Compare grown ports to more samples of existing signals and fixed diagnosis wiring; charge upkeep and evaluate repair choices at matched spend.

**LG11. Regrow two independent routes from a damaged learner and audit what they still share.** Parts: LF14 pinned world, LF20 healthy-reference disagreement, DG18 slow blueprint, DG19 split-and-regrow ensembles, NB21, POP1 survivor sets, FP1. *Mechanism:* From each of two surviving compartments, rebuild a small complementary route in a shadow against the same frozen environment. Compare histories on ungraded observations before rejoining, and map which failures the branches still share because they inherit one blueprint or gateway. Spend permanent space only on a route with a distinct surviving path; agreement is never a substitute for the old-task audit. *New:* Damage recovery can create a new independent execution path rather than restore the precise common cause that destroyed both copies. *First test:* Lesion a tiny shared-trunk learner and vary whether the environment, trunk or blueprint also changes. Compare split-regrowth with same-trunk replicas, one independent checkpoint and equal-budget repair without redundancy.

**LG12. A learner that grows its own diagnostics and waits for evidence before committing.** Parts: LF2 signed responses, LF4 relaxation, LF7 independent realizations, LF9 compositions, LF11 nuisance checks, LF12 crossed transplants, LF13 restoration curves, LF14 pinned worlds, LF15 fixed prefixes, LF17 challenge trees, LF18 rotation, LF20 historical veto, LF21 cohort contrasts, LF22 abstention, DG1 local switches, DG2 shadow decoders, DG5 assimilation, DG6 competitive growth, DG7 reversible fate, DG8 receptor gates, DG10 new connections, DG11 consumer checks, DG14 temporary scaffolding, DG15 boundary regeneration, DG17 regeneration gates, DG19 split-and-regrow, DG22 reopening, MX3, MY3, Q9, B13, V10, NB21, NB25, Z6, BI4, BR2, GP2, KT4. *Mechanism:* Let a local compartment propose growth and a corresponding disposable diagnostic port. On probation, cross new/old branches, readouts and routes on ungraded inputs with known coverage limits; distinguish benign interface shifts, downstream access loss and behavior changes. Delay permanent commitment, pruning or regeneration until a shadow trial supports the indicated action through a common continuation. Keep recent and old service checks separate and reserve an explicit abstention path when the ports cannot distinguish the alternatives. *New:* Structural development becomes a source of independent probes, and those probes in turn decide which new connections should mature and which old ones can be retired. *First test:* Combine PR #126's symbolic agent and PR #127's shared-readout growth toy. Interleave harmless widening, route starvation, joint seam damage and a rebuild that erases recent learning. Compare equal-budget frozen growth, a labelled canary with the same repair menu, and a same-signal controller without growable ports. Report alarm lead, localization, time to stable recovery, uncovered failures and new-task gain separately.
