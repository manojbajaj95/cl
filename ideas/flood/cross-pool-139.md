## Merge proposals

**ACG1. Give a later reader evidence before it spends labels.** Parts: AL4-01 admission test, AL4-02 ambiguity witness, AL4-03 weakest-contrast matrix, AL4-04 counter-selection factorial, AL4-05 matched old-head agreement, AL4-06 unlabeled query, AL4-07 nuisance-regime holdout, AL4-08 equal-information controls, AL4-12 audit gate disagreements, AL4-13 opposite future labels, NP4-13 future evidence response, PL5-10 held-out effects. *Mechanism:* On a toy circuit, match corrections on present performance and give every baseline the same paired views and swap metadata. Freeze each map before a later reader nominates a distinction without labels. Admit only within measured contrast and noise bounds; on denial return a pair that explains the uncertainty rather than claiming the distinction is unrecoverable. *New:* An admission test should predict which new distinctions are cheap to teach beyond what old-head agreement predicts, even when two new tasks require opposite labels for the same distinction. *First test:* Draw later readers only after freezing the maps and reverse a planted nuisance correlation. Compare against standard alignment with matched coverage, teach a blind sample of disputed cases and measure false admissions and labels to target.

**ACG2. Compose the scope of a read map, not just its coordinates.** Parts: AL4-09 complementary maps, AL4-10 minimal rescue view, AL4-11 scope after a second counter, AL4-14 compression updates scope, AL4-15 audit amortization, AL3-09 composed maps, NP4-19 equivalence under future operations. *Mechanism:* When a second toy edit or a smaller interface removes a distinction, narrow the map's advertised scope. Recheck carried distinctions on new witnesses and offer an extra permitted view only when it addresses the failed contrast at justified cost. *New:* A smaller or composed map can keep high average agreement while losing the one distinction a new reader needs; the useful artifact says which reads still have evidence. *First test:* Apply two benign toy corrections and then shrink the read interface. Compare direct stitching, composed maps with scope tests and replay at matched bytes and views. Count false admissions, label cost and overhead as later reader diversity rises.

**ACG3. Recheck a choice when a solver update could change its ordering.** Parts: AL4-16 solver change footprint, AL4-17 equal-tail cancellation, AL4-18 no-repair witness, AL4-19 fixed-policy comparisons, AL4-20 unseen capability fallback, AL4-21 held-out solver changes, AL4-22 discovery and invalidation factorial, AL4-23 skipped-fork audit, AL3-16 continuation contract, HS10 declared change envelope. *Mechanism:* Discover candidate choices for a planted toy task, then compare clean forced branches under the same finishers. Skip revalidation only when a conservative change bound is below the last verified margin in the same regime. Cancel an exact common tail only if state, remaining budget and scoring semantics match. Unknown changes widen the audit. *New:* A verified preference can survive some solver changes without a new label, but uncertainty about a newly available repair is not evidence of a stable sign. *First test:* Hold out a solver upgrade that combines previously separate capabilities. Cross clean and assisted discovery with pooled and footprint-conditioned rechecks at matched calls; randomly audit skipped choices and report missed flips with uncertainty.

**ACG4. Accumulate evidence debt across changes instead of renewing old preferences by default.** Parts: AL4-24 solver versus goal change, AL4-25 shared capability probe, AL4-26 accumulated uncertainty, AL4-27 discriminating finisher, AL4-28 withdraw without deleting evidence, AL4-29 scoped equivalence, AL4-30 longitudinal cost, AL3-24 targeted revalidation, AL31 conditions for reopening, NP3-22 evidence withdrawal. *Mechanism:* Anchor each comparison to its last clean verification and add conservative change bounds until the old margin no longer holds. Keep competing branch outcomes when withdrawing a preference. Re-score compatible outcomes for a changed benign task threshold instead of assuming a changed solver or replaying everything. *New:* Two individually small changes can jointly invalidate a preference. Keeping the evidence and its accumulated uncertainty lets the next learner reopen only the comparisons it cannot justify. *First test:* On a toy transformation stream change a solver twice and then change the allowed tolerance. Compare accumulated bounds, full replay and ordinary relabeling; report stale preferences, wrongly withdrawn preferences, cost and retention.

**ACG5. Carry an explicit account of what each retained decision still depends on.** Parts: AL4-01 admission test, AL4-02 ambiguity witness, AL4-03 weakest-contrast matrix, AL4-04 counter-selection factorial, AL4-05 matched old-head agreement, AL4-06 unlabeled query, AL4-07 nuisance-regime holdout, AL4-08 equal-information controls, AL4-12 audit gate disagreements, AL4-13 opposite future labels, NP4-13 future evidence response, PL5-10 held-out effects, AL4-09 complementary maps, AL4-10 minimal rescue view, AL4-11 scope after a second counter, AL4-14 compression updates scope, AL4-15 audit amortization, AL3-09 composed maps, NP4-19 equivalence under future operations, AL4-16 solver change footprint, AL4-17 equal-tail cancellation, AL4-18 no-repair witness, AL4-19 fixed-policy comparisons, AL4-20 unseen capability fallback, AL4-21 held-out solver changes, AL4-22 discovery and invalidation factorial, AL4-23 skipped-fork audit, AL3-16 continuation contract, HS10 declared change envelope, AL4-24 solver versus goal change, AL4-25 shared capability probe, AL4-26 accumulated uncertainty, AL4-27 discriminating finisher, AL4-28 withdraw without deleting evidence, AL4-29 scoped equivalence, AL4-30 longitudinal cost, AL3-24 targeted revalidation, AL31 conditions for reopening, NP3-22 evidence withdrawal. *Mechanism:* Store contrast witnesses for admitting later readers and clean branch evidence for keeping or withdrawing local preferences. Preserve the last independent verification point, retest when its assumptions fail and audit samples of decisions made without new labels. Keep all interventions in synthetic task fixtures. *New:* An edit earns a durable place only when it tells later learners what remains justified, where to request new evidence and when to withdraw the old decision. *First test:* Run a proposed toy sequence with two local edits, a new reader and two solver upgrades. Compare the two certificate types, separately and together, against alignment, replay and fresh clean branching at matched information and cost.
