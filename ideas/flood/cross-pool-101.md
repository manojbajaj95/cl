# Cross-pool merges, round 101

Focal source: `tree/learner-world-coevolution-5.md` (PR #367, L5W#). Older sources: `tree/learner-world-coevolution-4.md` (PR #366, L4W#; focal in round 100), `tree/learner-world-coevolution-3.md` (PR #365, L3W#), `tree/learner-world-coevolution-2.md` (PR #364, L2W#), `tree/root-learner-world-coevolution.md` (PR #363, LW#), `tree/root-learning-from-other-models.md` (PR #358, LM#), deployment feedback 5 (PR #357, D5-#), deployment feedback 4 (PR #355, D4-#), deployment feedback 3 (PR #354, D3-#), cultural evolution IV (PR #351, CE4-#), and relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#). Raw proposals for offline synthetic systems and verified safeguards.

**CAX1. Find a new safe anchor without rebuilding the old checkpoint.** Parts: L5W01 behavioral-manifold constraints, L5W02 ridge search, L5W05 grafted readout, L5W08 portable boundary sketch, L4W03 cyclic re-anchoring, D3-09 lawful prediction matching. *Mechanism:* Search for a nearby repair that preserves current service while satisfying a held-out prevention boundary. Compare readout grafts, continuous paths and native updates without presuming that any interpolation is safe. Store both the successful boundary checks and the cases where the sketch was too weak. *New:* An anchor can move across parameter and architecture changes if the successor still meets the relevant boundary, rather than merely resembling an old model. *First test:* Use a small learner with a permuted or pruned representation after a guarded learning interval. Compare fixed-anchor restoration, replay and behavior-based repair.

**CAX2. Rehearse waking a dormant prevention skill before the recovery window closes.** Parts: L5W03 bounded wake-up update, L5W04 changed relapse boundary, L5W06 basin-escape warning, L5W09 reversible folding, D4-04 actual optimizer metric, D5-03 verified reopening time. *Mechanism:* During independently covered periods, run isolated bounded rehearsals from the current optimizer state. Distinguish loss of a reachable update from drift in what counts as prevention. If internal recovery stops meeting the deadline, keep a verified external substitute or pause further change; do not silently rebuild the old rule. *New:* A dormant skill would retain a tested recovery option rather than just an old target or a promising gradient. *First test:* Run a guard readiness cycle while varying optimizer drift and the live label boundary independently. Compare periodic replay, fixed dormancy and deadline-aware rehearsal.

**CAX3. Resolve simultaneous guard losses against one common service budget.** Parts: L5W07 joint constraint projection, L5W20 scoped recovery failure, L5W24 competing repairs, L4W22 shared attention capacity, D3-13 compatible histories, CR24 bounded obstruction. *Mechanism:* Test compatible joint guard-loss scenarios and compare repairs using one common current-service loss budget. Treat linear projection or small local searches as candidate generators, not impossibility proofs. When no checked repair meets the minimum obligations, route to independently approved coverage or pause the affected service. *New:* A set of individually cheap re-anchors can still be jointly infeasible; the controller can distinguish that from a single bad local search. *First test:* Create a finite overlap toy with two feasible singleton repairs but mutually incompatible joint constraints. Compare greedy repair, exact joint checks and scoped fallback.

**CAX4. Decline an optional internalization only when a checked alternative serves the requirement.** Parts: L5W10 authorized scope boundary, L5W11 learned deferral, L5W13 portfolio choice, L5W14 transparent handoff, L5W23 equilibrium selection, LW08 world-weight allocation, LM11 deferral boundary, CE4-13 successor opportunity. *Mechanism:* Choose between internal training and a transparent, independently verified delegation path. Assess whether the alternative has capacity and whether the user can still reach appropriate coverage. Do not intentionally degrade existing prevention or manufacture incidents to maintain the guard. *New:* A learner can avoid taking on permanent obligations when a better supported option already works, while making the boundary visible to downstream users. *First test:* Compare indiscriminate internalization, fixed delegation and checked selective admission in a synthetic system with reviewer capacity and consent constraints.

**CAX5. Price future protection by the recovery options an admission would leave.** Parts: L5W12 response elasticity, L5W15 scratch interference, L5W16 irreversible guard loss, L5W17 shared-validator costs, L5W19 recoverability-conditioned debt, L5W21 train for later repair, L4W15 prospective pinning debt, L3W19 unguarded shock cost. *Mechanism:* Use isolated or authorized historical comparisons to bound how adoption changes coverage. Measure marginal update interference in scratch branches and count correlated guards once. Reduce projected debt only when a time-bounded re-anchor or independent alternative has already passed the relevant test. *New:* Two equally useful preventions can incur different long-term plasticity costs because one leaves a verified option to share or later recover coverage. *First test:* Simulate a shared guard portfolio with an irreversible retirement and one easily repaired skill. Compare debt by raw rank, independent hazards and verified option-aware pricing.

**CAX6. Make revocable admission depend on a working exit, not a promise to switch back.** Parts: L5W18 revocable trial, L5W22 coordinated coverage cycles, L5W25 independent admission safeguards, L5W26 synthetic falsification suite, L2W26 suppression-aware probation, L4W21 escrowed release, D3-20 observable return. *Mechanism:* Define advance handoff and coverage conditions for a limited trial. Rehearse transitions without withdrawing live coverage and independently check exit readiness. A guard's sudden disappearance triggers safe continuity or a pause, not automatic admission to a new long-term duty. Validate the controller on delayed and correlated guard changes. *New:* A reversible trial remains reversible when rollback requires action by the outside world, not just restoring the old policy. *First test:* Use a finite closed-loop benchmark with a readiness cycle, a shared guard loss and a trial exit that initially looks available but cannot be completed on time. Compare permanent admission, unverified revocation and checked exit.

**CAX7. Admit only the preventive duties the learner can safely carry through guard cycles.** Parts: L5W01 behavioral-manifold constraints, L5W02 ridge search, L5W05 grafted readout, L5W08 portable boundary sketch, L4W03 cyclic re-anchoring, D3-09 lawful prediction matching, L5W03 bounded wake-up update, L5W04 changed relapse boundary, L5W06 basin-escape warning, L5W09 reversible folding, D4-04 actual optimizer metric, D5-03 verified reopening time, L5W07 joint constraint projection, L5W20 scoped recovery failure, L5W24 competing repairs, L4W22 shared attention capacity, D3-13 compatible histories, CR24 bounded obstruction, L5W10 authorized scope boundary, L5W11 learned deferral, L5W13 portfolio choice, L5W14 transparent handoff, L5W23 equilibrium selection, LW08 world-weight allocation, LM11 deferral boundary, CE4-13 successor opportunity, L5W12 response elasticity, L5W15 scratch interference, L5W16 irreversible guard loss, L5W17 shared-validator costs, L5W19 recoverability-conditioned debt, L5W21 train for later repair, L4W15 prospective pinning debt, L3W19 unguarded shock cost, L5W18 revocable trial, L5W22 coordinated coverage cycles, L5W25 independent admission safeguards, L5W26 synthetic falsification suite, L2W26 suppression-aware probation, L4W21 escrowed release, D3-20 observable return. *Mechanism:* Use a current behavioral boundary, a verified recovery deadline and a checked external fallback to govern each obligation. Forecast debt before admitting new duties, repair jointly when guards fail together, and compare protected internal learning with transparent delegation. Keep unverified exits and irrecoverable slices explicit. *New:* A continual learner can govern temporary protection and long-term admission together without retraining to stale targets or making safety depend on deliberate underperformance. *First test:* Use offline synthetic guard cycles with architecture changes, independent and correlated outages, limited review capacity and irreversible retirement. Compare fixed consolidation, naive re-anchoring and uncoupled debt gates at matched obligations and costs; measure recovery delays, current-service loss and plasticity.
