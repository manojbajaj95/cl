# Cross-pool merges, round 115

Focal source: `tree/population-learners-5.md` (PR #383, PL5-#). Older sources: `tree/population-learners-4.md` (PR #382, PL4-#; focal in round 114), `tree/population-learners-3.md` (PR #381, PL3-#), `tree/population-learners-2.md` (PR #380, PL2-#), `tree/root-population-learners.md` (PR #379, PL#), test-time learners V (PR #378, T5-#), learning from other models (PR #358, LM#), deployment feedback 4 (PR #355, D4-#), and relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#). Raw proposals for harmless synthetic populations.

**PNX1. Spend newcomer probes on the write most likely to change the decision.** Parts: PL5-01 competing decoder predictions, PL5-02 signed probes, PL5-03 bundled probes, PL5-04 old-choice probe, PL5-07 task-level displacement, PL4-06 boot decoder, PL10 discriminating question, T5-01 matched evidence. *Mechanism:* Select an approved scratch trial that separates candidate decoders on the next desired effect or on a protected old choice. Compare tasks on matched items instead of raw logits. If the uncertainty cannot be resolved within budget, import only the supported effect and keep the rest provisional. *New:* Onboarding can use fewer writes without counting uncertain effects or lost local choices as successful transfer. *First test:* Use toy forks whose decoders agree on familiar tasks but disagree on a held-out edit. Compare random, bundled and disagreement-led probes at equal trial work.

**PNX2. Judge a translation by its second use, not its first success.** Parts: PL5-05 post-write probes, PL5-06 supported partial code, PL5-08 one missing feature, PL5-09 reciprocal teaching, PL5-10 hold out an effect family, PL2-14 future plasticity, D4-24 native lesson response. *Mechanism:* After a first import, check a second authorized lesson on the receiver's changed state. If the missing effect reflects a missing feature, compare a local feature trial with partial import or asking the receiver to choose a better teaching example. Keep the earlier local choice intact. *New:* A decoder can fail because the first write moved the receiver, not because the next lesson lacks value. *First test:* Hold out a synthetic effect family and use fork pairs that agree before a seed edit but differ afterward. Compare static decoders, re-probing and reciprocal fixture selection.

**PNX3. Retire a source from the clock and the decision threshold, not just the average.** Parts: PL5-11 retained-event clock, PL5-12 affine moment transport, PL5-13 tail ranks, PL5-14 recalibrated deferral, PL5-17 missing-source training, PL4-17 detachable moments, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Maintain a retained-event reference in a synthetic stream, and compare state after retirement against it. Check the tail and abstention response separately from mean accuracy. Reuse algebraically transported state only where the feature transformation is known and verifies against fresh retained data. *New:* The same retained decisions can still be served with the wrong confidence because the removed source changed the clock or calibration. *First test:* Vary arrival bursts, affine feature changes and tail shifts in harmless toy streams. Compare subtraction, full retained-only replay and checked state reconstruction.

**PNX4. Replace contaminated inference state only with independently supported state.** Parts: PL5-15 cache descendants, PL5-16 retrieval geometry, PL5-18 source-selected observations, PL5-19 allowed-only summary, PL5-20 clean paired updates, PL4-27 selector dependency, PL3-28 abstaining replacement, PL2-17 unresolved attribution. *Mechanism:* Check whether surviving cache entries, index geometry or summaries were shaped by the retired source even after its entries are gone. Compare an independently collected retained stream with a merely filtered old stream. Rebuild joint statistics from approved paired data where supported, and leave unresolved interactions off rather than infer them from stale outputs. *New:* Deletion and independent reconstruction become distinct claims when the source chose what the learner observed. *First test:* Use synthetic retrieval and summarization streams with planted selection effects and a reproducible joint statistic; compare entry deletion, independent reconstruction and full retained-only replay.

**PNX5. Let newcomers repair the shared language without forcing universal membership.** Parts: PL5-21 translation-stable vocabulary, PL5-22 newcomer-induced split, PL5-24 complementary policies, PL5-26 teaching neighborhoods, PL4-07 transport semantic basis, PL02 retain disagreement, PL27 divergent seed, LM26 checked capability handshake. *Mechanism:* Give a newcomer a way to split an existing symbol when approved held-out examples distinguish its effects. Verify the split across versions and connect forks only where reciprocal transfer works, preserving distinct policies if they enable new shared lessons later. *New:* A growing population can test whether different local capabilities improve the language instead of treating easier onboarding as the only goal. *First test:* Use synthetic forks where the original members cannot distinguish two effects and one later joiner can. Compare a fixed global code, universal relearning and negotiated neighborhoods on transfer and retained diversity.

**PNX6. Change who can teach a joint statistic when one contributor retires.** Parts: PL5-23 paired sufficient evidence, PL5-25 independent successor teacher, PL30 last executable teaching path, PL2-28 retirement-stable audit, PL11 evidence lineage. *Mechanism:* Record a joint statistic as depending on the approved paired evidence, not as two independent confirmations. Validate an independent current-version teacher before retiring the old teaching edge, then simulate delayed reconnection and check that descendants use the new route. If approved paired evidence is inadequate, report that coverage was not retained. *New:* Retirement can preserve a legitimate population-level behavior without relying on a stale member to teach it again. *First test:* Compare local state replacement, deleting every joint statistic and a verified successor edge in a toy population with delayed reconnection.

**PNX7. Let a population learn new members and retire old teaching dependencies without losing valid distinctions.** Parts: PL5-01 competing decoder predictions, PL5-02 signed probes, PL5-03 bundled probes, PL5-04 old-choice probe, PL5-07 task-level displacement, PL4-06 boot decoder, PL10 discriminating question, T5-01 matched evidence, PL5-05 post-write probes, PL5-06 supported partial code, PL5-08 one missing feature, PL5-09 reciprocal teaching, PL5-10 hold out an effect family, PL2-14 future plasticity, D4-24 native lesson response, PL5-11 retained-event clock, PL5-12 affine moment transport, PL5-13 tail ranks, PL5-14 recalibrated deferral, PL5-17 missing-source training, PL4-17 detachable moments, CR29 versioned relearning, PL5-15 cache descendants, PL5-16 retrieval geometry, PL5-18 source-selected observations, PL5-19 allowed-only summary, PL5-20 clean paired updates, PL4-27 selector dependency, PL3-28 abstaining replacement, PL2-17 unresolved attribution, PL5-21 translation-stable vocabulary, PL5-22 newcomer-induced split, PL5-24 complementary policies, PL5-26 teaching neighborhoods, PL4-07 transport semantic basis, PL02 retain disagreement, PL27 divergent seed, LM26 checked capability handshake, PL5-23 paired sufficient evidence, PL5-25 independent successor teacher, PL30 last executable teaching path, PL2-28 retirement-stable audit, PL11 evidence lineage. *Mechanism:* Use recipient-specific scratch probes to establish what codes mean and let new forks correct those meanings. Track dependencies in inference state and joint teaching routes, and verify a replacement from independent current evidence before retiring an old contributor. Keep local choices and unresolved overlaps explicit. *New:* Population membership, code meaning and source retirement can be checked over time instead of inferred from either current agreement or a one-time clean checkpoint. *First test:* Use harmless synthetic populations with newcomers, correlated source arrivals, jointly supported statistics and delayed reconnections. Compare a fixed global translator with checked neighborhoods and compare entry deletion with independent successor routes at matched work.
