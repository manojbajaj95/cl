Raw proposals for finite synthetic software learners; match allowed observations, old-service floors, and full resource budgets.

**DFX1. Do not spend delayed credit until the causal explanation changes the legal write.** Parts: DF01 paired causal worlds, DF03 answer versus survey effect, DF04 disputed source credit, DF12 event-time precedence, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing, CE4-24 bounded corruption. *Mechanism:* Run a consented matched-channel comparison in a finite synthetic stream only when plausible causal models support different legal changes. Apply any common correction using event-time records; keep disputed credit pending and rebase it against the actual intervening behavior when the determining evidence arrives. *New:* A learner could continue useful shared training without mistaking faster feedback or a friendlier survey for an improvement to the answer. *First test:* Simulate delayed feedback and two matched worlds with identical pooled ratings but different utility. Compare arrival-order updates, a fixed causal sidecar, and partial commits with event-time rebasing.

**DFX2. Carry a decision across feedback channels only where its comparison survives.** Parts: DF02 menu conditions, DF05 protocol translation, DF08 transport boundary, DF25 conditional guarantee, CE2-13 transferable proof obligation, CE4-14 retain control arm. *Mechanism:* Store what alternatives and controls are needed to transfer an allowable decision set, rather than a conversion factor for rating scales. After changing the channel or menu, reuse an old guarantee only if its defining comparison and near-boundary negative control still work. *New:* Calibration could transfer a permission to update without silently widening its scope or copying an old score. *First test:* Randomize two channels over shared synthetic cases, remove one alternative in selected menus, and compare score normalization, pooled training and a contrast-based translation on old intersections.

**DFX3. Keep a reachable path for a quiet user to correct a shared policy.** Parts: DF06 nonresponse bounds, DF10 contradiction reachability, DF15 missing versus rejected correction, DF16 migration bridge, DF20 right to reopen, CE4-12 reversible deadline, L4-18 lazy repair. *Mechanism:* Track which link prevents correction rather than treating silence as rejection or consent. Before migration, keep a specific consented bridge where the missing comparison can change a lawful decision by its deadline; hold tentative scope separately from a returning cohort's right to reopen. *New:* An edit would preserve a concrete opportunity to learn from quiet users, not a stale estimate of what they would say. *First test:* Simulate separate delivery, access and response failures over a toy migration. Compare completion-weighted updates, charging extra invitations uniformly, and a bridge chosen by whether it can change the next decision.

**DFX4. Audit whether the act of measuring changed the preference.** Parts: DF09 induced preference versus drift, DF13 measurement footprint, DF14 washout, DF30 fixed pre-edit distribution, L2-08 matched exposure, CE4-23 exposure denominators. *Mechanism:* Separate feedback collected under recent policy exposure from independent reference outcomes. Maintain bounded washout assumptions and a frozen pre-edit evaluation target; if return to the reference exposure is unavailable, report nonidentification rather than letting learner rollback stand in for washout. *New:* Reverting software and restoring a comparison population become distinct tests, so apparent retention cannot come solely from changed participation. *First test:* Generate matched pooled scores from true drift, changed traffic, and query carryover. Compare a snapshot-only reference, reweighting with no washout, and a consented exposure-controlled reference in a finite simulator.

**DFX5. Learn disagreement about authority without treating it as another vote.** Parts: DF07 output versus persistent update, DF19 joint decision boundary, DF22 trial versus default, DF23 reliability versus authority, DF24 separate rule revision, CE28 evidence versus decision, L3-18 one direction with two roles. *Mechanism:* Keep private output correction, trial consent, durable policy authority, and challenges to the decision rule as separate effects. Learn which evidence can inform each, while requiring that any shared-artifact change meet its active authorization rule before deployment. *New:* The learner can respond to a complaint immediately without redefining who may change the persistent policy or counting a trial as consent to a default. *First test:* In a toy workflow show the same outcomes under two disclosed rules and introduce a reliable observer with no governing authority. Compare scalar preference learning and an explicit equal-cost rule/evidence ledger.

**DFX6. Compose concessions only across independently supported choices.** Parts: DF11 evidence interference, DF18 cycle co-presence, DF21 conditional trade, DF29 rule precedence, IP19 multi-writer collision, SD2-05 third edit. *Mechanism:* Record each concession with its preserved right and the population in which it was tested. Admit a joint update only if the conditional witnesses can hold under the same exposure and the combined edits preserve precedence on old intersections. Check a third ordinary write that might break both. *New:* Current agreement on separate edits would not substitute for evidence that the edits can coexist under the same participants and follow-up opportunities. *First test:* Create a three-scope finite choice graph with an apparent cycle from disjoint participation and two trades relying on the same concession. Compare a pooled social-choice baseline and exact joint feasibility.

**DFX7. Find the missing old probe rather than repeat the probe that always passes.** Parts: DF17 minority counterexample, DF26 shared untested exposure, DF28 blind spot in retention proof, DF31 withdrawn corroboration, L4-21 hit versus no-hit contrast, CR29 versioned witness. *Mechanism:* Maintain a small pair of candidate updates that agree on current probes and differ on a rare protected case. Recheck only a fresh separating probe after a new correction or a withdrawal invalidates supporting exposure. Accumulate unresolved joint effects rather than resetting the budget when another update passes the same suite. *New:* Repeated non-diagnostic passes would no longer retire uncertainty about the same old behavior. *First test:* Use a finite model where two changes pass all original probes but jointly alter a rare output, and some supporting outcomes are descendants of withdrawn exposure. Compare full replay, a fixed probe budget, and adaptive counterexamples.

**DFX8. Teach a successor to reopen an unanswered comparison without retaining an answer.** Parts: DF27 recovery query, DF32 preserve incomparability, L4-02 teaching versus answer bits, CE4-20 reusable outcome-to-constraint map, LE5-22 independent reopening routes. *Mechanism:* Store only a distinguishing exercise and a noncommitment constraint for the unresolved region. On recurring work, a second independent actor must be able to realize the comparison from ordinary permitted observations. Use the result to authorize a scoped edit without reconstructing an old answer or resolving a different unresolved pair. *New:* A learner can retain which unanswered question matters and regain future learnability without treating incomparability as a stale preference. *First test:* Construct two toy policies that tie on old observed behavior and differ on a recurring contrast. Compare exact replay, a cached policy, and two substitutable exercises under matched queries; test that no shared update prematurely resolves the contrast.

**DFX9. A deployment learner that keeps the option to hear a correction.** Parts: DF01 paired causal worlds, DF03 answer versus survey effect, DF04 disputed source credit, DF12 event-time precedence, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing, CE4-24 bounded corruption, DF02 menu conditions, DF05 protocol translation, DF08 transport boundary, DF25 conditional guarantee, CE2-13 transferable proof obligation, CE4-14 retain control arm, DF06 nonresponse bounds, DF10 contradiction reachability, DF15 missing versus rejected correction, DF16 migration bridge, DF20 right to reopen, CE4-12 reversible deadline, L4-18 lazy repair, DF09 induced preference versus drift, DF13 measurement footprint, DF14 washout, DF30 fixed pre-edit distribution, L2-08 matched exposure, CE4-23 exposure denominators, DF07 output versus persistent update, DF19 joint decision boundary, DF22 trial versus default, DF23 reliability versus authority, DF24 separate rule revision, CE28 evidence versus decision, L3-18 one direction with two roles, DF11 evidence interference, DF18 cycle co-presence, DF21 conditional trade, DF29 rule precedence, IP19 multi-writer collision, SD2-05 third edit, DF17 minority counterexample, DF26 shared untested exposure, DF28 blind spot in retention proof, DF31 withdrawn corroboration, L4-21 hit versus no-hit contrast, CR29 versioned witness, DF27 recovery query, DF32 preserve incomparability, L4-02 teaching versus answer bits, CE4-20 reusable outcome-to-constraint map, LE5-22 independent reopening routes. *Mechanism:* Carry distinct records for behavior, exposure, evidence and authorization. Commit common improvements only when they preserve a lawful route to learn unresolved corrections. Rebase delayed feedback, protect independent witnesses and old precedence, and explicitly report when no reference comparison is available. *New:* Continual improvement would be measured partly by which future disagreements remain observable and correctable rather than by current aggregate ratings alone. *First test:* Enumerate benign synthetic deployments with delayed observations, changed menus, silence, withdrawal, migration and repeated shared edits. Compare matched stacks of replay, fixed probes, inverse-exposure weighting, static authority and fresh adaptation; report worst-scope retention, authorized corrections and deadlines.
