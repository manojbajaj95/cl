# Cross-pool merges, round 36

Focal sources: `tree/make-before-break.md` (PR #157, MB#) and `tree/precondition-writes.md` (PR #158, PW#). Crosses with forbidden combinations (PR #154, FC#), disjunctive protection (PR #155, DP#), mergeable writes (PR #156, MW#), finite safe regions (PR #152, FS#), slack erosion (PR #153, SL#), unregistered dependence (PR #150, UD#), X1/Y1 override budgets, and earlier rounds 1–35. These are raw proposals. Continuous service includes requests that span a cutover, and a route that needs another training step is not a live fallback. A supplier is one way to establish a guard, not necessarily the only one. Check guards at publication against the deployed transition and current obligations. Charge peak overlap, unused reservations, proof work, deadlines and whether reclaimed capacity actually helps later tasks.

**MPC1. Turn a retirement receipt into an entry ticket for a later writer.** Parts: MB1 capacity receipts, MB16 interference-stable release, PW03 combined guard suppliers, PW16 negotiated entry, MW01 useful alternatives, DP2 make-before-break, UD21 shared cuts. *Mechanism:* After cutover, issue a receipt naming which resource became free, which consumers still read it and which writer effects the replacement can tolerate. An unrelated learner can combine several receipts to establish its entry condition, but must check shared consumers at the live start rather than add freed ranks or bytes blindly. *New:* Reclaimed capacity can become a composable write prerequisite instead of a local success metric. *First test:* Simulate three writers needing different overlaps of two retired routes. Compare per-writer refitting, independent receipts and joint receipt checking on accepted B work, false admissions and peak state.

**MPC2. A drain acknowledgement should pay for one commit, not license a second cutover.** Parts: MB4 drain a computation, MB12 scratch-slot vacancy, PW05 spendable guards, PW12 check through publication, FC20 common-mode backups, MW09 selective coordination, FS07 concurrent writes. *Mechanism:* Treat draining as a capability scoped to the retired route version and state. Commit only against a serialized snapshot that confirms no live or in-flight requests remain and that the next writer is still eligible. Split the resource among multiple writers only after proving their combined use cannot destroy a shared fallback. *New:* On a busy learner, permission to overwrite can be consumed even when the numerical predicate that once justified it still looks true. *First test:* Replay held requests and concurrent handovers through a small stateful transducer. Compare a once-checked flag, per-version reservations and full serialization, measuring missed deadlines and scratch reuse.

**MPC3. Replace a backup before the dispatcher forgets how to reach it.** Parts: MB7 detector–route–deadline witness, MB10 dispatch forgetting, PW04 event expiry, PW14 smaller guards, DP9 route identity, SL9 rare exceptions, UD7 negative lookups. *Mechanism:* Bind a fallback's admission guard to the dispatcher's actual behavior on rare inputs, including changes to the priority of previously excluded routes. Refresh after a declared routing threat or independently observed increase in latency, even if weights and common outputs are unchanged. Let a different verified fallback fulfill the same guard without rebuilding the original. *New:* The first thing lost can be the right to claim a route is timely, not the route's ability in isolation. *First test:* Vary dispatcher competition and load at a fixed checkpoint, then run ordinary B updates. Compare timestamp expiry, weight-change alarms and dispatch-scoped expiry on rare requests and unrelated C.

**MPC4. Teach a replacement under the guards that its eventual neighbors will need.** Parts: MB3 build footprint, MB15 future editability, MB20 unknown future requests, PW06 make room for a future write, PW09 usefulness checks, FS15 future options, SL4 rotating weakness, DWM3 useful substitutes. *Mechanism:* During overlap, train candidate routes against the same old-service floors but choose among them using a past-only bank of families of guards that later writers might require. Favor a replacement whose safe region contains useful starts without using the eventual writer's target. Evaluate admissions and utility separately: permissive guards do not excuse a useless write. *New:* The reason to build a replacement now can be the independently invented write it will make possible later, not extra capacity immediately after cutover. *First test:* Build routes under equal bytes and steps, then reveal a held-out writer family. Compare accuracy-first, static margin and guard-aware selection at matched post-cutover service.

**MPC5. Allow two handovers to run only when their temporary overlap is jointly safe.** Parts: MB11 peak-byte frontier, MB13 slice-by-slice handoff, MB17 conflicting handovers, PW02 sufficient versus necessary order, PW03 jointly supplied guards, FC2 cardinality budgets, MW19 higher-order conflicts, FSR5 ordered allowances. *Mechanism:* Put overlap peaks, live backup sets and drain barriers into a joint trace contract. Check which slices can change order, where two replacement builds compete for the same scratch capacity, and whether a third build removes all surviving routes. Publish an allowed order or a partial schedule plus an admission check, not an inferred universal ordering. *New:* Two individually safe migrations can be incompatible during construction, even if their final writes commute. *First test:* Enumerate staggered build, switch and drain phases for three rule learners. Compare endpoint merges, fixed serialization and verified slice scheduling at equal peak bytes and worst-prefix service.

**MPC6. Buy the missing fact that makes a handover legal rather than rebuilding it.** Parts: MB2 independently live, MB18 readiness expiry, PW07 long and short checks, PW15 acquire a guard fact, PW20 bits for admission, UD12 independent audits, SL6 fact–procedure crossover, LWA4 missing-edge probes. *Mechanism:* If a cheap guard test is inconclusive, choose a scoped question about a missing dependency, state compatibility or current premise before buying retraining. Test the replacement with the old route disabled on a shadow so compatibility cannot be supplied accidentally by what will be retired. Charge the answer, probe address, and check; use the broad verifier or refuse when the fact cannot settle the guard. *New:* One piece of current evidence can revive the right to reuse a completed replacement without acting as a replay target for the replacement itself. *First test:* Hide one compatibility bit across otherwise matched checkpoints. Compare recompute-all, refit-first and selective queries on false admits, peak overlap and cost.

**MPC7. Rebase a retiring route against every obligation acquired while it was away.** Parts: MB5 honest abort, MB19 held-out retirement challenge, PW08 intervening obligations, PW10 task revisions, PW13 optimizer rebase, MW10 stale writes, UD20 authorized changes, USR6 dual budgets. *Mechanism:* A returning retirement plan first identifies current versions and obligations added since validation. Reuse certificates only where their writer and consumer premises still hold; independently check new requests and pending optimizer effects before freeing the old resources. If the plan aborts, leave later acquisitions intact instead of rolling the entire learner backwards. *New:* The correct abort target can be today's live state with the retirement withheld, not yesterday's state restored. *First test:* Introduce a rare exemption and an unrelated skill during a delayed build. Compare checkpoint rollback, full refit and scoped rebase, including whether the next public update revives a retired default.

**MPC8. Let a recovered route earn admission as a live guard supplier.** Parts: MB6 recovery to fixed-weight service, MB8 reroute versus relearn, PW01 weakest usable entry, PW18 common entry rules, DP11 live versus recovery, DP15 second-route replay, FS14 fallback exchange. *Mechanism:* Keep the old route serving while a bounded background wake builds the replacement. Require independently checked fixed-weight dispatch within deadline before using it to satisfy any guard that promises continuous service. Reuse that entry proof for several later writers only where their distinct task floors and threats are also checked. *New:* A recovery witness can prepare a reusable resource without being miscounted as a live route before the preparation finishes. *First test:* Cross retraining-only, slow reroute and qualifying fixed-weight backups in matched learners. Compare replay, retaining both routes and guarded handoff on actual later uptake of the released capacity.

**MPC9. Transport a handover receipt only if the new coordinate system preserves its footprint and its customers.** Parts: MB14 exact morphism, MB16 later interference, PW17 coordinate transport, PW22 deployed semantics, MW13 factorization, FS10 writer transport, UD1 producer–consumer pairs, DWM5 proof transport. *Mechanism:* For a new adapter or basis, prove that proposed writer inputs map into the certified domain and that the receiver still consumes the replacement with the same timing and state assumptions. Reuse verified subsets, but recheck mixed-coordinate interference and new dispatch paths before publishing. *New:* A migration can preserve the proof that the replacement serves while invalidating the separate proof that the vacated coordinates can be overwritten. *First test:* Hold outputs fixed across exact permutations, bounded rotations and changed consumers. Compare equal-span transport, full re-verification and scoped receipt reuse on independently generated follow-on writes.

**MPC10. Return a budget conflict with the option that would make it go away.** Parts: MB21 online value versus plumbing, MB22 infeasible handovers, PW11 adaptive scripts, PW21 proof break-even, FC7 learned conflicts, DP18 safe timeout fallback, FS17 conflict cores, X1/Y1 override budgets. *Mechanism:* When a release is rejected, separate a proven conflict in a finite menu from unverified feasibility at the search cap. Identify a scoped remedy—a longer overlap, an alternate route, a narrower writer or a legitimately changed service policy—while keeping acquisition-anchored protection for all unchanged tasks. On timeout, execute only a checked fallback or no write. *New:* A rejected handover can tell the planner where to spend capacity instead of treating every failure as a reason to freeze the incumbent. *First test:* Enumerate feasible and impossible migrations under tight scratch and deadline caps, then run bounded search on a learned host. Compare blind retries, fixed freeze and conflict-directed expansion at equal search compute.

**MPC11. A continual learner that makes room for new writes without interrupting old work.** Parts: MB1 retirement receipts, MB2 independent service, MB3 build footprint, MB4 in-flight drain, MB5 abort, MB6 live promotion, MB7 detector deadlines, MB9 latency drift, MB10 dispatcher retention, MB11 peak bytes, MB12 scratch reuse, MB13 slice handover, MB14 exact morphism, MB15 future editability, MB16 later interference, MB17 conflicting handovers, MB18 expiring readiness, MB19 independent challenges, MB20 unknown requests, MB22 budget conflict, PW01 entry envelopes, PW02 alternate schedules, PW03 joint suppliers, PW04 event expiry, PW05 guarded debits, PW06 readiness training, PW07 tiered checks, PW08 intervening obligations, PW09 accepted utility, PW10 task versions, PW11 adaptive scripts, PW12 atomic admission, PW13 optimizer rebase, PW14 smaller guards, PW15 missing facts, PW16 negotiated entry, PW17 coordinate transport, PW18 shared entries, PW19 order effects, PW20 admission bits, PW21 reuse break-even, PW22 deployed semantics, FC#, DP#, MW#, FS#, SL#, UD#, X1, Y1, DWM1, FSR4, USR6. *Mechanism:* Build and validate an independent replacement while the old route serves. Switch new traffic, drain in-flight old work, and issue a scoped retirement receipt that another writer can use as a guard supplier. Merge multiple suppliers only with checked overlap, order and threat constraints. At live publication rebase against new acquisitions, expiry events and deployed state, and return abstention or an explicit scoped conflict if a guard cannot be established. *New:* The learner can pass forward certified opportunities to learn, not just retained answers or a checkpoint that happened to work once. *First test:* Use an enumerable stateful route world with delayed writes, rare exceptions, changing load, a restart and a late learner that consumes the receipt. Compare matched blue-green deployment with incremental checks, verified merge scheduling, fixed redundant routes and replay. Report worst-prefix service, deadline misses, peak overlap, verified reuse, net B gains and full verification cost separately.
