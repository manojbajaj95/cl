**CET1. Remember which comparison failed, not which verdict to repeat.** Parts: CE01 new predicate, CE02 comparison rewrite, CE03 typed citation edge, CE06 split holding, P01 path-dependent targets, IP20 optimizer change. *Mechanism:* Learn a typed rewrite on a case-comparison graph. A bounded turning-point stack selects candidate reasons, not answers. Revoking one use of a reason must leave unrelated verdicts intact and create two independently editable branches where a split is warranted. *New:* History dependence could be stored as the ability to revise a reason in the right context rather than as multiple stored policy outputs. *First test:* Make the same toy verdict arise on two paths; revoke one inference at a later reversal. Compare a history-aware outcome table, replay, and a factored rule graph with and without learned rewrites.

**CET2. Keep a dissent inactive but ready for the next decisive observation.** Parts: CE04 activation test, CE05 referral through disagreement, CE18 transmitted contrast, CE30 typed delegation, P04 reversal curves, CR24 impossibility witness. *Mechanism:* Carry a rival explanation with its smallest separating experiment. A disagreement maps to a specific query for a specialist; its reply resolves only that subquestion, leaving the local decision rule intact. If no timely experiment separates the explanations, keep both and use the backed fallback. *New:* A successor could inherit a reusable way to resolve ambiguity even if no earlier learner ever saw the resolving event. *First test:* Give two toy derivations identical verdicts until a rare stream reversal. Compare storing both whole specialists, confidence routing, and carrying just the contrast and query translator.

**CET3. Translate a pending edit without moving its authorization deadline.** Parts: CE07 amendment translation, CE08 prior validator, CE09 live commitment, CE10 role-scoped meaning, ER4-21 joint cutover, FW07 live transition. *Mechanism:* Jointly learn a replacement interpretation and a partial translator for pending toy proposals. Judge activation with the currently active validator; after activation check each translated proposal under the new rules without restarting its owed work or deadline. Keep unresolved proposals on a supported old path. *New:* The migrated unit could be a particular unfulfilled obligation and its proof rather than an entire frozen interpreter. *First test:* Change a shared term used by two roles while a pending example awaits evaluation. Compare version pinning, global replacement, and partial translation with staged activation.

**CET4. Learn a conflict operator that survives withdrawal of one constituent.** Parts: CE11 incompatibility operator, CE12 severable re-derivation, P05 vector drive, P06 cooperative coupling, SD2-05 third-edit blocker. *Mechanism:* Use offline order swaps to propose an operator over a class of incompatible edits. Ratify the operator first on allowed paths. Withdraw one constituent and require the remaining decision to be derived from still-valid witnesses rather than copied from its old output. *New:* An edit conflict could teach a reusable composition rule whose consequences remain independently revisable. *First test:* Use two individually useful toy updates and a third that breaks their composition. Compare replay in one chosen order, static priorities, and transferable conflict operators on withheld uses and withdrawals.

**CET5. Borrow grammar that exposes future correction handles.** Parts: CE13 new construction, CE14 recurring repair, CE15 borrowed distinction, CE16 scoped override, CE17 reanalysis, P13 loop transfer, CR22 substitute test. *Mechanism:* Promote a repeated repair into a construction with explicit argument and scope slots. Hold old expressions fixed in meaning; teach a new mentor to fill one slot on ordinary examples. Retain the new construction only if it yields a legal different correction under unseen nesting. *New:* The transferable part of a grammar could be a mechanism for future changes rather than a vocabulary of compressed old solutions. *First test:* Build two toy instruction grammars whose examples agree on shallow forms. Compare macros, pooled grammar induction, and slot-bearing constructions on deep examples and second-teacher corrections.

**CET6. Make a handoff condition reusable without copying the mentor's route.** Parts: CE19 perturbation and repair, CE20 interchangeable checkpoint, CE22 fixed chorus and variable solo, CE24 control return, P08 transition-cycle memory, DS05 repairable landing, LE5-22 independent repair routes. *Mechanism:* Show two perturbed finite executions implementing the same shared constraint by different routes. Induce a state predicate at which the apprentice can resume under its own policy, and check recovery on a route induced by neither mentor. *New:* A small checkpoint predicate could transmit the right to resume useful work instead of a checkpoint snapshot or demonstration suffix. *First test:* Interrupt two toy workflows at the same visible milestone but with different pending work. Compare cloning mentor continuations, full snapshots, and predicates trained with substitutions.

**CET7. Teach the successor to replace a check and teach the next learner why.** Parts: CE21 reconstruction exercise, CE23 conditional check substitution, CE25 replaceable goal slot, CE26 teaching a successor, T05 data-free cycling, CR29 fresh version binding. *Mechanism:* Hand down an exercise and a declared goal slot rather than an answer. Learners may rehearse candidate witness substitutions offline, but must demonstrate both branches of the check and verify the substitute against fresh, version-bound observations before passing it down. *New:* A lesson could transmit both the freedom to choose a new goal and the ability to catch a later student's shortcut. *First test:* Run three generations of toy programs with evolving goals and a spurious witness. Compare stored solutions, self-generated rehearsal, and exercises requiring an independently checked counterexample.

**CET8. Preserve unfinished duties across role changes without copying prior policy.** Parts: CE27 role-valid substitute, CE28 informational versus binding effects, CE29 office handoff, CE31 standing to challenge, CE32 reciprocal uptake, P11 lesson activation, BM4-05 versioned witness. *Mechanism:* Separate observed recommendations from commitments that recognized roles can create. Infer the circumstances in which counterparties can call an unfinished obligation or challenge a substitution. Transfer its invocation and fallback to an independent successor; an activation score can surface the obligation but cannot ratify it. *New:* A learner could retain why a duty is owed and who can call it while rewriting how the duty is satisfied. *First test:* In a synthetic task queue replace one role, repeat an advisory event, and then issue a rare valid challenge. Compare frequency gates, copied policies, and a versioned role ledger with independent implementations.

**CET9. Ask, then bind only the part the answer identifies.** Parts: LL01 clarification policy, LL02 deferred coordinate, LL05 discharge event, LL06 relational scope, LL07 transformation commutation, LL08 partial identification, CE04 activation test, CE18 transmitted contrast, P04 reversal curves. *Mechanism:* Write a shared adapter for the identified behavior, and emit a decision-linked question only when an unresolved contrast becomes relevant. Bind the answer into a local slot; discharge the query on later requests by checking a live witness, not by remembering a fixed timeout. *New:* The writer could carry a recoverable distinction across installations without encoding an unsupported answer or keeping the teaching file live. *First test:* Use a permuted toy record stream with paired plausible rules. Compare updated-description T2L, updated-prompt DnD, and explicit ask/bind dispatch; count queries and old-call regressions.

**CET10. Translate a narrow amendment across two live callers.** Parts: LL03 exception-first write, LL04 live subcall, LL09 counterfactual edit port, LL10 witness substitution, LL11 fallback rotation, LL12 commitment receipt, LL13 confidence-relative correction, LL14 write authority, LL15 independent support, LL16 amendment rule, CE03 typed citation edge, CE07 amendment translation, CE09 live commitment, CE28 informational versus binding effects, FW07 live transition. *Mechanism:* Make generated factors carry a small revision port and typed dependency on a live caller. A scoped amendment can replace a premise or witness without voting twice on independent evidence or writing through a caller that lacks correction authority. Require an executable fallback while a new adapter is validated. *New:* Authoring a skill could install a testable change interface rather than just an initial behavior. *First test:* In a finite toy workflow, replace an equal-valued witness and introduce a late amendment from each caller. Compare complete regeneration, an equal-byte symbolic sidecar, and the port; probe withheld exceptions before and after commitment.

**CET11. Require both a split witness and a usable dispatch cue.** Parts: LL17 matched-budget birth certificate, LL19 pre-action separator, LL20 phase-local split, LL22 split amendment coordinates, CE05 referral through disagreement, CE20 interchangeable checkpoint, R03 nonadditive control, R06 learned latch control, DS05 repairable landing. *Mechanism:* Enumerate the legal common writes for one quantized toy skill before allocating children. Buy a separator only when it can select a child before the relevant act or amendment. Fork just the conflicting phase or revision port and check reconvergence through both children. *New:* This separates a real lack of shared capacity from a routing information deficit or simple training noise. *First test:* Compare regenerated T2L/DnD with the same active query/router, a stronger single adapter, and matched grow/freeze and gradient surgery. Include shareable, separable and unobservable conflicts; serve old calls only through the resulting children.

**CET12. Merge away the children while retaining a way to grow them again.** Parts: LL18 inherited promise, LL21 one-factor inheritance, LL23 merge then resplit, LL24 transfer split structure, CE21 reconstruction exercise, CE26 teaching a successor, R09 amplitude-ordered forgetting, D11 recycle capacity, P13 loop transfer. *Mechanism:* During a compatible interval, replace two adapters with a shared update and a compact separating witness. Keep neither child as a recovery shortcut. On a later incompatible request, test whether the witness lets the writer reuse the old split structure while deriving new child factors from fresh evidence. *New:* A lifelong writer could recycle deployment capacity without losing the ability to specialize again, even when old child weights would be wrong. *First test:* Run incompatible, compatible, and differently incompatible toy phases. Compare retained experts, fresh growth, and regeneration with identical witness and prompt budgets; charge the always-live shared promise and score later correction on both branches.

**CET13. Update the writer from contrasts that matter after its next write.** Parts: LL25 refused-write contrast, LL26 cheap later correction, LL27 equal-now edit contrasts, LL28 causal timing, LL29 negative permissions, LL30 independently authorizing sources, LL31 omitted opportunity, LL32 missing evidence kind, CE23 conditional check substitution, IP20 optimizer change, R10 online identification. *Mechanism:* Train the writer on pairs matched for present behavior but differing in how a later authorized edit, missing observation, or absent opportunity should affect the factors. Carry useful and rejected effects separately; abstain when no lawful observation distinguishes them. *New:* The writer would learn how to preserve available corrections as it accumulates skills, not simply how to reconstruct better initial adapters. *First test:* Train tiny T2L and DnD analogues on matched targets, then stream refusals and changes in corrective evidence. Compare ordinary sequential fine-tuning and replay with contrast training; check retention of older served and editable skills.

**CET14. Pay for history state only when it preserves a usable learning distinction.** Parts: R01 depth benchmark, R02 additive readout, R04 off-grid interpolation, R05 noisy extrema, R07 in-weight state, R08 context stack, R11 path-dependent streams, R12 register from path, R13 worst-case bound, R14 readout-only bank, P10 inferred drive, CE02 comparison rewrite, LL06 relational scope. *Mechanism:* Factor a small path-dependent rule into one shared trunk and a persistent readout candidate. Keep a turning point only if it changes a sanctioned downstream comparison or later correction. Test both additive and interacting path targets; charge the estimator state and side-controller state alike. *New:* A bank can be falsified as a learning interface even when it is an efficient predictor of familiar returns. *First test:* Match bytes, visibility, and training work across an exact stack, learned latches, context and readout banks on finite software streams, including off-grid and noisy histories. Test same-input outputs and next correction after return.

**CET15. Erase a bounded support without silently erasing a lawful substitute.** Parts: D01 band erasure, D02 forget-set-free reset, D03 default bias, D04 coercivity class, D05 decrement law, D06 structural certificate, D07 boundary refinement, D08 source trust, D09 continuous-index shards, D10 vector field, D12 thermal band, LL15 independent support, CE12 severability, CR29 fresh version binding. *Mechanism:* Apply each candidate reset to an explicitly modeled bank, checking threshold states and keeping shared support where an independent source still justifies it. Treat a deterministic relay certificate as evidence only about that bank, not about the trunk, provenance ledger, or whole learner. Make any vector or thermal reset meet the same finite damage bound before using it. *New:* Structural erasure and cultural severability could work together: verify what was reset, then separately verify what still has a basis to survive. *First test:* Use overlapping synthetic regimes and obsolete evidence. Compare exact counterfactual retrain, tagged shards and an explicit support graph against each bounded reset; inspect bytes, off-band changes and fresh lawful correction.

## Prior-art review: PR #344 — A LoRA-Writing Learner

**The base mechanism is already published.** [Text-to-LoRA: Instant Transformer Adaption](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06105) (T2L, **arXiv:2506.06105**, §§3–5) conditions a hypernetwork on a natural-language task description plus module and layer embeddings to generate LoRA factors in one forward pass. It trains via adapter reconstruction or end-to-end supervised fine-tuning; §4.2 tests the SFT writer on unseen tasks against a jointly trained multi-task adapter. [Drag-and-Drop LLMs: Zero-Shot Prompt-to-Weights](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16406) (DnD, **arXiv:2506.16406**, §§2.2–2.6, 3) instead embeds batches of task prompts with a pretrained encoder and uses a cascaded convolutional decoder trained to reconstruct tokenized LoRA checkpoints. It uses unlabeled prompts at inference and evaluates held-out datasets, including cross-domain transfer; this is not learning online from labeled outcomes. Neither paper's reported experiments establish successive in-place revisions with old-skill retention or endogenous splitting of one live skill. Generating two adapters from two supplied conditions is not discovering that a split is warranted, nor does it supply a legal routing cue.

**Controls used for every row.** Use small analogues of description-conditioned T2L and prompt-batch-conditioned DnD on the same frozen executor, with matched initial quality and inputs rendered in each format. Give regeneration the revised description/prompts containing the same actually observed evidence, not hidden labels. Match rank, total persistent bytes, update compute and allowable observations; charge routers, old factors and questions. Add the row-specific control named after “also.” Every row tests unchanged old cases after the operation, not just the target or the initial adapter; split rows test through the resulting children and use an oracle branch only as an upper bound. Verdicts mean “not demonstrated by these two papers,” not “new to the literature.”
