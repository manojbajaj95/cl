# Cross-pool merges, round 40

Focal sources: `tree/self-closing-witnesses.md` (PR #165, SW#) and `tree/rejected-edit-witnesses.md` (PR #166, RE#). Crosses with witness examples (PR #163, WE#), finite edit guards (PR #164, FG#), training readers (PR #161, TR#), unannounced consumers (PR #162, UC#), recovery witnesses (PR #139, RW#), immune memory (PR #129, IM#), and earlier rounds 1–39. These are raw proposals. Freeze the witness and independently check the region it induces, including optimizer and sampler state. Refused writes may supply shadow evidence, never live permission. Keep the original payloads, acquisition floors and blind demand fixed; distinguish a demonstrated failure, a scoped impossibility and an unanswered check. Count teacher overlap, retained evidence and reaccreditation after a refit.

**SRW1. Use a rejected edit to find the first irreversible branch split.** Parts: SW1 replacement gate crossing, SW4 paired relations, RE02 off-policy gate pairs, RE05 causal prefixes, WE3 delayed optimizer response, FG16 live versus shadow paths. *Mechanism:* Replay a legal but refused edit in shadow and search for the earliest prefix that separates source and substitute in a way that causes later old-task damage. Teach the correction there, then freeze the new buffer and prove closure over both trajectories. Still test the original payload, not just the shorter diagnosis. *New:* A tiny delayed optimizer discrepancy can be a better teaching target than a dramatic mismatch at the final gate. *First test:* Pair small Adam learners with equal initial deltas and different slot histories. Compare final-error replay, earliest numerical mismatch and earliest damage-causing prefix on full-state closure and useful admissions.

**SRW2. Treat a repaired refusal as a test that can spread, not a blacklist entry.** Parts: SW2 closure after refits, SW11 mixed-region composition, RE07 correction classes, RE10 distractor edits, RE17 displaced refusals, IM# scoped quarantine, UC08 held-out editor objectives. *Mechanism:* Group failures by a shared correction checked across allowed continuations, not by payload resemblance. After each refit, test for new refusals on jointly induced paths and quarantine only the correction or composition implicated by a separating continuation. Restore rights only after independent verification, even for a previously admitted edit family. *New:* A refusal memory could generalize across unrelated-looking edits and still localize interference when two individually working corrections interact. *First test:* Mix visually similar failures with opposite fixes and dissimilar failures with a common fix. Compare edit clustering, permanent rejection and correction-based repair on frozen transfer and lost prior admissions.

**SRW3. Buy a verifier-friendly corridor instead of another corrective example.** Parts: SW14 return corridors, SW15 examples for proof, RE14 checked shadow receipts, RE20 verifier-induced memory, WE8 separating data, FG17 dependency slices, WFG6 rejection diagnosis. *Mechanism:* If a complete shadow check succeeds but the cheap verifier refuses, try a truthful example that moves reachable states out of the verifier's ambiguous gate region without changing the intended correction. Reuse proof slices only while their premises hold, and count proof accessibility separately from a real change in safe admission. *New:* One training example can shrink the proof instead of improving the edit, and the two benefits can be measured independently. *First test:* Cross an exact and an interval verifier on the same payloads. Compare correction-targeted examples, proof-targeted examples and stronger verification for bytes, soundness and acceptance.

**SRW4. Retire a teacher with questions a later refusal can actually answer.** Parts: SW12 checkable source summaries, SW20 invariant tails, RE12 refusal-selected questions, RE15 post-retirement refusals, RE22 anonymous refusal bits, TR21 bounded unforeseen jobs, UC19 minimum information, TUC7 changed obligations. *Mechanism:* Before retirement, retain a scoped set of independently labeled separators and an invariant for longer admitted refreshes. For a new refusal, ask which separator would distinguish the missing correction. If the retained evidence cannot settle it, require lawful fresh evidence or abstain; never substitute the current buffer's prediction for the removed teacher's answer. Refits must earn a new closure proof. *New:* Teaching a learner to ask an answerable question can preserve some repair capacity without leaving the original state reserved forever. *First test:* Hide two teacher variants with equal visible outputs but distinct responses to late edits. Sweep the retained evidence budget and compare self-labeling, fixed summaries and targeted separator use.

**SRW5. Make room for two corrections whose safe order does not commute.** Parts: SW8 prefix drift budgets, SW11 mixed-region composition, RE09 nonlinear joint repair, RE18 stable serving route, FG07 signed products, FG13 residual guards, UC11 state-dependent effects, WFG4 private witnesses. *Mechanism:* Independently train two useful providers whose witness updates must interact to repair a refusal. Verify the joint reachability region and actual publication order, reserving tighter discrepancy budgets before expanding steps. Keep the old route serving while composing in shadow, and issue a residual allowance only where the first published update leaves one. *New:* One witness could teach a safe starting state for another instead of contributing an additive portion of the final correction. *First test:* Enumerate joint and serial orders for two substitutes crossing a shared ReLU gate; compare additive certificates, exact fixed-menu planning and closure-trained menus.

**SRW6. Charge a witness for the opportunities its own refit takes away.** Parts: SW13 expensive branch removal, SW16 overlapping liabilities, SW18 peak overlap, RE13 refusal rent, RE17 shifted frontiers, RE21 fixed demand, TR17 byte rent, UC20 borrowed room. *Mechanism:* Rank refits by the total verified utility they add and subtract on a fixed editor bank. Measure bytes saved after dropping combinations of branches, not by summing independent estimates, and reject improvements that require unaffordable deployment overlap. Recheck any old admission displaced by the change before evicting evidence. *New:* An example that rarely fixes a refusal can be the cheapest way to keep a whole shared closure proof small. *First test:* Plant competing and overlapping correction classes. Compare frequency eviction, per-example attribution and measured joint gains at the same byte-time and peak allocation.

**SRW7. Keep rare refusal repairs deterministic even when the ordinary refresh is stochastic.** Parts: SW6 stochastic contracts, SW19 seed versus coverage, RE02 off-policy pairs, RE19 refusal-bit controls, WE5 update noise, FG05 rare-slice floors, IM# executable challenges. *Mechanism:* Couple allowed sampling runs to bound bad-prefix probability for common requests while checking a declared rare branch for every allowed sampler path. Store a witness targeted at the rare correction and independently test whether its learned effect persists without a rejection marker. *New:* A witness could be indispensable to a deterministic exception contract even when random-rollout metrics cannot detect its absence. *First test:* Vary sampler clocks and insert a rare legal suffix excluded by matched-seed evaluation. Compare seed matching, uniform hard checks and split contracts under a fixed probe and witness budget.

**SRW8. Let a refused write specify the boundary that a recovery witness must cross.** Parts: SW7 fencing unsafe loops, SW20 invariant tails, SW21 independent source failure, RE01 scoped repair targets, RE18 shadow learning, RW# bounded recovery, FG14 accommodation strategies, WFG5 live repair. *Mechanism:* Keep a working route online while a recovery continuation runs in shadow. Derive a required exit region from the failed edit, test that recovery restores the current skill independently of teacher agreement, and prove the replacement stays in that region for the declared refresh horizon. Use the repaired path only after a live guard check; a successful wake alone grants no admission. *New:* Recovery can be aimed at a region from which a frozen write is admissible, not merely at the first point where old accuracy returns. *First test:* Create wakes that restore current scores but leave a hidden gate vulnerable. Compare score-triggered publication, verified recovery alone and guard-targeted recovery on the unchanged payload.

**SRW9. Retain a proof that teaching cannot help without teaching a blanket refusal.** Parts: SW3 damaging counterexamples, SW5 fixed witness grammar, RE04 ambiguous refusals, RE06 no lawful example, RE16 negative evidence, WE6 correction expressivity, FG22 scoped incompatibility, IM# minimal quarantine. *Mechanism:* Store separate records for a demonstrated correction, inexpressibility in the witness grammar, and impossibility under the full accommodation budget. Bind negative records to the region and version where the argument holds; when a new provider changes the premises, recheck instead of applying a learned rejection label. *New:* A learner could conserve examples by remembering why a proposed repair cannot work without making the corresponding payload permanently inadmissible. *First test:* Use finite hosts with identical refusal scores but different feasible remedies, then introduce a provider that changes only one impossibility premise. Measure false blocks and useful adaptation.

**SRW10. Transport a refusal's teaching effect across hosts, not its rejection verdict.** Parts: SW9 deployed precision, SW10 optimizer clocks, SW17 worst authorized surprise, RE03 minimal teaching witnesses, RE08 transfer across hosts, RE11 lawful current examples, UC03 coordinate invariants, TR22 portable training reads, WFG7 versioned teachers. *Mechanism:* Export the far-side correction as truthful examples or a checked synthetic effect with a scoped state relation. On a new host, first verify the relation covers actual arithmetic, optimizer clocks and effect semantics; refit only with lawful local data and redo closure if the transfer premise fails. Never infer admission from the original refusal or its repair. *New:* What transfers can be the reason to teach a distinction even when the new host has different gates and must prove a different set of accepted edits. *First test:* Cross exact coordinate transforms, rounded deployment and changed clocks. Compare copied buffers, retraining and checked effect transfer on frozen requests from a held-out editor.

**SRW11. A continual learner that learns from refusals without letting them write the rules.** Parts: SW1 replacement gate crossings, SW2 refit closure, SW3 damage search, SW4 paired relations, SW5 fixed grammar, SW6 stochastic coverage, SW7 unsafe loops, SW8 prefix budgets, SW9 execution precision, SW10 optimizer clocks, SW11 mixed regions, SW12 independent source summaries, SW13 branch costs, SW14 return corridors, SW15 proof examples, SW16 overlapping liabilities, SW17 worst surprises, SW18 overlap cap, SW19 seed controls, SW20 invariant tails, SW21 independent source failure, SW22 compiled control, RE01 repair targets, RE02 off-policy pairs, RE03 teaching minima, RE04 refusal diagnosis, RE05 causal prefixes, RE06 lawful examples, RE07 correction classes, RE08 host transfer, RE09 nonlinear repairs, RE10 distractors, RE11 truthful teaching, RE12 targeted questions, RE13 rent, RE14 shadow receipts, RE15 post-retirement requests, RE16 negative evidence, RE17 shifted frontiers, RE18 stable service, RE19 marker controls, RE20 verifier artifacts, RE21 fixed demand, RE22 information floor, WE#, FG#, TR#, UC#, RW#, IM#, WFG1, WFG6, TUC3, TUC7. *Mechanism:* Diagnose refused frozen writes in shadow, then derive lawful evidence for corrections or separate scoped negative certificates. Freeze any new witness and independently check the source, substitute and permitted edited trajectories it must cover; verify behavior even when source and witness agree. Before reclaiming a training bank, discharge and drain its readers. Admit a later write only against current effects, obligations and prefixes; let abstention or a checked fallback stand when the retained evidence is insufficient. *New:* A learner could accumulate independently reusable teaching material for unfamiliar updates while preventing its repair process from silently moving the admission boundary. *First test:* Use a finite stateful ReLU learner with a retiring teacher, rare gate crossings, coupled suppliers and delayed blind editors. Compare matched counterexample-guided repair plus condensation and incremental checks, fixed witnesses, and the full loop. Report verified utility on the fixed bank, old service and bounded recovery separately, retired bytes, displaced refusals, proof cost and peak overlap.
