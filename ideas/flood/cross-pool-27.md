# Cross-pool merges, round 27

Focal sources: `tree/forgetting-cycles.md` (PR #138, FC#) and `tree/recovery-witnesses.md` (PR #139, RW#). Crosses with Y12 spin-echo refocusing, Y1 hysteresis, Y7 Red Queen parasite, Z6 cohort clocks, practice order (PR #121, PO#), detection versus recovery (PR #101), label-free detection (PR #126, LF#), first point of loss (PR #134, FL#), past selves (PR #135, PA#), momentum fossils (PR #136, MF#), merger certificates (PR #110, MC#), and earlier rounds 1–26. These are raw proposals. Freeze the valid task panel and compare both directions under the same serving budget; a cycle need not mean the same items came back. Separate frozen cues from training continuations, audit-mode witness scoring from target-blind deployment selection, and no witness found from exact full-state loss. Charge probes, witness selection, stored state, and any time spent out of service.

**FCR1. Refocus a forgetting cycle at the phase where its losses line up.** Parts: FC3 item closure, FC4 curriculum reversal, RW7 burial depth, RW9 re-burial, Y12 spin echo, IR8 echo as a relearning test, PO4. *Mechanism:* Fix a menu of target-blind pulses and schedule each at several predeclared delays after a common curriculum block. In audit mode map which verified losses reappear on the same held-out items, and whether the timing of the best pulse shifts under the reverse order. Use the resulting phase map to schedule a pulse from upcoming public lessons, not old scores. *New:* The same refocusing operation might be a repair at one training phase and a source of regression at another. *First test:* Train a small transducer on three recurring rule families and compare scheduled pulses to sham, equal-norm random pulses and simple order reversal. Match training steps and test all three families after the next shared block.

**FCR2. Measure how much training it takes to close both halves of a hysteresis loop.** Parts: FC12 hint budget, FC15 repair commutation, RW9 re-burial, RW19 bounded burial, Y1 override budget, PO#, MF4 half-life maps. *Mechanism:* Sweep a public curriculum mixture up and down through the same levels; at each level, fork both paths through an identical future. Log separate thresholds for when service fails, when a legal frozen cue stops working and when no continuation in the budget wakes the skill. Use the width and direction of the measured gaps to choose where a scheduler changes course. *New:* One loop can have three different boundaries; a learner could schedule against the one that actually matters for service. *First test:* Use conflicting rule tasks with one untouched cohort. Match dwell time and cumulative counts, compare Adam with reset-slot and slower-sweep arms, and check whether apparent hysteresis vanishes when both branches get more settling time.

**FCR3. Train a Red Queen that tries to hide already-learned skills.** Parts: FC5 forecast a missing edge, FC18 irreplaceable probes, RW5 bounded obstructions, RW22 witness search amortization, Y7 Red Queen, Z3 opponent ledgers, PA13 verified regression cycles. *Mechanism:* On development replicas, frozen versions propose valid challenge families while a challenger picks legal public updates that make a previously live skill fail within a fixed update budget. A defender picks blind wake-up recipes. Save the rare challenger–defender pairs that improve prediction on fresh shifts, not the pairs that merely repeat a verifier loophole. *New:* Practice against futures that make installed repairs stop working, rather than harder examples of the old task alone. *First test:* Use a symbolic planner with independently enumerable solutions. Compare an equal-cost static curriculum and ordinary adversarial replay on held-out shift types; score new-task progress, reappearance of verified misses, and false wake-ups on a never-trained obligation.

**FCR4. Give each cohort a repair clock that can be handed to its successor.** Parts: FC3 item closure, FC19 gateway rehearsal, RW15 carrier relays, RW20 wake-up multiplexing, Z6 cohort clocks, DG#, MF7 age-resolved audit, MFH3 separate clocks. *Mechanism:* Before a cohort reaches a scheduled consolidation or reset, train a younger cohort on public structure and try to hand it the smallest complete set needed by a wake-up recipe. Confirm the handoff in forks with the donor intact, removed, or replaced by a same-age sham; check independently whether an old route can now retire. *New:* Capacity can age out while a bounded recovery obligation survives, without making old weights or slots permanent. *First test:* Stagger module allocation in a three-rule learner; compare fixed-age freezing, global slot reset and relay migration at equal total state, with rare held-out instances and simultaneous handoffs.

**FCR5. Teach the alarm to choose an experiment, not declare a loss.** Parts: FC7 donor–recipient crossover, FC21 pay for disambiguation, RW2 blind selection, RW8 false revival, PR #101 detection versus recovery, LF#, MY3 correctability meter. *Mechanism:* When a label-free alert fires, use only deployment-legal signals to choose a neutralized donor cue, a public training fork, or deferral. Train the selector on separate development worlds to spend its next probe where the possible outcomes would change the repair choice. Keep the live decision blind and grade detection, diagnosis and restoration independently afterward. *New:* An alarm can be worth keeping even if it is a mediocre classifier, provided it routes scarce diagnostic actions well. *First test:* Hide access-only, training-recoverable and verifier-drift regimes in a rule stream; compare a fixed diagnostic order, direct repair and a stratified labelled canary at equal evaluation and repair cost.

**FCR6. Bisect the first operation that takes away a safe return path.** Parts: FC13 gateway cuts, FC14 reader versus memory, RW12 joint carriers, RW17 cross-witness carriers, FL#, PA9 divergence, Y3 seed-log bisect, MC#. *Mechanism:* Find pass-to-fail intervals for each sealed audit witness rather than assuming recoverability decreases monotonically. In each interval, replay from snapshots and replace a single operation at a time; confirm whether the substitution restores a return path without simply removing the new skill. Attempt the same repair with distinct witnesses and log disagreement instead of treating the intersection as all of memory. *New:* The first operation that breaks today's answer may be different from the first one that rules out an affordable recovery tomorrow. *First test:* Search small streams with alternating masks, slot updates and quantization, then validate on unplanted runs. Compare against endpoint block ablation and a single boundary search at equal replay cost.

**FCR7. Put a mortgage on a wake-up recipe that borrows optimizer state.** Parts: FC8 cue-revealed twins, FC17 colliding histories, RW14 weight–slot synergy, RW16 selective resets, MF5 step counters, MF18 slot order, OM#, MFH4 stale resurrection. *Mechanism:* Record a portability condition that names the expected weight–slot groups, counter convention and public schedule. At a checkpoint migration, either retain a compatible package, recompile from public data or withdraw the recipe. Audit combined and individual swaps against first-update and linearized controls; separately test wake-ups that would revive an expired answer. *New:* A recipe can promise conditional access without implying that the skill traveled in the weights alone. *First test:* Compare lossless restore, coupled migration and independent slot resets on matched-output twins carrying opposite valid obligations, with a separate expired-premise arm. Resume identical public training and score both later retention and stale relapse.

**FCR8. Give a merged checkpoint a return ticket for each training branch.** Parts: FC1 expiring triangle certificates, FC20 release portfolios, RW1 witness grammar, RW6 portability, RW13 last complete carrier, MC#, NC#, CT5. *Mechanism:* At a merge, require a declared panel of valid obligations, and for each branch record a target-blind return recipe or explicitly mark its service unavailable. A proposed compaction is allowed only when every promised recipe still has a demonstrated complete carrier set at the cut; every audit conditions on the witness menu and reference state. Recheck promises under shared future updates, not just immediately after merge. *New:* A merge could preserve the right to recover service without keeping either branch permanently live or retaining its whole history. *First test:* Merge synthetic learners with overlapping tasks and compare against a live-only merge and equal-byte multi-checkpoint routing; include an exact collision in a finite host as a negative control.

**FCR9. Run the old interviews in parallel before allowing any repair to edit the learner.** Parts: FC6 answer-blind hints, FC11 cue then fork, FC15 repair commutation, RW10 novice differential, RW18 wake then distill, PA7 inaccessible versus lost, LA2, RP#. *Mechanism:* Let frozen donors name candidate cues on disjoint shadow forks, then run predeclared public continuations independently. Compare cue-only, continuation-only and cue-plus-continuation arms from the same cut. If a target-blind selector chooses an awakened shadow, distill its outputs on public probes only, and check whether the repair survives next training without teaching obsolete answers. *New:* One can separate combinations that genuinely expose a buried skill from repairs that only work because an earlier repair changed the test subject. *First test:* In a rules learner with hidden routing and incidental spurious shortcuts, compare parallel discovery against sequential repair and pre-shock replay escrow. Use matched novices, cue-only solvers and answer-balanced cue classes.

**FCR10. Learn when to combine two repairs and when to reschedule the curriculum instead.** Parts: FC4 reverse order, FC15 commuting repairs, FC22 next-shift retirement, RW3 public structural keys, RW20 multiplexing, PO#, Y2/Q1 order comparisons, MFH10 resumed corrections. *Mechanism:* Cross both repair orders with both public lesson orders from one cut, then replay a common public future from all four endpoints and a no-repair control for each lesson order. Train a scheduler to choose temporary wake-up, durable repair or lesson reordering based on public signatures of interaction, rather than a single present-time pairwise score. *New:* Some apparently incompatible wake-ups may become compatible if the lesson that consumes their shared carriers moves earlier or later. *First test:* Test interchangeable and overlapping lessons at fixed counts in a small sequence learner. Compare greedy weakest-task repair and a fixed practice mix; score transient service gaps and post-remix durability separately.

**FCR11. A continual learner that can budget, certify and retire its own return paths.** Parts: FC1 expiring triangles, FC2 rotation null, FC3 item closure, FC4 curriculum reversal, FC6 answer-blind cues, FC7 donor crossover, FC11 delayed cues, FC13 gateway cuts, FC15 repairing in order, FC17 exact collisions, FC18 unique probes, FC21 paid diagnosis, FC22 next-shift retirement, RW1 witness grammar, RW2 blind selector, RW5 bounded obstruction, RW6 portability, RW7 burial depth, RW8 false revival, RW9 re-burial, RW12 joint carriers, RW13 last complete set, RW14 slot synergy, RW15 relay migration, RW17 cross-witness support, RW18 crystallization, RW20 multiplexing, RW22 amortized search, Y12 spin echo, Y1 hysteresis, Y7 Red Queen, Y3 bisect, Z6 cohort clocks, Z3 opponent ledgers, PO#, LF#, FL#, PA#, MF#, MC#, LA1, IR8, MFH4. *Mechanism:* Let frozen past selves propose challenges and let an independent checker confirm valid regressions in both directions on a fixed panel. On held-out items, keep profile rotation distinct from same-item loss and return. In offline audits, test balanced frozen cues, then common target-blind continuations, and record which joint carrier sets support which recipes. At deployment a blind scheduler may refocus a reversible cycle, switch lesson order, or relay the last working return path before a cohort dies. Retire a promise only under an explicit change of validity or release policy; report bounded unresolved misses separately from verified full-state collisions. *New:* A learner could treat temporary unavailability as a budgeted liability with a named recovery path, rather than either protecting everything forever or pretending that endpoint accuracy means nothing went missing. *First test:* Use an enumerable rule stream with rotating task demands, hidden access changes, a checkpoint merge and fresh post-repair lessons. Compare equal-budget replay, always-live protection, checkpoint portfolios and a labelled canary with a fixed repair menu. Score verified edges, time out of service, blind wake-up yield, stale revivals, next-shift retention, unrelated old tasks and new-task progress separately.
