**ART1. Commit a common consequence without closing either next move.** Parts: L3-10 neutral and opposite endpoints, L3-11 asymmetric margins, L3-19 cheapest legal next moves, CE3-01 shared implication, CE3-02 off-optimum common move, CE3-03 conditional intersection, CE3-04 agreed lower bound, CE3-08 both futures reachable, CE2-05 partial commit, L2-16 two-way port. *Mechanism:* Find an observable improvement licensed under all live readings, not an average of their fitted deltas. Among equal present effects, choose only a placement that leaves both future moves available at their independently measured retention margins; keep a neutral response for a non-resolving outcome. *New:* A common write could be selected for what it keeps possible, even when it is not a component of either reading's optimum. *First test:* Enumerate a quantized head with equal present improvements but different reachable corrections. Compare robust partial commit plus a signed basis, revised-evidence writer regeneration with routed children, and exact search; include no-fit and exposure-only controls.

**ART2. Rebase an unused amendment on the licensed effect, not on old coordinates.** Parts: L3-03 transport dormant meaning, L3-12 nonlinear opposition, L3-21 repair a stolen direction, CE3-09 cheaper new encoding, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing, CE3-11 compositional residual, CE2-13 transfer proof obligation, IP20 optimizer change, D07 boundary refinement. *Mechanism:* Measure what the intervening write actually did on paired inputs, then recompute the residual against the original licensed behavior. Refit the smallest port that still produces both semantic effects, declining an endpoint if no retaining move realizes it. *New:* A revision can make a deferred edit cheaper or impossible without changing what was originally authorized. *First test:* Reparameterize or collapse a feature between selection and application in a two-layer toy. Compare adapter transfer, port rebasing and regeneration plus replay after a nonlinear and an invertible transformation.

**ART3. Choose an exercise that can still authorize a write after the common commit.** Parts: L3-13 latent future comparison, L3-14 jointly authorized feedback, L3-23 transfer the chooser, L3-24 crossed examples, CE3-14 admissible-write query, CE3-15 ordinal inequalities, CE3-16 implementable distinction, CE3-18 negative control, CE3-21 preserve experiment power, CE3-22 post-commit applicability, L2-08 matched exposure, CE2-25 finish a pending write. *Mechanism:* Preserve a planned common comparison only if both groups still can see and rate the same pair under matched presentation after deployment. Translate its outcome into a constraint on realizable edits; a negative control checks that the exercise has not changed meaning. Leave a slot neutral when independent evidence cannot narrow it to a feasible option. *New:* An installed write could preserve not just future update capacity but also the real observation that authorizes using it. *First test:* Use two equally useful shared writes, one that removes exposure to a decisive pair and one that preserves it. Compare active-query regeneration and a common commit chosen only for retention; disjoint exposure must earn zero splits.

**ART4. Delete child behavior but keep the amendment contrast on the right slice.** Parts: L3-01 correction contrast, L3-02 reconstruction embargo, L3-04 independent deadlines, L3-05 shared third intervention, L3-06 slice rejoin, L3-07 paraphrase-invariant scope, CE3-05 rare old consequence, CE3-06 mixture robustness, L2-19 separate teachable paths, R03 nonadditive control. *Mechanism:* Merge only service slices with matched old and current support. Delete child serving weights for those slices; retain a compact feedback-to-effect contrast and one shared amendment requirement. Route authorized feedback by scope and deadline, then test whether either group's unseen correction can still be learned without recovering its old policy. *New:* This separates learning differences that survive a merge from service differences that were merely obscured by traffic mix or wording. *First test:* Use identical pooled scores with true and exposure-induced convergence; paraphrase feedback, delay one cohort, and change the desired endpoints. Compare shared-plus-residual distillation, a symbolic table and kept children at equal budgets.

**ART5. Release one option and lend its rank without lending its authority.** Parts: L3-15 retire one endpoint, L3-16 lease a reservation, L3-17 updated neutral return, L3-18 one vector with two roles, L3-20 time-share scratch capacity, L3-25 opportunity cost, CE3-12 independent residuals, CE3-13 independent withdrawal, CE28 evidence versus decision, D11 reclaim capacity. *Mechanism:* Account separately for a slot's physical occupancy, its authorized use, and each outstanding return obligation. Release or lend capacity only if the remaining authorized endpoint and corrected neutral state stay reachable before the declared return event. Do not infer authority from who last occupied the slot. *New:* One learner could reclaim idle update capacity without silently delegating another learner's right to finish its edit. *First test:* In a finite rank ledger alternate ends, a borrower and an early owner recall after an intervening correction. Compare permanent slots, task-free growth and charged scratch regeneration; check no silent entitlement transfer.

**ART6. Retire only the route whose evidence actually expired.** Parts: L3-08 targeted route collapse, L3-09 scoped expiry, L3-27 neither endpoint fits, CE3-07 disprovable hold, CE3-17 exercise assumptions, CE3-23 binding old-task floor, CR29 versioned witness, D06 limited structural certificate. *Mechanism:* Keep a falsifier and enabling conditions for each dormant route. If a new observation rules out both reserved effects, check whether a new common effect is feasible; retire only the invalidated route and keep any other independently justified option. Spend the next query on whichever barrier actually binds. *New:* A shared learner can change its mind about a dormant amendment without resetting its other obligations or preserving stale reasons. *First test:* Plant two quiet routes and a change that invalidates just one; also hold one residual solely because of rare old behavior. Compare periodic refresh, all-or-none merge and revalidation of the specific binding constraint.

**ART7. Transfer an exercise by its feasible-write partition, not its answers.** Parts: CE3-19 match authorization partition, CE3-20 teach the second successor, CE3-25 representation-stable outcome, CE3-26 ordinary task as experiment, CE2-14 intervention substitution, CE2-17 shortcut control, CE23 conditional witness, LE5-22 independent repair routes, L3-23 local endpoints. *Mechanism:* Give successors the relation between exercise outcomes and authorized writes, withhold the resolving result, and require each successor to realize the same distinction under its own observations. Replace a purchased comparison with already-due work only when it supplies matched evidence and has a backed fallback. *New:* The portable object may be a recipe for deciding what can be written, not a recipe for restoring a predecessor's answer or its gradient. *First test:* Remove an affordance across three toy learners and let ordinary work expose either an equivalent comparison or only its proxy. Compare stored demonstrations, original exercise replay and fresh active learning.

**ART8. Admit jointly callable reservations rather than individually safe ones.** Parts: L3-22 overlapping callable ports, L3-26 safe reopening plan, CE3-24 shared resolving outcome, IP19 multi-writer collision, SD2-05 third-edit blocker, CR24 bounded impossibility. *Mechanism:* Enumerate arrivals where one comparison may inform two residuals. Share the observation only if the eligible sign pairs compose and both old promises persist. If a third write removes the affordable shared path, keep separate ports or decline one reopening instead of spending the same evidence as two authorizations. *New:* Joint feasibility can fail even when each retained edit and its diagnostic are viable in isolation. *First test:* Use two tiny queues and one third edit that deletes a shared direction; cross both arrival orders with one missing comparison. Compare isolated slot checks, regenerated adapters and exact joint scheduling.

## Prior-art check: PR #348 — LoRA Learner 3

Extends the reviews in [round 84](cross-pool-84.md#prior-art-review-pr-344--a-lora-writing-learner) and [round 85](cross-pool-85.md#prior-art-check-pr-346--lora-learner-2). [Text-to-LoRA (arXiv:2506.06105)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06105), §§3–5, emits LoRA factors from a task description and module/layer embeddings; its writers are trained by adapter reconstruction or supervised fine-tuning. [Drag-and-Drop LLMs (arXiv:2506.16406)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16406), §§2.2–2.6 and 3, generates adapters from prompt batches using a convolutional decoder trained on prompt-checkpoint pairs; its inference prompts are unlabeled. Both demonstrate held-out adaptation, not repeated rejoining and reopening of one live skill with separated amendment rights. The gaps below are relative to these two papers, not a general novelty verdict.

**Every row:** Compare the stronger of T2L and DnD regenerated with the same revised evidence, per-skill distillation and a routed library. Include the cue-conditioned shared adapter and shared-plus-residual control; charge held and peak rank, memory, update and serve compute, failed writes, and questions. Check old A/B immediately after each revision and on another held-out correction, serving through the current route. For credited splits require real repeated ratings of the same request and candidate pair from both cohorts under matched randomized presentation, plus feasible children, a pre-action cue and failure of the strongest comparable shared policy for serving splits. Disjoint exposure and provisional forks do not count.
