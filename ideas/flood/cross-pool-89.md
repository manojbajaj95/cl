**DRX1. Replace a correction route by its available continuations, not its current success rate.** Parts: D2-01 replaceable experiment family, D2-08 authorized surface handoff, D2-10 audited surrogate, D2-23 equivalent closure outcomes, DF16 migration bridge, CE4-26 alternate lesson. *Mechanism:* Fit a replacement for one route component after migration and test whether each relevant report still authorizes the same next action. A proxy that passes today's detection test can fail when a later change expands exposure beyond the region with direct corroboration. Keep the direct component or a lawful fallback on that boundary. *New:* The certificate can be reused across successive migrations without assuming that two equally successful routes teach the same corrections. *First test:* Create a finite pair of interfaces with equal current detection and different post-migration writable actions. Compare a fixed proxy, full resynthesis, and transport of only the affected route component; charge overlap audits.

**DRX2. Reserve a second challenge after the first correction drains a scarce invitation.** Parts: D2-02 two-write route contract, D2-07 single-use invitation, D2-09 post-correction viability, D2-25 shared prefix not shared discharge, L3-22 jointly callable ports, SD2-05 third-edit blocker. *Mechanism:* Model a consented single-use invitation as a consumable. Share ordinary work before the decisive observation, but reserve a distinct eligible suffix for each challenged decision. Validate both arrival orders and a third intervening write; one correction must not consume the last route for another. *New:* Two individually retaining writes can conflict over diagnostic opportunity even when their parameter effects commute. *First test:* Enumerate a tiny queue with two old disputes, a shared prefix, and one expiring invitation. Compare individual reachability checks and exact joint diagnosis; run with alternative suffixes both present and absent.

**DRX3. Price the cheapest lawful route that works under aliased context.** Parts: D2-03 cut and reopening cost, D2-04 aliased starting states, D2-05 discrimination bound, D2-06 vector cost frontier, D2-21 access in both worlds, DF06 silence bounds, CE4-10 per-reading access. *Mechanism:* Check witness access using one observation-conditioned policy for indistinguishable starting states, not an oracle that knows the answer. Track discrimination and separate costs for elapsed time, burden and service disruption. If no admissible route survives, report a cut under those bounds and a permitted repair if one exists. *New:* A cut tied to information and layered costs can rule out a nominally open path that cannot actually distinguish opposite decisions. *First test:* Use a finite partially observed model with a reachable but uninformative report, an infeasible oracle route and a lawful route that becomes costly. Compare exact belief-state search and a route checker that ignores observation quality.

**DRX4. Keep two opposed witnesses through a rotation without confusing uncertainty with indifference.** Parts: D2-11 opposed witnesses, D2-12 reachable outcome order, D2-14 jointly feasible worlds, D2-16 indifference versus unknown, D2-19 bounded compression, DF32 preserve the question, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing. *Mechanism:* Represent related comparisons by compatible worlds and concrete witnesses for opposed outcomes. Rebase the witnesses on observable effects when coordinates change. A smaller representation may conservatively admit more possibilities, but must not manufacture a positive witness or certify indifference from absent evidence. *New:* A learner can preserve the difference between two authorized equivalent choices and a comparison it still cannot decide, even when labeled old scores match. *First test:* Rotate a finite feature map with two linked comparisons and a true-indifference control. Compare open-label replay, independent intervals and joint behavioral witnesses; test targets where one artificial combined world would be inconsistent.

**DRX5. Release only the scope that a new witness actually settles.** Parts: D2-13 alternative evidence chains, D2-15 explicit overlap, D2-17 local reopening, D2-18 authorization-aware join, D2-20 witness-triggered release, DF31 withdrawn corroboration, CR29 versioned witness. *Mechanism:* Require an overlap proof before a report can close an old comparison. Join independently supported constraints only where their scopes agree. If one evidence chain expires, reopen precisely those contexts that lack another valid chain, keeping the original disagreement witnesses callable. *New:* Closure can attach to independently replaceable evidence rather than to a model version or order of feedback delivery. *First test:* In a three-context toy give two closure proofs backing only one shared context, revoke one proof, and reorder reports. Compare version invalidation, embedding transfer, and an equal-byte provenance ledger.

**DRX6. Leave the question open when the permission to ask expires.** Parts: D2-22 dormant reopening obligation, D2-24 expiring opt-in, D2-26 independent failure witnesses, DF20 right to reopen, CE4-12 reversible deadline, L4-20 recheck authorization. *Mechanism:* Track open comparison state separately from permission to gather more evidence. An expired opt-in blocks the old route without settling the question. When context changes, test both whether the proposed write presumes an answer and whether an authorized reopening path exists; these failures can occur independently. *New:* A learner could remain honest about an unresolved decision without treating expired permission as something it may retain or reuse. *First test:* Simulate four equal-scoring writes that respectively preserve both properties, settle by fiat, strand the route, or do both. Include later fresh consent and a no-consent control; compare abstention replay and exact supervised diagnosis.

**DRX7. A deployed learner that preserves both undecided comparisons and lawful ways to resolve them.** Parts: D2-01 replaceable experiment family, D2-08 authorized surface handoff, D2-10 audited surrogate, D2-23 equivalent closure outcomes, DF16 migration bridge, CE4-26 alternate lesson, D2-02 two-write route contract, D2-07 single-use invitation, D2-09 post-correction viability, D2-25 shared prefix not shared discharge, L3-22 jointly callable ports, SD2-05 third-edit blocker, D2-03 cut and reopening cost, D2-04 aliased starting states, D2-05 discrimination bound, D2-06 vector cost frontier, D2-21 access in both worlds, DF06 silence bounds, CE4-10 per-reading access, D2-11 opposed witnesses, D2-12 reachable outcome order, D2-14 jointly feasible worlds, D2-16 indifference versus unknown, D2-19 bounded compression, DF32 preserve the question, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing, D2-13 alternative evidence chains, D2-15 explicit overlap, D2-17 local reopening, D2-18 authorization-aware join, D2-20 witness-triggered release, DF31 withdrawn corroboration, CR29 versioned witness, D2-22 dormant reopening obligation, D2-24 expiring opt-in, D2-26 independent failure witnesses, DF20 right to reopen, CE4-12 reversible deadline, L4-20 recheck authorization. *Mechanism:* Maintain linked records for still-admissible outcomes, evidence that can resolve them and contingent routes that make such evidence obtainable. Recheck route and closure contracts across successive updates, charging permissions as consumable and distinguishing limited access from low diagnostic power. If a legal route expires, report the unavailable evidence without committing an answer or reusing expired consent. *New:* The two retention failures can be discovered and repaired independently even when the old labeled outputs are unchanged. *First test:* Enumerate finite synthetic streams with rotating features, transport failure, aliased start states, a consumed invitation and a third intervening write. Compare exact product-state diagnosis, constrained replay, conservative exploration and a charge-matched provenance ledger; audit whether unresolved comparisons can still be closed by valid future evidence.
