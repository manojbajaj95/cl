# Cross-pool merges, round 81

Focal source: `tree/non-neural-learners-3.md` (PR #340, DS# and FW#). Raw proposals for benign synthetic software learners. Freeze permitted edits, reader deadlines, sustained service and full costs.

**FWR1. Buy a destination and a way back at the same first-edit hazard.** Parts: DS01 destination row, DS05 repairable landing, FW13 labeled next write, SM02 directional synonym, CR17 reversible retirement. *Mechanism:* Choose a representation with matched current output and first-escape rate. Compare its entire destination distribution and the microstates it reaches. Admit a preferred landing only when a distinct ordinary correction can return it to an old-compatible route in budget. *New:* Two choices with the same one-step odds may leave very different recovery options for the next teacher. *First test:* Enumerate a toy three-answer Markov editor with two landings per destination; compare a fixed hazard mask, explicit routing plus repair and exhaustive search.

**FWR2. Keep both writers' destination preferences after either writer acts.** Parts: DS07 neutral proposals, DS10 independent rows, DS13 distributed compilers, SM10 writer-specific edits, IP19 third-writer collision, SD2-05 third-edit witness. *Mechanism:* Compile scoped destination distributions at two addresses and certify both edit orders on the actual subsequent proposal kernel. Reuse the smallest third-edit witness when a neutral-looking edit erases one row. Do not approve from unchanged predictions or unchanged total hazard. *New:* Local software edits might compose without silently taking away each other's future choices. *First test:* Use a two-address rule system with equal service and one hidden shared degree of freedom. Compare paired certificates, additive explicit tags and exact joint enumeration.

**FWR3. Route credit without losing the write's matching debit.** Parts: FW02 labeled credit, FW08 tick-level equality, FW10 conserved debit, FA05 local capacity, SMA23 delayed credit, BM4-22 compatible receipts. *Mechanism:* Route one ordinary outcome through a matched timed schedule and pair each admitted write with the actual capacity debit at its arrival junction. Translate the debit obligation across a legal cutover rather than billing whichever path a stale receipt names. Do not count copied receipts twice. *New:* Fixed service can leave a useful choice of who pays for a later update, even when the target edge is fixed. *First test:* Construct two toy queue schedules with equal throughput and a delayed outcome, then verify conservation and recipient service at every tick.

**FWR4. Reject service-equivalent endpoints when a live transition misses a burst deadline.** Parts: FW04 burst tails, FW05 matched slack, FW07 live transition, ER4-01 regenerating prefix, ER4-21 joint cutover, SMA18 pending work. *Mechanism:* Search for a joint switch on a prefix where the new route can reconstruct pending work. Keep the same old slack and latency contract throughout, including under the declared burst library. Where no prefix works, charge a bounded period on the old route or decline the rewrite. *New:* Future edit direction could be purchased without borrowing capacity from a live old reader. *First test:* Enumerate a finite queue with two consumers and an in-flight burst. Compare endpoint sorting, drain-and-restart and scoped prefix transitions.

**FWR5. Keep the useful part of a rejected direction without authorizing its write.** Parts: DS11 kernel uncertainty, DS12 rejected proposal, SM14 scoped abstention, CR13 unresolved constraint, BM4-14 restricted report. *Mechanism:* Use scratch proposals to estimate directional advantages under an explicitly fixed budget. Carry only a scoped constraint from rejected choices; test whether a fresh ordinary observation supports a lawful correction after a real task change. Until then, do not carry or apply the rejected delta. *New:* A proposal can teach the system where a useful future correction might be without giving it permission to edit now. *First test:* Plant a kernel change and match the accepted rows across two symbolic learners. Compare discarding rejected proposals, queued deltas and scoped follow-up.

**FWR6. Require a routing synonym to survive actual integer traffic.** Parts: FW01 reachable write sites, FW09 circulation cost, FW11 scaling control, FW12 integer traffic, FA06 bounded lifetime, IP24 precision threshold. *Mechanism:* Enumerate the reachable edge-time cells after quantization and charge every circulation. Allocate address precision only when it changes a realizable write under the same deliveries and deadlines. Compare to the best explicit address at the same number of bits and operations. *New:* Compact forwarding could be useful exactly when its apparent freedom survives discrete execution and beats the explicit control. *First test:* Use finite graphs with several sizes and one fractional-only route. Compare exact integer planning to continuous mixtures and a charged explicit address.

**FWR7. Swap teacher roles without re-estimating the learned preference.** Parts: DS02 role swap, DS04 transfer contrast, DS06 coarse and fine detail, FW06 independent traffic, LE5-21 unknown scale, SD2-26 relation transfer. *Mechanism:* Transport a destination preference as a relation between public roles, not a cached answer or supplier identifier. Require the coarse destination and fine correction order to survive an observed role swap and an ordinary step from the new source. Decline unobserved mappings rather than assuming symmetry. *New:* A learner might preserve which kind of update it favors even when a different teacher supplies the evidence. *First test:* Use held-out role and scale swaps with fixed task service; compare a static destination table, recalibration and a transported relational rule.

**FWR8. Keep feedback meaning separate from where its write lands.** Parts: FW03 matched mixtures, FW13 destination as label, DS08 information capacity, DS09 balanced flow, FA07 service-neutral flow, CR18 single-origin evidence. *Mechanism:* Factor the accepted destination label from the physical site chosen by equal-service routing. Intervene on each separately with fixed hazard, delivered traffic and issued evidence. Count joint independent choices only if each can change a realized correction while the other and all local conservation obligations stay fixed. *New:* The same service might carry independent controls for what an update means and where it takes effect. *First test:* Cross all feasible labels and destinations in a small timed graph; use matched explicit tags and a collapsed carrier as controls.

**FWR9. A continual learner that keeps delivering the same service while choosing its next write.** Parts: DS01 row, DS02 role swap, DS03 acceptance persistence, DS04 contrast transfer, DS05 repairable landing, DS06 coarse and fine, DS07 neutral proposal, DS08 information capacity, DS09 balanced edits, DS10 independent rows, DS11 uncertain kernel, DS12 rejected proposal, DS13 compiler composition, FW01 reachable site, FW02 labeled credit, FW03 mixtures, FW04 bursts, FW05 slack, FW06 independent traffic, FW07 live transition, FW08 timing, FW09 circulation cost, FW10 paired debit, FW11 explicit control, FW12 integer traffic, FW13 label and site, SM02 directed synonym, SM10 selective writer, SM14 abstention, FA05 local capacity, FA06 bounded reach, FA07 service-neutral flow, IP19 collision ledger, IP24 threshold, CR13 unresolved constraint, CR17 reversible retirement, CR18 single-origin evidence, SMA18 pending work, SMA23 delayed credit, SD2-05 third-edit witness, SD2-26 relation transfer, ER4-01 regenerating prefix, ER4-21 joint cutover, LE5-21 unknown scale, BM4-14 restricted reports, BM4-22 compatible receipts. *Mechanism:* Certify service, hazard and reachable future destination rows separately. Compose conditional editors only after checking joint effects, return paths and in-flight old work. Route independent feedback over an integer timed schedule and settle the actual debit. Explicitly price any claimed control against external tags, masks and repair. *New:* The architecture could make useful future edits more likely without claiming static equivalence is enough to protect old behavior. *First test:* Enumerate benign rule and queue fixtures with correlated proposals, integer traffic, burst deadlines and independently changing editors. Compare exact planning and an equal-budget stack of explicit addresses, masks, scoped corrections and conservative switching.
