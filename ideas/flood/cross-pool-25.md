# Cross-pool merges, round 25

Focal sources: `tree/first-point-of-loss.md` (PR #134, FL#) and `tree/past-selves-adversaries.md` (PR #135, PA#). Crosses with Y3 seed-log bisect and blame, E25/Y7 Red Queen and PH19 Hopfield dreaming, Z3 opponent ledgers and historical self-play, function-matched twins (PR #123, FT#), fossil beliefs (PR #132, FB#), SGD information dumping (PR #133, ID#), survival memory (PR #112, SP#), and replay deadlines (PR #113, RP#). These are raw ideas for synthetic learning environments. An ancestor can propose a challenge and demonstrate prior competence, but an independent checker decides whether the obligation still applies. Separate live failure, a bounded reader's failure and exact full-state collision. Charge archives, inverse inputs, queries and repair work; keep assessment families out of discovery and repair.

**LA1. Make a regression witness bisect the learner, not just the checkpoint list.** Parts: FL1 carrier liveness, FL2 instruction minimization, PA1 counterexample walks, PA9 first consequential divergence, Y3 seed-log bisect, X2 blame, ID3 first-loss cuts. *Mechanism:* Turn an independently qualified ancestral challenge into a fixed probe along the training log. Survey blocks first, using ordinary bisection only where failure persists, then replay the implicated block to inspect operation boundaries. Replace one legal operation at the earliest actionable cut and rerun the same suffix; compare with adjacent- and wrong-operation shams. *New:* The historical challenger supplies a loss predicate specific enough to identify and test a repairable instruction, not just to blame a release. *First test:* Plant a transient miss, a persistent carrier release and a misleading adjacent cast in a tiny fixed-point learner. Compare to checkpoint-only bisect and uniform operation scans at matched replay and query costs, grading fresh live recovery rather than reconstructability alone.

**LA2. Let the old opponent demonstrate which escape route stopped working.** Parts: FL5 recovery-path witness, FL9 lift a dormant consumer, PA2 live opponents, PA7 inaccessible versus lost, PA20 recovery from mistakes, E25 Red Queen, LG6. *Mechanism:* A frozen opponent steers a synthetic game to a rare but still-valid recovery state. Start old and current players at the same reached state and cross old/current access paths in a shadow without providing the winning move. When forced access restores success, repair the gateway; when it does not, test a local consumer repair before training every found failure. *New:* A historical opponent can expose loss of recovery and distinguish it from a search failure at the preceding branch. *First test:* Use a tiny maze duel with separate planted policy and route faults. Compare historical, sibling and independent matched opponents, and then compare gateway-aware versus generic repairs with the same verified challenges.

**LA3. Use a stale ancestor to veto the wrong rollback.** Parts: FL6 operation contracts, FL21 prospective write choices, PA8 cross-examination, PA14 stale negatives, PA18 better solutions, FB9 scope, FB19 provenance, CT6. *Mechanism:* Pair a witness that still satisfies an unchanged task with an older witness whose answer is now wrong under a revised world. Apply an independently checked scope rule before trying either witness's rollback suggestion, and require the proposed single-operation repair to preserve the current solution on the revised slice. *New:* The archive can retain examples of what should not be restored and use them to qualify a local intervention. *First test:* In a toy route planner with a genuine rule change and an unrelated forgotten obligation, compare scoped cross-examination to old-output matching, pooled replay, and a current critic with the same checker and repair menu.

**LA4. Turn a function-matched twin into a witness for tomorrow's release.** Parts: FL4 precision/budget surface, FL18 optimizer regressions, PA4 margin-matched exchanges, PA10 silent losses, FT1/FT4/FT18 (PR #123), CT2, IR5. *Mechanism:* Search for a still-valid skill challenge whose current launch results match across history twins but whose outcomes diverge under a declared common training continuation. Keep the full first-step shortcut checks, then try substituting individual later operations in paired shadows. Use the result to block the particular proposed write or narrow its scope rather than claiming current forgetting. *New:* A witness can object to a stipulated future protocol even when neither version loses the task today. *First test:* Train nonlinear twins with alternate practice orders, matched raw outputs and first-step controls. Compare against drift and margin probes, optimizer reset/swap and fixed-feature controls; score in-flight old loss at matched new-task progress.

**LA5. Keep both sides of a failed repair in an opponent ledger.** Parts: FL11 conflict-at-commit arbitration, FL17 next-consumer repair, PA6 debate the regression, PA19 nearest collateral loss, Z3 opponent ledgers, Q4 executable obligations. *Mechanism:* One frozen auditor submits a verified lost task and another searches for the closest valid task harmed by its proposed fix. Record restore and preserve evidence separately, then evaluate the same alternative operation against both before committing. Run the repaired learner on a fresh panel rather than replaying only the two argument cases. *New:* A failed repair leaves an executable preservation constraint instead of disappearing into a net reward or being misreported as evidence against the original regression. *First test:* Plant an interference at a shared classifier readout with neighboring old tasks. Compare two-sided operation selection with net-score repair, single-auditor repair and an equal-budget centralized planner.

**LA6. Buy escrow when a witness can still name the distinction, not when it merely sees a failure.** Parts: FL7 consumer-before-release, FL14 common-state discriminator, PA16 counterexample escrow, PA17 budget bids, SP13 repair windows, RP1 record-before/replay-after, RE1. *Mechanism:* A challenger gets a bounded pre-write audition to show that two old histories remain distinguishable using live state that is about to be released. If the auditor can name an independent discriminator, capture a compact old target for later repair; otherwise keep the finding as diagnosis only. Recheck its semantic validity before settlement and distinguish exact collisions from smooth deterioration. *New:* The witness would buy time-limited preservation of an informative target, not another rehearsal at a supposed universal deadline. *First test:* Compare adjudicated escrow, ordinary stored-target replay, fixed pre-shock escrow and post-shock target-blind replay in both an exact reset toy and a continuous model at matched bytes and calls.

**LA7. Make the Red Queen compete to expose spurious attractors before erasing them.** Parts: FL8 branch-local normalization, FL15 consumer-local regeneration, PA5 within-family failures, PA21 challenge teaching, E25 Red Queen, PH19 Hopfield dreaming, Y7, GP1. *Mechanism:* Let a historical challenger propose reachable cases near a basin boundary; independently distinguish a useful rare endpoint from a spurious attractor. Compare repair by suppressing competition at the nominated consumer with anti-Hebbian sleep on verified spurious endpoints. Save how the challenger reached the boundary only if the learned search finds different verified cases after repair. *New:* A dreaming learner can retain the ability to challenge a removed attractor without eliminating a legitimate old response that happens to look similar. *First test:* Use a tiny associative memory with overlapping old and new patterns. Compare ancestral search and verified suppression against unlabeled dreaming, equal-token boundary replay and an ordinary self-play challenger at equal archive and update costs.

**LA8. Keep the ancestor that offers a new cross-examination, not the oldest executable copy.** Parts: FL3 sufficient carrier sets, FL12 verified handoff, FL22 reclamation, PA11 archive selection, PA12 unwitnessed regions, PA13 non-transitive losses, SP4 shared fork dictionary, Y8, KT8. *Mechanism:* Track which independent check families each archived policy can drive and which operation diagnoses disappear when it is removed. Retire a policy only after a replacement keeps its useful challenge coverage on fresh worlds or the remaining blind spot is explicit. Preserve a short witness trace where trace and checker suffice; retain execution where a live opponent is necessary. *New:* Archive diversity is measured by diagnostic jobs that remain possible, not by age, output disagreement or mean teacher accuracy. *First test:* Create three toy policy generations where the middle alone exposes a delayed loss. Compare budgeted witness handoff with evenly spaced checkpoints, standalone regressions and diversity sampling matched for bytes and queries.

**LA9. Let a private lineage jury grade the repair protocol, not choose the repair.** Parts: FL18 optimizer regression tests, FL19 cross-address routing, PA15 secret jury, PA22 blind release budget, ID21 expiring maps, FB22 audit allocation, FCU5. *Mechanism:* An open set of ancestors nominates legal operation replacements and estimates transfer across versions. A disjoint jury with independently held task families then checks for collateral loss and unsupported generalization of the loss address, using the same observation budget. Only aggregate release outcomes reach training; otherwise a sequence of repairs can train directly on the judge. *New:* A release can distinguish a repair policy that travels to new update regimes from one that merely overfits available ancestral challenges. *First test:* Calibrate on two optimizer schedules, then switch precision or order under hidden task families. Compare to repeated visible challenge tuning, fresh random tests and full matched-cost rechecking.

**LA10. Learn which earlier version to ask when a diagnosis cannot be proved.** Parts: FL4 budgeted recoverability, FL16 side-information renewal, FL20 optimizer interface search, PA3 omitted questions, PA6 debate, PA17 verified budget bids, SP9 decision-changing probes, Q9 tomography, KT4. *Mechanism:* When candidate loss stories imply different repairs but present readers cannot separate them, let historical policies bid with proposed queries rather than predictions of who is right. Test whether those observations actually change a legal repair choice. Record abstention if no allowed reader resolves the dispute; only an admissible collision supports exact loss. *New:* Historical search can buy an observation that makes a diagnosis actionable instead of substituting ancestor confidence for missing evidence. *First test:* In a symbolic tool toy with indistinguishable first symptoms and different viable repairs, compare lineage-based query selection with a same-menu active planner, generic disagreement and direct labelled probes at matched repair cost.

**LA11. A learner that gives its past selves the right to challenge the exact change that hurt them.** Parts: FL1 carrier liveness, FL2 minimization, FL3 carrier sets, FL4 reader budgets, FL5 recovery path, FL6 operation contracts, FL7 rescheduling, FL9 dormant consumers, FL11 arbitration, FL12 handoff, FL14 escrow, FL17 repair queues, FL18 regressions, FL21 prospective choices, FL22 retirement, PA1 counterexample walks, PA2 live opponents, PA3 omitted queries, PA4 matching, PA6 debate, PA7 paired interventions, PA9 divergence, PA11 archive diversity, PA13 non-transitivity, PA14 stale negatives, PA15 jury, PA16 escrow, PA17 budgets, PA18 defense, PA19 collateral loss, PA20 recovery, PA21 learned discovery, PA22 blind release, Y3, Z3, E25, PH19, FT1, FB9, ID3, SP13, RP1, Q4, KT4. *Mechanism:* Use live historical opponents and independent challengers to find qualified old failures, then index each by the earliest operation and named reader for which the evidence supports a change. Test local replacement against sham operations on the same suffix. Escrow only still-valid distinguishing targets, stage a bounded repair, and let a separate lineage jury check new behaviors and unchanged old obligations before promotion. Maintain an archive for coverage of distinct repair stories and disclose unsupported addresses rather than relabeling every failure as erasure. *New:* A regression archive can retain the right to test an intervention, not just the ability to reproduce a bad end score. *First test:* Join a small adversarial symbolic game to the finite-state loss fixtures proposed in PR #134 and a compatible nonlinear learner. Plant a changed rule, a lost gateway, a transient read failure and an exact full-state merger. Compare with same-budget historical self-play plus replay, direct regression testing, checkpoint-only blame and a central same-information repair planner. Report unique verified failures, operation attribution, dormant blind spots and stable live recovery at matched acquisition.
