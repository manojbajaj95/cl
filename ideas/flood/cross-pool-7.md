**T1. Mint addresses from certified ignorance.** Parts: CA1 residual minting, BC1 deficit vector, CC07 aliased-write witness, CI10 debt, S1. *Mechanism:* Before allocating a new write route, fit two causal effect models that agree on all past probes but disagree on whether the existing address span suffices. Ask the split query from their disagreement. Grow only on a confirmed deficit and initialize the new route along the missing behavior coordinate. *New:* Capacity expansion comes with a witnessed reason, distinguishing genuinely missing write access from missing information about old addresses. *First test:* In a toy sparse-address MLP, alternate alias-only and planted out-of-span tasks; compare false growth and learned-task loss against residual thresholds and equal-budget direct extension search.

**T2. Open edits that remember their rivals without storing branches.** Parts: WC02 anamorph edits, WS1 rival-carrying witnesses, EL5 open-world holes, OC4 sketched certificates, Z7. *Mechanism:* Compile an underdetermined edit into a small sketch of allowed latent changes and a rival-facing output constraint. Install only the projection all surviving interpretations share; later observations intersect the sketch instead of recreating entire candidate checkpoints. *New:* The installed skill exposes a consensus behavior while leaving actionable uncertainty over its hidden implementation. *First test:* Use a tiny multiview generator with two equally good depth rules; compare recovery after a disambiguating second view with one committed latent and equal-byte branch storage.

**T3. Counterfactual link-time inference.** Parts: PS3 payload swap matrix, CF9 fork epistasis, CC08 interaction packets, EA9 critical-pair rewrites, Q1. *Mechanism:* Build a matrix of payload/selector swaps and 2×2 decision forks before linking a bundle. Compile only the necessary interaction edges into local rewrites tested on a held-out composition, retaining the disqualifying fork as part of the bundle's type. *New:* The linker can treat an interaction as either a needed semantic operation or a correctable collision based on its causal role. *First test:* In a two-hop nonce association world, withhold links whose marginal writes all pass, then compare correct composed actions and retention against independent slot linking and full joint fitting at fixed forwards.

**T4. Selective undo in behavioral seam coordinates.** Parts: WC07 uncertain seams, ME2 behavior coordinates, EA13 discarded-fiber receipts, OC18 retention receipts. *Mechanism:* Log only the change in a seam distribution that a lossy edit destroyed. To selectively undo, transport the receipt through intervening seam contracts, leaving downstream writes intact whenever their licensed marginals do not depend on the restored detail. *New:* Undo stores what another module would need at the seam, rather than a full inverse delta or a checkpoint. *First test:* In two toy sketch-completion modules, replace one collaborator before selective undo; compare restored strokes and retention of later edits with an equal-byte delta log.

**T5. Learn the editor from failure without letting it drift.** Parts: ME3 hindsight relabeling, ME7 editor invariant set, BC8 revocable premises, CI11 evidence-vs-drift, CC14 interventional pivots. *Mechanism:* Failed writes become verified training examples of the effects they actually caused, stamped with the pivots that justify the relabeling. When pivots change, invalidate only affected relabels; keep a held-out panel of old edit problems as the acceptance gate. *New:* An editor's self-training labels are explicitly revocable behavioral claims, rather than a static log of hindsight successes. *First test:* Train a tiny compiler across two coordinate shifts and one support shift; compare regret on old edit problems against unversioned hindsight and ordinary supervised examples.

**T6. A patch language that can ask about progress, not only permissions.** Parts: EL2 editable conflict cores, BC6 realizability depth, OC9 splitting queries, WS10 twin witnesses. *Mechanism:* Return either a minimal conflicting source core or the shallowest tested backend failure with one contrast query. Use the answer to decide whether to narrow intent or open a deeper port; when no query resolves the distinction, preserve the ambiguity in the artifact. *New:* The same user-facing question can carry separate evidence about what was meant and what is reachable. *First test:* On a two-layer linear/nonlinear toy, generate known intent conflicts and shallow-port failures; compare unnecessary port openings and number of clarifying labels with a confidence-only compiler.

**T7. A context override read from a perturbation channel.** Parts: WC10 lenticular weights, PS12 binding IDs, CA10 late-bound contrasts, MX1, MY4 format as a route, MZ3 trust dial. *Mechanism:* Store a correction policy in the differential response to two sanctioned views. Resolve its selector from current binding contrasts, keeping the ordinary answer independent of whether the paired read is enabled. Train the differential channel to respond only within a specified margin band. *New:* The correction is readable through a declared intervention, not a persistent task tag that competes with the base prior. *First test:* In an A3-derived reader, compare anchored and weak-prior cases plus menu reversals against an equal-size head and prompt-only edits, with same-length filler and entropy-matched no-lesson arms.

**T8. Joint forecasts that expire with the policy, not the clock.** Parts: CF3 coupling sketches, CF6 policy axis, CI2 disagreement-region certificate, CC07 aliases, R9. *Mechanism:* Cache a joint fork forecast and a calibrated envelope over nearby continuation policies. Probe only at disagreement-region states where crossing that envelope could change which write is safe. A probe can either update the coupling estimate or leave a certificate of unresolved ambiguity. *New:* Verification is triggered by policy drift that can change a compositional decision, not routine cache age or score movement. *First test:* Use a two-decision symbolic rollout with controlled policy drift; compare joint sign accuracy per rollout with periodic refresh and marginal-only forecasting.

**T9. An edit quotient with a named memorization rival.** Parts: EA3 square-zero ideal, WS9 lookup rival, CA8 code-distance allocation, EL6 transformations. *Mechanism:* Learn write coordinates whose declared composition is square-zero, then demand that each semantic write beat a named lookup rival on unseen transformations before it receives an address. Mint at maximal code distance only among addresses satisfying the quotient's scope. *New:* The algebra cannot count a write as installed merely because it reproduced its stored witnesses. *First test:* Train a small dense model on nonce renamings; compare held-out transformation effects after two writes against a rank-matched residual bank and explicit memorization.

**T10. Self-simulation in a reader's instruction set.** Parts: AM1 memory ISA, RS3 rival transition algebra, SL2 learning automaton, BI8 edit substitution. *Mechanism:* compile a finite set of memory commands into a reader; learn an automaton over those commands using distinguishing suffixes. A lightweight rival transition table says which responses must still split after a learned substitution, so different native edits can share one command only when the reader still separates the required suffixes. *New:* an executable memory ABI learned from what the reader can become after later edits, rather than the reader's immediate logit shifts. *First test:* on a tiny symbolic router, hold out three-command suffixes and a reader swap; compare against immediate-effect clustering and sequential lesson replay with same-length filler and entropy-matched no-lesson arms.

**T11. An evidence packet can buy a scoped exception without carrying the old teacher.** Parts: OK7 quantified veto query, EC03 factorial licensing rectangles, EI12 safe prefixes, BC8 revocable premises. *Mechanism:* send the four outcomes from toggling procedure and context, plus a reversible edit prefix all survivors agree on. Ask whether any protected case lies in a precisely named disputed region before installing its remaining clause; attach the answer as a revocable premise, not a global override. *New:* the memory capsule carries a useful partial edit and the exact permission question needed to extend it. *First test:* in the PR #57 synthetic world, compare approved scope and harmful overrides per authority answer with yes/no per example and all-or-nothing certificates.

**T12. A one-write proof for when a reachability frame is really missing capacity.** Parts: BA12 dormant-path obligations, OS4 retention-blocked credit, SE6 lossless edit joins, CA1 residual minting. *Mechanism:* preserve dormant obligations as guarded relations over shared write variables. Before minting, join the currently reachable guards and the dormant guards the candidate could reopen; issue a dual blocker only when no single write satisfies that join, and use its missing response axis to propose new capacity. *New:* growth evidence accounts for behavior a write would make reachable, not merely current activations or per-guard feasibility. *First test:* enumerate a finite recurrent router with one dormant branch; compare mint precision and reopened-branch violations with marginal certificates and in-book residual tests.

**T13. Refunding a compositional fork across versions.** Parts: FA8 continuation seams, FA13 refundable composition, BA16 shared-middle couplings, EI14 public semantic reference, ET12 cycle checks. *Mechanism:* record each fork's arm-specific seam traffic and a coupling to a public reference transition. To remove a downstream edit after a policy upgrade, splice in a replacement continuation using the shared seam; if support moved, report the residual as a new paired-measurement request instead of subtracting stale scalar credit. *New:* a continuation effect can be selectively withdrawn with an explicit off-support remainder, even when its original scalar credit no longer applies. *First test:* simulate a three-stage finite policy with known seam histograms; remove the middle write after a policy change and compare effect recovery and rollout cost with log subtraction and full reruns.

**T14. An editor that learns what it is allowed to know.** Parts: ME2 behavioral coordinates, ME3 hindsight, ME7 editor gate, CA1 span minting, CA10 contrast addresses, CF3 coupling, CF6 policy axis, CF9 epistasis, CI2 disagreement regions, CI10 debt, WS1 rival witnesses, WS9 lookup rival, WC02 coupled projections, WC07 seams, OC4 affine sketches, OC9 splitting queries, OC18 receipts, EL2 conflict cores, EL5 open holes, BC1 deficit vectors, BC8 revocable premises, CC07 alias witnesses, CC08 interaction packets, PS3 swap matrix, PS12 binders, EA9 rewrites, EA13 discarded fibers, AM1 memory ISA, RS3 rival algebra, SL2 learning automaton, BI8 substitution, OK7 veto query, EC03 factorial licensing, BA12 dormant promises, EI12 safe prefixes, OS4 capacity blocker, SE6 lossless joins, FA8 continuation seams, S10. *Mechanism:* Carry open hypotheses and checked behavior coordinates as first-class editor state. A failed installation proposes either one separating experiment or a witnessed new port; successful writes ship typed binding interfaces and an undo receipt. The editor learns from observed effects without treating arbitrary drift as evidence, relinks only invalidated joins after an upgrade, and leaves explicit holes instead of silently settling them. It installs only safe prefixes until a scoped permission arrives, carrying dormant promises through the same seam used to replay the effect of later policies. *New:* Continual learning updates a jointly versioned theory of what the learner knows, what it can write, and what measurements distinguish the two. *First test:* Combine one alias ambiguity, one nonseparable two-step fork, and one checkpoint swap with a reactivated promise in a tiny symbolic stream. At equal probes and bytes compare with a stacked editor using witness replay, fixed ports, and full joint refitting; score unresolved-scope calibration as well as retained behavior.
