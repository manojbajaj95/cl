# Cross-pool merges, round 16

Focal sources: `tree/redundant-skill-populations.md` (PR #116, POP#) and `tree/adapter-compatibility-contracts.md` (PR #117, AC#). Crosses with the round 15 breadth cards (BR#), neuro-inspired specialization and repair, fleet economics, and NOOA behavioral contracts. A holder counts only if it can still perform the skill and a live caller can reach it. Replication is not coding, and a common trunk can invalidate many private copies at once. A base upgrade can preserve what an adapter reads and still change the downstream effect of what it writes. These are raw and unfiltered.

**FP1. Breed a pair of specialists that can each rebuild the other.** Parts: POP2 diverse failure directions, POP7 functional repair, POP12 incompatible donors, NB20 subfunctionalization and NB21 regrowth (PR #46), E22 gene duplication (PR #45), BR5. *Mechanism:* Duplicate a working skill and train the two copies to release complementary input regions while retaining a tiny overlap where they can still teach each other. Select the overlap by whether it identifies a replacement under held-out trunk shifts, not by how many common examples it contains. *New:* Specialization reduces duplicated service cost without accidentally eliminating the evidence needed to repair a failed specialist. *First test:* Use a shared-trunk classifier with two independently disappearing feature families. Compare full replication, disjoint specialists and reciprocal repair overlap at equal bytes, update calls and initial skill coverage.

**FP2. Sell a shared feature only after the purchaser proves every exposed skill has a route.** Parts: POP1 surviving sets, POP5 feature barter, POP21 private bypass, AC1 finite write response, AC8 shadow prices, NB02 transferable write territory (PR #46), M5 invariance easements and M6 pay-to-fork (PR #71), BR6. *Mechanism:* Turn contested trunk writes into a coupled optimization: migrate at-risk clients to independent carriers, then admit the new gradient. A cheap route that shares the same projected failure does not count. Charge the trade against finite downstream write responses, not just activation drift. Keep a minimum per-client floor even when the aggregate payout favors the trade. *New:* The price of occupying a feature is a verified surviving set, not a penalty on the magnitude of the update. *First test:* Plant a trunk direction that supports multiple low-frequency skills. Compare protected coverage versus progress to all-client projection, fixed private bypasses and measured interference without migration.

**FP3. Let a specialist refuse narrowly enough that a router can recover.** Parts: POP14 context query, POP16 diagnostic abstention, POP18 last accessible path, AC14 requires-clauses, N34 Liskov memory (PR #41), X6, BR5. *Mechanism:* Give each specialist a parent-typed service contract plus a precise child-only capability, with explicit requirements on the downstream base. When the evidence is insufficient, return a typed reason distinguishing unsupported context from a known local failure. Route the parent call to a checked substitute or ask one context question; a child may not narrow the accepted parent contract without a fallback. *New:* A fallback can preserve fleet coverage even when classifiers agree on confident scores but disagree about whether an input belongs to their role. *First test:* Use three skills with overlapping contexts and remove task IDs. Compare against score voting, top-k routing and generic abstention at matched calls and worst-skill floor.

**FP4. Make an insurance pool measure shared causes rather than holder headcounts.** Parts: POP1 survivor sets, POP2 failure directions, POP6 victim-only correction, E10 insurance and E24 effective population size (PR #45), CF3 coupling sketches (PR #79), BR5. *Mechanism:* Price a reserve by the attainable sets of providers after common trunk shocks. Use paired held-out updates to separate independent private outages from shared-cut failures. Spend reserves on one independent carrier when another private copy would duplicate the same vulnerability. *New:* Insurance on a fleet is useful only if a payout can reach a surviving computation; nominal portfolio diversity is not redundancy. *First test:* Use eight cheap private heads with one hidden common failure. Compare worst-tail old service and recovery cost to fixed replication, marginal-hazard insurance and the size-matched central adapter bank.

**FP5. Rebuild a missing step from its neighbors without learning an incompatible shortcut.** Parts: POP9 neighbor-conditioned repair, POP13 path witnesses, POP17 sub-outcome quorum, NB21 boundary regrowth and NB23 functional extrusion (PR #46), N7 contract replay (PR #41), Q4. *Mechanism:* Train a replacement stage using live upstream and downstream contracts on intervened intermediate values, not just successful final outputs. Switch routing only when a single complete path passes a held-out end-to-end check; do not combine incompatible locally successful witnesses. *New:* A sparse boundary contract can carry repair evidence for a composite skill without freezing either neighbor or assuming locally good stages compose. *First test:* Remove the middle stage of a three-step symbolic workflow whose neighbors share an accidental shortcut. Compare paired boundary queries to end-to-end distillation, local-only testing and retaining a spare stage.

**FP6. Use staggered replicas to teach a successor how to handle its next lesson.** Parts: POP3 differentiable handoff, POP7 repair teacher, POP11 public transfer queries, AC7 future trainability, E16 staggered replicas and E18 succession exams (PR #45), BR4/BR10. *Mechanism:* Keep one lagged holder as a temporary source of behavioral witnesses. Teach a new holder across paired challenge curricula that include an admissible next trunk update, then rotate the lagged slot only if the replacement still serves through that update. Account for the delay and any stale answers it causes. *New:* A succession protocol buys continued adaptability rather than just matching a donor at the instant of handoff. *First test:* Use a two-regime fleet with deliberately brittle immediate distillation. Compare an initial-response match and a client-gradient match on post-next-update service against fixed rotation, keeping the oldest holder, and centralized distillation with the same examples.

**FP7. Enforce version contracts on the caller that actually sees the change.** Parts: POP15 overlapping competence, POP24 revision redundancy, AC5 write transport, AC14 requires-clauses, N34 Liskov memory (PR #41), P19 stable ABI (PR #39), Q5, BR4. *Mechanism:* Run old and new specialists on identical parent-typed calls and on version-tagged calls. Preserve the old route on their overlap unless the successor passes its obligations there; test the transported write on the parent route even if old activations match; permit genuine revisions only when caller-visible state identifies the active contract. Log coverage without assuming that a caller version and a model version move together. *New:* A rollout can preserve live callers and still revise stale behavior; a globally compatible output is not required. *First test:* Simulate old and new workflow clients during a staggered upgrade. Compare compatibility routing with newest-only replacement, full-version replication, and a version gate without parent-contract probes.

**FP8. Move a skill without moving the private examples that justify it.** Parts: POP8 behavioral constraints, POP23 evidence transport, N7 contract replay (PR #41), E20 fleet funding (PR #45), BR7/BR8. *Mechanism:* Have a candidate successor make paired predictions on public cases. Ask a specialist with local access to adjudicate only cases that separate plausible successor behaviors, and release the checked response rather than training examples. If public cases cannot identify the skill, keep it local and report uncovered traffic rather than claiming transfer. *New:* The scarce shared resource is adjudication across a boundary, not a digest of all private experience. *First test:* Partition examples across three agents in a synthetic workflow and hold out a region private specialists can distinguish. Match shared messages and calls against logit distillation, random public queries and a centralized adapter bank with unrestricted access.

**FP9. Negotiate a trunk upgrade using pairs of compatible private repairs.** Parts: POP6 victim-only inverse, POP19 mixed versions, POP22 dependency-specific sync, AC13 broadcast repair, AC16 composition-closed contracts, M2 cross terms (PR #71), BR6, Q1. *Mechanism:* Generate a small boundary translator for each affected route, then use finite write-response probes and test combinations only where two routes use the same changed feature. Install a trunk upgrade only when the selected package has at least one surviving compatible path per live skill; test alternate orderings where the combined repair may differ from either alone. *New:* A repair can be safe alone and unsafe when composed; negotiation concerns the set of installed translators, not isolated averages. *First test:* Construct two stage adapters sharing a changed trunk coordinate with a repair-order interaction. Compare dependency-based interaction tests with testing each translator alone, all-pairs probing, and a full frozen trunk.

**FP10. Rehearse the failure of the router before funding another specialist.** Parts: POP14 context query, POP18 accessible paths, POP20 disconnected domains, E24 effective population (PR #45), B8 intervention budget (PR #48), BR5/BR7. *Mechanism:* Induce controlled outages on a shadow fleet and test the actual routing policy on ambiguously typed old calls. If live specialists remain but requests are not reaching them, buy a contextual query or an independent local route rather than an additional copy. Treat exploration traffic and failed routing calls as costs. *New:* Survival and discoverability can require different investments even when the fleet has the same holder count. *First test:* Hide task IDs in a two-region fleet and plant separately correlated compute loss and routing ambiguity. Compare targeted routing investment with always replicating, task-ID oracle routing, and generic top-k retries.

**FP11. Keep a contract sketch whose errors cannot all fall on one fleet.** Parts: AC2 bilinear registry, AC9 contract sketching, AC10 worst-client retention, AC22 finite nonlinear response, POP1 survivor sets, POP2 diverse failures, IC2 coded skills (PR #56), E24 effective population (PR #45), BR5/BR6. *Mechanism:* Build a shared sketch of read–write responses but reserve probes for any direction whose omission could break every route serving an obligation. Keep a small client-local witness when a common sketch misses it; use jointly exercised writes at multiple scales to check hidden interactions. *New:* Compression protects the last viable route, not the average client or the most frequently registered direction. *First test:* Extend the PR #117 adapter probe with a rare write direction shared by the only holders of one skill. Compare pooled SVD, a worst-client sketch, and coverage-aware sketching at equal bytes and probe calls.

**FP12. A shared base that can change without leaving its last specialist stranded.** Parts: POP1 survivor sets, POP2 failure diversity, POP3 handoff, POP5 barter, POP7 functional repair, POP9 neighbor repair, POP13 path witnesses, POP14 routing queries, POP17 sub-outcome quorum, POP19 mixed trunks, POP22 dependency sync, POP23 evidence transport, POP24 revisions, AC1 finite response, AC2 read/write registry, AC5 write transport, AC7 trainability, AC8 shadow prices, AC9 sketching, AC10 worst client, AC13 local repair, AC14 requires-clauses, AC16 composition, AC22 downstream curvature, N7 contract replay, N34 Liskov memory, NB20 specialization, NB21 regrowth, NB23 turnover, E10 insurance, E16 staggered replicas, E24 effective population, BR4, BR5, BR6, X6, Q4. *Mechanism:* Register the client read, the downstream response to the client write, and the allowed caller surface as different obligations. Before accepting a base upgrade, trace which reachable service paths are jointly threatened. Migrate endangered roles to genuinely independent carriers; where that is cheaper, ship a response translator or rebuild a stage with witnesses from compatible neighbors. Retire a duplicate only after a successor survives both a routing test and a held-out next update. *New:* A fleet can spend redundancy on the links that fail together, while compatibility tests specify what each local replacement owes its callers and neighboring stages. *First test:* Combine the PR #116 synthetic fleet with the PR #117 finite-response probe, then plant a shared consumer failure, a lost private adapter and a no-task-ID route. Compare equal-byte replication, a central bank, activation-only ABI and a stack of finite-response checks with fixed placement; charge storage, probes, user-facing routing calls and repair steps.
