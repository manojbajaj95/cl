# Cross-pool merges, round 117

Focal source: `tree/open-ended-learners-2.md` (PR #385, OE2-#). Older sources: `tree/root-open-ended-learners.md` (PR #384, OE#; focal in round 116), [round 116](cross-pool-116.md) (SCX1, SCX2, SCX4, SCX6), population learners V (PR #383, PL5-#), test-time learners root (PR #373, TT#), learner–world co-evolution IX (PR #372, L9W#) and VIII (PR #371, L8W#), deployment feedback 5 (PR #357, D5-#) and 4 (PR #355, D4-#), relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#), and sleep and dreaming II (PR #334, SD2-#). Raw proposals for harmless synthetic environments.

**ORC1. Rehearse a skill only when it restores enough other skills to justify the work.** Parts: OE2-01 competence signatures, OE2-02 co-lapse graph, OE2-03 revival graph, OE2-04 latent versus lost, OE2-05 recurring lapses, OE2-08 novelty parity, OE08 frontier impact, SCX2 retained frontier, TT28 frozen shadow. *Mechanism:* Distinguish spontaneous success, success with a hint and verified loss. When several skills lapse together, train one in scratch state and test the others without further training before treating the cluster as a revival route. Match work against hinting and against teaching an entirely new task. *New:* Co-lapse can nominate a lesson, but only a directed intervention shows whether it revives the other skills or deserves the same budget as invention. *First test:* Build harmless toy tasks where skills lapse together from either a shared dependency or a common schedule. Compare uniform rehearsal, correlation-based rehearsal and rehearsal ranked by verified revival.

**ORC2. Keep novelty search honest when the proposer chooses what to measure.** Parts: OE2-06 lapse-anchored proposer, OE2-07 repertoire turnover, OE2-09 lapse control, OE2-10 archive validity, OE06 competence novelty, PL5-18 independent observations, PL5-10 held-out effects, TT33 checkpoints. *Mechanism:* At fixed checkpoints, estimate invention, lapse and rediscovery separately using an independent sample that the proposer cannot select. Adjust step size only when rising lapse transfers to held-out skills. Report whether apparent repertoire growth comes from fast turnover of easy entries. *New:* An archive can be both a curriculum and a biased measurement device; independent sampling separates a real stability change from a changed choice of probes. *First test:* Plant an easy class the proposer can repeatedly rediscover while held-out tasks decay. Compare permanent novelty, naive lapse-driven novelty and externally checked lapse feedback.

**ORC3. Store the shortest objective that regrows the intended skill, not its nearest shortcut.** Parts: OE2-11 objective compressibility, OE2-12 counterexample refinement, OE2-13 neighbor exclusions, OE2-17 short-reward generalization, OE2-20 verifier memory, OE2-24 start distribution, OE02 stored skill reward, OE25 judge retention, SCX6 independent checker, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation. *Mechanism:* Bundle a compact objective with a versioned start generator and independent acceptance cases. If regrowth finds an unintended optimum, add a small behavioral contrast and retest starting states and neighboring valid skills. Compare storage cost including the checker and starts. *New:* A short reward may compress the policy or merely omit a distinction; regrowth and independent acceptance must both pass. *First test:* Use synthetic puzzles with two policies that score equally from easy starts but diverge later. Compare trajectory replay, reward-only restoration and a contrast-checked objective bundle at matched bytes and training work.

**ORC4. Price a reward by where it can regrow the skill and whether the successor stays editable.** Parts: OE2-14 objective consolidation, OE2-15 cross-model reward, OE2-16 recovery radius, OE2-18 internal targets, OE2-21 format pricing, OE13 re-grounded goals, SCX4 observable goal identity, L9W14 observable inputs, L8W22 scratch round trip, D4-24 future gradient. *Mechanism:* Compare restoring a skill after small drift, architecture change or a changed toy world. Use internal targets only where independent behavioral witnesses still identify their meaning; otherwise favor observable objectives or retain a fallback. Count the time and bytes to recover and verify one fresh authorized amendment on the resulting learner. *New:* The best storage format depends on the expected change and on what can still be taught afterward, not just the size of its payload. *First test:* Cross feature rotation, model swap and a world-rule revision. Compare activation rewards, observable rewards and replay on recovery cost, old behavior and response to a later lesson.

**ORC5. Let the objective library grow without letting it rewrite the meaning of archived skills.** Parts: OE2-19 shared objective library, OE2-22 objective entries, OE2-23 shaping-equivalent novelty, OE2-25 reward rewriting, OE2-26 admission by regrowth, OE28 law charter, SCX1 amendable task environments, D5-26 retained teaching responses, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Propose a reusable library term or a shorter rewrite after a newly found behavior. Compare old and new objectives on independent starts and corrections before merging entries, treating equivalence as scoped to the verified world version. Retain the old bundle or decline admission if the new objective cannot reproduce the behavior and later lesson. *New:* Reward compression can lower storage without converting a convenient new judge into the definition of what the old skill was. *First test:* Run toy streams with incidental discoveries, genuinely equivalent shaping changes and a rule amendment that breaks one equivalence. Compare unconditional objective merging, frozen objectives and versioned checked rewrites.

**ORC6. Make a competence archive that can regrow real skills without fooling its own novelty signal.** Parts: OE2-01 competence signatures, OE2-02 co-lapse graph, OE2-03 revival graph, OE2-04 latent versus lost, OE2-05 recurring lapses, OE2-08 novelty parity, OE08 frontier impact, SCX2 retained frontier, TT28 frozen shadow, OE2-06 lapse-anchored proposer, OE2-07 repertoire turnover, OE2-09 lapse control, OE2-10 archive validity, OE06 competence novelty, PL5-18 independent observations, PL5-10 held-out effects, TT33 checkpoints, OE2-11 objective compressibility, OE2-12 counterexample refinement, OE2-13 neighbor exclusions, OE2-17 short-reward generalization, OE2-20 verifier memory, OE2-24 start distribution, OE02 stored skill reward, OE25 judge retention, SCX6 independent checker, SD2-09 distinguishing continuation, OE2-14 objective consolidation, OE2-15 cross-model reward, OE2-16 recovery radius, OE2-18 internal targets, OE2-21 format pricing, OE13 re-grounded goals, SCX4 observable goal identity, L9W14 observable inputs, L8W22 scratch round trip, D4-24 future gradient, OE2-19 shared objective library, OE2-22 objective entries, OE2-23 shaping-equivalent novelty, OE2-25 reward rewriting, OE2-26 admission by regrowth, OE28 law charter, SCX1 amendable task environments, D5-26 retained teaching responses, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Use independent measurements to separate loss, latent competence and real invention. Choose rehearsal by verified revival, retain versioned objectives with independent acceptance witnesses, and price storage by regrowth cost and future editability. Validate archive rewrites across changes to the solver and the world. *New:* One archive can guide invention, rehearsal and recovery while keeping separate evidence for what is new, what has returned and what still means the same thing. *First test:* Cross harmless synthetic task families with joint lapses, misleading rewards, alternate starts and world-rule changes. Compare permanent novelty, uniform replay and checked objective archives at matched work and bytes.
