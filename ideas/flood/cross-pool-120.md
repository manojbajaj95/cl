Raw proposals for harmless synthetic environments.

**DCX1. Treat recovery codes as a channel with measured capacity, not an exact guarantee.** Parts: OE5-01 gradient-decodable codes, OE5-02 key multiplexing, OE5-03 key length bound, OE5-04 step-count addressing, OE5-05 optimizer-only keys, OE5-06 reserved port, OE4-08 planted keys, OE4-07 drift robustness, PL5-10 held-out effects. *Mechanism:* Compare separate, shared and step-addressed keys against operation-only controls. Count all receiver-side information, including optimizer state, shared seeds and reserved parameters, and estimate recovery under held-out task changes rather than isotropic noise alone. *New:* A large radius on artificial noise need not imply a large usable codebook under later learning; even orthogonal keys can collide at their decoded outputs. *First test:* Plant harmless toy rules and increase codebook size under matched bytes, write steps and later-task interference. Track target recovery and unintended recovery in other slots.

**DCX2. Protect measured recovery endpoints without assuming that a local cone stays safe.** Parts: OE5-07 revival basins, OE5-08 pullback Fisher, OE5-09 commuting updates, OE5-10 linear geometry, OE5-11 future-gradient placement, OE4-02 revival-null directions, OE3-12 preserve recovery, TT28 frozen shadow, D4-24 later teaching response. *Mechanism:* Use local geometry to propose writes, but validate them by actual recovery after writes at different magnitudes. Run revival before and after a write on matched scratch copies, comparing old behavior, improvement and the effect of a fresh correction. *New:* A write can preserve recovery in one order without preserving the new skill in the other; the two checks distinguish tolerance from interchangeability. *First test:* Start with two-layer linear toys with known singular modes, then use nonlinear toy rules to test where the local masks, pullback penalty or endpoint checks stop predicting recovery.

**DCX3. Free capacity only after testing that dormant skills can still be amended.** Parts: OE5-12 expression–revival frontier, OE5-13 dormant support versus free weights, OE5-14 compression walk, OE5-15 unit tags, OE5-16 brief reactivation, OE5-17 reactivation edits, OE5-18 excitability allocation, L8W17 plasticity audit, D5-26 retention of teaching responses, CR29 versioned relearning. *Mechanism:* Propose compression or reallocation using unit tags and scratch reactivation. Before committing, verify recovery, then apply a lawful correction to a toy skill and archive the corrected version; check that unrelated behaviors and current version routing survive. Measure released capacity with a matched subsequent write. *New:* Unused in the active model does not mean unused by recovery or by the next edit; free storage is whatever passes all three checks. *First test:* Compress a toy learner, measure revival and correction before and after compression, and compare tagged reactivation, ordinary rehearsal and no reactivation at the same capacity and work.

**DCX4. Classify composed keys by independent evidence of a new skill.** Parts: OE5-19 specificity of key recall, OE5-20 key composition, OE5-21 key-space search, OE5-22 novelty by recovery, OE5-23 dormant alternatives, OE3-03 signed revival, OE06 forgetting-horizon novelty, OE08 retained frontier, OE25 independent verifier, CR24 bounded recoverability. *Mechanism:* In harmless synthetic worlds, compare each composition against its ingredients, known variants and a fixed budget of allowed lessons. Use independent probes to separate unintended recall, switching among old answers and a stable new combination. Report empirical specificity only, not access control or proof of irrecoverability. *New:* Search can propose new behaviors from the archive without counting collisions and old variants as invention. *First test:* Give a toy rule family alternating solutions and genuinely compositional tasks. Compare task search, unverified key mixing and mixing admitted only after behavior and continuation checks.

**DCX5. Use dormant skills as a prior only when a matched successor learns better.** Parts: OE5-24 partial revival before learning, OE5-25 pretraining for revivability, OE5-26 revival after domain training, OE4-19 bounded working set, OE2-22 stored objectives, PL03 successor stress test, L8W22 scratch round trip. *Mechanism:* Across held-out shifts, warm a scratch successor with a predicted useful dormant skill and compare against random revival, normal warm starts and stored objectives. Check both whether it learns a new task faster and whether its original recovery route still works after returning to dormancy. *New:* A base trained for dormancy is useful if it improves later learning and keeps recovery cheap; recall accuracy alone cannot establish either. *First test:* Pretrain small synthetic sequence learners on two curriculum families, perform a controlled domain shift, then measure update-matched acquisition and recovery on a third family.

**DCX6. Build a dormant skill archive that tests its own storage and recovery claims.** Parts: OE5-01 gradient-decodable codes, OE5-02 key multiplexing, OE5-03 key length bound, OE5-04 step-count addressing, OE5-05 optimizer-only keys, OE5-06 reserved port, OE4-08 planted keys, OE4-07 drift robustness, PL5-10 held-out effects, OE5-07 revival basins, OE5-08 pullback Fisher, OE5-09 commuting updates, OE5-10 linear geometry, OE5-11 future-gradient placement, OE4-02 revival-null directions, OE3-12 preserve recovery, TT28 frozen shadow, D4-24 later teaching response, OE5-12 expression–revival frontier, OE5-13 dormant support versus free weights, OE5-14 compression walk, OE5-15 unit tags, OE5-16 brief reactivation, OE5-17 reactivation edits, OE5-18 excitability allocation, L8W17 plasticity audit, D5-26 retention of teaching responses, CR29 versioned relearning, OE5-19 specificity of key recall, OE5-20 key composition, OE5-21 key-space search, OE5-22 novelty by recovery, OE5-23 dormant alternatives, OE3-03 signed revival, OE06 forgetting-horizon novelty, OE08 retained frontier, OE25 independent verifier, CR24 bounded recoverability, OE5-24 partial revival before learning, OE5-25 pretraining for revivability, OE5-26 revival after domain training, OE4-19 bounded working set, OE2-22 stored objectives, PL03 successor stress test, L8W22 scratch round trip. *Mechanism:* Measure which keys recover which skills under changing weights and what collateral skills wake or fade. Protect actual recovery endpoints, reclaim weights only after a future-lesson check, and let verified key compositions and partial revival feed the next curriculum. *New:* A single archive can support capacity accounting, checked revision and invention while keeping empirical recovery separate from guarantees about what the model must remember. *First test:* Combine harmless toy streams with controlled drift, versioned corrections, key collisions and novel compositions. Compare replay, static keys and checked recovery at matched bytes and update work.
