Raw proposals for finite software learners; fix resource costs, deadlines and admissible observations.

**NMR1. Retain the rule for a correction, not the discarded answer.** Parts: L4-01 correction equivalence, L4-02 teaching bits, L4-03 scope of teaching, L4-05 child-blind decoder, L3-02 reconstruction embargo, CE2-02 non-cloning contrast. *Mechanism:* Construct several old child pairs with identical permissible teaching information and different discarded behavior. Require identical entire retained state across the pairs, then evaluate new authorized targets that no old child serves. The common decoder is frozen before these pairs exist. *New:* An edit summary could retain what one example teaches without being a compressed serving policy. *First test:* Cross hidden serving variations with correction rules in an exact finite toy. Compare equal-byte lossy children, fresh regeneration, and a symbolic summary; track reconstruction and fresh correction separately.

**NMR2. Use a negative witness to carry a correction into a new anchor.** Parts: L4-04 obligation beyond the children, L4-06 unaffected scope, L4-07 rejecting witness, L4-09 reset anchor, L4-19 behavioral constraints, CE3-10 behavioral rebasing, CE2-13 carry proof obligation. *Mechanism:* Save only an effect requirement and a contrast the wrong edit would violate. Recompute the residual against the actual new shared behavior after unrelated revision D; the stored contrast does not itself authorize the edit. Reacquire authorization from fresh matched evidence. *New:* A retained constraint can stay useful after both the child policies and the old anchor become wrong. *First test:* Change the shared default before a novel correction and include a misleading original answer. Compare transfer of deltas, explicit rebasing, and regeneration plus charged replay.

**NMR3. Repair only the part of a slot that the shared revision broke.** Parts: L4-10 leave intact port unchanged, L4-11 localize conflict, L4-12 input-factor repair, L4-14 precision change, L4-15 equivalent address, L3-21 lost direction, D07 refine boundaries, LE5-09 preserve next response. *Mechanism:* Use paired probes of the realized low-rank product to distinguish changed input response, output coupling and quantization loss. Replace only the failed address and retest the old effect contract and unhit route. Reject gauge-only changes as evidence of repair. *New:* A local repair could restore a future effect at less cost than reallocating the whole slot, even across a change in precision. *First test:* Hit only one endpoint by collapsing a feature or changing its precision in a quantized model; include unhit D′. Compare signed spare, repicked basis, exact refit and equal-byte regeneration.

**NMR4. Repair a route only if the next ordinary correction still fits.** Parts: L4-13 retaining two-write path, L4-16 keep the unchosen future, L4-17 scoped refusal, L4-24 relation not old signs, L3-22 callable together, IP19 response collision, SD2-05 third edit, CR24 bounded impossibility. *Mechanism:* Enumerate retaining repair paths through the declared finite interface, checking each served intermediate. After exercising a repair, require the next ordinary correction to reach the intact option. If every candidate breaks D or the other route, preserve the impossibility witness instead of reversing D. *New:* A repaired endpoint is useful only if its implementation does not silently spend another option's next write. *First test:* Use a small lattice with one legal two-step repair and another that strands the intact route after a third write. Compare single-step refit, repicked spare, exact bounded search and separate adapters.

**NMR5. Buy repair evidence only when a valid correction can use it.** Parts: L4-18 lazy repair, L4-20 inherit evidence requirements, L4-21 distinguish hit from noise, L4-25 keep failure witness, CE3-17 exercise assumptions, CE3-23 query the binding floor, CE3-25 durable observations, CR29 fresh version binding. *Mechanism:* Mark a broken direction inactive. Preserve the cheapest discriminating probe and its assumptions; use it only when results can arrive in time and there is a feasible correction to license. After relocation, reuse evidence only if it tests the same effect in the current frame. *New:* Not every detected collision deserves immediate repair, and a moved port does not inherit authority by default. *First test:* Match visible effects of D and D′ while only D destroys a dormant route; vary whether E needs it and whether the probe arrives in time. Compare eager repair, fresh allocation and query-matched regeneration.

**NMR6. Swap edit summaries while holding live service and discarded variation fixed.** Parts: L4-08 exchanged summary, L4-22 remove one rule, L4-23 randomized discarded differences, CE2-22 exercise failure rehearsal, D06 scoped certificate, SD2-09 separating suffix. *Mechanism:* Exchange or delete scoped summaries while keeping the shared serving adapter fixed. A valid intervention changes the corresponding future correction only, and the whole retained state must not encode randomized discarded answers in the exact fixture. *New:* The test separates effects caused by edit semantics from effects caused by hidden child storage or accidental behavioral ties. *First test:* Cross swapped and removed summaries with randomized discarded outputs, then score novel amendments. Include hidden restore as a positive control and same-output fresh learning as a negative control.

**NMR7. Price a repair over two reopenings, not just the first.** Parts: L4-26 price against staying branched, L4-27 novel second reopening, L3-25 opportunity cost, L3-27 neither choice, CE3-05 rare old effect, CE3-08 keep both futures, D11 reclaim capacity, LE5-22 independent next paths. *Mechanism:* Compare rejoin and continuing to branch over two declared revision cycles, charging probes, failed repairs, decoder state and idle rank. Keep distinct policies if the projected savings cannot justify rejoin under the same old-service floors. Require the second reopening to learn a newly authorized direction rather than replay a saved endpoint. *New:* The cost of maintaining the ability to correct may reverse an apparent first-cycle memory win. *First test:* Run two tiny delete–rewrite–reopen cycles with durable agreement controls and a second conflict outside the old options. Compare retained children, signed-spare repair, shared-plus-residual and exact refit.

**NMR8. Choose a first factor that can be completed by an ordinary lesson.** Parts: CE4-01 two factor families, CE4-02 retaining detour, CE4-03 next ordinary gradient, CE4-04 alternate writable layer, CE4-05 feasible subproblem, CE4-06 input dependence, CE4-07 unseen magnitudes, CE4-19 adaptation cost, L4-12 input-factor repair, L3-21 lost direction. *Mechanism:* Among rank-one writes with equal present behavior, minimize the cost of the worst lawful completion across the input- and output-factor families. Evaluate actual restricted gradients and retaining two-step paths, including a later layer freeze; export constraints for the next fit, not precomputed endpoint factors. *New:* The initial placement can buy useful future learning without a separate spare direction, and repair only needs to cover restrictions not already tolerated. *First test:* Enumerate a tiny quantized head with equal-behavior placements. Reveal a held-out magnitude after D and vary which layer freezes. Compare a signed spare, exact finite planning and revised-evidence regeneration at equal peak rank.

**NMR9. Keep one lawful observation prefix and its control on the ordinary path.** Parts: CE4-08 ordinary choice, CE4-09 informative outcome, CE4-10 per-reading access, CE4-11 adaptive prefix, CE4-12 reversible deadline, CE4-13 later actor, CE4-14 preserve control, L4-20 recheck authorization, CE3-17 exercise assumptions. *Mechanism:* Choose an initial deployment action from which an authorized actor can complete a contrast before an existing deadline in every declared possible world. Verify that the updated policy preserves the comparison's outcome interpretation, and leave a lawful resumption if the observation is interrupted. *New:* A common edit can preserve an option to collect binding evidence rather than merely reserving another gradient. *First test:* In a tiny task queue compare two same-quality writes that retain the same test state but differ in whether a control remains and a successor can reach it by the deadline. Compare a fixed test itinerary and an independently scheduled query.

**NMR10. Pay for an observation that supplies the corrective direction.** Parts: CE4-15 writable distinctions, CE4-16 outcome as training, CE4-17 alternate edit interface, CE4-18 no-op outcome, CE4-20 reusable outcome-to-constraint map, CE4-25 outcome gives direction, L4-18 lazy repair, CE3-25 durable observations. *Mechanism:* Evaluate each candidate observation by the retaining move it licenses and the gradient it makes available at the active interface. If it shows that present behavior already suffices, do not spend an update. Otherwise fit a fresh update from the observation; if D changed the writable layer, use the alternate paired contrast only if it still licenses the same effect. *New:* One ordinary observation can pay both for resolving scope and for identifying a repair, without caching either possible answer. *First test:* Use a two-layer toy in which one equally informative observation yields zero writable gradient and another reveals a new input/output contrast. Compare a query-then-refit stack and a best fixed signed basis, including a case requiring no further write.

**NMR11. Complete one correction without destroying the evidence needed for the next.** Parts: CE4-21 tolerate unrelated D, CE4-22 next pending experiment, CE4-23 exposure denominator, CE4-24 bounded corruption, CE4-26 alternate lesson, L4-16 keep the unchosen future, L4-27 novel second reopening, SD2-05 third edit. *Mechanism:* Choose among lawful completions by testing which preserves a matched comparison or a usable alternate for the next queued edit under allowed disturbances. Reject exposure-induced pseudo-agreement and use a separately generated witness when the corruption bound can otherwise change the selected correction. *New:* A two-edit sequence can be evaluated for keeping the next edit learnable, not just for retained outputs after the first edit. *First test:* Use two pending finite changes and an unrelated third that removes a diagnostic or biases exposure. Compare precise exposure accounting with pooled ratings and regeneration plus paid alternative probes; measure timely next correction and rare old service.

## Prior-art check: PR #350 — LoRA Learner 4

Extends the reviews in [round 84](cross-pool-84.md#prior-art-review-pr-344--a-lora-writing-learner), [round 85](cross-pool-85.md#prior-art-check-pr-346--lora-learner-2) and [round 86](cross-pool-86.md#prior-art-check-pr-348--lora-learner-3). [Text-to-LoRA (arXiv:2506.06105)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06105), §§3–5, generates module- and layer-conditioned LoRAs from descriptions, trained through adapter reconstruction or supervised fine-tuning. [Drag-and-Drop LLMs (arXiv:2506.16406)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16406), §§2.2–2.6 and 3, generates LoRAs from unlabeled prompt batches using a convolutional decoder trained to reconstruct paired checkpoints. Both generate adapters on held-out tasks; neither paper tests discarding serving branches while retaining only amendment semantics, then locally repairing a future correction after an intervening write. This is a comparison with the two papers, not a general novelty claim.

**Every row:** use a genuinely usable signed spare selected before D, and for repair compare a repicked basis or fresh allocation with matched probes and write budget. Also compare T2L and DnD regeneration with revised evidence, per-skill distillation and charged routing, including a cue-conditioned shared adapter. Report unrestricted and embargo-matched regeneration separately; bill all retained fields, peak bytes and rank, evidence, failed writes and serving costs. Under the embargo forbid recoverable old child state in every component; in exact toys check equality of the entire retained state across variation confined to discarded child behavior. Check rare and ordinary old service after rejoin, D, repair and E, along with D and the intact route. Count a split only on real repeated ratings of the same request and candidate pair both groups saw under randomized matched presentation; never credit disjoint exposure, and require feasible routing and a failed equally budgeted shared solution for serving splits.
