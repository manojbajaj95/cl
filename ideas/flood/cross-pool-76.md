**STR1. Buy a balanced basket that keeps the second teacher useful.** Parts: LE5-01 shared trunk, LE5-02 balanced basket, LE5-04 separated effects, LE4-07 shared circuit, LE3-04 public gradient. *Mechanism:* Choose an ordinary A basket that advances A while leaving a prescribed range of useful finite B updates. Define the range on held-out behavior, not weight overlap, and require the shared trunk to serve old reads. *New:* A common trunk might be cheaper than isolation without making its second teacher decorative. *First test:* Compare exact basket search, orthogonal gradients and matched joint replay on crossed useful and nuisance dimensions.

**STR2. Hold the second source's learning response fixed rather than its current score.** Parts: LE5-09 response matching, LE5-11 already-solved examples, LE5-16 localize the write, ER2-01 future training, BM4-26 separating observation. *Mechanism:* Measure the effect of a fixed ordinary B support step on separate B probes before and after a real A write. Find the smallest public witness that separates harmful and harmless A writes; use matched diagnostic copies only to locate the mechanism. *New:* A lesson can protect future accommodation even when no current B prediction moves. *First test:* Plant a feature change and an optimizer-only change with identical endpoint predictions; compare the native B step, an output-only control and exact finite tests.

**STR3. Pass the interruption using forward repair rather than an endpoint promise.** Parts: LE5-10 productive recovery, LE5-14 exposed prefixes, LE5-22 independent reopeners, LE4-20 interrupted handoff, LE3-02 prefix exits, RB11 actual fallback. *Mechanism:* For each exposed prefix require immediate B continuation or a bounded ordinary repair that preserves A and every due old read. Certify the repair against common shortages; when an A-only step is unavailable, test whether a separate lawful repair source can actually replace it. *New:* Temporary closure could be distinguished from closure that strands a real entrant. *First test:* Interrupt at every prefix of a benign two-feature curriculum. Compare an endpoint test and unconditional repair with a fixed jointly feasible exit policy.

**STR4. Let the first extension uncover a new teaching signal for the second.** Parts: LE5-06 substitute gradient, LE5-13 amplification, LE5-18 new residual, LE4-14 compositional reuse, ER31 interpreter sharing, TP20 decoder composition. *Mechanism:* Choose a precursor that makes the completed A factor disambiguate B's ordinary comparison labels. Keep the factors distinct and test a new target requiring both; do not count zero-shot transfer as a second useful write. *New:* Shared structure could make a second supplier more useful after the first one acts, without translating private labels. *First test:* Cross point and pairwise inputs in an unseen composition task; compare shared factors with separate heads and equal-budget replay.

**STR5. Keep a useful coordinate movable without retaining a complete duplicate.** Parts: LE5-12 gradient turnover, LE5-15 alternate carrier, LE5-21 unknown units, ER3-13 redundant carriers, ER3-14 availability time, ER2-20 protected distinction. *Mechanism:* Train two lawful carriers of one old feature and follow custody of the cue through A's native steps. After a carrier changes, test B's finite update over the declared scale range and ensure every old reader can still get its cue by its deadline. *New:* A shared feature could move while remaining teachable to a source whose optimizer and units stay fixed. *First test:* Swap redundant carriers in a tiny regressor and cross cue arrival time with hidden B scaling. Compare static masks, full duplication and adaptive carrier choice.

**STR6. Do not pay for two directions supported by the same irreplaceable example.** Parts: LE5-03 removal robustness, LE5-05 second directions, LE5-07 counterfeit cone, LE5-24 consumed directions, LE4-03 correlated shortage, DA07 joint uncertainty. *Mechanism:* Withhold precursor anchors in plausible correlated groups and execute two useful updates from each surviving source. Measure remaining continuation over matched successful A branches; ignore rank that only moves a nuisance branch. *New:* Teaching diversity may depend on independently supported sequences rather than independent-looking first gradients. *First test:* Plant two orthogonal first gradients sharing one indispensable example and a genuine independent control; compare rank, leave-one-out and exact joint sequence tests.

**STR7. Charge for a lost teaching option only after checking whether its examples vanished.** Parts: LE5-17 stream versus state, LE5-23 contingent debit, LE5-25 generic plasticity control, LE3-17 dependence debit, LE4-12 exported debt, BM4-03 outstanding loss. *Mechanism:* Fix a progress-matched reference before A acts. Compare the actual B stream and a matched resampling in each checkpoint, plus a different C task of equal difficulty. Debit verified incremental B cost rather than charging all deterioration to the seller. Keep old damage separate. *New:* A contract could target selective loss of trainability rather than poor supply or a broadly slower learner. *First test:* Cross checkpoint and sample relationship independently and plant generic slowing as a null; compare endpoint payouts and reference-based debit.

**STR8. Buy a third-party repair and require it to leave the original extension intact.** Parts: LE5-19 target-blind repair, LE5-22 independent reopeners, LE3-20 forward repair, LE2-13 return cost, VD24 adverse witness, RB12 joint certificate. *Mechanism:* Reserve small lawful repair menus from two uncorrelated sources. Test each on the actual post-A learner, counting one example once and carrying unresolved adverse receipts. Release payment only after a fresh B write succeeds and A and the old reads survive. *New:* Repair diversity can be purchased even when permanently open training routes are too expensive. *First test:* Compare full replay, exact constrained refit and one or two target-blind repair menus in a finite learner with a planted outage.

**STR9. Charge once for a public interface only if both local continuations can use it.** Parts: LE5-08 useful sharing, LE5-20 compact public interface, LE5-26 joint route test, ER32 typed rewrite, SK05 fixed shared key, OS19 executable order. *Mechanism:* Price the common bottleneck and the private adaptation separately. Require both native updates in one feasible order and hold trainable rank fixed; expose only the fixed common key and each site's lawful local observations. *New:* A combined lesson can make two routes cheaper by sharing the right representation, not simply by pooling both example budgets. *First test:* Compare separate purchased interfaces, a jointly planned shared bottleneck and the strongest exact finite scheduler at equal rank.

**STR10. Switch teachers and readers together only at a prefix both can reconstruct.** Parts: ER4-01 regenerating prefix, ER4-02 finishing-time distribution, ER4-13 cue deadline, ER4-21 joint interface, LE5-14 exposed prefixes, LE3-02 interruptible detour, VD24 adverse receipt. *Mechanism:* A live source switch may reuse the new representation only once the pending reader group can resume from lawful local observations on one interface generation. Before that point, price a bounded overlap with the old path, including the earliest read deadline and any unsettled receipts. If no joint transition can finish on time, keep serving old work and decline the new route. *New:* The availability of a cheaper teaching route would depend on service continuity during the transition, rather than on an endpoint reconstruction. *First test:* In a toy grammar with two readers, vary overlap length and send a source outage just before or after the earliest regenerating prefix. Compare dual serving, draining, and an exact joint switch policy.

**STR11. Retain ordinary progress when a candidate teaching interface is abandoned.** Parts: ER4-03 first closed-loop disagreement, ER4-06 useful abort, ER4-07 counterexample allocation, LE5-10 forward recovery, LE5-19 target-blind repair, BM4-03 open loss. *Mechanism:* Stage a shared event-level update lane that remains legal on both old and proposed paths, alongside candidate-only learning. At the first closed-loop disagreement test a scoped separating cue on independent episodes. If the candidate still cannot serve the reader, discard its edits, retain the independently valid work, and keep all observed old losses on the books. *New:* A failed rewrite need not erase useful training or force a replacement source to inherit an invalid representation. *First test:* Cross retained old work with candidate divergence in a finite curriculum. Compare full rollback, unconditional adoption and the shared lane plus held-out commit test.

**STR12. Reuse a cue for a second teaching route only if its meaning survives a producer change.** Parts: ER4-10 causal binding, ER4-11 cell refinement, ER4-15 stable cue meaning, ER4-20 successor learnability, ER4-22 scoped admission, ER4-26 dependency erase test, LE5-20 compact public interface, ER31 interpreter sharing, BM4-26 separating observation. *Mechanism:* Use lawful matched interventions to distinguish a reusable cue from correlated decoys, then admit a new reader only on the confirmed domain with a paid local refinement elsewhere. Replace the old producer during overlap and verify downstream decision effects, successor learning and removal of any unnecessary historical dependency. *New:* A learner could buy a shared cue rather than renting permanent access to an old teacher or duplicate reader. *First test:* Hold out cue inversions, a new supplier and a new decision family in a toy grammar. Compare an equal-bit private adapter, byte recomputation and scoped shared-cue reuse.

**STR13. Remove the first teacher during a scheduled interval and cross-run the replacement at regenerating prefixes. Keep only the history distinction that changes the safe next action; preserve unresolved receipts and old deadlines through the transfer. *New:* Offline rehearsal could teach which live state is portable rather than merely improving the replacement's endpoint skill. *First test:* Use a toy grammar with visibly identical histories and interrupt each at the same observed prefix. Compare full-episode replay, suffix-only replay and paired handover rehearsal against held-out live handoffs.

**STR14. Rehearse both rescue branches but let only a real receipt choose one.** Parts: SD05 shared hidden draw, SD16 repair loop, SD21 pending outcome, SD24 disjoint evidence, SD25 rescue continuation, ER4-06 useful abort, LE5-22 independent reopeners, BM4-03 unresolved loss. *Mechanism:* Generate paired repair trajectories under the same plausible disturbances, preserve legitimate A work in both, and train a bounded rescue continuation for each admissible outcome. Keep simulated trajectories out of the evidence that licenses a live repair. Bind the real pending receipt to the matching continuation; if it is missing, use only a backed action valid across all remaining possibilities. *New:* Rehearsal could reduce the real cost of reopening a teaching route without manufacturing evidence that it reopened. *First test:* Vary common disturbances, missing receipts and repair-source outages in a finite learner. Compare unconditional replay, exact online repair and conditional rehearsal at equal work.

**STR15.
