**LPR1. Pay for a substitute teacher only after its continuation survives old exceptions.** Parts: LE2-01 tested substitute cost, LE2-02 exception escrow, LE2-03 substitution gap, LE01 dependence buyout, ER25 explicit dependency, HW20 independent paths. *Mechanism:* Make the hidden supplier-specific input an explicit, lawful argument before the test. Draw an independent entrant from a sealed pool; release the price only if it reaches the new target within the advertised sample budget and held-out old exceptions survive both the original lesson and its continuation. *New:* The buyout purchases an observed drop in future dependence rather than delivery of a lesson or agreement on its current output. *First test:* Compare equal-cost replay, interface extraction alone and a contract with no exception escrow. Hold out entrant sources and change one binding after the buyout.

**LPR2. Do not price two histories as equal until the next legal lesson agrees.** Parts: LE2-10 retention price class, LE2-14 state and rights, LE2-15 continuation witness, PX10 causal policy equivalence, SK05 immutable shared key. *Mechanism:* Build a finite graph over behavioral classes and purpose-scoped rights. Use a common probe to separate aliases that differ on the same next lesson, including a write at another site; where the probe cannot certify all advertised replies, split the class or keep an unresolved edge. Never carry a right missing from the acquisition path. *New:* Path pricing can distinguish a cheap matching snapshot from a truly interchangeable continuation. *First test:* Compare set-based bundle caps, output-only classes and exact finite enumeration on two histories with equal current outputs but different permitted replies after the next write.

**LPR3. Quote an option on recovery instead of selling the entire repair path in advance.** Parts: LE2-13 cost of return, LE2-16 contingent path, PC18 branch-dependent roles, DA01 shared safe action, RB11 actual fallback. *Mechanism:* Offer a common useful prefix and quote a bounded right to buy recovery only after an ordinary report distinguishes branches that still need it. Back the return and the genuine forward alternative under the same deadlines. If the evidence does not change the safe decision, do not consume a scarce observation merely to settle an explanation. *New:* A contingent path can pay for preserving old service without forcing every buyer to stock the same recovery data. *First test:* Compare an all-upfront bundle and exact contingent planning under matched delivery and verification costs, with hidden no-show and benign arrival branches.

**LPR4. Pay for a retaining order without lending the seller all of the buyer's old data.** Parts: LE2-11 order refund, LE2-20 restricted old summary, OS19 feasible permutation, DA19 decision bracket, BM4-14 restricted probe reports. *Mechanism:* Give the seller a fixed lawful interface that returns a bounded decision bracket for candidate lesson orders, not raw old examples or reusable labels. Use the bracket only for the negotiation; confirm the winning order on independent held-out old cases, retaining unknown when the released summary is insufficient. *New:* A market could pay for the order in which training preserves skills without forcing buyers to surrender old-task access. *First test:* Compare a raw-data benchmark, a fixed order and the strongest equal-budget summary-based selector; add a rare old counterexample outside the summary's claimed coverage.

**LPR5. Buy an entrant route from every supported intermediate state.** Parts: LE2-21 buyout edge, LE2-22 retention-equivalent switch, LE2-24 reachability covenant, TP25 replace source, CX26 fresh implementation, ER32 typed rewrite. *Mechanism:* At each reached acquisition state check for a bounded entrant-only continuation that preserves original old service and lawful rights. Price interface migrations as explicit edges and verify the new caller's behavior without the incumbent's trace. Voiding the quote does not release the learner from old commitments. *New:* A seller could be rewarded for teaching a convention that remains escapable throughout training, not just at a convenient final checkpoint. *First test:* Contrast static anti-exclusivity terms and a final-state substitution test with exact prefix reachability on a toy market where a cheap intermediate lesson creates lock-in.

**LPR6. Treat plasticity spent today as a measurable option cost rather than a generic damage fee.** Parts: LE2-12 retention bracket price, LE2-23 spent plasticity, LE2-17 learner-specific bound, BM3-01 exposure debit, WS04 residual weight edit. *Mechanism:* Measure what useful future lessons remain executable after the candidate and a cost-matched non-weight alternative whose old retention floor is the same. Charge the incumbent only for the verified loss of reachable future work and separately log actual old-read damage already served; do not infer either from endpoint fitting. *New:* An offer can become more expensive because it reduces the learner's future ability to adapt, independently of its immediate accuracy. *First test:* Use sequential benign reasoning tasks with equal current success and different unseen future lesson cost. Compare a static regularizer and exact finite continuation accounting.

**LPR7. Buy an adversarial acceptance test without buying a veto over the whole learner.** Parts: LE2-26 rival testing, LW19 independent referee, VD11 scope by contrast, EL21 claim-specific renewal, BM3-22 separate observation right. *Mechanism:* Pay a second supplier for verified counterexamples only inside a sealed task and old-exception contract. Independence must come from separately produced outcomes, not price or supplier name. A valid counterexample can block the scoped payout or trigger refit; neither a report nor a lack of reports licenses arbitrary new writes. *New:* A market could buy active falsification while limiting the incentive to manufacture failures or settle unsupported scopes. *First test:* Compare a random holdout, an unrestricted bounty and a sealed scoped audit in synthetic worlds with copied claims and missing adverse receipts.

**LPR8. Settle a supplier exit by keeping the rights and liabilities that actually survive.** Parts: LE2-25 priced exit, LE2-06 rule half-life, LE31 scoped default release, VD24 adverse-route transfer, BM4-03 unsettled loss, LW09 exposed descendants. *Mechanism:* Track which permissions and prior learning depend on an exiting source. Distinguish lawfully retained service from replay rights that end, and test whether downstream exposed lessons need refitting. Carry observed old loss and missing adverse outcomes to the successor until settled; refund only unused support. *New:* Exit can be priced against the real recovery work rather than assuming removing the supplier reverses what was already learned. *First test:* Compare parameter subtraction, full replay and scoped continuation on a finite stream where one downstream skill depends on the exited lesson and another does not.

**LPR9. Let two teaching units share startup cost only when a new buyer can compose them.** Parts: LE2-19 granularity choice, LE2-07 downstream royalty, LE2-08 self-continuation, ER31 shared interpreters, TP20 decoder composition, RB12 joint certificate. *Mechanism:* Sell a reusable decoder and two scoped units with an option to compose them. Hide the original lesson payloads during an entrant's composition test and charge shared startup once. Pay downstream royalties only for actual authorized reuse while preserving the independent rights and tests specific to each unit. *New:* The best unit of sale could emerge from what future buyers can cheaply compose rather than the seller's segmentation of its dataset. *First test:* Match setup, lesson bytes and old-service spend across monolithic teaching and independent units, then test an unseen caller with only lawful local evidence.

**LPR10. A teaching market that buys a cheaper next lesson without selling the old learner.** Parts: LE2-01 substitute sample cost, LE2-02 exception escrow, LE2-03 substitution gap, LE2-04 portability of convention, LE2-05 durable commitment, LE2-06 rule half-life, LE2-07 downstream royalty, LE2-08 self-continuation, LE2-10 retention price class, LE2-11 order refund, LE2-12 edge bracket, LE2-13 cost of return, LE2-14 state and rights, LE2-15 continuation probe, LE2-16 contingent path, LE2-17 teaching dimension, LE2-19 priced granularity, LE2-20 restricted summary, LE2-21 buyout edge, LE2-22 retaining switch, LE2-23 spent plasticity, LE2-24 reachability covenant, LE2-25 exit cost, LE2-26 rival audit, LE2-27 planner control, LE01 portability buyout, LE31 scoped default release, ER25 explicit dependency, ER31 shared interpreters, ER32 typed rewrite, HW20 independent paths, PX10 policy equivalence, SK05 fixed key, PC18 branch roles, DA01 same safe action, DA19 decision bracket, RB11 actual fallback, RB12 joint certificate, OS19 executable ordering, BM4-03 outstanding loss, BM4-14 restricted report, TP20 decoder composition, TP25 public replacement, CX26 independent implementation, BM3-01 exposure debit, BM3-22 separate observation and write, WS04 residual weight edit, LW09 downstream exposures, LW19 independent referee, VD11 scoped verdict, VD24 adverse-route transfer, EL21 clause-specific renewal. *Mechanism:* Price lawful paths by the old and new work their reached learner states can still do. Contract for lower sample cost with independent substitute suppliers, preserve exceptional old behavior and future rights throughout training, and charge actual diagnosis and restoration. Test unsupported exits and confirm genuine new reuse before paying a durability or portability premium. *New:* A learner could improve its bargaining power by learning public, executable continuations rather than obtaining cheap access to more supplier-specific data. *First test:* In benign finite learning markets, withhold entrants, old exceptions, rights and future tasks. Compare exact planning and the strongest matched stack of lawfully sourced replay, bundle prices, scoped verification and independent fallback. Track held-out retention, substitute training cost and real continuation reuse.
