## Merge proposals

**CWX1. Teach a formalizer to state what its proof actually settles.** Parts: CVW-01 all optima, CVW-02 explicit negative quantifier, CVW-03 joint completion, CVW-04 specification strength, CVW-05 blind trace reconciliation, CVW-06 private evidence boundary, CVW-09 sufficient reasons, CV08 evidence receipt, CV19 proved invariance, AL4-02 ambiguity witness. *Mechanism:* Bind each checked claim to a declared domain, premise slice and observable trace. Independently encoded subclaims may be combined only with a shared completion. When two compatible completions disagree, return a checked pair or ask for the missing premise. A private case contributes only its checked public predicate over the commitment and permitted metadata, not an inferred trace or a broader theorem. *New:* A verifier can learn to choose the strongest supported statement even when independent encodings agree on an answer, a valid theorem admits bad behavior, or choosing one optimum hides alternatives. *First test:* In toy packing, plant tied optima, incompatible subclaim completions and one weak specification. Compare independent formalization, proof-only review and premise receipts at matched checker calls. Measure overclaims, justified coverage and transfer to a new layout with the same authorized observables.

**CWX2. Keep process credit stable when the same work is split into different steps.** Parts: CVW-07 credit under refinement, CVW-08 prefix commitment, CVW-11 equal-size edit contrast, CVW-12 local versus propagated error, CVW-13 alternate optimization proxies, NP4-19 equivalence under declared operations. *Mechanism:* Bind refinements to the same underlying execution and require their checked credits to compose. Keep a prefix judgment frozen before revealing the ending. After reveal, distinguish a propagated error from a fresh local mistake and use paired repairing and irrelevant edits to test the attribution. Vary the training proxy without changing the terminal verifier. *New:* The same execution should receive stable total credit across trace layouts without throwing away the intermediate evidence that identifies a violation. *First test:* In a toy sorter, split or combine moves and plant a hidden intermediate violation. Compare endpoint grading, a common potential with an explicit violation flag, and refinement-constrained grading at equal labels. Measure credit agreement on held-out decompositions, localized error detection and live old-task rankings.

**CWX3. Measure the errors created by choosing candidates and by changing who submits them.** Parts: CVW-10 selected-answer risk, CVW-14 cross-lineage failures, CVW-16 preregistered edit forecasts, CVW-17 suppressed requests, CVW-18 parser-only adaptation, CVW-19 overlap bounds, CVW-20 pending verdicts, CVW-21 per-population guarantees, CVW-22 shadow independence, CV13 random coverage audit, CV28 policy-induced audit, CV32 protected audit budget. *Mechanism:* Separate internal candidate selection from public request exposure in a factorial audit. Calibrate the winner under the whole search and stopping procedure, and use a randomized nonbinding exposure arm to measure disappeared requests. Use shadow and cross-lineage cases to check whether errors correlate; report covered groups, unsupported mass and pending cases separately. Use paired renderings to adapt only the parser and settle edit forecasts independently of release decisions. *New:* A release can improve apparent accuracy by changing which questions arrive or by hiding winner errors in marginal calibration. The audit lets a later reviewer tell those effects apart and compare group guarantees. *First test:* In a synthetic grading stream vary candidate count, proposal correlation, request visibility and label delay independently. Compare selection-aware calibration plus randomized exposure, frozen replay and the joint audit at equal independent labels. Report selected-answer error, request mix, old/new coverage, and unsupported or pending mass.

**CWX4. Pay for independently checked coverage, not for winning an argument.** Parts: CVW-15 two-sided challenge, CVW-23 checked failure-family bounty, CVW-24 settled forecast after abstention, CVW-25 standalone objection, CVW-26 independently checked neither, CVW-27 supplier cost quotes, CV29 evidence lineage, PL5-10 held-out effect family. *Mechanism:* Accept challenges as reusable generators with checked eligibility rather than as collections of paraphrases. Sample each family independently, track shared evidence to avoid paying twice, and test objections against held-out defenders. Settle a random subset of sealed forecasts even after abstention. Use quotes to buy checking capacity, not as evidence for a verdict; independently score valid defenses and checked double rejections. *New:* A buyer can learn which checks discover genuinely different errors and which objections travel across defenders without confusing payout, persuasion or agreement with truth. *First test:* In an arithmetic toy market, give some suppliers many cheap paraphrases and others an unseen failure family. Compare per-item payments with strong deduplication, family bounties and round-robin cross-play at equal payouts and checker calls. Track verified incremental coverage, valid outputs wrongly rejected, old rare faults and realized checking cost.

**CWX5. Check what a partial history settles before assigning success or credit.** Parts: CVW-28 evaluation stopping event, CVW-29 counterfactual contribution, CVW-30 recovery versus fulfillment, CVW-31 legal-history verdict, CVW-32 observable consequences, CV10 separating observation, AL4-10 smallest rescue view. *Mechanism:* Use authoritative completion events to decide when to evaluate. For a bounded partial order, either certify equal scores across all legal histories or return two checked histories with opposite scores and the smallest allowed observation that distinguishes them. Score fulfillment and restored invariants separately. Attribute completion to an agent only where a matched simulator intervention supports the contribution claim. *New:* A verifier can avoid guessing a timeline, giving credit to the wrong agent, or calling compensation fulfillment, while still learning which missing observation is worth buying. *First test:* Use concurrent toy reservation and release events with masked ordering, exogenous completions and a recovery action. Compare timestamp replay, bounded model checking and reusable history witnesses at matched calls. Test on an unseen score predicate and measure justified coverage, attribution error and detection of downstream public effects.

**CWX6. Keep a separate record of what was proved, what was selected, and what was never observed.** Parts: CVW-01 all optima, CVW-02 explicit negative quantifier, CVW-03 joint completion, CVW-04 specification strength, CVW-05 blind trace reconciliation, CVW-06 private evidence boundary, CVW-09 sufficient reasons, CV08 evidence receipt, CV19 proved invariance, AL4-02 ambiguity witness, CVW-07 credit under refinement, CVW-08 prefix commitment, CVW-11 equal-size edit contrast, CVW-12 local versus propagated error, CVW-13 alternate optimization proxies, NP4-19 equivalence under declared operations, CVW-10 selected-answer risk, CVW-14 cross-lineage failures, CVW-16 preregistered edit forecasts, CVW-17 suppressed requests, CVW-18 parser-only adaptation, CVW-19 overlap bounds, CVW-20 pending verdicts, CVW-21 per-population guarantees, CVW-22 shadow independence, CV13 random coverage audit, CV28 policy-induced audit, CV32 protected audit budget, CVW-15 two-sided challenge, CVW-23 checked failure-family bounty, CVW-24 settled forecast after abstention, CVW-25 standalone objection, CVW-26 independently checked neither, CVW-27 supplier cost quotes, CV29 evidence lineage, PL5-10 held-out effect family, CVW-28 evaluation stopping event, CVW-29 counterfactual contribution, CVW-30 recovery versus fulfillment, CVW-31 legal-history verdict, CVW-32 observable consequences, CV10 separating observation, AL4-10 smallest rescue view. *Mechanism:* Give each verdict a scoped statement and evidence receipt, plus the selection, exposure and observation conditions needed to interpret it. Keep certified execution refinements separate from policy scores and audit selection. Buy independent checks for missing premises, new error families and ambiguous histories; leave unsupported cases unknown. *New:* The verifier can learn where to request its next check without treating search success, a sparse submission stream or a persuasive objection as proof. *First test:* Run a proposed synthetic packing-and-reservation stream with competing optima, split traces, a changed submission policy and partially ordered events. Compare the combined record and its components with matched proof search, calibration, random audits and bounded checking. Report justified conclusions, false accepts and rejects, abstentions, independently scored outcomes and old-task retention.
