# Cross-pool merges, round 28

Focal sources: `tree/fact-skill-entanglement.md` (PR #140, FE#) and `tree/delayed-release-edits.md` (PR #141, DR#). Crosses with fact-free inheritance (PR #137, FF#), fossil beliefs (PR #132, FB#), Z1 write escrow, Y4 kinetic proofreading, Z7 bivalent poised edits, replay deadlines (PR #113, RP#), survival memory (PR #112, SP#), merger certificates (PR #110, MC#), conservative extension (PR #122, CE#), recovery witnesses (PR #139, RW#), and earlier rounds 1–27. These are raw proposals. Score direct binding, exact stale derivatives on changed-answer queries, procedure use with independently supplied premises, and untouched tasks separately. A successful binding swap is not evidence that every consumer reads it, and a slot write that leaves launch outputs unchanged can still change the next update. Charge escrow, probe and controller costs; give external release schedules the same permitted signals as the slot arms and calibrate exact heavy-ball equivalence.

**FER1. Make contrasts, not confirmations, unlock a write.** Parts: FE1 replace–remove–supply, FE12 visible contrasts, FE13 crossed evidence, DR3 event-keyed release, DR6 progress clock, Z1 write escrow, Y4 kinetic proofreading. *Mechanism:* Give an approved correction separate receipts for a moved binding, an answer-changing counterfactual, and correct procedure use at a supplied value. Release only when distinct checks pass on a shadow fork; repeated unanimous tests on cases where both bindings give the same answer buy no credit. A deployment gate uses a public readiness signal calibrated on separate worlds, not sealed retention scores. *New:* Evidence that the correction is true and evidence that the skill is ready to consume it unlock different stages. *First test:* Hold example counts constant while varying genuinely answer-changing contrasts and correlated repeats in a route ranker. Compare count-gated escrow and an equally keyed external delta at matched new-task progress.

**FER2. Poise a correction against an explicit unknown, not against the old answer.** Parts: FE3 redundant readers, FE10 observational equivalence, FE18 readiness, DR2 temporary pulses, DR4 prerequisite stages, FF7 typed unknown, Z7 bivalent poised edits, FB#. *Mechanism:* For closed-book calls, release a temporary block on obsolete defaults while an independently revocable positive write waits for evidence that the reader follows the new binding. Supplied-premise calls keep the procedure available. If the second stage expires, keep the unknown path rather than allowing a slow opposite pulse to reinstate the expired value. *New:* The two poised outcomes can be a verified correction and a durable abstention, not a toggle between two assertions. *First test:* Use a skill with two redundant read routes and one stale cache. Compare a paired-slot implementation against an explicit unknown router plus timed deltas under delayed, conflicting and withdrawn confirmations.

**FER3. Decompile a stale branch while its successor is still in escrow.** Parts: FE2 stale-only cuts, FE6 shared writer, FE8 dose response, DR4 staged prerequisites, DR5 paired moment brake, DR12 release subspace, FF4 decompile before forgetting, MFH3 separate clocks. *Mechanism:* On a shadow, first seek a stage that makes held-out queries follow independently supplied premises without changing direct fact recall. Only then release the binding correction and check that the repaired branch follows it closed-book. Temporarily inhibit writes that threaten unrelated procedures; remove the brake once the branch passes continuation tests. *New:* A correction can change how an answer is computed before changing which answer should be returned. *First test:* Compare cache-first, binding-first and simultaneous edits on a mixed-route MLP, with matched direct-recall change and total steps. Give an external controller the same readiness and preconditioner signals.

**FER4. Give a pending edit separate deadlines for truth, readability and release.** Parts: FE19 two-hop propagation, FE22 buy a contrast or repair, DR1 deadline edit, DR7 multiple futures, DR13 restart, RP#, SP#, SV1 three clocks, RE2 disagreement escrow. *Mechanism:* Keep a public expiry rule, a short-lived receipt sufficient to validate changed-answer behavior, and a latest safe commit point on separate clocks. If a predicted shock will make the receipt unreadable before the shadow can certify the release, buy one discriminating test now; if authorization expires first, cancel instead of rushing. Count receipt bytes and time out of service. *New:* Pending edits can miss their window even when both the binding and the skill remain individually recoverable. *First test:* Randomize evidence latency, a decoder shock and new-training speed independently. Compare fixed-time release, always-retain replay and a deadline-aware controller at the same storage and validation budget.

**FER5. Supersede a half-written fact without refunding the part already learned.** Parts: FE9 provenance, FE19 selective propagation, FE21 operator regret, DR8 in-flight supersession, DR19 source revocation, DR22 correction plus deletion, MC#, FI2 sunset clauses. *Mechanism:* When a correction is withdrawn mid-flight, distinguish uninstalling its remaining slot contribution from replacing effects already delivered to direct and composed queries. Use provenance to propose cancellation, then measure the remaining response on shadow continuations before writing only the residual; do not assume source tags add linearly after passing through Adam. *New:* Revocation can have separate obligations for already-visible consequences, pending writes, and unrelated promises that share their carrier. *First test:* Supersede one of two corrections at different release fractions; compare tagged slot cancellation, external pending-delta cancellation and scratch replay on exact stale derivatives and untouched tasks.

**FER6. Stage a two-hop correction as a conservative extension at every cut.** Parts: FE4 cross-consumer transport, FE19 two-hop chains, DR4 dependent release, DR9 conflict tests, DR20 continuation bounds, CE#, CT1 static and training tests, FCR10 repair order. *Mechanism:* Declare which descendants must change, which callers must not change, and which calls may temporarily return unknown. Exercise each stage against that contract, including between-stage restarts and crossed release orders. Defer a downstream release when an upstream stage has not reached the declared propagation boundary. *New:* A multi-hop correction can have a valid intermediate interface without requiring every consumer to update atomically. *First test:* Use two rules sharing a bridge fact but only one changed policy binding. Compare staged slot release, equally gated external writes and an all-at-once update; report worst intermediate and post-resume violations.

**FER7. Turn an entanglement map into a calendar of cheap and expensive writes.** Parts: FE5 collateral floor, FE6 shared writer, FE15 matched-multiset hysteresis, DR5 correction brake, DR6 progress clocks, DR18 denominator cleanup, Y1 hysteresis, MFH8 leased protection. *Mechanism:* Estimate the damage needed to reach the same direct edit at several public training phases. Cross post-edit weights with retain-only and donor moments to ask whether an expensive phase reflects a genuinely coupled reader or only the currently active writer. Schedule the change in a cheaper window if it satisfies the deadline; otherwise buy explicit protection. *New:* A fact and procedure can be hard to separate on one training route and cheap to separate on another, even at matched serving accuracy. *First test:* Reorder identical batches, compare cost maps against static salience and margin-only timing, and test whether matched-step retain-only preconditioning closes the apparent gap.

**FER8. Hand a successor an unrealized correction with holes instead of inherited momentum.** Parts: FE4 transport across consumers, FE20 cross-generation map, DR11 portable pending writes, DR13 restart, FF17 programs with fact holes, FB#, MFH5 portable repair, CT10 host-chosen preservation. *Mechanism:* Export a response envelope for which consumers should follow a supplied binding, which must stay invariant, and what should happen on an unresolved value. Let the successor fill the binding from its own authorized source and choose slots, a route or a scheduled delta on its own feature basis; transmit neither the retired value nor its donor slots. *New:* An inherited edit can specify how a future binding should take effect without specifying what that binding is. *First test:* Move between widened and permuted hosts trained in opposing worlds. Compare the envelope against raw-slot transport where coordinates allow it, immediate re-editing and a same-budget external delta scheduler; test stale-answer resurrection after a restart.

**FER9. Search for the shortest public future that wakes the wrong thing.** Parts: FE7 counterfactual-isomorphic resurrection, FE17 directional thresholds, DR14 crossed deletion state, DR15 relapse loop, DR20 deletion certificates, RW5 bounded obstruction, RW8 false revival, FCR7 slot portability. *Mechanism:* Start from matched corrected cuts with opposite-exposure and never-exposed histories. Apply the same declared menu of public continuations to crossed weights and slots, including a slot-only swap at identical post-correction weights. Score whether each recipe wakes the desired procedure on fresh supplied premises, revives a retired direct answer, both, or neither. Use offline findings to restrict allowed release paths; a deployment selector cannot use sealed scores. *New:* A recovery witness and a counterexample to deletion can be the same program, so a repair menu should certify what else each path wakes. *First test:* Compare paired-moment cleanup with weight-only deletion and an equally selective external controller. Run first-update, linearized and dwell-time controls, then search the bounded menu on held-out public training routes.

**FER10. Reserve a release window for the consumer, not a slot for the fact.** Parts: FE8 dose response, FE14 head concentration, FE16 competing costs, DR9 release conflicts, DR10 multiplexing, DR12 safe subspace, MC#, OS9 bundled refreshes, Z1 escrow. *Mechanism:* Group pending writes by the consumers their staged effects might disturb, including when different facts share no obvious storage. Admit overlapping releases only if observed joint effects stay inside the service contract; otherwise reorder, combine or hold them as a bundle. Recheck after a public prerequisite changes the active reader. *New:* A queue that budgets downstream service may prevent collisions between edits that look independent in parameter space. *First test:* Use synthetic two-hop rules with shared and disjoint readers. Compare per-fact queueing and norm-based slot allocation with consumer-based scheduling, holding admitted workload, bytes and public probes fixed.

**FER11. An escrow ledger that pays out a fact change only when the right skills can spend it.** Parts: FE1 supply controls, FE2 stale-only cuts, FE3 redundant readers, FE5 collateral floor, FE6 shared writer, FE7 resurrection, FE8 dose response, FE9 provenance, FE12 visible contrasts, FE13 crossed evidence, FE15 history dependence, FE18 readiness, FE19 two-hop propagation, FE20 transfer, FE21 operator regret, FE22 contrast pricing, DR1 deadlines, DR3 keyed release, DR4 stages, DR5 brakes, DR6 progress clocks, DR7 multiple futures, DR8 supersession, DR9 conflicts, DR10 multiplexing, DR11 portability, DR12 safe subspaces, DR13 restart, DR14 crossed moments, DR15 relapse, DR18 denominator history, DR19 revocation, DR20 bounded certificates, DR22 composable deletion, FF#, FB#, Z1, Y4, Z7, RP#, SP#, MC#, CE#, RW#, SV1, RE2, MFH5, FCR9. *Mechanism:* For each pending edit, map the consumers that should follow a new binding, the stale derivatives that must stop, and the procedures that must survive independently of the binding. Put the correction and any temporary unknown fallback in separate revocable stages. Buy distinguishing evidence before it decays, release by authorized evidence and public readiness, and keep a receipt for already-delivered and still-pending effects. On supersession, merge or transfer, recheck the permissible training paths and reject paths that wake obsolete facts even when they restore useful skills. *New:* An edit becomes a staged obligation on a changing learner, with separate promises for facts, procedures, and future training, rather than a delta that counts as paid once it passes a checkpoint. *First test:* Use an enumerable route world with changing tolls, a shared restriction procedure, two overlapping corrections, delayed evidence and a restart. Compare immediate surgery, external escrow with identical signals and same-budget replay. Score changed bindings, stale derivatives, supplied-premise generalization, worst intermediate service, future relapse and new-task progress separately.
