# Cross-pool merges, round 32

Focal sources: `tree/learned-invalidation.md` (PR #148, LI#) and `tree/minimal-wake-anchors.md` (PR #149, WA#). Crosses with wrong-wake fixed points (PR #146, WF#), bounded burial (PR #142, BB#), rented constraints (PR #145, RC#), fact–skill entanglement (PR #140, FE#), fossil beliefs (PR #132, FB#), fact-free inheritance (PR #137, FF#), delayed-release edits (PR #141, DR#), X5 virtual-memory paging, X4 Popperian retirement, Y14 sheaf-consistent stores, and earlier rounds 1–31. These are raw proposals. Treat a missing graph edge as unknown unless independently checked. Separate direct recall, exact stale derivatives, and execution on supplied premises; a converged wake can still be wrong. Distinguish a frozen unanchored wake from one that receives target-bearing side information. Charge the latter for every bit, address, query, restart and fallback, and compare against equal-budget replay and explicit caches. Report bounded misses separately from full-state loss.

**LWA1. Renew an anchor when a consumer actually crosses its boundary.** Parts: LI6 per-edge slack, LI7 event-triggered edits, LI9 cumulative drift, WA3 drift loans, WA6 change-frontier anchors, WA17 live premises, RD4 early refresh, DR6 progress clocks. *Mechanism:* Track the current fact and each estimated consumer's remaining slack separately. On a cumulative crossing, revalidate that consumer's cached answer and reissue its anchor only if the old anchor no longer distinguishes the remaining candidate wakes. Use sampled non-crossing and unknown-edge canaries to catch cases the slack model misses. *New:* Cache validity and anchor validity need not expire together; silence on the serving surface can hide a shrinking reserve on either one. *First test:* Feed incremental toll updates with reversals into a threshold learner; compare periodic anchor refresh, entity-wide invalidation and latest-value recomputation at matched bytes and probes. Check direct recall on every intermediate step.

**LWA2. Treat a delayed write as pending until its invalidations and anchors agree.** Parts: LI12 version vectors, LI13 predicted blast radius, WA15 epoch-keyed wake, WA18 revision parity, DR8 supersession, DR13 restarts, FER5 residual corrections, ECR7 closure escrow. *Mechanism:* On a shadow, stage the revised binding, cache invalidations, and new wake anchors under one version. Promote them together for affected callers only after the direct write and sampled downstream checks pass. A restart at an ambiguous cut must recompute or return unknown rather than treat an old anchor as current. Cancel undelivered slot effects separately from changes already visible. *New:* A correction can fail even if both its new answer and its new anchor are valid, when each becomes visible at the wrong point in training. *First test:* Interrupt and supersede a partially released edit at several doses in a two-hop rule learner. Compare independent updates, an explicit transactional store, and scheduled external deltas using the same signals.

**LWA3. Stitch local wake anchors together without spending one bit twice.** Parts: LI4 read receipts, LI11 edge edits, LI18 graph completeness, WA7 symmetry generators, WA19 co-resident parity, Y14 sheaf stores, BB17 one-shot recovery, BCE3 agreement on overlaps. *Mechanism:* Record which local alternatives each anchor distinguishes and identify shared consumers before combining the anchors. For binary toy worlds, check whether the joint parity constraints actually leave just one admissible completion: one parity bit cannot resolve several independent flips. For nonbinary worlds, enumerate viable joint completions on a small cover. *New:* Anchors can agree on pairwise overlaps yet leave a global ambiguity, or contradict one another because their receipts refer to different versions. *First test:* Build three overlapping skill regions with planted shared and independent symmetries, then test unseen covers. Compare per-skill labels, a shared sketch and fallback to revalidation at equal bits and probe cost.

**LWA4. Spend the next audit on a missing edge that could make the anchor lie.** Parts: LI17 active crossings, LI18 completeness, LI23 deceptive independence, WA9 active disagreement query, WA14 paired-world audit, WF5 independent wakes, FE3 redundant reader, Q9 tomography. *Mechanism:* On audit forks, select a verified rebind that would separate two plausible dependency graphs and two candidate wake completions. Sample outside the learned graph and keep a fixed random-audit allocation so the learned graph does not choose all its own tests. A newly found edge invalidates any lease proof that depended on its absence. *New:* A probe can buy both an invalidation correction and a cheaper way to distinguish an unsafe wake from a safe one. *First test:* Hide a dependency behind a rare exception and a redundant route. Compare graph-scoped, random and disagreement-directed audits using fresh calls and opposite private obligations; track false graph confidence as well as wake misses.

**LWA5. Retire a stale route with a counterexample, not a ban on its answer.** Parts: LI14 unlearning as invalidation, LI16 forget an edge, WA6 change-frontier label, WA11 retired-version filter, X4 Popperian retirement, FB# fossil beliefs, FF# inheritance, FI5 historical isolation. *Mechanism:* Attach a counterexample to the obsolete binding–consumer relation: under current premises the old response is invalid on a separating request, although it may still be correct for explicit history or unrelated consumers. A scoped anti-anchor can veto a retired completion, but a veto is not evidence that a remaining completion is right. Abstain or revalidate if all candidates are excluded. *New:* Retirement can remove the authority of a dependency without erasing the answer token, its legitimate uses, or the procedure that once derived it. *First test:* Revise time-indexed tolls while holding unrelated matching answers fixed. Compare a negative filter, a positive anchor and an explicit versioned store, logging false vetoes and revived stale derivatives.

**LWA6. Page in the fact, the procedure or the anchor depending on what missed.** Parts: LI5 dependency signatures, LI15 unregistered dependencies, LI21 two-level caching, WA3 input versus state anchors, WA21 payload death, X5 virtual-memory paging, BB# bounded burial, RC# rented constraints. *Mechanism:* A cache miss can mean the current fact is absent, a competent procedure is dormant, or a wake has several plausible endpoints. Route those separately to authoritative lookup, a bounded public wake, or minimal side information. Use public signals and a fixed fallback when the diagnosis is inconclusive; distinguish restored capability from answers retrieved out of a store. *New:* A pager for learned behavior can fault on ambiguity even when all its facts and weights are resident. *First test:* Mix independent fact evictions, routing drift and stale fixed points in a synthetic serving trace. Compare LRU, margin-only paging and typed faults at matched storage, post-wake B progress and request latency.

**LWA7. Read the same anchor as a steering instruction or a receipt, depending on the blast radius.** Parts: LI8 oscillating facts, LI13 precommit impact, WA2 steering versus verification, WA5 bifurcation labels, WF2 path alarms, RC16 shadow-fork grants, FER6 staged contracts. *Mechanism:* If independent checks bound the affected consumers, try injecting the anchor at a wake bifurcation on a shadow. If impact is unresolved, leave live state unchanged and use the same bits to verify candidates after bounded restarts. For both routes, fall back if no completion passes the current-premise and untouched-task tests. *New:* The value of a steering bit depends on which consumer obligations a wrong turn can disturb, not just its power to select a basin. *First test:* Cross narrow and overlapping dependency regimes with steering and verification at equal bits and backward passes. Include an idempotent wrong wake and measure B and untouched C after the next shared updates.

**LWA8. Let a successor inherit the invalidation grammar, not the evidence that points to the ancestor's answer.** Parts: LI11 change an edge, LI16 remove a dependency, LI19 schema transfer, WA8 deterministic public probes, WA12 payload sketches, FE20 cross-generation maps, FF17 programs with holes, MFH9 alien-world handoff. *Mechanism:* Export which operations can create, delete or redirect dependency edges and how to choose a separating probe. Strip old binding values, answer sketches and donor slots. Have the successor instantiate new anchors only from its own valid premises or a separately authorized current source, then challenge the imported grammar on independent rebinding interventions. *New:* A handoff can preserve how to invalidate a derived answer without preserving which obsolete answer should ever have filled its slot. *First test:* Transfer across opposite premise worlds and permuted feature bases; compare schema-only transfer, raw metadata transplant and cold start, charging new anchor acquisition and checking stale resurrection.

**LWA9. Anchor an intermediate dependency when the final answer is unchanged.** Parts: LI2 unlabeled history, LI10 learned propagation, LI23 hidden dependencies, WA4 attractor teaching dimension, WA10 signed sketches, WA17 current-premise anchors, FE19 two-hop chains, WED6 canceling paths. *Mechanism:* When two updates cancel at the final output, use a verified single-leg rebind on a fork to separate candidates with the same answer but different dependency paths. If needed, store a small receipt for that contrast rather than a checksum of the unchanged output. Keep clean probes on both legs so a cached intermediate with the wrong scope cannot masquerade as current. *New:* An anchor can preserve what must change on the next revision, even when nothing should change at the current endpoint. *First test:* Use equal-total route costs with one hidden shared leg and one independent leg. Compare endpoint sketches, intermediate anchors and an exact dependency store on unseen follow-up revisions at equal bytes.

**LWA10. Charge leases for the future ambiguity they introduce into the invalidation map.** Parts: LI3 predicted invalidation sets, LI20 cost model, LI22 reported reasoning versus causal use, WA1 bit curve, WA22 total-bit frontier, RC5 collateral, WF10 safe radius, BB21 measure net gain, ECR1 edit-bit charges. *Mechanism:* Offline, vary the declared lease radius and revision mix, and measure when plausible maps and wake completions begin to collide under public observations. Use the resulting cost curve to choose a narrower lease, buy active crossings, reserve anchors, or keep live service. Verbal reports of dependencies can propose a probe but cannot certify an edge. *New:* A lease can increase the work of knowing what needs invalidation even when it costs little to restore old outputs once. *First test:* Randomize lease release in matched route learners and hold out a revision regime. Compare scope-limited leases, unanchored wake, reserved side information and saved deltas against the same post-wake service and B floors.

**LWA11. A memory system that can invalidate a belief without waking it back up.** Parts: LI3 predicted invalidations, LI4 read receipts, LI5 dependency signatures, LI6 slack, LI7 threshold events, LI9 cumulative drift, LI10 propagation, LI11 edge edits, LI12 version vectors, LI13 precommit impact, LI14 deletion, LI15 unregistered edges, LI16 remove dependencies, LI17 active crossings, LI18 completeness, LI19 transfer, LI20 cost ledger, LI21 two-level caching, LI23 hidden dependencies, WA1 bit curves, WA2 steering versus verification, WA3 drift loans, WA5 bifurcation labels, WA6 change-frontier labels, WA7 symmetry generators, WA8 probe addresses, WA9 active labels, WA10 sketches, WA11 retired filters, WA12 state anchors, WA14 paired-world audits, WA15 epochs, WA17 current-premise stamps, WA18 revision parity, WA19 co-resident checks, WA21 payload loss, WA22 total bits, WF#, BB#, RC#, FE#, FB#, FF#, DR#, X5, X4, Y14, BCE3, ECR7, WED3. *Mechanism:* Maintain an explicitly incomplete dependency map with cumulative slack and scoped version receipts. When a fact changes, revalidate affected outputs and audit possible missing edges before updating the cache and its wake anchors. Page between authoritative retrieval, an unanchored wake, anchored recovery and abstention according to what is missing. Combine local anchor claims only after testing global ambiguity; release pending edits and retire old dependencies without making a historical answer a live default. *New:* A cached answer could carry a small record of what would invalidate it and what evidence would recover the current procedure, without carrying the ancestor's answer into every future release. *First test:* Use an enumerable route world with sub-threshold changes, an unseen dependency, a partial edit restart and a burst of callers. Compare an oracle-graph cache with charged metadata, entity-wide invalidation, always-recompute, equal-bit replay and saved deltas. Report missed edges, false evictions, wrong and stale wakes, anchor costs, downtime and new-task gain after wake separately.
