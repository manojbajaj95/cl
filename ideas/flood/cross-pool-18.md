# Cross-pool merges, round 18

Focal sources: `tree/generator-side-forgetting.md` (PR #120, GF#) and `tree/practice-order-interference.md` (PR #121, PO#). Crosses with round 17 (FCU#, PR #119 CU# and PR #118 FG#), order comparisons in Y2/Q1, and older pools on executable contracts, diagnostic forks, and population repair. Exposure, prompted reachability, valid fresh breadth, and independent solver accuracy are different measurements. At fixed practice counts, distinguish short-lived competence excursions from order effects that survive re-mixing on the deployment horizon. An allocation footprint is not by itself evidence of permanent hysteresis. These are raw and unfiltered.

**GP1. Use an old verifier to repair a grammar without teaching to its test.** Parts: GF1 inverse-witness synthesis, GF3 counterexample basis, FG4 diagnostic cofactor (PR #118), AH13 causal litmus tests (PR #47), RL3 verifier twins (PR #51), FCU9. *Mechanism:* Search for the smallest missing constructor consistent with several fixed executable witnesses and targeted negative cases. Test the repaired grammar on a different interpreter or held-out semantic classes; a verifier that accepts one construction cannot authorize all plausible ones. *New:* Repair recovers a way to generate fresh instances rather than just a cached passing problem or a shortcut around one checker. *First test:* Delete a production in a finite string grammar with two plausible explanations. Compare witness-guided repair against frozen prompts, example replay and an oracle knowing which production vanished.

**GP2. Separate curriculum starvation from loss of a generator's competence.** Parts: GF7 admission counterfactuals, GF8 prefix tomography, GF20 search currency, CU8 odometry (PR #119), B8 active intervention budgets (PR #48), BR7. *Mechanism:* At a fixed attempt budget, compare ordinary exposure, prompted reachability, forced-prefix continuation and raw admissions scored by independent checkers. Spend the next observation at the first unresolved boundary: cue, construction, or admission. If low probability and incapacity are still observationally equivalent, report that rather than attributing the loss. *New:* A curriculum learns where to intervene without conflating an absent accepted sample with a broken generator. *First test:* Plant each failure separately and in pairs in a small proposer–solver world; compare diagnosis costs and recovery to more resampling, verifier-only fixes and uniform old-family prompting.

**GP3. Install a new goal vocabulary under an executable compatibility contract.** Parts: GF4 migration, GF11 conservative extension, GF14 composition, N34 Liskov memory (PR #41), P19 stable ABI (PR #39), Q5, BR1. *Mechanism:* Translate old goal requests into typed derivations of the new language. Admit a vocabulary change only after fresh compositions still have valid old meanings and a fixed-budget generation route. Surface unrepresentable classes with a concrete counterexample and serve them via a costed legacy route if available. *New:* Behavioral subtyping applies to the question generator: a migration must preserve the ability to ask new instances, not merely accept legacy strings. *First test:* Rename and split primitives in a tiny arithmetic language, including one removed constructor. Compare paired-semantics migration against static ID aliases, archive replay and a frozen old grammar.

**GP4. Find the order that breaks generation even when it leaves solver accuracy intact.** Parts: GF6 role seams, GF9 exposure-pinned credit, PO2 function-matched twins, CU11 excursions and CU14 interleave (PR #119), FCU8, Y2, Q1. *Mechanism:* Replay the same multiset of proposer and solver practice blocks in opposite orders on copies. Measure the vector of changes in fresh generator reachability, admission and independent solver service. Separate immediate mixed-role interference from a persistent loss using a shared later continuation and a same-order seed control; deploy a different ordering or a separated role surface only for reproducible harms. *New:* A finite commutator can identify forgetting in the training-data source despite an unchanged endpoint solver score. *First test:* Use shared-parameter symbolic self-play with matched practice histograms and opposite role orders. Compare against a gross-progress scheduler, fixed role isolation and the direct two-order comparator without automatic correction.

**GP5. Remember what makes two old question families different.** Parts: GF13 fracture maps, GF15 constructive counterexamples, GF22 exchangeable task bases, FO3 question barcodes (PR #50), IC2 coded skills (PR #56), Q4. *Mechanism:* Keep small executable transformations that turn a valid member into a near miss or move it into a different family. Rebuild a compact generation basis only if the transformations remain executable in both directions for withheld compositions. When possible, share witnesses across several family pairs and buy independent probes for distinctions a shared witness cannot resolve. *New:* An old question family can be compressed by its separations from neighboring families without treating surface diversity as semantic breadth. *First test:* Merge two deliberately aliased families in a finite generator; compare held-out distinguishability and bytes against example coresets, label balancing and a complete saved grammar.

**GP6. Give a forgotten question a second way to be assembled.** Parts: GF17 solver-to-proposer bridge, GF18 host transplant, GF19 separate blind spots, NB20 subfunctionalization and NB21 regrowth (PR #46), E10 insurance (PR #45), FP1. *Mechanism:* Split a generator into a public grammar route and an inverse-construction route based on typed partial proofs from independently authored tasks. Train route diversity under deliberately different observable failures; after either route goes down, regenerate only from the survivor's checked constraints instead of letting the solver simply provide old answers. *New:* Redundancy is measured by fresh construction under different evidence failures, not by the number of replicated proposers. *First test:* Run arithmetic and rewrite generators with independently deleted vocabulary and proof-state access. Compare equal-byte identical replication, one frozen proposer and independently qualified cross-repair.

**GP7. Reward lessons for what they enable after an evaluator swap.** Parts: GF7 verifier drift, GF9 exposure-pinned credit, GF16 expiring validity witnesses, FG7 judge bridge (PR #118), RL3 verifier twins (PR #51), FCU3. *Mechanism:* Train the proposer on paired practice blocks with the same family counts and starting difficulty; prefer blocks whose net cross-task effects persist under independent evaluators. If old and new checkers disagree on plausible fresh questions, use the conflict to constrain training rather than treating either score as ground truth. *New:* Curriculum credit rewards transportable capability instead of selection effects or a generator adapting to an easier judge. *First test:* Simulate a verifier upgrade that preserves scores on ordinary problems but reverses a shortcut-sensitive family. Compare held-out generation and cross-task transfer to frontier-only selection and matched proportional replay.

**GP8. Qualify identical-looking generators on the next practice window.** Parts: GF14 composable generation, GF22 exchangeable bases, PO1 relaxation clock, PO2 matched twins, PO3 allocation odometer, DF3 train-versus-apply (PR #114), BR2. *Mechanism:* Construct two proposers matched in fresh breadth and current solver service after a shared re-mixing stream. Give them the same unseen practice window, then test whether either stops generating an old family under new compositions. Compare allocation sketches against ordinary logits and the direct fork before using the sketches as a release gate. *New:* The retained capability is generative optionality after another lesson, not just a broad sample at the current checkpoint. *First test:* Train tiny shared-feature proposers with blocked and shuffled histories and identical minibatch pools. Score fresh generation after re-mixing and after a new window; include fixed random features and same-order seed twins.

**GP9. Spend a small reorder buffer on the blocks that steal the generator's last constructor.** Parts: GF3 missing-production probes, GF9 exposure-pinned credit, PO10 pair kernels, PO11 higher-order residuals, PO15 reorder buffers, CU11 excursions (PR #119), Y2/Q1. *Mechanism:* Estimate direct short forward forks for competing practice orders. Start with pair effects, buy triplet tests only when the pair model cannot distinguish plausible schedules, and reorder within a charged buffer without changing the minibatch histogram. Protect both the solver floor and old-family reachability; retain the raw comparator when the approximation fails. *New:* Makes a limited reorder buffer protect particular question-producing capabilities rather than using an overall learning-rate or mixing heuristic. *First test:* Stream blocked symbolic tasks with one planted triplet conflict. Compare measured retention and delay against fixed shuffling, pair-only planning, direct two-order tests at equal forwards, and a no-buffer control.

**GP10. Give the solver and proposer separate recovery clocks before rewiring either.** Parts: GF6 role seams, GF8 prefix tomography, PO1 order clock, PO6 annealing bursts, PO9 optimizer carryover, CD4 input shaping (PR #74), SM1 seed log (PR #43). *Mechanism:* When a blocked curriculum regresses, run identical re-mixing on copies with and without a matched optimizer-state transfer. Measure recovery of fresh proposal construction and untouched solver competence separately. Use a short, costed corrective sequence only if it shortens the slower recovery without shifting damage to the other role. *New:* Separates momentum carryover, a transient excursion and persistent feature allocation before prescribing a more expensive role split. *First test:* Extend the PR #121 hosts with a proposal head, and compare momentum versus plain SGD, equal-step re-mixing versus a short burst, and role separation. Match starting service and compare actual recovery curves, not only endpoint scores.

**GP11. Keep a generator's question families independent enough to survive a quota change.** Parts: GF19 separate blind spots, GF21 cold-start contracts, PO7 critical-period ordering, PO13 gating overlap, PO20 commuting parameterizations, NB20 reciprocal degeneration (PR #46), E22 gene duplication (PR #45), FP1. *Mechanism:* Duplicate a scarce proposal feature only when opposing orders repeatedly remove an old family's last constructive route. Give each branch complementary role support and an independent executable witness. Protect the old floor during a quota-constrained curriculum, then test whether forks that use independently routed updates have less residual order dependence. *New:* Turns a measured persistent allocation conflict into targeted structural separation rather than imposing specialist slots for every task. *First test:* Use a capacity-limited proposer with two intersecting grammars. Compare conditional duplication with equal-width independent adapters, always duplicating and a permutation-only scheduler, including recovery under a shared continuation.

**GP12. A self-play learner that keeps both the old questions and the ability to practice them.** Parts: GF1 inverse synthesis, GF3 missing productions, GF4 migration, GF6 role seams, GF7 admissions, GF8 prefix tests, GF9 pinned exposure, GF11 extension, GF13 fractures, GF14 composition, GF17 inverse constructors, GF19 independent routes, GF21 cold starts, PO1 order clock, PO2 matched twins, PO3 allocation, PO6 anneal, PO9 optimizer carryover, PO10 pair kernel, PO11 higher order, PO13 gating overlap, PO14 permutation, PO15 buffer, PO20 commuting writes, PO23 robust evaluation, CU3, CU11, CU15, CU18, FCU8, FCU9, Y2, Q1, BR7, FP1. *Mechanism:* Track reachability and fresh breadth separately from exposure and solver competence. Diagnose failures at cue, construction and admission boundaries. At a fixed practice histogram, select blocks by measured excursions and residual allocation effects; buy a stronger diagnostic only if it changes the decision. Repair the smallest identified constructor or provision a separate route when order alone cannot protect it. Judge the repaired system after a common continuation and an independent next lesson. *New:* Coordinates the generation interface and the practice sequence so that symptom-free solver outputs do not hide a disappearing curriculum. *First test:* Combine the PR #120 symbolic domains with the PR #121 matched histogram and common-continuation protocol. Plant generator starvation, admission drift and a nonlinear order effect. Compare uniform archive prompting, shuffled and random-block practice, direct two-order divergence, and an equal-budget stack of generator repair and order control. Report fresh breadth, old solver floors, paid calls and the rate of unresolved diagnoses.
