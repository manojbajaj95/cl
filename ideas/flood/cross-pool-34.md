# Cross-pool merges, round 34

Focal sources: `tree/finite-safe-regions.md` (PR #152, FS#) and `tree/slack-erosion.md` (PR #153, SL#). Crosses with unregistered dependence (PR #150, UD#), stored slack (PR #151, SS#), learned invalidation (PR #148, LI#), wake anchors (PR #149, WA#), rented constraints (PR #145, RC#), future edit cost (PR #144, EC#), X1/Y1 override budgets, and earlier rounds 1–33. These are raw proposals. Keep the region of safe writer moves distinct from the region of tolerable errors in fact units. A safe endpoint is not a safe serving path, and a correct current answer is not evidence of a correct revision boundary. Track early and missed legitimate flips separately; forecast unchanged-fact forgetting and failure under fact moves separately. Charge proofs, stored slack, precision and reacquisition, and measure attained B progress alongside old-task service.

**FSR1. Find a route through writer space that does not squeeze out the next authorized revision.** Parts: FS03 gate-changing paths, FS05 baseline boundaries, FS18 deployable boundaries, SL3 curvature, SL4 rotating weakness, UD15 finite regions, SS20 multi-hop slack, USR6 separate override budgets. *Mechanism:* Intersect a verified old-service region with a requirement that designated fact perturbations still produce their oracle answers at every serving prefix. Search for paths that hand off between substitute features rather than pinning the initial gate pattern. Track the fact-direction tests in fact units; do not use distance to a weight-space face as a substitute. *New:* A path can preserve every current decision yet lose the ability to handle the very next revision; a different route to the same B endpoint might preserve both. *First test:* Enumerate paths in a two-writer ReLU toll host, then compare with projection and endpoint-only search on matched B gains and held-out fact directions.

**FSR2. Use an atomic switch to trade one fallback for another, but attach a precision bill.** Parts: FS02 substitutable features, FS14 atomic fallback trade, SL11 asymmetric storage cells, SL16 late precision debt, RC6 revocable lease, WA6 change-frontier anchors, LWA2 version-aligned release. *Mechanism:* When the old and new implementations occupy disconnected serving-safe components, build the candidate off line. Verify it at its actual deployed precision and on old and newly binding tolerances, price any extra bits required, then publish the writer and its corresponding slack metadata together. If a wake anchor refers to the old component, refresh it or retire that wake before the switch. *New:* The cheapest atomic endpoint in writer space may become the most expensive one once its new consumers or tighter tolerances come due. *First test:* In a finite host with disconnected components and a late-arriving rare consumer, compare atomic switch, fixed fallback and a serving-safe detour where one exists. Charge shadow bytes, anchor refresh and restored precision.

**FSR3. Forecast which clock will make a lease unsafe, not just when to wake.** Parts: FS20 conditional history certificates, FS21 overlapping patches, SL1 margin-matched histories, SL7 two first-passage clocks, SL9 rare exceptions, RC4 lease term, SS12 latent erosion, USR7 anchor reserve. *Mechanism:* At predeclared landmarks where A is still correct, estimate separately the risk of a future B update breaking A at unchanged facts and the risk of a fact move creating an incorrect response. Tie each forecast to its own intervention: shorten the write lease for the first, revalidate or restore precision for the second. Use independent audits for rare readers and never treat a correct oracle flip as forgetting. *New:* The same alarm time can imply two opposite remedies; a wake reservation is wasted if the only failing object is the stored fact. *First test:* Cross B order and exogenous fact drift at matched current margins. Compare the two-clock policy with one slack threshold, fixed lease terms and labelled canaries at equal intervention cost.

**FSR4. Invalidate the proof that covered yesterday's boundary, not every write that relied on it.** Parts: FS11 cached proof cells, FS13 summaries for future writers, SL5 recover slack, SL8 matched damage, SL19 refresh weakest tolerance, LI12 versions, LI18 completeness, UD12 independent audits. *Mechanism:* Index each certified cell by the fact ranges, writer version and downstream read assumptions it used. When a tolerance erodes or a new dependency is found, invalidate only certificates that no longer cover their assumptions, but reserve independent checks outside the index. Reopen a cell by re-certification rather than assuming the surrounding geometry changed uniformly. *New:* A learner can lose confidence in a previously available write without losing the old task or even losing the safe write itself. *First test:* Alternate verified boundary shifts and nuisance shifts over a tiny proof cache. Compare global recomputation, edge-indexed invalidation and incremental proof repair; report stale permits and lost safe opportunities separately.

**FSR5. Sell a joint write allowance that comes with an order, not two independent limits.** Parts: FS07 concurrent composition, FS09 forbidden coalitions, SL4 rotating weak direction, SL12 joint budgets, UD4 jointly harmful writes, X1 override types, USR1 canceling leases, EC15 commuting edits. *Mechanism:* Admit writes as a verified sequence and record which combined effects consume each old consumer's tolerance. Allow independent scheduling only when all promised prefixes are checked; otherwise give the transactions an explicit order or hide intermediate states behind an atomic publish. Use measured fact-region changes to tighten the next offer without confusing them with exact writer constraints. *New:* A temporary risk market can trade order and coordination to obtain useful writes that no allocation of independent radius quotas can admit. *First test:* Cross reinforcing, canceling and gate-changing writes on a shared reader. Compare independent boxes, a verified sequence and atomic merge at fixed B goals; score worst prefixes and time waiting for a safe commit.

**FSR6. Rent reclaimed precision only if a later caller can actually buy it back.** Parts: FS12 discard proof artifacts, FS15 future safe options, SL14 merge slots, SL15 drift reserve, SL16 precision debt, EC14 reserve budgets, RC5 collateral, Y6 plasticity credit. *Mechanism:* Intersect all current tolerances, including direct recall, before quantizing or merging fact slots. Sell the freed bytes to B either as permanent capacity or as a revocable lease backed by a priced source for precise reconstruction. If a tighter consumer arrives and no source can recover the distinction, record an irreversible trade rather than inventing recovery from slack. *New:* Two equally cheap compressions today can leave different numbers of usable writer options tomorrow, because only one can be undone within the service deadline. *First test:* Choose endpoints at matched current B gain and old retention, then introduce fine-grained callers. Compare a lease with reserved reacquisition, fixed precision and a drift-only allocator, charging source queries and all backups.

**FSR7. Ask for the cheapest witness that changes a veto into an admitted write.** Parts: FS17 conflict cores, FS19 counterexamples for search, SL6 fact–procedure crossover, SL13 binding-consumer rank, SL22 extra coordinates, UD18 impossibility receipts, EC1 witness bundles, WA9 active labels. *Mechanism:* When proposed B progress falls outside the certified region, separate real conflicts from missing evidence about a fact boundary, a read link or the writer's effect. Buy the test that could distinguish them and reopen the largest useful objective improvement, not the largest volume of parameters. If every legal write fails in the finite oracle, price a new interface or a relaxed B target instead. *New:* One carefully chosen fact or wake contrast can be worth more as proof of a usable update than as an extra training example. *First test:* Enumerate writers in small models with genuine and spurious conflict cores. Compare uniform probes, cheapest-first auditing and objective-directed purchases at equal total evidence cost.

**FSR8. Migrate a consumer through the narrowest certified bridge, not through the largest overlap.** Parts: FS10 adapter transport, FS14 fallback handoffs, SL17 equivalent procedures, SL18 rare-reader guarantees, UD9 joint migration, SS18 base-invariant slack, LI11 edge edits, USR3 adapter leases. *Mechanism:* Construct a handoff that preserves the same rare-case and supplied-premise obligations before and after a routing switch. Transfer only the boundary information that survives a verified change of representation; remeasure mixed fact directions when the replacement composes them differently. If there is no safe visible bridge, require an atomic handoff or keep the old route. *New:* A small overlap containing the right exceptional requests can matter more than broad agreement on the traffic that both implementations find easy. *First test:* Switch between two equivalent toll executors with different internal thresholds and identical common-case answers. Compare activation alignment, endpoint checks and certified handoffs on rare and independently sampled trajectories.

**FSR9. Hold onto the cheap edit path, not just the cheap edit.** Parts: FS15 future objective options, FS16 easier region geometry, SL10 beneficial shrinkage, SL20 same rate different hazards, SL21 same slack different rates, EC5 writer portability, EC17 payback horizon, RD1 rate forecasts. *Mechanism:* Buy matched training examples that correct a stale wide boundary while retaining reachable paths to a declared menu of useful future repairs. Track cost to encode the intended revision separately from cost to find and execute a safe path. Choose the training intervention by held-out lifecycle bill, not by maximizing slack or minimizing the first edit's size. *New:* Less slack can be progress when it restores a legitimate threshold, yet the same correction can be a bad buy if it strands later safe writes. *First test:* Cross curricula and a frozen writer menu on repeated and reversing revisions. Match first-edit cost and accuracy, then evaluate second-edit work and future B progress against uniform training and replay.

**FSR10. Reserve a wake only for the part of a safe region that it can really recover.** Parts: FS04 moving factors, FS08 implication proofs, FS22 replay break-even, SL5 recover slack, SL9 exceptions, SL19 weakest tolerance, RC16 proof-carrying leases, WA21 payload loss, USR4 independent backups. *Mechanism:* Separate always-live constraints from leases that allow temporary loss. For the latter, audit a frozen public wake on lease-reachable states, checking the current premises, separating directions and retained B rather than just the restored endpoint. Attach a priced anchor or keep a fallback when opposite obligations remain indistinguishable, and remove a redundant fallback only when the replacement actually covers its callers. *New:* Restoring an answer can be cheap even when restoring its safe revision region is impossible within the same lease. *First test:* Cross wake and sham across release doses in a rule host that supports shallow re-alignment and true erasure. Compare replay and saved deltas at matched post-wake service and future edit cost.

**FSR11. A continual learner that protects its ability to change its mind.** Parts: FS01 gate premiums, FS02 substitutable features, FS03 safe paths, FS04 movable factors, FS05 baseline boundaries, FS07 composition, FS08 implication, FS09 forbidden coalitions, FS10 transport, FS11 proof cells, FS12 proof retention, FS13 writer summaries, FS14 fallback exchange, FS15 future options, FS16 tractable regions, FS17 conflict cores, FS18 deployment, FS19 evidence purchase, FS20 policy histories, FS21 overlapping patches, FS22 break-even, SL1 matched histories, SL3 curvature, SL4 rotation, SL5 boundary repair, SL6 crossover, SL7 distinct clocks, SL9 rare exceptions, SL10 useful shrinkage, SL11 asymmetric cells, SL12 joint tolerance, SL13 binding rank, SL14 intersection, SL15 reserve, SL16 precision debt, SL17 equivalents, SL18 guarantees, SL19 weakest refresh, SL20 rate versus time, SL21 slack versus rate, SL22 extra coordinates, UD#, SS#, LI#, WA#, RC#, EC#, X1, Y1, USR6, LWA4, ECR9. *Mechanism:* Track acquisition-anchored writer regions and current-premise tolerance regions separately. Permit new writes by certified path, verified order, or atomic endpoint as the serving contract demands. Meter erosion on independent fact probes, refresh only the proofs whose assumptions changed, and rent precision or protected directions only against a tested source or wake. Buy evidence where it can reopen useful writes; maintain a fallback when a proof or an anchor no longer distinguishes what is current. *New:* A learner could preserve not just current answers but future room to learn and revise them, without treating all slack as good or all uncertified moves as impossible. *First test:* Use an enumerable ReLU route world with rare overrides, rotating fact directions, overlapping writers, delayed edits and lease expiry. Compare tuned projection, replay, exact priced recomputation and saved deltas. Report attained B gain, future edit work, proof and metadata costs, prefix failures, both first-passage forecasts, and correct versus stale wake outcomes.
