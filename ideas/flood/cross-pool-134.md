## Merge proposals

**ACB1. Compare storage formats by what they can rebuild on the next base.** Parts: HM01 rate and distortion curve, HM02 post-upgrade comparison, HM03 contract regenerator, HM04 distilled examples, HM09 data-size boundary, HM12 prompt or steering or LoRA, HM24 byte accounting, HC02 minimal contract, NP07 raw-string readouts, NP4-19 future-operation equivalence. *Mechanism:* Store each representation at the same byte budget and give its regenerator the same authorized task evidence. After an upgrade, measure behavior in raw strings on new instances and after one later correction. Charge any per-fleet regenerator overhead separately and amortize it over the same number of adapters. *New:* A representation can compress current outputs yet be expensive to adapt later. Report the upgrade and amendment curves separately rather than calling same-base compression portability. *First test:* Compare compressed weights plus a compatible transport or re-adaptation baseline, distilled examples, Text-to-LoRA and test-led rebuilds on synthetic rules and arbitrary lookup tables. Hold out rule families and report target accuracy, retention and reconstruction cost.

**ACB2. Keep shared test provenance and separate common obligations from adapter-specific ones. Use only generated data and check whether records retain planted mock identifiers before retaining them in the fleet ledger. *New:* Tests earn their bytes if they predict which component needs repair under an unseen upgrade, rather than just stabilizing a committee that agrees for the wrong reason. *First test:* Compare disagreement and random selection at the same label budget across several toy upgrades. Track held-out failures, rebuild agreement, new tests per upgrade, storage and measured rebuild work before extrapolating fleet cost.

**ACB3. Make repair history compete with equally informative replay.** Parts: HM13 repair-grown constraints, HM14 constraint mismatch, HM15 target refresh, HM16 all-old-correct controls, HM17 repeat-offender check, HM18 dormant constraints, HM19 constraint-or-counter decision, HM21 self-chosen targets, HM25 held-out neighbors, HC19 repairs become constraints, HC27 equal-information controls, NP4-13 responses to future evidence. *Mechanism:* First test whether past repairs predict regressions on later updates beyond task and difficulty. Only then claim a benefit from history-based targeting. Given the same examples, compare replay and constraints; rotate inactive entries out of training but keep them in evaluation. Retest any targeted counter under later updates. *New:* A useful repair ledger predicts where behavior will break again, and its retention gains should not come from keeping the new model close to stale outputs on tasks that ought to improve. *First test:* Retrain a toy spatial adapter through shifting tasks. Compare fixed-neighbor KL, equal-example replay, broadly chosen preservation probes and repair-history constraints at equal compute with a target-gain floor; report negative flips on a sealed neighbor split.

**ACB4. Version a shared behavior expectation without silently retiring its witnesses.** Parts: HM20 portable labels and tolerances, HM22 co-load checks, HM23 authorized supersession, HC19 repairs become constraints, NP4-21 shared lineage, NP3-22 withdrawal of evidence, AL13 recompute after retraction. *Mechanism:* Store portable output expectations with provenance, not old-base logits. Mark superseded entries rather than deleting evidence; on a change, recheck co-loaded sets whose active obligations depend on that entry. Keep genuinely overlapping claims separate until tested. *New:* One authorized rule change can invalidate a shared expectation without withdrawing other adapters' obligations or unrelated evidence for the same behavior. *First test:* Change a synthetic conversion convention and migrate one co-loaded adapter. Compare versioned tests against append-only replay without supersession and a full rebuild; count stale obligations, missed regressions and repeated checks.

Measure when compressed weights or stored data are cheaper and when repair history adds predictive value. Do not force a ledger constraint when it blocks a verified target improvement; record the conflict and test the exception separately. *New:* The same record earns a role in continual learning only if it both rebuilds behavior after upgrades and helps protect or deliberately revise it during later training. *First test:* Run a proposed synthetic stream with several retrains, an upgrade and an authorized rule change. Match bytes and task information against stored weights plus transfer, stored data plus retraining and fixed-probe replay. Compare target gain, held-out retention, revision accuracy and total rebuild cost.

## LoRA prior-art check: PR #403

[NOLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02556) already compresses adapters with a random basis, [LoRA-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16559) supports training-free transfer between compatible subspaces in its demonstrated setting, and [Text-to-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105) generates adapters from descriptions. Regeneration is not the claim: compare its equal-byte behavioral cost after an upgrade and a later amendment. [Positive-Congruent Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09161) targets negative flips, [Self-Preservation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12602) selects preservation targets for replay, and [Why Constrained Updates Still Forget](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30191) already identifies probe mismatch and gradient staleness. The proposal must show that repair provenance improves probe selection or later repair beyond those controls at matched access and compute. [No Universal Signal Predicts Sample-Level LLM Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13607) studies inference-time signals, not prior repairs; that difference motivates the prediction test but is not evidence that history works. This is a targeted baseline comparison, not a priority claim.
