# Cross-pool merges, round 39

Focal sources: `tree/witness-examples.md` (PR #163, WE#) and `tree/finite-edit-guards.md` (PR #164, FG#). Crosses with training readers (PR #161, TR#), unannounced consumers (PR #162, UC#), declared footprints (PR #159, DF#), room-making writes (PR #160, RM#), finite safe regions (PR #152, FS#), recovery witnesses (PR #139, RW#), crossed evidence (PR #143, XE#), and earlier rounds 1–38. These are raw proposals. A training witness is not a retirement receipt or a safety proof. Check reachable states of the replacement as well as the source; bind edit proofs to actual effects, acquisition floors and served prefixes. A recovery that runs after a service failure does not make the failure safe. Count released state only after the source is inaccessible and its storage is reused.

**WFG1. Give a teacher substitute the edits that neither teacher nor student would choose.** Parts: WE2 nonlinear boundaries, WE18 replacement-created states, FG01 tangent blind spot, FG03 far-side slack, FS# finite safe regions, TR7 update-effect distillation, UC08 independent editor. *Mechanism:* Fit a tiny witness buffer on the reachable union of source and replacement states, including states after a permitted finite edit crosses a hidden gate. Use a closed-set or tube check that includes optimizer state and actual edit effects before using the buffer to discharge a training reader. *New:* The expensive teacher queries can target where an independent editor lands the refresh rather than where either unperturbed training trajectory goes. *First test:* Compare matched-byte trajectory condensation and boundary-aware witnesses on a finite ReLU host with a frozen hidden-gate edit bank; measure verified reuse and worst old-task performance.

**WFG2. Teach the distinction that survives a teacher-free refresh instead of padding today's margin.** Parts: WE8 separating examples, WE14 redundant annotations, FG02 blind-editor guard, FG04 hazard location, XE# crossed evidence, RM01 frozen payloads, UC02 actual crossings, TUC4 dependency-changing curricula. *Mechanism:* Choose truthful examples where a feature and its usual shortcut imply different corrections after a fact or gate change. Keep the later teacher-free optimizer and batches fixed. Test whether the data choice preserves old behavior through an independently authored edit whose guard was false before the provider, without leaving targets or hidden teacher calls. *New:* A curriculum could retire an annotation channel and create room for a later edit by teaching the same missing distinction once. *First test:* Hold exposure, labels and compute fixed; compare crossed and ordinary curricula on actual source dependence, finite holdout admissions and attained B utility.

**WFG3. Certify a refresh that spends both an edit allowance and an optimizer clock.** Parts: WE3 delayed optimizer response, WE5 update noise, FG06 frozen update rule, FG13 residual guard, TR1 read reservations, DF2 delayed slot effects, TUC5 consumable receipts. *Mechanism:* Describe a refresh and edit as stateful transitions with clocks, sampler and slots. Couple randomness to compare single-step transitions, then check the declared reachable trajectories under the actual publication order. After the first call, export only a verified residual family, not the original permission at a new clock value. *New:* A witness can match today's parameter jump while changing which second edit can run safely tomorrow. *First test:* Match first-step deltas in paired Adam hosts and vary delayed edits and sampling. Compare weight-only checks, moment-aware simulation and a scoped residual proof.

**WFG4. Let two private witness buffers protect one rare slice without agreeing on a global model.** Parts: WE4 counterfactual teacher responses, WE16 alternate retirements, FG07 signed product, FG08 shared selector, FG10 cross-term basis, RM07 complementary suppliers, UC11 state-dependent effects, DF8 reader receipts. *Mechanism:* Each provider learns its own task and a scoped witness interface: one bounds feature gain, the other bounds the signed readout. Check that both use the same slice key on every allowed edited state, and bound omitted cross-terms before combining the offers. Release only the reader obligations the chosen witnesses actually discharge. *New:* Two limited witnesses can prove a joint finite reaction without either party reconstructing the other's teacher or relying on matching aggregate scores. *First test:* Plant rare slices where globally identical offers either cancel or help. Compare independent fits, exact joint verification and signed summaries at equal private fitting budgets.

**WFG5. Build a repair witness while the certified route is still serving.** Parts: WE9 refresh compatibility, WE15 shared buffer, FG14 bounded strategy, FG16 live versus shadow paths, RW# recovery witnesses, FS# path safety, TR11 separate handovers. *Mechanism:* When a frozen edit breaks the current guard, choose from a bounded menu of witness-driven accommodations that can run in shadow while a checked route serves. Switch only after validating the resulting guard and its deployment prefix. Keep recovery-only actions out of the live strategy if they cannot finish before the route is needed. *New:* A witness can be part of a strategy that accommodates a series of previously unseen edits without counting a skill recovered after the fact as continuously available. *First test:* Play a short enumerable edit game with a peak-memory cap and open requests; compare fixed safe schedules, offline recovery and a verified accommodation policy.

**WFG6. Turn a rejected finite edit into the next witness example, not the next exception.** Parts: WE6 data expressivity, WE19 minimal counterexamples, FG18 witness collection, FG22 scoped refusal, RM08 actionable guard requests, DF20 veto repair, TUC3 targeted evidence. *Mechanism:* Minimize a failed edit/input pair to locate a missing correction near the boundary. Ask a provider to synthesize a lawful witness or find a truthful example that teaches it, with the refused payload and service floor unchanged. Keep structural inexpressibility, a genuine incompatibility and an incomplete proof as distinct outcomes. *New:* Rejection could expose exactly what a training stand-in must express without giving it permission to shrink the editor's contract around the failure. *First test:* Mix weak buffers, impossible edits and incomplete verifiers in a finite oracle; compare random witness growth and counterexample-guided repair on independently frozen payloads.

**WFG7. Reclaim two teachers in succession without promoting a student to ground truth.** Parts: WE11 cross-teacher transfer, WE17 cascades, WE21 hidden decoder state, FG15 effect inclusion, FG17 premise slices, TR20 stale receipts, DF5 physical aliases, FSR4 targeted invalidation. *Mechanism:* Carry the original acquisition reference and an explicit cumulative error account across replacements. Check that later editor effects stay in the proved region or prove the added region separately. Unbind queued and future reads, audit latent witness decoders for old dependencies, and reuse physical storage only when the remaining claims no longer need it. *New:* A cascade can shrink as readers change without accumulating invisible reference drift or passing off a hidden decoder dependency as retired capacity. *First test:* At fixed current accuracy, vary delayed jobs, decoder aliases and a dormant gate change. Compare independent rechecks, rolling reference resets and premise-scoped reuse.

**WFG8. Give the rare task a hard floor and let common mistakes rotate.** Parts: WE7 reachable corrections, WE10 tomorrow's mistakes, FG05 task scoring, FG21 fixed edit family, XE# fact crossings, FS# finite bounds, UC20 lost opportunities. *Mechanism:* Choose witness examples that teach corrections for the error patterns a fixed edit family can actually induce. Prove a per-edit error cap for common cases and a separate hard bound for rare slices under the same declared task semantics. Reserve no credit for narrowing the edit set or letting rare slices disappear from the witness search. *New:* A small witness set can target the ways edits concentrate errors without demanding that every edit preserve the same common examples. *First test:* Plant rotating common failures and one synchronized rare failure; compare aggregate replay, fixed-subset protection and selective witnesses on a blinded, unchanged editor bank.

**WFG9. Keep a joint setting open for editors whose refresh jobs have not been revealed.** Parts: WE12 teachable distinctions, WE15 shared witnesses, WE20 today's data versus tomorrow's buffer, FG11 unopened consumers, FG12 later partners, UC17 reusable establishment, TR16 complementary menus, RFW6 preserving continuations. *Mechanism:* Train providers against past jobs and commit a checked region covering several possible continuation interfaces before revealing independent payloads. At reveal, check whether each frozen payload and its later refresh can use the same witness or need a new buffer; charge lost admissions when a reservation or edit exhausts the shared region. *New:* Leaving room for multiple editors can mean reserving a useful correction, not reserving a copy of every source bank. *First test:* Vary the overlap and conflict of held-out editor/refresh pairs. Compare per-job witnesses, fixed-menu planning and provider-trained shared interfaces at equal total bytes.

**WFG10. Keep recovery cheap for declared wake jobs without weakening the finite edit guard.** Parts: WE1 reachable-span compilation, WE13 stable features, WE22 break-even, FG09 partner premises, FG19 information cost, FG20 verifier control, RW# bounded recovery, TR17 byte rent. *Mechanism:* Replace a pinned source with a witness restricted to the state span of a declared wake routine, leaving live service checks independent. Admit later edits only if they preserve the wake routine's premises or supply a checked alternative; compare against sending the original factor or doing incremental verification. *New:* A learner can trade the byte-time of holding a source for the cost that future editors incur preserving a bounded recovery path, without conflating recovery with serving. *First test:* Sweep wake horizons, retained rank and edit families, including editors that leave live accuracy intact but push the wake outside its verified span. Compare stored factors, witnesses and retraining.

**WFG11. A learner that turns future training dependencies into guard-scoped teaching material.** Parts: WE1 reachable spans, WE2 nonlinear boundaries, WE3 delayed updates, WE4 counterfactual responses, WE5 noise, WE6 expressivity, WE7 minimal corrections, WE8 separating examples, WE9 refresh compatibility, WE10 future mistakes, WE11 teacher transfer, WE12 teachable distinctions, WE13 stable features, WE14 annotation retirement, WE15 shared witnesses, WE16 alternate sources, WE17 cascades, WE18 replacement reachability, WE19 destructive suffixes, WE20 data-buffer tradeoffs, WE21 hidden decoder, WE22 break-even, FG01 tangent blind spots, FG02 blind editors, FG03 far-side slack, FG04 hazard features, FG05 scoring, FG06 update rules, FG07 signed products, FG08 slice selectors, FG09 partner premises, FG10 cross-terms, FG11 unopened consumers, FG12 later partners, FG13 residual room, FG14 bounded strategies, FG15 editor transport, FG16 publication paths, FG17 dependency slices, FG18 next counterexample, FG19 information costs, FG20 verifier controls, FG21 fixed demand, FG22 scoped refusals, TR#, UC#, DF#, RM#, FS#, RW#, XE#, TUC1, TUC7, FSR4. *Mechanism:* Learn replacement examples or choose current data to eliminate a versioned training read. Prove continuity of admitted jobs on their actual reachable states, close and drain accesses, then spend the freed bank on useful provision for a fixed, blinded family of finite edits. Compose suppliers by their signed effects and assumptions; check live obligations, served prefixes and residual room at each admission. Use failures to choose new teaching material or return a scoped refusal without weakening the old contract. *New:* The same learning process could decide what knowledge to teach forward and what checked kinds of change that knowledge can support, without keeping the original teacher or assuming the next editor is already known. *First test:* Use a finite stateful ReLU learner with crossing facts, rare gates, an optimizer bank and complementary private providers. Compare matched trajectory condensation plus incremental verification, fixed-menu planning and joint witness/guard learning. Report service, actual source reuse, frozen-write utility, recovery separately from service, and all teaching, proof and overlap costs.
