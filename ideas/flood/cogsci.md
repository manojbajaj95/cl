# Flood: cognitive science and child development

Raw, unfiltered ideas for a new continual-learning primitive, drawn from cognitive science and developmental psychology. Nothing is pre-killed. Each entry gives the **mechanism**, *why it might be new* (with near neighbours where I know them), and the **cheapest first test**, which always includes a **retention check** (old tasks still work) and the **simplest same-budget control** that could tie it.

Screened against `reviews/idea-ledger.md` and `reviews/flood-triage.md` (PR #6) and the neuro-bio flood (`ideas/flood/neuro-bio.md`). Where an idea touches a ledger entry (self-quiz replay, iterated learning, metamemory, inoculation, Baldwin/Lamarck, L1 checkpoints, N1 round-trip, SM1 seed log), the difference is stated. Default model sizes are CPU-scale (GPT-2 small, Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B, small MLPs); lesson/memory tests carry the filler and entropy-matched-temperature arms by default (ledger gotcha).

Default retention check, written **[R]** below: accuracy (or KL to the pre-update model) on a fixed held-out panel of every earlier task, reported as the drop versus the pre-update checkpoint, with seed CIs.

**Proposer's picks for the head-to-head** (novelty only, not filtering): C01 representational redescription (mastery-triggered format ladder), C11 reconsolidation windows (recall unlocks editing), C29 pretend frames (sandboxed counterfactual learning that can be promoted or discarded), C51 protégé replay (a from-scratch student as a data-free forgetting detector), C50 protection by copying.

## A. Stages, critical periods and developmental schedules

**C01 · Representational redescription (Karmiloff-Smith).** Skills pass through three formats: implicit (procedural weights), explicit-1 (an internal, inspectable but non-verbal code), explicit-2 (a verbal rule the model can state). The primitive is a *redescription operator* that takes a skill already at behavioural mastery and re-encodes it into the next format, triggered only after mastery, not during learning. Once redescribed, the weight copy can be pruned and regenerated from the higher format on demand. *Why new:* N1 round-trip goes code→weights→code on request; RR is an automatic, mastery-triggered, one-way ladder with a predicted performance dip during redescription (U-shape) and a format-specific forgetting profile. Near: skill distillation to text (2611.09454 decompiling), self-explanation. **Test:** GPT-2 small, 6 arithmetic-rule tasks in sequence; after each reaches 95%, train a head to emit the rule and a decoder to re-instantiate a LoRA from it; prune the LoRA. [R] on tasks 1–5 after task 6, vs. keeping LoRAs (upper bound) and vs. rule text in context (control). Kill if regenerated skills lose >10 pp versus kept LoRAs.

**C02 · Mastery-gated critical periods.** Each module has a plasticity window that opens when its input statistics are first seen and closes when a *mastery signal* (stable prediction error) says the module has learned enough, not on a timer. Closed modules stay closed unless a large, persistent surprise reopens them (adult plasticity). *Why new:* plasticity-loss work tries to keep everything plastic; critical-period CL papers use fixed schedules. Here closure is earned and reopening is event-driven. Near: PackNet, HAT, NB04 permeable scars. **Test:** MLP, 10 permuted-MNIST tasks; per-layer windows closed by EMA loss plateau. [R] and new-task speed vs. EWC and vs. a fixed-time schedule with the same total closed fraction.

**C03 · Starting small (Elman 1993) as a forgetting shield.** Learn each new task first with a deliberately crippled input channel (short context, blurred inputs, low-rank attention), then widen. The prediction: early coarse learning lands in shared low-frequency features and later detail lands in task-specific high-frequency ones, so old tasks see less interference. *Why new:* curriculum learning is about speed; this uses developmental resource limitation as an interference-placement tool. **Test:** GPT-2 small, 4 synthetic grammars in sequence, context window grown 8→64 within each task vs. full window throughout. [R] on earlier grammars; control: equal-step random curriculum.

**C04 · Stage transitions as phase changes you can trigger.** Piagetian stages appear as abrupt reorganizations. Detect a stage-transition precursor (rising variance of internal representations, critical slowing down) and, only at that moment, run consolidation of old tasks, betting that the network is maximally reorganizable then and old knowledge is best protected by rehearsing exactly during the shift. *Why new:* replay schedules are uniform or loss-triggered; this schedules replay on a dynamical precursor of representational reorganization. Near: grokking-forgetting papers. **Test:** modular arithmetic tasks that grok in sequence; replay budget concentrated at variance peaks vs. spread uniformly. [R] at equal replay tokens.

**C05 · Zone of proximal development as a write filter (Vygotsky).** Only update on examples the model fails alone but solves with a scaffold (hint, worked step, tool). Examples it solves alone are skipped; ones it fails even with scaffolding are deferred. *Why new:* difficulty-based selection exists, but the selector here is the *scaffold gap*, which measures learnability rather than difficulty, and deferral creates a queue that is revisited as competence grows. Near: OPSD hinted teacher, prioritized replay. **Test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a stream of 3 arithmetic task families; ZPD filter vs. loss-based selection vs. random at equal update count. [R] on family 1.

**C06 · Developmental pruning burst.** Children overproduce synapses then prune ~40%. Grow capacity freely during a new domain (overproduce adapters/neurons), then run one pruning burst gated by use across *all* tasks, not just the new one. The new object is the overproduce-prune cycle with a global use count. *Why new:* grow-then-prune exists (DEN, PackNet); the cross-task use ledger and the burst timing after a domain stabilizes are the pieces to test. **Test:** MLP 10 tasks; overgrow 2× then prune by cross-task activation mass vs. by new-task magnitude. [R] at equal final size.

**C07 · Perceptual narrowing as reversible specialization.** Infants lose discrimination of non-native phonemes but can recover it. Let a model narrow its input embedding to current-domain distinctions (merge rarely used token directions into shared ones) while storing a tiny *re-differentiation key* per merged cluster, so lost distinctions can be restored quickly when the old domain returns. *Why new:* vocabulary trimming is a compression trick; framing it as reversible narrowing with a stored split key is a CL operation. **Test:** GPT-2 embedding, domain A then B; merge 30% of A-only tokens; measure relearning steps to recover A with key vs. without. [R] on A after recovery.

**C08 · Sensitive-period inheritance across model versions.** When a new base model is trained, it inherits not the old model's weights but its *plasticity profile*: which layers were plastic when, recorded as a schedule. *Why new:* transfer of optimizer state (SM25 Lamarckian) moves how to learn; this moves *when each part should stop learning*. **Test:** two seeds of a small MLP family; record closure times on model 1, replay schedule on model 2 vs. default. [R] across 10 tasks.

**C09 · Assimilation vs. accommodation switch (Piaget).** For each incoming example, decide whether to *assimilate* (map it into an existing schema slot: write only to a schema-specific small adapter) or *accommodate* (change the schema itself: write to shared weights). The decision is made by a learned schema-fit score. *Why new:* MoE routing chooses an expert; this chooses the *level* of the write (instance vs. schema) per example. Near: complementary learning systems. **Test:** stream of 5 relational datasets where 2 share a schema; switch vs. always-shared vs. always-adapter. [R] and transfer to the schema-sharing task.

**C10 · Overgeneralization phases as diagnostics ("goed").** Children overregularize irregulars after learning the rule. Track per-item U-shaped curves during CL; items that dip are ones whose memorized form is being overwritten by a new rule. Use the dip as an alarm to replay exactly those items. *Why new:* forgetting detectors watch loss; this uses rule-vs-exception competition structure to target replay at exceptions. **Test:** past-tense model (Rumelhart-McClelland style) on GPT-2 small; dip-targeted replay vs. loss-targeted replay at equal budget. [R] on irregulars.

## B. Memory systems from cognitive psychology

**C11 · Reconsolidation windows.** Retrieving a memory makes it labile for a short window, during which it can be updated cheaply; outside the window it is protected. Implement: an old skill's adapter is plastic only during steps where it was strongly *recalled* (high routing weight) in the last k steps. *Why new:* most CL protects or replays; this makes recall the key that unlocks editing, giving targeted updates of old knowledge without global plasticity. Near: NB01 write escrow, GRACE. **Test:** fact-edit stream on GPT-2 small where 20% of facts are later corrected; reconsolidation-gated edits vs. MEMIT sequential edits. [R] on uncorrected facts.

**C12 · Retrieval-induced forgetting as a pruning primitive.** Practising some items suppresses related competitors (Anderson). Deliberately use it: to retire a stale lesson, rehearse its *corrected* sibling with an inhibitory loss on shared cues, rather than unlearning the stale one directly. *Why new:* unlearning attacks the target; RIF removes it indirectly via competitor practice, which might be gentler on neighbours. **Test:** 50 fact pairs (stale, corrected) on GPT-2 small; RIF arm vs. gradient-ascent unlearning vs. plain overwrite. [R] on 200 unrelated facts and relearning-attack resistance.

**C13 · Encoding specificity keys.** Memories are recalled best in the context in which they were encoded. Store every update with a compact *encoding-context vector* (task, date, tool state, mood-like internal statistics) and train the model so the update fires most strongly when the current context matches. *Why new:* context-dependent gating exists (XdG), but the key here is the full internal state at write time, captured automatically, not a task ID. Near: L1 version tags (a scalar tag; this is a rich, continuous key). **Test:** split CIFAR on a small CNN with automatically captured BN statistics as keys vs. task-ID gating vs. none. [R] with keys available vs. withheld.

**C14 · Spacing-effect scheduler with a learned forgetting curve per item.** Classic, but the new piece: the model *predicts its own retention curve per item* from internal signals (gradient interference with the current batch), and schedules replay at the predicted 90% retention point, not on a fixed Leitner schedule. *Why new:* spaced replay exists in CL (2010s); a self-predicted, interference-driven spacing is not standard. Near: metamemory (#9 killed: area crowded) — this differs by using the forecast only to time replay. **Test:** MLP 20 tasks; interference-timed replay vs. uniform vs. Leitner at equal replay steps. [R].

**C15 · Desirable difficulty writes.** Learning under mild interference (interleaving, generation, varied context) slows acquisition but improves retention. Add deliberate difficulty to *new* learning (noise in inputs, partial cues, interleaving with old tasks) and test whether old tasks are better protected as a side effect. *Why new:* the claim is that difficulty makes the *new* memory less interfering with old ones, not just more durable. **Test:** GPT-2 small, 4 tasks; new task learned with partial-cue generation vs. full examples at equal steps. [R] on old tasks and retention of the new one 2 tasks later.

**C16 · Directed forgetting tags.** Humans can be told "forget this list" and later recall it poorly but relearn it fast. Give the model a *forget-cue* token: data trained under it is suppressed at recall but kept in a compact form so relearning is cheap. *Why new:* programmable half-life (killed fix) is about expiry; this is reversible suppression with savings, which can be measured (relearning speed). **Test:** 100 facts, half under forget cue; recall, relearning steps, and [R] on other facts vs. unlearning by gradient ascent.

**C17 · Proactive-interference release (Wickens).** Interference builds when successive tasks share a category and releases when the category switches. Order the task stream to maximize release: interleave categories by a learned similarity metric so consecutive tasks are dissimilar. *Why new:* task-order effects are studied but rarely optimized online with a release model. **Test:** 12 tasks in 3 categories; release-ordered vs. blocked vs. random. [R] at the end.

**C18 · Chunking with stored chunk boundaries.** Expertise is chunked. When a sub-sequence of actions or tokens recurs, compile it into a single new token with its own embedding and keep the boundary so it can be un-chunked if the sub-sequence later needs editing. *Why new:* vocabulary growth (killed B2 as prompt-pool CL) froze the backbone; this is about the *unchunk* operation, editing inside a chunk without retraining everything that used it. **Test:** agent action sequences in a toy gridworld; chunk/unchunk vs. plain fine-tune when one inner step changes. [R] on other chunks.

**C19 · Source monitoring.** People forget *where* they learned something before *what*. Train a source head: for each belief, which update (or document) it came from. Use source confidence to decide what to revise when a source is discredited. *Why new:* data attribution is post hoc and expensive; a trained-in source head makes "retract everything from source S" a forward pass. Near: L1 (versions), data provenance. **Test:** 5 sources × 40 facts, one source later declared bad; retract via source head + targeted unlearning vs. TracIn. [R] on other sources.

**C20 · False-memory pressure test (DRM).** The DRM paradigm induces false recall of unseen lures. Use DRM-style lure probes as a *forgetting-and-confabulation meter* after each update: how much does a new update make the model "remember" lures near old items? *Why new:* CL metrics count lost correct answers, not gained false ones. Primitive candidate: a confabulation budget as a constraint during learning. **Test:** GPT-2 small, word-list tasks; lure-recall rate before/after a new task, and a training penalty on lure recall. [R] on true items.

## C. Infant and child learning mechanisms

**C21 · Core-knowledge priors as a frozen kernel.** Infants start with a few core systems (objects, agents, number, geometry). Pretrain a small frozen "core" module on synthetic physics/agency/number data, and force all later learning to go through adapters that read but never write it. *Why new:* frozen-backbone CL freezes everything pretrained; this freezes a deliberately chosen, tiny, abstract kernel and tests whether it acts as a forgetting-free anchor for downstream tasks. **Test:** small transformer, core kernel from synthetic counting/objects; 6 downstream tasks; [R] vs. same-size kernel pretrained on random data.

**C22 · Violation-of-expectation triggered learning.** Infants look longer at impossible events and learn there. Gate learning by *surprise against a world model*, and store only surprising events into episodic memory, everything else into slow weights at a low rate. *Why new:* surprise-based replay exists; the split (surprising → episodic, expected → slow) with a predicted retention advantage is the testable claim. **Test:** synthetic physics stream with rare rule violations; VoE split vs. uniform. [R] on common physics after rare-event learning.

**C23 · Mutual exclusivity as a write-collision rule.** Children assume a new word labels a new object. When a new label arrives, prefer to bind it to an *unused* representation rather than overwrite an existing binding. *Why new:* a learned bias that steers writes to free capacity at the semantic level (labels) rather than the parameter level. Near: orthogonal projection. **Test:** GPT-2 small, novel-word stream with ME loss vs. none; [R] on old word meanings.

**C24 · Fast mapping vs. slow mapping split.** Children fast-map a word after one exposure but take months to fully learn it. Every new fact gets a fast, tentative binding (context or tiny adapter) and graduates to slow weights only after repeated, consistent confirmation. *Why new:* CLS-style dual memory is old; the *graduation criterion* (cross-context consistency, not repetition count) and the tentative binding's decay are the pieces. **Test:** fact stream with noisy one-shot facts, some later contradicted; consistency graduation vs. count graduation vs. direct write. [R] on graduated facts.

**C25 · Shape bias emergence as a learned inductive bias that protects old categories.** After a few categories, children generalize by shape. Train for an emergent "second-order" bias on early tasks, then check whether later tasks learned *under* that bias interfere less. *Why new:* the CL benefit of acquired inductive biases, not of capacity, is rarely measured. **Test:** Smith et al. toy object-name task, small CNN; [R] with vs. without the early bias-forming phase at equal steps.

**C26 · Babbling: self-generated motor noise to keep plasticity.** Infants babble: random actions explored for their own sake. Interleave short bouts of self-generated random-target prediction ("babble batches") between tasks to keep units plastic. *Why new:* plasticity injection by noise (shrink-perturb) touches weights; babbling touches the *data*, using self-generated targets with no task. Near: NB14 exploratory write noise, random-label pretraining. **Test:** MLP, 20-task plasticity benchmark; babble batches vs. shrink-perturb vs. none. Retention [R] plus new-task speed.

**C27 · Overimitation as a fidelity knob.** Children copy causally irrelevant steps; adults drop them. Imitate demos with full fidelity first, then prune steps that ablation shows are unnecessary. The retained "ritual" steps might act as anchors that keep the skill retrievable later. *Why new:* the claim that irrelevant but copied structure aids retention is testable and odd. **Test:** toy agent demos with irrelevant steps; full-fidelity vs. pruned vs. never-copied. [R] after 3 later skills.

**C28 · Object permanence for memory slots.** Infants learn that hidden objects still exist. A memory slot that has not been read for a long time is "hidden", not deleted; the model is trained to predict its contents still exist and reactivate them when cues reappear. *Why new:* retention probes stale memory; this trains an explicit permanence prediction. **Test:** KV memory agent, items unseen for N steps; permanence loss vs. none. [R] on old items.

**C29 · Pretend play as sandboxed learning.** Children learn in pretend frames ("this banana is a phone") without changing their beliefs about bananas. Train new or counterfactual skills under a *pretend frame token*; learning inside the frame must leave outside-frame behaviour unchanged (a KL penalty outside). *Why new:* L1 tags versions; pretend tags *counterfactual worlds* that can later be promoted to reality or discarded. **Test:** GPT-2 small, counterfactual facts under pretend tag; outside-frame KL drift [R] vs. untagged training.

**C30 · Exploratory play phases between tasks.** Children's play is free exploration with no goal. After each task, the agent spends a short budget on intrinsically motivated exploration of the *old* environments, which may refresh old skills more cheaply than replay. *Why new:* replay replays stored data; play generates new data in old worlds. **Test:** MiniGrid 4 envs sequentially; play phases vs. replay at equal env steps. [R] on old envs.

**C31 · Curiosity by learning progress (Oudeyer) over old tasks too.** Choose which task to train next by the rate of change of competence, including *negative* progress (forgetting). Old tasks being forgotten become attractive again. *Why new:* learning-progress curricula maximize progress; adding forgetting rate as attractive turns the curriculum into a self-repair loop. **Test:** 8 tasks; LP-with-forgetting vs. LP vs. round-robin at equal steps. [R].

**C32 · Selective trust (epistemic vigilance).** Children track informant reliability and learn more from reliable ones. Keep per-source reliability and scale write strength by it; reliability is updated by later agreement with consensus. *Why new:* noisy-label CL weights samples; per-source trust that evolves and gates writes is a CL-specific primitive. Near: C19, surprisingly-popular fleet memory (killed). **Test:** 5 sources, 2 unreliable; trust-gated writes vs. uniform. [R] on clean facts.

**C33 · Natural pedagogy cues (Csibra & Gergely).** Ostensive cues ("look, this is how") make infants treat information as generic and transferable. Tag data as *pedagogical* (generalize widely) or *incidental* (store locally). The tag decides write scope: shared weights vs. an episodic adapter. *Why new:* the write-scope decision comes from the intent of the teacher, not the content. **Test:** mixed stream; tag-scoped writes vs. all-shared. [R] and over-generalization errors.

**C34 · Theory-of-mind for one's own past self.** A model learns to model *what its earlier version believed* (a false-belief task about itself), so it can explain and undo changes. *Why new:* L1 reproduces past selves by tag; this trains a *predictor* of the past self that can answer "what did v3 think and why did that change". **Test:** GPT-2 small, 4 updates; predict v_k answers on probes; use predictions to restore v_k behaviour. [R].

**C35 · Bootstrapping via placeholders (Carey).** Children learn number words as placeholders before they understand counting, then a sudden induction gives them meaning. Learn new concepts first as *empty symbols* bound only by relations among themselves, then fill them with grounding later. *Why new:* old knowledge is touched only when grounding fills in, possibly reducing interference during the long placeholder phase. **Test:** symbolic relational tasks; placeholder-first vs. grounded-first. [R].

**C36 · Analogy as transfer with bookkeeping (Gentner structure mapping).** New tasks are learned as a *mapping* onto an old task (relation alignment) plus a residual. The old task is never touched; only the mapping and residual are stored. *Why new:* task arithmetic adds vectors; this stores an explicit alignment between tasks' internal relational structures. **Test:** pairs of isomorphic toy tasks; mapping+residual vs. full fine-tune. [R] on the source.

**C37 · Category learning by contrast sets.** Children learn "dog" faster when shown "not dog". Each write includes automatically generated near-miss negatives drawn from *old* categories so the new boundary is drawn without moving old ones. *Why new:* contrastive CL uses random negatives; near-miss old-category negatives make the new write explicitly respect old boundaries. **Test:** split CIFAR; near-miss old negatives vs. random negatives vs. replay at equal samples. [R].

**C38 · Learning to learn words (vocabulary spurt).** After ~50 words, toddler learning accelerates. Measure whether a model shows a learning-rate spurt after N tasks, and train a meta-signal that predicts the spurt; after the spurt, lower plasticity in old regions without hurting speed. *Why new:* a predicted, measurable regime change in CL used as a schedule. **Test:** 30 small tasks; detect spurt, then compare spurt-gated protection vs. constant. [R].

## D. Metacognition, sleep and offline processes

**C39 · Sleep stages as distinct operators.** NREM replays and consolidates; REM recombines and abstracts. Run two offline operators: NREM = replay of recent episodes into slow weights; REM = generate hybrids of old and new episodes and train on them only if they stay consistent with both. *Why new:* sleep-replay CL exists; the paired operator with consistency-gated recombination is the piece. Near: dreaming papers, SM weight dreaming, W5 tides. **Test:** MLP 10 tasks; NREM only vs. NREM+REM at equal offline steps. [R].

**C40 · Infantile amnesia as format migration.** Early memories are lost because representations change. Make it a feature: when representations are reorganized, migrate old memories into the new format with a translator trained on a small anchor set, rather than preserving the old format. *Why new:* the forgetting cause is representational drift; the primitive is a *format migration* step, like a database migration. Near: B1 schema migrations (CL-bench flood). Differs by being learned on anchors inside the model. **Test:** encoder retrained; translator on 5% anchors vs. replay. [R].

**C41 · Feeling of knowing gates retrieval vs. learning.** Before answering, the model estimates whether it knows; if yes, answer from weights; if no, retrieve or learn. The FOK signal is trained against later correctness. *Why new:* close to metamemory (killed #9: crowded). Differs: FOK used as a *write gate* (don't learn what you already know), cutting redundant writes that cause interference. **Test:** fact stream with 50% repeats; FOK-gated writes vs. always-write. [R].

**C42 · Self-explanation consolidation.** Students who explain worked examples to themselves learn more. After each task, the model writes an explanation, then trains on the explanation *and* the original data. *Why new:* self-quiz replay (falsified) did not beat teacher QA. Differs: explanation of *why* across tasks, tying new to old explicitly. **Test:** compare to teacher-written explanation and filler at equal tokens. [R].

**C43 · Offline rehearsal by imagined practice (mental simulation).** Athletes improve by imagining practice. Use the model's own world model to simulate old-task episodes and train on them during idle time. *Why new:* generative replay exists; for agents, imagined rollouts in a learned world model for *old* envs are less tested. **Test:** Dreamer-style tiny agent on 3 envs; imagined old-env rollouts vs. real replay. [R].

**C44 · Judgments of learning as an update budget.** Students allocate study time by perceived difficulty. The model allocates its update budget to items with low JOL, and stops on items with high JOL; JOL calibration is itself tracked. *Why new:* budgeted CL; the JOL signal predicts retention. Near: C14. **Test:** 20 tasks; JOL-allocated vs. uniform budget. [R].

## E. Concepts, theories and conceptual change

**C45 · Theory theory: beliefs as a revisable causal graph.** Children act like scientists, revising theories. Keep knowledge as a small causal graph alongside weights; an update first edits the graph, and only graph-consistent weight updates are allowed. *Why new:* close to B6 edge-addressable weights; the difference is the gating by graph consistency rather than one-to-one mapping. **Test:** synthetic causal worlds; graph-gated vs. ungated. [R].

**C46 · Conceptual change without forgetting the old theory.** Children keep naive theories alongside scientific ones (impetus vs. Newton). Store a superseded theory under a "naive" tag instead of erasing it; this allows explaining errors and reverting. *Why new:* L1 tags versions in time; this tags *competing theories* that coexist and are chosen by context. **Test:** two physics regimes; coexisting tags vs. overwrite. [R].

**C47 · Concept differentiation (heat vs. temperature).** Children's single concept later splits into two. Primitive: a *split* operation that duplicates a representational direction and trains the copies apart only on discriminating examples, leaving all other uses unchanged. *Why new:* NB20 subfunctionalization duplicates genes; this is targeted concept splitting in a pretrained model. **Test:** GPT-2 merged concept probes; split vs. fine-tune. [R].

**C48 · Coalescence (merge two concepts).** The inverse: two concepts discovered to be one (morning star / evening star). Merge their directions and redirect all dependent behaviour. *Why new:* merging concepts inside a model, with redirection, is the inverse of C47. Near: redirect table fix (reviewer pattern). **Test:** synthetic aliases; merge vs. fine-tune. [R].

**C49 · Essentialism as invariance prior.** Children believe categories have hidden essences. Train each category with an invariance to surface transformations, so new tasks that change surface statistics don't move category cores. *Why new:* invariance training for robustness, applied as a CL protection. **Test:** rotated-MNIST CL; essence loss vs. none. [R].

## F. Social and cultural learning

**C50 · Protection by copying (cultural ratchet lock-in).** In cumulative culture an innovation survives because many people copy it, not because one person guards it. In a fleet sharing one lesson/adapter store, an item's protection from overwriting grows with the number of independent agents that re-derived or adopted it, and items nobody copies decay. *Why new:* iterated learning (ledger #17, killed fix) is about refining content across generations; this is about *retention as a social quantity*. Convergent-edit promotion (HW1, stopped at the variance gate) promoted edits by rediscovery; this protects *already learned* items by adoption count, which needs far less cross-family spread. Near: E31 Tasmania. **Test:** 8 Qwen2.5-0.5B agents on the G1 router with a shared store under a capacity cap; adoption-weighted retention vs. LRU vs. utility counters. [R] on returning regimes; control: one agent with 8× the episodes.

**C51 · Protégé replay (learning by teaching).** Tutors retain more than learners. After each task, the model teaches a much smaller student from scratch on a small curriculum it writes itself; wherever the student fails on old tasks, those items become the teacher's replay set. *Why new:* distillation flows one way; here the student is a *probe of what is no longer transmissible*, a cheap forgetting detector that needs no stored old data. **Test:** GPT-2 small teacher, 2-layer student, 5 tasks; student-failure replay vs. random replay vs. loss-ranked replay at equal tokens. [R].

**C52 · Faded scaffolds (graded internalization).** In apprenticeship the scaffold is withdrawn gradually as success rises. A new skill starts fully in context (retrieved demos, tool), and each success removes a fraction of the scaffold tokens while a small adapter is trained to replace the missing part, until the skill runs on weights alone. *Why new:* context-to-weights methods (KAFT-style, context distillation) move a whole prompt at once; fading makes internalization a success-controlled schedule and gives a per-skill "internalization level". Watch for double dosing (M3 killed: no overshoot), which fading should avoid by construction. **Test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B, 4 rule skills; success-controlled fading vs. one-shot context distillation vs. keeping the context. [R] and tokens at inference.

**C53 · Names as composable skill handles.** Labels speed children's category learning. Each learned skill gets a word-like name trained as its handle, and names compose ("count-then-sort") to call skill combinations never trained together. *Why new:* L1 tags whole model versions; names here address *pieces*, and the test is zero-shot composition of handles. Near: ToolkenGPT (B2 killed for frozen backbone) — difference: handles are trained into an unfrozen model and composition is the target. **Test:** 6 string skills on GPT-2 small, names learned per skill; accuracy on unseen pairs vs. natural-language descriptions of the same skills. [R].

**C54 · Learnability bottleneck as a retention test.** Language stays learnable because each generation relearns it from limited data. After each update, train a fresh small learner on a fixed small sample of the model's outputs; knowledge that the fresh learner cannot recover is flagged as "non-transmissible" and is either refactored or replayed. *Why new:* unlike iterated learning (#17), the bottleneck is a diagnostic over the *current* model, and the claim is that transmissible knowledge forgets less. **Test:** correlate per-task transmissibility with forgetting after the next task across 3 seeds; gate on target variance first (ledger gotcha). [R].

## G. Agents and embodiment

**C55 · Affordance-indexed skills.** Children learn what objects let them do. Store agent skills keyed by object affordances ("graspable", "openable") rather than by task; a new task only learns a composition over affordance skills. *Why new:* options/skill libraries key by task or goal; an affordance key makes skills reusable across tasks that share objects and puts new learning in the composer, away from old skills. **Test:** MiniGrid with keys/doors/boxes in 4 task orders; affordance library vs. task-keyed library vs. single policy. [R].

**C56 · Learned cognitive offloading.** People offload to notes what they expect to forget. The agent learns, per fact, whether to put it in an external note or in its weights, trained on later retrieval success and on interference with old tasks. *Why new:* memory routing is crowded; the new target is *interference avoided* as the routing reward, not access cost. **Test:** agent with a notebook on a fact stream; learned offload vs. always-weights vs. always-notes. [R] on old weight-stored facts.

**C57 · Body schema as the adaptation layer.** Infants learn a model of their own body before tasks. Keep a small self-model (action → effect); when actuators or tools change, adapt only the self-model and leave the skills that call it untouched. *Why new:* CL under embodiment change usually fine-tunes policies; a dedicated self-model layer predicts zero forgetting for skills if the change is purely bodily. **Test:** gridworld with permuted action mapping; self-model-only update vs. policy fine-tune. [R] on 4 old skills.

**C58 · Habit/goal arbitration with devaluation probes.** Repeated skills become habits that ignore outcome changes. Skills start as model-based plans, are cached into habits after repetition, and are pulled back to planning when a *devaluation probe* (outcome value changed) fails. *Why new:* the devaluation test as a continual-learning probe for stale cached skills. **Test:** toy reward-devaluation task; arbitration vs. pure habit vs. pure planning; [R] and compute.

**C59 · Joint-attention credit in fleets.** Infants learn words when they and the adult attend to the same thing. In a fleet, a shared lesson is written only when two agents independently attended to the same evidence span (overlapping attention or citations). *Why new:* fleet memory without evidence agreement writes noise; this is a cheap two-witness write rule. Near: kinetic proofreading (#21 physics). **Test:** 4 agents, 30% noisy lessons; joint-attention gate vs. majority vote. [R].

**C60 · Growth curriculum for embodiment drift.** A child's body grows slowly, so control is always adapting. Train agents with slow, ordered drift in their dynamics during learning and test whether later abrupt changes cost less forgetting. *Why new:* domain randomization is i.i.d.; growth is ordered and might teach the network to keep a separable "body" factor. **Test:** gridworld step size drifts; growth vs. randomization vs. fixed. [R].

## H. Wilder ones

**C61 · A child model that is periodically reborn.** A "child" learner is fast and forgetful and is reset every K tasks; an "adult" learns slowly only from the child's stable outputs. *Why new:* CLS-like, but the periodic rebirth keeps plasticity by construction. Near: C24, reset methods. **Test:** permuted MNIST 20 tasks; reborn child + adult vs. one model vs. shrink-perturb. [R].

**C62 · Naps vs. one sleep.** Children's naps help consolidation. Hold total offline steps fixed and vary how often consolidation happens (every update vs. every task). *Why new:* frequency of offline consolidation as the variable; predicts an optimum. **Test:** frequency sweep at equal offline steps on 10 tasks. [R].

**C63 · Imaginary companion as a veto critic.** A small internal persona trained only on old tasks objects to updates that would harm them, scaling down those gradient components. *Why new:* a learned critic instead of a fixed penalty (EWC). **Test:** veto critic vs. EWC vs. A-GEM on 10 tasks. [R].

**C64 · Narrative replay.** Children retell their day. Replay old experiences as ordered, causally linked stories instead of shuffled samples. *Why new:* ordered narrative replay vs. i.i.d. replay; predicts better retention of temporal dependencies. **Test:** episodic agent; narrative vs. shuffled at equal tokens. [R].
