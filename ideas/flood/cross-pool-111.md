# Cross-pool merges, round 111

Focal source: `tree/root-population-learners.md` (PR #379, PL#). Older sources: `tree/test-time-learners-5.md` (PR #378, T5-#; focal in round 110), `tree/root-test-time-learners.md` (PR #373, TT#), learning from other models (PR #358, LM#), deployment feedback 5 (PR #357, D5-#), deployment feedback 4 (PR #355, D4-#), deployment feedback 3 (PR #354, D3-#), and relearnability as memory (PR #336, CR#). Raw proposals for harmless synthetic populations.

**PEX1. Transport the improvement without importing the donor’s answer to a local disagreement.** Parts: PL01 bridge packet, PL02 disagreement preservation, PL04 traveling exception, PL07 four-corner fusion, PL08 functional cherry-pick, PL14 slice-specific ancestry, T5-22 diagnose scope before splitting, D3-09 behavioral witness identity. *Mechanism:* Describe a proposed transfer as a common behavioral improvement plus scoped contrasts rather than a tensor diff. In recipient scratch state compare both update orders and verify that the shared change improves both branches while preserving each locally valid exception. Where an exception cannot be identified from shared fixtures, leave it local. *New:* Transfer can preserve the practical ability to disagree, even if the recipient implements the gain differently or has diverged since the most recent shared checkpoint. *First test:* Use benign ordering rules with a common improvement and contradictory local exceptions. Compare parameter averaging, routed standalone patches and recipient-verified behavioral transfer.

**PEX2. Merge only if each descendant can still learn its own next correction.** Parts: PL03 successor stress test, PL05 joint teaching, PL09 live alternative, PL16 continuation swap, PL22 inherit the disagreement, PL24 conditional optimizer transfer, D4-24 future gradient witness, TT34 joint sandbox. *Mechanism:* Test the proposed shared state against later authorized corrections for each branch and compare the functional update responses with separate forks. Keep a small alternative and an observation that would reopen it. Test transferred optimizer state separately from transferred behavior, and retain independent routes when joint service hides asymmetric loss of learnability. *New:* A merge can preserve current service yet fail because one of the two valid next updates becomes harder or impossible. *First test:* Create two synthetic forks with matching current predictions and deliberately different responses to a late exception. Compare output-only acceptance, separate forks and continuation-checked merging at matched update work.

**PEX3. Send the question before the patch, and count evidence by origin.** Parts: PL06 bounded rejection, PL10 discriminating question, PL11 witness lineage, PL12 scoped abstention, PL13 evidence-first gossip, PL15 recipient receipt, LM26 capability handshake, D3-13 compatible histories. *Mechanism:* Share an approved diagnostic question before proposing a write; each peer answers using local fixtures and records scope and evidence lineage. Treat inherited duplicates as one observation, preserve unknowns, and issue a recipient-specific receipt only after the proposed improvement has been reproduced within the declared scope. *New:* The population can learn which forks disagree without conflating inherited votes with independent confirmation or extending a receipt to untested settings. *First test:* Build a synthetic population in which many descendants inherit one wrong convention while a few hold independent evidence. Compare majority vote, unconditional patch gossip and lineage-aware question-first transfer.

**PEX4. Allocate scarce trials to the missing complement rather than the most popular patch.** Parts: PL17 repair after refutation, PL18 price the complement, PL19 local audit, PL20 dependency cost, PL21 protected trial slots, TT25 reversible commitment, D5-26 teaching after compression. *Mechanism:* Allocate scratch trials to candidates that fill a declared capability gap, subject to owner-approved local evaluation and explicitly counted dependencies. Compare a compact dependent proposal and a self-contained one with the same eventual behavior. A failed local trial can nominate a narrowly scoped repair without committing anything to serving state. *New:* The trial budget buys measurable future learning options rather than copies of the best-looking standalone patch. *First test:* Use toy populations with complementary skills and a shared feature scheduled to disappear. Compare popularity-based selection, standalone gain and dependency-aware allocation at equal trial work.

**PEX5. Keep the lineage evidence needed to challenge what descendants inherit.** Parts: PL23 negative genealogy, PL25 leave-one-ancestor-out, PL26 stable skill identifier, PL27 protected divergent seed, LM32 behavioral lineage contract, D5-18 approved replacement lesson. *Mechanism:* Attach a versioned behavioral fingerprint and failed witness to each transferable skill. Test a leave-one-ancestor-out successor in scratch state where dependencies are unclear, preserving a divergent seed only if it supports an otherwise lost update. Replace expired fixtures with approved behavioral equivalents instead of assuming old identifiers still denote the same skill. *New:* Lineage retention can preserve useful disagreement and prevent repeated failed writes without retaining every ancestor checkpoint. *First test:* Construct descendants that independently learn one shared convention while only one branch can adapt to a new exception. Compare checkpoint quotas and witness-guided retention by recovery, storage and future learning.

**PEX6. Retire an obsolete teacher without breaking independent coverage.** Parts: PL28 stale-copy check, PL29 teaching-path cut, PL30 last executable path, PL31 independent relearning, PL32 preserve convergent implementation, CR29 versioned relearning, T5-18 discrete core and continuous envelope. *Mechanism:* Audit the known teaching routes of an obsolete toy convention, separating independent implementations from stale ancestral copies. Test detachment and current-version recovery on scratch copies and keep an executable current-version path for rare valid behavior. If evidence cannot distinguish independent coverage from replay, do not claim complete retirement. *New:* Retirement can remove a source and its downstream re-teaching routes without erasing behavior that other learners still legitimately need. *First test:* Use synthetic genealogies with both inherited and independently derived formatting rules. Compare deletion by checkpoint age, deletion by behavior and route-aware retirement after a version change.

**PEX7. Share improvements across learners without collapsing the differences needed for later learning.** Parts: PL01 bridge packet, PL02 disagreement preservation, PL04 traveling exception, PL07 four-corner fusion, PL08 functional cherry-pick, PL14 slice-specific ancestry, T5-22 diagnose scope before splitting, D3-09 behavioral witness identity, PL03 successor stress test, PL05 joint teaching, PL09 live alternative, PL16 continuation swap, PL22 inherit the disagreement, PL24 conditional optimizer transfer, D4-24 future gradient witness, TT34 joint sandbox, PL06 bounded rejection, PL10 discriminating question, PL11 witness lineage, PL12 scoped abstention, PL13 evidence-first gossip, PL15 recipient receipt, LM26 capability handshake, D3-13 compatible histories, PL17 repair after refutation, PL18 price the complement, PL19 local audit, PL20 dependency cost, PL21 protected trial slots, TT25 reversible commitment, D5-26 teaching after compression, PL23 negative genealogy, PL25 leave-one-ancestor-out, PL26 stable skill identifier, PL27 protected divergent seed, LM32 behavioral lineage contract, D5-18 approved replacement lesson, PL28 stale-copy check, PL29 teaching-path cut, PL30 last executable path, PL31 independent relearning, PL32 preserve convergent implementation, CR29 versioned relearning, T5-18 discrete core and continuous envelope. *Mechanism:* Exchange questions and scoped receipts before weights, verify shared improvements against branch-specific next updates, budget trials for missing complements, and retire obsolete teaching routes only after verifying remaining coverage. Preserve independent disagreement and the evidence that can reopen a shared decision. *New:* A population can trade reusable improvements without treating agreement, inherited evidence and shared future learnability as the same thing. *First test:* Build harmless synthetic fork populations with inherited votes, complementary skills, legitimate scope differences and obsolete conventions. Compare independent forks, indiscriminate merging and verified selective exchange on coverage, cost and subsequent learning.
