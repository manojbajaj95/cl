# Cross-pool merges, round 13

Focal source: `tree/survival-probability-memory.md` (PR #112, SP#). Crosses: B27 world-persistence hazards (`ideas/flood/cl-bench.md`, PR #48), Q8 reachability-based forgetting (round 4), the rate–drift law (PRs #6 and #105; round 11 RD#), and optimizer memory, merger certificates, and lossy checkpoints (PRs #109–111; round 12 OS#). The probability of reaching a compatible region before a failure region is conditional on the declared curriculum and update policy. It is neither the stored answer nor a guarantee of service. These are raw and unfiltered.

**SV1. Expiry needs three clocks, not one hazard.** Parts: SP8 transport versus relabeling, SP13 repair windows, B27 world persistence (PR #48), Q8, RD9, OS6, OS10. *Mechanism:* Track separately when a claim stops being true in the world, when the learner can no longer decode its description, and when it loses the last available legal repair. A still-true claim can warrant a residual even when the learner can't read it today; an obsolete claim can be retired without treating that as neural forgetting. *New:* A fitted lifespan becomes a budget over semantic validity, decoder validity, and repair access rather than a single age penalty. *First test:* Add independent truth flips, decoder drift and intervention windows to the B27 transmitter toy. Compare oracle-separated and learned clocks with one global decay, Q8 eviction and equal-byte explicit intent.

**SV2. Spend a fork only if it changes the retention contract you would fund.** Parts: SP9 diagnostic value, SP19 preserve write order, SP22 observation cost, B27, RD7, OS4. *Mechanism:* Maintain competing explanations for at-risk writes: changed curriculum, expiring world rule, or hidden optimizer memory. Buy the cheapest payload-blind observation that can change the decision among protect, carry, or retire under the worst plausible explanation; otherwise make the decision without estimating survival everywhere. *New:* Measure the value of a probe in a concrete saved write, replay window, or stored bit, not improvement in a risk score. *First test:* Enumerate a chain with overlapping observations and distinct optimal interventions. Compare decision regret and all-in cost against always probing, fixed probe budgets and direct margin thresholding.

**SV3. A decoder that stores its own migration trigger separately from the thing it decodes.** Parts: SP5 rank transport, SP21 survival of the read protocol, RD1/RD6, OS1/OS8, Q8. *Mechanism:* Read a live bit from the ordering of two fixed legal fork outcomes while both exceed an explicit separation margin. Track evidence that the sign is losing separation even while direct predictions agree. On a certified impending loss, route to a new fork dictionary or carry the bit externally; do not infer the bit from its risk estimate. *New:* The read protocol becomes an expiring dependency of the rate–drift code, rather than an uncharged observer that always works. *First test:* Use the opposed-committor chain from PR #112, then change its transition law to contract or invert the contrast. Compare charged migration with direct intent, pooled hazard and same-budget raw read trajectories.

**SV4. Teach the optimizer to answer a question on a copy, not the old task by accident.** Parts: SP1 differential register, SP3 soft parity, SP24 raw trajectories, OS2/OS4, RD2. *Mechanism:* Write two payloads with matched installation behavior and initial service risk. Read them via a fixed payload-blind fork; require the decoded bit to select a legal discharge on the live host, which must then serve the old task after its moment is deleted. Charge all fork transitions and retained state. *New:* Temporary optimizer memory earns credit only if the fork response contains identifiable payload and can be turned into direct service, not merely predict survival. *First test:* Train a two-feature momentum oracle with ambiguous support, then swap buffers. Test payload decoding and post-discharge behavior against direct state probes, a matched trajectory decoder and a carried explicit bit.

**SV5. Protect the exit that strands the learner, not the exit that hurts most.** Parts: SP13 recoverable versus irreversible, SP16 wait versus act, SP17 competing exits, OS6, RD4, Q8. *Mechanism:* Attach competing-exit risks to old rules: a service breach with an affordable repair, or a quieter event that closes every legal repair path. Allocate backup to the latter, while permitting recovery from the former within a declared service allowance. Refuse to buy a backup that cannot reach the live host in time. *New:* A small invisible drift can deserve protection ahead of a much larger reversible error because it destroys a future action, not merely margin. *First test:* Build matched terminal-loss chains with different exits from the repairable set. Compare irreversible failures, time below the service floor and backup bytes against maximum-loss replay and item-hazard eviction.

**SV6. Retire a fork dictionary entry only when no live decision can miss it.** Parts: SP4 shared fork dictionary, SP10 overlap limits, SP12 forget a law, B27, Q8, RD4, OS9. *Mechanism:* Maintain reachability from live obligations to fork evidence, residual carriers, and the legal repair actions each supports. Retire an entry only if remaining observable evidence keeps every reached write/repair decision unchanged across its plausible effects. Mark claims under a nonoverlapping stream as unknown rather than transferring their fitted hazard. *New:* Reachability garbage collection operates on decision dependence instead of retrieval count; a silent entry can keep many otherwise cheap descriptions usable. *First test:* Use a three-class B27 stream with a late returning class and one shared fork entry. Compare decision errors, bytes and cost to rebuild against usage-based eviction, Q8 hazard-only ordering and full memory.

**SV7. Find the point where every payload-blind read becomes the same.** Parts: SP2 challenge–response, SP20 matched-mean counterfeit, SP23 capacity before collapse, OS3/OS11, RD1/RD5. *Mechanism:* For a finite learner, compute the transcript distributions of admissible payload-blind fork policies as the live state drifts. Track where two writes become indistinguishable within the read budget, separating genuine full-state collisions from missing measurements and noisy overlap. Use the first such horizon to choose between extra read compute, a different write and an external code. *New:* A rate curve can depend on the permitted reader and its compute, not just on the number of distinguishable physical states. *First test:* Enumerate two optimizer states with identical margins and equal pooled risk, one readable by a two-step fork and the other pair exactly merged. Compare with current-margin characterization and direct optimizer inspection, charging the extra operations.

**SV8. Leave no old obligation exposed to a hidden change of curriculum regime.** Parts: SP7 unknown regime versus switching, SP11 transferable counterexample, SP14 joint first failures, B27, OS5/OS6, RD8. *Mechanism:* Price a compressed write under two separately declared stream contracts: one unknown fixed regime and one allowed to switch within the episode. Use shared fallback only when the correlated failure set stays within the stated service allowance; a counterexample route revokes just the unstated transport assumption, not the old rule. *New:* Rate–drift forecasts become conditional on a legal regime grammar and joint losses, rather than averages over easy-to-sample paths. *First test:* Build two individually benign kernels whose allowed alternation closes a repair window. Compare residual costs and joint service failures under fixed-regime calibration, switched certification and unconditional full intent.

**SV9. Allocate fork reads like expiring options on a shared batch.** Parts: SP9 diagnostic value, SP13 repair windows, SP22 priced observations, MC7 last exercise (PR #110), MC10 shared replay (PR #110), RD4, OS9. *Mechanism:* Two obligations can compete for the same live update budget but share a diagnostic fork. If the fork differentiates which repair is needed, reserve one live update for whichever needs it; if both can need it, carry a discriminator or use a checked joint repair. Estimate the option's expiry from the last legal intervention, not the first fall in confidence. *New:* A shared probe can retire multiple prospective updates without assuming the underlying failures are independent. *First test:* Enumerate a two-obligation finite editor with a shared batch, stochastic demand and one live replay slot. Compare against earliest-deadline and highest-hazard policies at equal calls and storage.

**SV10. Use censored returns to decide what survival evidence is worth carrying.** Parts: SP10 overlap-limited revaluation, SP15 placement by avoidable risk, SP19 decision-preserving compression, B27, Q8, RD1, OS6. *Mechanism:* Keep just enough stopped trajectories to discriminate future decisions about where to carry a still-live rule. Treat a trace that ends before either boundary as censored rather than a failure or success; if return behavior is insufficiently observed, retain a conservative backup instead of declaring the item obsolete. *New:* The unit of retention is evidence needed to place a live payload, not an estimate of every item's lifetime. *First test:* Vary observation censoring independently of real survival in the B27 three-class toy. Compare storage, regret and late-return coverage against dropping unfinished traces, fixed backup allocation and a hazard fit that ignores the decoder.

**SV11. A memory controller that knows when to wait, when to replay, and when to let go.** Parts: SP1 differential register, SP4 shared fork dictionary, SP5 rank transport, SP7 curriculum families, SP8 transport and relabeling, SP9 diagnostic value, SP10 overlap limits, SP12 forgetting a law, SP13 recoverable breach, SP14 joint failure, SP16 wait/act boundary, SP17 competing exits, SP19 decision compression, SP21 read survival, SP22 observation price, SP23 capacity, SP24 raw-trajectory null, B27 world persistence, Q8 reachability GC, MC7 last replay exercise (PR #110), MC8 reopen a write (PR #110), MC17 scope revocation (PR #110), RD1, RD2, RD4, RD5, RD7, RD8, RD9, OS1, OS2, OS4, OS6, OS9, OS11, OS12. *Mechanism:* Track three live clocks: how long a rule remains valid in the world, how long the current decoder distinguishes it, and how long the learner can still act on it. Install a writable effect where possible; use shared payload-blind forks only when their outcomes change a decision. Before the relevant clock runs out, choose a legal replay, migrate the code, or carry a minimal discriminator with a checked recovery route. Garbage-collect evidence only when it no longer supports any reachable decision. Never let a precise forecast stand in for the rule it is about. *New:* Memory management becomes a joint allocation of persistent bits, read computation, and expiring repair opportunities under a stated service contract. *First test:* Compose the B27 silent-return world with an enumerable optimizer that has one ambiguous fork, one exact merger and one time-limited shared replay slot. Match old service floors and new-task progress; compare total bytes, probe calls, late repairs and wrong retirements against fixed decay, full intent, Q8 and highest-risk replay.
