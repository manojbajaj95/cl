# Weight-space programming for continual learning

## Bottom line

Weights are not a stable address space: the same function can be represented by permuted or rescaled coordinates, and a model upgrade can also change the function and erase features. Current work shows how to install, generate, merge, transfer, or route weights under particular conditions. It does not make those operations interchangeable. The promising frontier is **continual specialization after the source checkpoint is gone, with individual changes still testable and removable**. Portability, restoration, and retention should be measured separately instead of inferred from one average accuracy.

This is a targeted survey of arXiv papers and source pages available through November 2026, not a systematic review. Numbers below are author-reported, not independently reproduced. Publication status varies; recent preprints are evidence of overlap, not established results.

## What the methods buy, and where they break

| Family and sources | Demonstrated capability | Failure or gap for continual learning |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight-space programming (WSP)**: [Sparks](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/), [WeightBench, 2611.18553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18553), [WeightCode, 2611.25476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25476) | An executable procedure inspects and writes weights rather than retaining only a tensor delta. WeightCode reports 0→99.8% exact match on held-out SCAN compositions; ten procedures rerun on five independently trained ViTs retained 83–110% of source gains on average, and six second-round procedures retained earlier noise robustness while adding contrast robustness. | Portable programs and successive edits are **already** prior art. Compatible architectures, informative examples, and checked behaviors are material; prose-program execution, unlimited succession, independent removal, fresh checkpoints after data retirement, and reliable abstention are not established by these results. A large editor used at install time is a real cost even when absent at inference. |
| **WSP caveats**: [Exploring WSP, 2611.09382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382), [WSP writes ICL, 2611.26841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26841), [spatial comparison, 2611.27089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27089), [memorization challenge, 2611.08070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08070) | Local edits can produce genuine gains; the ICL study often finds a residual task vector materialized in weights and reports near fine-tuning performance for finished runs on its tasks. | Wording shifts, unsuccessful runs, and the editor's prior knowledge matter. In the spatial comparison, rank-one supervised LoRA greatly outperforms the tested hand/programmed edits. Evaluate from fixed budgets, count non-completions, and include strong scripted and gradient baselines. |
| **Neural functionals and symmetries**: [NFN, 2302.14040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14040), [DWSNets, 2301.12780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12780), [NFT, 2305.13546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13546), [transformer NFN, 2410.04209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04209) | Permutation-equivariant/invariant layers reason over model weight tensors; architectures can operate on weights instead of individual examples. Useful for learned optimization, representations, and predicting properties of networks. | Permutation equivariance does not guarantee positive scale equivariance or invariance to real feature drift. A weight-level train/test split can leak initialization or training recipe; hold out entire checkpoint lineages and task families. |
| **Graph metanetworks and alignment**: [GMN, 2312.04501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04501), [scale-equivariant GMN, 2406.10685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10685), [Git Re-Basin, 2209.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836), [WSL survey, 2603.10090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10090) | Neural computation graphs support variable architectures; scale-equivariant GMNs account for positive rescaling where the architecture admits it. Re-Basin aligns hidden permutations before weight merging. | Graph coverage and exact symmetry can improve sample efficiency, but neither solves incompatible features or lost capacity. Even exact alignment produces no retention guarantee for a newly added patch; separate gauge changes from real release shifts. |
| **Hypernetworks for CL**: [task-conditioned hypernetworks, 1906.00695](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695), [prototype-augmented hypernetworks, 2505.07450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07450) | Protect previously generated weights by regularizing outputs for old task embeddings; prototype-conditioned head generation and distillation amortize task parameters. | Old task identifiers, embeddings, or a functioning router are usually required; output regularization, conditioning capacity, and inference storage/compute must be counted as tasks accumulate. Keeping generated weights stable does not protect outputs of a changed shared trunk. |
| **Weight generation**: [p-diff, 2402.13144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13144), [Cond P-Diff, 2408.01415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01415), [Text-to-LoRA, 2506.06105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105), [Drag-and-Drop LLMs, 2506.16406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16406), [LoRA Diffusion, 2412.02352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02352), [ORAL, 2503.24354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24354) | Diffusion/autoencoding sample plausible models or conditional adapters. Description- or prompt-conditioned hypernetworks generate adapters quickly once trained; ORAL also conditions on architecture. LoRA Diffusion synthesizes personalization weights using a hypernetwork; it is not the same method as diffusion on weights. | These amortize a substantial checkpoint corpus and may reproduce its task/base/coordinate biases. Fast generation is not reliable lifelong consolidation or transfer to a held-out release. Compare with trivial copy, PCA, and retrieval; [Adapters as Data, 2611.07775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07775) makes linear/weight-space baselines particularly important. |
| **Task vectors and merging**: [task arithmetic, 2212.04089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089), [TIES, 2306.01708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708), [DARE, 2311.03099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03099), [AdaMerging, 2310.02575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02575) | Same-origin deltas can sometimes be added, scaled, or negated; TIES trims and elects signs, DARE drops/rescales deltas, and AdaMerging fits mixing coefficients using unlabeled target inputs. | Sign conflicts and redundant coordinates are not the only failure modes: [composition validity, 2608.09490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09490) finds prompt-conditioned functional interactions. Common origin, coefficient tuning, and calibration access matter. For LoRA, merge effective products or use a factor-aware method; factors are not canonical. Negating a delta is not guaranteed behavioral rollback. |
| **Continual merging and subspaces**: [holistic continual merging, 2509.23592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23592), [aligned visual CL, 2506.03189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03189), [single-LoRA continual merging, 2512.23017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23017), [CoSO, 2505.11816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11816), [sketched Jacobians, 2112.05095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05095) | Linearization, calibration, orthogonal subspaces, and continual consolidation reduce interference under varying training/storage assumptions. Sketched Jacobians provide retention results under linear/wide-network assumptions. | The nullspace can saturate, function-linear approximations expire, and compression may erase rare skills. [Controlled forgetting experiments, 2507.23311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23311) find that shared knowledge survives linear merges more readily than task-specific knowledge. Include rare-skill tails, router-free and task-aware comparisons, and bytes across the whole lineage. |
| **Libraries and routing**: [LoraHub, 2307.13269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13269), [MoLE, 2404.13628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13628), [MoErging review, 2408.07057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07057) | Few-shot selection/composition and token/input routing reuse existing specialists. Routing can avoid forcing all skills through one static update. | Expert counts and routing errors can grow with tasks. Routing may require labeled mixtures and is not equivalent to task-agnostic CL. On overlapping and truly novel inputs, known-domain routing accuracy is poor evidence of safe routing. |
| **Model editing and removal**: [ROME, 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262), [MEMIT, 2210.07229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229), [AlphaEdit, 2410.02355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355), [GRACE, 2211.11031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11031), [WISE, 2405.14768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14768), [reversible routed LoRA, 2603.11239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11239), [MDM-OC, 2507.20997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20997) | Closed-form edits write facts; nullspace editing protects measured activations; modular memories and orthogonal deltas support sequential edits and selective reversal. | An exact rollback of tensor arithmetic or isolated module does not imply old behaviors survive mixed inputs, nor is it evidence of unlearning. Factual paraphrases may not cover skill execution. Measure removal, collateral retention, and router misfires on every release. |
| **Base upgrades**: [Trans-LoRA, 2405.17258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258), [LoRA-X, 2501.16559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16559), [LoRASuite, 2505.13515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13515), [Cross-LoRA, 2508.05232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05232), [GRAFT, 2611.18638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638), [Ariadne, 2610.32581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32581) | Weight/subspace transport, activation bridges, or teacher behavior can recover specializations. Ariadne uses activation effects, Procrustes maps, and ridge regression to port across depth/width. GRAFT retrieves and bridges across backbones. | Neither feature transport nor a closed-form effect fit is novel by itself. Distinguish paired inference with a legacy source (often available in these setups) from a new target arriving after source retirement. Include direct copy as a positive control on short continuations and failures on distant lineages. |
| **Operational checks**: [UpgradeBench, 2608.20918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918), [Heirloom, 2611.00650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00650), [MergeProbe, 2606.19549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19549), [Audit Before You Merge, 2611.10684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) | UpgradeBench evaluates actual release sequences and already tests decision gates; copying can fail even within a family. Heirloom distills a private specialist without original labels using the old model during migration. MergeProbe predicts interference; audited LoRA merging highlights incorrect provenance. | Deployment gates, held-out probes, and decisions are not new individually. Frozen probe budgets, late source retirement, independent deletion, open-set routing and cumulative error form a stronger joint test; it must still beat saved-teacher replay with equal resources. |

## Adjacent techniques worth importing

- [Tracr, 2301.05062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062) and [WSP with model checking, 2611.11604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11604): compile or verify explicit behavior in restricted models. Use deliberately identifiable toy tasks to test whether a method detects where its guarantees stop. Model checking on finite instances is not a neural retention certificate.
- [Conformal Risk Control, 2208.02814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814): evaluate selective systems as risk–coverage curves, with explicit assumptions. Never claim an exchangeability-based guarantee across an arbitrary new lineage or an unrepresented input mode.
- [Composable Interventions, 2407.06483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06483): test interventions singly and together, including order and counterfactual deactivation. A useful weight-space interface needs evidence about these changes, not just end-to-end average scores.
