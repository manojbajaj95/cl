# Repair-reveal next: keep the residual decision visible, release only the backing it no longer needs

**Status:** Ideas only. The example is a paper check, not a learner result. All first tests are paper or CPU proposals; no GPU.

**Parent:** [Repair-and-reveal steps](repair-reveal-steps.md) ([PR #289][parent]). **Review seeds:** [Flood triage, #289 verdict][triage] scores **#05** and **#18** at 3. #05 selects a naturally due C repair that preserves the separation needed for a different B decision in the actual public read. #18 retires only the repaired route's fallback liabilities that admit a common safe handover, preserving the residual joint calls. Follow the [idea ledger][ledger]: every first test must exercise normal old tasks, and an interaction has to beat the strongest stack of its parts.

**Verdict:** A useful object to look for is a **release-specific separating margin**: not enough to identify the old optimizer state, but enough to keep apart histories that disagree about backing a named release. A tiny example shows why marginal clearance is insufficient, not that joint design beats the same-report stack. If ordinary batch selection, a check and a correlated allocator can share the same report and reproduce the result at equal cost, keep the engineering shortcut and drop the primitive claim.

## Contract and strongest controls

- **Freeze the work and the read.** A is original work, B is installed work and its backed obligations, C is installed and incoming successor work. Fix the natural due C pool, coverage, deadlines, native optimizer, native report and precision before blind arrivals; charge displaced C records still owed. At decision time the complete public view defines a cell of reachable histories. A repair must improve the named B route, get it live before dependence changes, and preserve old service even in cells that will later refuse a release. A separator must work on executed, rounded reports, including clipping, ties and legal drift; a hidden slot or a later report is not evidence at this cut.
- **Prove a handover, not a smaller ledger.** For each proposed release, exhibit one implementable dispatch policy for all histories in the cell, including correlated residual calls, existing pinned borrowers and all advertised suffixes. It may respond to public call arrivals, not unobserved histories. No retroactive label or expiry counts as payment. Grant only against capacity remaining after the handover under **every** reachable joint call pattern. Keep canceled, late, and refused releases in the service accounting. Neither a tied marginal nor a decrease in expected load certifies that released capacity can be spent.
- **Let the stacked rival share the report.** Compare fixed due order, equal-progress repair selection without a separation constraint ([#273 #03][useful]), [detect-before-shrink][shrink] and [precision-aware checks][precision] followed by repair, then the best repair + lawful audit + exact correlated allocator with report reuse. Include never-release, full-release, [maintenance-duty transfer][handover], and a collapsed-margin or stranded-borrower negative. Give every arm the same information when it is legally available. An unrestricted same-information planner with the same actions is a ceiling, not a rival this proposal can beat by definition. Name the actual binding resource, e.g. a check tick, planning cost, or carried bytes.
- **Price service on the clock.** Match per-queue C progress, coverage, bytes, checks, precision, clocks, and starting old-task margins, or report a Pareto tradeoff. Measure on-time backed admissions, unused reserve, misses by cohort, and safety failures. Test independent held-out A, earlier B (both the repaired route and its existing fallback borrowers), and installed C before repair, at every exposed handover or refusal, and after real reuse on a later blind arrival. Do not average away a stranded borrower.

## Paper check: equal repair gain, different spendable release

Use SGD with step size 1 on one of two due C records with the same x target 1 and y targets 0 or 1. Their losses are `(x−1)²/2 + |y−t|/16`. Start x = 0 and y in {14/64, 22/64}; the ordinary post-step report is `floor(4y)`. The record with t = 0 maps y to {10/64, 18/64}, giving bins {0, 1} with at least 2/64 clearance from a bin edge. The record with t = 1 maps y to {18/64, 26/64}, giving bins {1, 1}. Both move x to 1, lower the served record loss by 129/256, and lower the equally weighted two-target C loss by 1/2. Charge the other record as still due after this cut in both arms.

The old pool has three units. On the pre-cut service, B_x uses one unit; borrowers p and q may each call for one. Fix four possible traces before the record: for y = 14/64 the call pair is (1,0) or (0,1), and for y = 22/64 it is (0,0) or (1,1). Both cells have identical one-borrower marginals as sets. At the cut B_x can go live on x = 1 and no longer needs its dedicated unit. The only allowed later suffix for this check is one service window with these call patterns, not another optimizer update. Dispatch p and q by the same public call rule in all traces. With t = 0, bin 0 needs one reserved unit and can admit a new two-unit commitment; bin 1 needs two reserved units and must refuse it. With t = 1 the aliased bin needs two units and always refuses. Never-release cannot admit two; releasing two in the pooled cell strands a caller on (1,1). A toy old read y ∈ [0,1/2] passes at each cut, the old B_x request is served before and after handover, and the due C objective improves as above. This is a finite service fixture, not a substitute for independent held-out learner tasks. It separates equal-gain records at a fixed read and deadline; it does **not** beat a planner or allocator that selects t = 0 and uses the same report.

## A. Repair without erasing the residual decision

### 01. Preserve only the distinctions a release can spend — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Protect pairs of reachable histories whose indistinguishability would veto a particular on-time release, not pairs that differ only in private state. **Novelty / control.** [Precision-aware checks][precision] separate damaging transitions; the proposed object is a conflict graph indexed by the release and its handover policy. Compare the best whole-state separator under the same bits. **Cheapest first test.** Use six finite histories with two conflicting pairs; enumerate two equal-gain records where only one keeps both pairs apart after rounding. **Old-task retention.** Make both old B borrowers call in each cell and read rare A/installed C after the repair.

### 02. Guard both sides of a clip knee with a repair orbit — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select a lawful due record by the image of its complete reachable momentum interval, including saturation; accept a nonlinear repair path even if no local null direction exists. **Novelty / control.** [#273][useful] detects at a clip knee; here crossing the knee is permitted for the route being repaired while the residual decision survives globally. Compare [OGD][ogd] plus the best lawful early check. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-slot heavy-ball update with a saturating repaired coordinate and two equal nominal y margins, only one robust over both signs. **Old-task retention.** Read rare A and installed C on both saturation signs, and actually run the old B fallback in the refused cell.

### 03. Preserve separation through a skipped report — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Bind repair to a robust window of possible report ticks, so that a permitted delay cannot merge opposite release outcomes. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #07][parent] tolerates stale evidence; this is a forward invariance requirement across several future native observations, not reuse of a past observation. Compare fresh audit at the latest legal tick. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate delay 0/1 after two equal-gain updates and one idle momentum tick; include a repair whose initial margin vanishes on delay 1. **Old-task retention.** Exercise both B calls and ordinary A/C at each wait tick, including a delayed refusal.

### 04. Route-exchange an equal-gain repair — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When two existing B routes can serve the same repaired request, choose the repair that leaves the alternative route's public hazard visible; the roles may reverse on a blind next queue. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #24][parent] uses role swaps as an audit; this makes pre-certified role exchange part of the actuator. Compare a fixed primary route plus a separate check and exhaustively chosen route. **Cheapest first test.** Let two routes share a scarce C slot and flip which residual region is near a bin boundary across two blind arrivals. **Old-task retention.** Run pinned B callers on both original routes and repeat old A/C reads under each exchange.

### 05. Select a repair with a witness against nuisance drift — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Precertify a report margin against a specified family of ordinary intervening A/C updates, rather than freeze those updates while repairing B. **Novelty / control.** [OGD][ogd] protects old output locally; this protects a discrete residual decision from a live, shared optimizer suffix. Compare frozen updates plus paid catch-up and a separate read. **Cheapest first test.** Two due repairs and two legal A/C orders in a rotated quadratic; search for a repair whose residual gap survives both orders at native precision. **Old-task retention.** Measure A/C learning per slice, not only endpoint accuracy, and test B handover after each order.

### 06. Spend margin on a known future precision loss — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Before a scheduled decrease in report precision, select work that places opposite residual outcomes on opposite future bins; certify new releases only once that coarser read arrives. **Novelty / control.** [Precision-aware checks][precision] schedule checks around precision; this uses the same due repair to change which outcomes remain separable within a fixed future bit budget. Compare retaining the fine report with charged bytes and early certification where lawful. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two fixed quantizers and an equal-progress pair of C records, with the release request arriving only after the switch. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C through the precision change and two B borrowers on the fresh request or refusal.

### 07. Relay a separator across an ordinary second record — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A first repair deliberately leaves information in a state coordinate that the next compulsory C record transfers into its ordinary report; no release is granted in between. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #21][parent] carries a repair cut to new claims; the candidate transfers decision information without storing the previous report as side state. Compare paid report carry and the exact two-record planner. **Cheapest first test.** Use a two-coordinate quadratic with a cross term in the second update and a control without it; enumerate the two reachable aliases. **Old-task retention.** Run B on independent live backing and A/C at the uninformative interim cut and at the eventual release.

### 08. Establish a separator impossibility bound before choosing the batch — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Cover each legal equal-progress record with a reachable harmful/safe alias pair for the named release, yielding a finite certificate that the native repair/read menu cannot certify it. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #22][parent] seeks a merged-operation impossibility; this certificate is specifically for the repair-constrained observation menu, and can state which one extra lawful bit or tick would break it. Compare exact enumeration with a raw gap threshold. **Cheapest first test.** Give three natural repair records three distinct boundary collisions and add one optional legal check. **Old-task retention.** Filter witnesses by actual A/B/C service before the cut; then test fallback and old service after the mandated refusal.

## B. Release fallback backing without stranding old borrowers

### 09. Reuse only the load removed from a simultaneous window — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Retire named time-window liabilities, not a borrower's whole contract; a repair can eliminate its overlap with p while leaving overlap with q. **Novelty / control.** [Bounded Counter][escrow] budgets known rights and [maintenance-duty transfer][handover] hands off work; here the expendable quantity comes from a change to reachable overlap at fixed marginals. Compare exact time-indexed escrow and separate clearance. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three old callers on two ticks, with identical totals but different post-repair overlap. **Old-task retention.** Serve the late q call and an early p call alongside repaired B; measure A/C at both ticks.

### 10. Release capacity conditional on a public dispatch observation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve a branch-independent emergency unit now, then allow a second release only after an ordinary dispatch event has reduced the reachable set. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #12][parent] certifies public recourse for admission; here recourse retires a live liability while the same earlier borrowers keep their guarantees. Compare static worst-case allocation and a fully informed audit with charged latency. **Cheapest first test.** Give p an early public call and q a late possible overlap; enumerate one public event that does and one that does not justify the second release. **Old-task retention.** Execute both dispatch branches with simultaneous old requests, rare A and inherited C.

### 11. Prohibit circular release across mutually backing routes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify a set of release edges only when each reachable residual call graph has a common executable path to actually live capacity; two mutually promising repaired routes do not back each other by declaration. **Novelty / control.** [Invariant confluence][iconf] asks if independent updates compose; the proposed certificate includes time and termination of a fallback dispatch, not just a valid merged ledger. Compare a time-expanded max-flow allocator with separate verified repair. **Cheapest first test.** Use a three-route diamond with one cyclic release and one paid live sink, sharing the same total reserves. **Old-task retention.** Inject both existing B calls at once and check their completion plus A/C before any new work.

### 12. Keep a thin standby until the last pinned borrower drains — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Retire execution capacity in stages: free unpinned headroom immediately, but retain the minimum compatible state for prior versioned borrowers until a public drain. **Novelty / control.** [Maintenance-duty transfer][handover] reassigns requests; this additionally tests whether a *partial backing release* is spendable before the old version disappears. Compare full snapshot until drain and transfer followed by the same allocator. **Cheapest first test.** Pin two old requests at staggered deadlines, only one migratable; price version bytes, not just service slots. **Old-task retention.** Validate both replies against their original versions and run ordinary A/C through old-state reclamation.

### 13. Certify a correlated reserve with a missing-pattern witness — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Retain compact, checkable evidence that one otherwise maximal call combination cannot occur after this repair, while continuing to carry all the individual liabilities. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #10][parent] records obstructions to safe grants; here a missing *demand* pattern is positive evidence for reusing live backing. Compare an exact reachable-set table and a compressed marginal ledger. **Cheapest first test.** Three binary callers, all six nonempty proper subsets reachable; flip only the all-three pattern and compare how many units can be reused. **Old-task retention.** Replay all maximal pairs and the all-three negative with the independent reserve intact, then read A/C after reuse.

### 14. Make partial release portable across interchangeable standby hosts — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Carry a cut of residual borrower reachability, so a capacity release remains valid after any predeclared lawful host substitution without storing every handover schedule. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #26][parent] backs a grant with a common matching in one cell; test a certificate that survives a finite family of host maps, each with a public dispatch policy. Compare recomputed robust matchings and a table of host-specific witnesses. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two substitutions on a three-host, two-borrower graph; one preserves the cut, the other strands a pinned borrower. **Old-task retention.** Serve both borrowers per host map and replay original A/C without re-training after the substitution.

### 15. Lease a release without counting on taking capacity back mid-call — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Free capacity only to users whose complete service ends before residual risk reopens; book noninterruptible work against the full interval, not the initial slack. **Novelty / control.** Time-indexed escrow and [verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) handle expiring guarantees; the candidate's use is a lease earned by a repair that only removes liability for a proved window. Compare exact deadline scheduling atop an independent audit, with charged renewal. **Cheapest first test.** Two equal-value new jobs straddle different windows and an old residual call resumes on tick 2. **Old-task retention.** Run the resumed B call with the new job in flight and A/C on both sides of expiry.

### 16. Compose releases across repairs without counting the same absent call twice — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Track which joint load pattern each repair removes; a second repair can only spend the incremental reduction on the reachable call envelope after the first. **Novelty / control.** [Bounded Counter][escrow] prevents numeric double spends; this tests whether named changes to a nonadditive call envelope can be transferred without recounting credited exclusions. Compare exact joint recomputation and independent per-repair ledgers. **Cheapest first test.** Two repairs each eliminate the same high-load trace, then add a third that eliminates a distinct maximal trace. Enumerate all orders. **Old-task retention.** Attempt new reuse after every prefix while serving the worst remaining B callers and independent A/C.

## C. Make the same repair earn both the margin and the handover

### 17. Fit a quantized fork to two different release schedules — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose a due C step whose bins each have a common safe but different dispatch schedule; earn service from the fork, not from certifying a single preferred schedule. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #17/#26][parent] combine repair, joint checks and matching; this focuses the separator on whether partial release is possible under alternative *live borrower* handovers. Compare the best separate repair + passive report + correlated dispatch on the same clock. **Cheapest first test.** Use two pinned callers and two release windows where the benefit disappears if the bins merge or either live handover is forbidden. **Old-task retention.** Exercise both schedules with a late p/q burst, old A and installed C.

### 18. Admit a second release only when it keeps a third decision observable — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reject otherwise safe reuse if the incoming workload would overwrite the native evidence needed at the next independently owed handover, unless a lawful cheaper substitute preserves it. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #21][parent] tests sequential waves; here today's new beneficiary changes tomorrow's observation map, so spending backing consumes diagnostic capacity too. Compare report retention with charged bytes and an exact two-wave planner. **Cheapest first test.** Set up two equal-first-gain jobs, one erasing a future bin boundary and one not; prebind an independent second arrival. **Old-task retention.** Serve B at both waves including the job chosen first; check A/C while the later decision is still pending.

### 19. Choose a repair by the cheapest residual liability representation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Of two equal-progress valid repairs, prefer the one whose report cell has an exact small reserve certificate, without weakening the admission policy. **Novelty / control.** [Invariant confluence][iconf] and [Bounded Counter][escrow] give ways to avoid coordinating known constraints; here the applied learning action changes the complexity of the surviving joint constraints. Compare lossless frontier compression after unconstrained repair and exact planning with charged time. **Cheapest first test.** One C record leaves a joint-call set with an interval certificate; another leaves a parity constraint with too many intervals under a fixed memory budget. Check against a compact parity coder as well. **Old-task retention.** Enumerate the outer A/B/C service cases represented by both codes and rerun after encoding, decoding and reuse.

### 20. Buy a one-record bridge over a capacity cliff — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A lawful repair exposes a narrow release window between independent borrower waves; spend that window only if it can finish otherwise missed service, not simply report more eligible units. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #19/#21][parent] track repair frontiers and waves; this demands a simultaneous repair/observation gain that disappears with even one extra lawful audit tick. Compare a zero-extra-tick passive verifier and a paid audit allowed to reorder. **Cheapest first test.** Give two natural C records equal gain but opposite placement of the only safe one-tick reuse interval, and sweep the incoming deadline by one tick. **Old-task retention.** Serve the last pre-bridge and first post-bridge B calls; run A/C during rather than only after the window.

### 21. Reinvest repaired backing in preserving the next margin — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use certified released capacity to let the next compulsory C record arrive on time rather than delocalize it to a congested queue; that record preserves the next residual read and opens a later safe handover. No credit for future unearned releases. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #21][parent] carries evidence to a second wave; this is feedback from spendable capacity into the future legal repair menu. Compare independent rolling escrow, due-first scheduling and a matched two-cut planner. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-cut queue with one delayed C record and a reciprocal arrival-order control; remove the first release or swap the independently due second record. **Old-task retention.** Exercise all pre-existing B calls under both queues and report A/C on-time coverage, including the delayed control.

### 22. Factor the two alleged effects with a same-report stack — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Cross live repair on/off with margin preserved/collapsed and common handover allowed/blocked; compute optimal reuse in each, then let the strongest modular stack choose among those same legal records. **Novelty / control.** [#289 #23][parent] prices shared reports; this adds a separable, experimentally manipulated handover factor so margin, repair yield and operational backing cannot be mistaken for one another. **Cheapest first test.** Extend the paper fixture by pinning one earlier request to the original lane and include a negative with unchanged marginals but a new simultaneous call. **Old-task retention.** Run the pinned B request, both residual borrowers, rare A and installed C on every lawful arm and later blind reuse.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does margin-preserving due repair buy timely partial release over the best same-report repair + audit + allocator?** Start with 17, 20 and 22 on a finite queue, bind the C menu and deadlines before blind arrivals, and identify a binding resource. If an ordinary repair stack can choose the same record and dispatch on the same report for the same cost, call it a tie rather than a new primitive.
2. **How common is the release-specific margin under live momentum, quantization and mandatory old work?** Use 01–07; measure robust safe/harmful separation at execution precision against the best early check, not against gradient cosine. Spend no new work searching a menu that 08 proves insufficient.
3. **What is the smallest sound evidence that a repair removed, rather than transferred, a correlated liability?** Use 09, 11 and 13–16 against exact joint tables, time-expanded allocation and paid compression. Include unchanged marginals, a circular promise and two repairs that could claim the same absent peak.
4. **Can reuse replenish the next handover without eroding old service or consuming diagnostic capacity faster than it is restored?** Use 18 and 21 on independently timed overlapping waves; measure repeatable deadline service and retained bytes at the same old-task floors. A single lucky arrival or a subsidized future check is not a positive result.
