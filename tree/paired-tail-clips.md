# Paired tail clips: trade arrival order without keeping the erased history

**Status:** Ideas only. The examples below are arithmetic sanity checks, not pilot results; all proposed first tests are CPU or paper tests. No GPU.

**Parent:** [Outlier-only clipping](outlier-clipping.md) ([PR #240](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/240)). **Review seeds:** [Flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/d2ab1feaac9b1b23f1650970b1a5ca7f2d16272d/reviews/flood-triage.md#L353), score-3 cards #240#03 (reciprocal one-sided openers) and #240#12 (a bit for the collided class). Following the [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/d2ab1feaac9b1b23f1650970b1a5ca7f2d16272d/reviews/idea-ledger.md), require ordinary old-task retention, compare against the strongest components stacked at equal budget, and treat improvements as controls, not pilot nominations.

## The two obligations

- **Reciprocal teaching certificate.** Freeze the learner, the independently queued truthful B pools, protected core `K`, reachable envelope, deadlines and allowed suffixes before selecting either example. Write `R` and `L` for the complete updates, not just gradient clipping functions. Both must fix every state in `K` as read by the *existing* A/C proof, including its optimizer, randomness and clock dependencies. Certify that `R` fixes the reachable repaired landing of `L` and `L` fixes that of `R`, at actual execution precision. For a delayed second arrival, include permitted wait transitions in those landing sets. A repaired landing means the image of the tail that the opener actually repairs, not the full image including an untouched opposite tail. Check both legal prefixes on the full envelope separately: that opposite tail is still live, and the second example must still be eligible. Check both residual orders only at seams actually admitted. Equal final weights prove neither prefix safety nor interchangeability.
- **Admission certificate.** For a history `h`, let `q(h)` be the actual post-clip observation (including whatever order, phase or queue IDs the verifier already gets). Let `a(h)` be the required admission decision, or vector of decisions if several future suffixes are covered. Within each fiber of `q`, retain nothing for a constant `a), and use one verified binary value only when there are exactly two distinct values of `a). If only a single binary decision is certified, this is its minimal extra payload on a mixed fiber, not necessarily the cheapest verification method. More than two decision signatures cannot fit into one bit under a fixed observation: narrow the contract, replay, or pay for more information. Certify the class and bit from a trusted pre-loss source and bind them to the clip, envelope, queue/phase context and contract version. A hash that authenticates a history identifier does not establish its decision. Missing or stale evidence triggers replay or conservative refusal, not default admission. Account for the source checks, class identification and binding bytes separately from the payload; no free trusted observer.

**Shared falsifier.** Compare the best single opener from #232, a two-sided clamp plus a matched no-op, fixed thresholds, slot reset, equal-B-loss independent sample selection, and a truthful pair with a broken cross-landing. For audit compare #232#18 audit-first, [#216 minimal publication](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/216), quantized slot export, equal-byte hash/sketch, and checkpoint/replay. The joint rival stacks the best pair or clamp with the cheapest sound audit and ordinary cached A/C recertification. Also run the [#113 smooth/accumulation null](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/113) and a tail-free pack. Match B gain **per queue**, raw arrivals, permitted actions, steps, peak bytes (including verifier state), checks and deadlines, or report a Pareto tradeoff rather than claiming a matched win. Measure hazardous admits, honest refusals, worst old A/C slice and fallback success at each exposed prefix, wait, and suffix, not just at a common endpoint.

**Nearest precedents (provisional).** [Nonparametric Iterative Machine Teaching][nimt] selects training examples for a learner; it is the example-selection rival, not evidence for inherited old-task proofs across independent arrivals. [Partial-order reduction][por] already proves enabledness and commutation of transitions, and [CRDT model merging][crdt] already tackles order-independent combination; use these as serious controls, not as novelty claims about commutation. The narrower question is whether ordinarily available examples under an unchanged optimizer can make two *previously dependent* queue transitions safe to swap while reusing an old proof. For receipts, [automaton minimization][dfa] already quotients histories by continuation decisions; the bit count is not new. The testable delta is choosing and verifying an inexpensive admission predicate at the irreversible learning transition, then eliding it where the clip already makes history irrelevant. [Proof-of-Learning][pol] is a trace-verification rival, not a substitute for semantic provenance. Abstracts for NIMT and CRDT model merging were checked through HF Papers; this is not an exhaustive priority search.

## Paper fixtures

**P1 — Two lawful examples, two orders.** Use a scalar learner `h` with two fixed views predicting `max(h − 1, 0)` and `max(−h − 1, 0)`. Each queue independently supplies its own view with true target zero. One ordinary unit SGD step on half squared error yields `R(h)=min(h,1)` and `L(h)=max(h,−1)`. There are no slots or time reads. On `H={−2,−1/2,0,1/2,2}`, both fix `K={−1/2,0,1/2}`, and `R(−1)=−1`, `L(1)=1`. Waits are identity. Give old tasks gaps `A=2−h−d`, `C=2+h+d`, with zero floors and cumulative residual shift `d`. With no residual shift all prefixes serve both old tasks; if the unhandled tail remains, one of the two first residual moves `±1` is unsafe across the envelope. After both openers, either residual order is safe at both its intermediate and final prefix. Each opener reduces its own B loss at its tail from `1/2` to zero without undoing the other. A global two-sided clamp ties the final map; this fixture establishes legality, not an advantage over that control.

**P2 — Broken cross-landing despite a fixed core.** In a separate two-coordinate learner, keep `R(x,y)=(min(x,1),y)`; replace the left view by `max(−1−x−y,0)/sqrt(2)` with true target zero and the same optimizer. The update adds `max(−1−x−y,0)/2` to each coordinate; a power-of-two rational update despite the view normalization. Both fix `|x|,|y|≤1/2`. On `(2,−5/2)`, left then right reaches `(1,−5/2)`; right then left reaches `(5/4,−9/4)`. For old safety require `x≤2` and `x+y≥−3/2` before the seam, then test the same reads after a first residual move `x←x±1`, `y←y∓1`. Both opener prefixes meet the pre-seam floors; only left then right can admit the `+1` residual move. The left example was truthful but moved the right landing. Include `(−2,0)` in the left queue's test pack to exercise its active tail. Reject the pair as a two-order certificate, even if the narrower contract allows the good order.

**P3 — One mixed fiber and one uniformly repaired fiber.** Extend P1 with pre-clip histories `h=±5/2`; use `5/2` instead of `2` in the old gaps so even the planted histories can serve at the pre-clip prefix. Require provenance `h≤2`, not a bound on `|h|`. The independent trusted trace measures the predicate before the right clip (the left clip preserves it). Both opener orders put `2` and `5/2` at `+1` with identical old suffix safety, yet only the former is admissible. Store `[h>2]` there; absent or untrusted bits cannot admit that fiber. Both `−2` and `−5/2` reach `−1` and are admissible; that fiber needs no bit. If the trusted source only records floor-rounded integer `h`, it sees 2 for both right-tail histories: it cannot supply the bit, even though exact pre-state access could. As a separate stress case, add two standing decisions so that a single fiber has signatures `(allow,allow)`, `(allow,deny)`, `(deny,deny)`; this requires more than one bit unless observation or scope changes. None of these examples credits a repaired numerical state as proof that provenance held.

## Idea cards

**P** denotes a candidate primitive (reusable object or operation); **I** denotes an improvement or control. Each test inherits the shared A/C and budget rules.

### 01. Reciprocal landing contract — P, #240#03 seed
**Mechanism.** Issue a joint certificate with two core identities, two opposite-landing identities and two enabled-prefix obligations for one named pair of independently arriving B items. **Novelty / control.** Partial-order independence starts from transitions; here the fixed teaching pools supply transitions chosen to establish independence on a pre-certified core and repaired tail. Compare P1 against the global clamp, best single opener and equal-loss picks. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate both P1 orders with one delayed arrival and the P2 coupled distractor. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C after each opener, wait and exposed residual prefix; reject P2 even if only one order was observed in selection.

### 02. Compatibility graph over independently queued examples — P
**Mechanism.** Compile each item once into a guarded landing contract; form a bipartite graph whose edges are legal swaps, then reserve an edge without changing either queue's label or arrival. **Novelty / control.** Matching and machine teaching already select samples; the edge is a reusable proof about mutual landings, not a compatibility score. Compare exact joint teaching plus ordinary fresh certification at equal scans. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a 3-by-3 menu with redundant valid edges, a degree-zero item and a misleading low-loss edge; swap arrival rates after selection. **Old-task retention.** Check both orders for every reserved edge and A/C under degree-zero fallback.

### 03. Delay-indexed swap lease — P
**Mechanism.** Attach the interval of actual wait durations for which the first landing remains fixed by the other item and the second remains eligible; expire before the first unsupported delay. **Novelty / control.** Timed automata verify delay reachability and the parent prices audit deadlines; the proposed reusable object is a delay entitlement derived from a learning landing, not a queue timeout. Compare exact recertification plus a fixed worst-case timeout. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate waits under a fixed slot-decay law with both a reentrant safe cell and an unsafe hole; a contiguous lease must stop at the hole. **Old-task retention.** Probe A/C inside the lease, at expiration and on the rejected late-arrival path.

### 04. Reuse only what the old proof already ignores — I
**Mechanism.** Search for paired items in the existing proof's full state, then compare a weight-only screen against exact slot-and-clock identity. Do not declare a new projection to rescue old proof reuse. **Novelty / control.** Proof slicing and sparse optimizer no-ops are known; this tests how often the parent identity survives a pair under actual optimizer semantics. **Cheapest first test.** Re-run P1 on a finite state with nonzero momentum, weight decay and a step-based learning rate; include a true no-write optimizer as a positive control. **Old-task retention.** Check a slot-sensitive old read after one unrelated batch as well as immediate A/C, even when weights match.

### 05. Common second-item right across first-item substitutions — P
**Mechanism.** Reserve a second-queue item against an equivalence class of eligible first items whose reachable repaired landings all lie in its fixed set, while checking each first item's reciprocal obligation. **Novelty / control.** A generic contract or nearest-neighbor cache is known; the proposed reusable right survives substitution *without* renewing the old proof or widening the accepted envelope. **Cheapest first test.** Remove the preferred first item from a three-item pool after reservation; a near duplicate on the wrong side of a gate must not inherit the right. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C after both admissible replacements and the safe path when only the invalid replacement remains.

### 06. Prefix counterexample as a reusable negative edge — P
**Mechanism.** Store a minimal lawful prefix that invalidates a family of pairings sharing the failing guard, so a later pair search can rule out a class without rerunning every full order. **Novelty / control.** Conflict learning and counterexample-guided refinement already prune search; the candidate is a prefix-specific negative edge justified by the exact fixed-set failure under truthful teaching. **Cheapest first test.** Give six pairs one common coupled gate and one superficially similar exception; compare exact transition caching and guard-based pruning. **Old-task retention.** Replay the named failing old slice and verify that the exception retains A/C rather than being silently excluded.

### 07. A swap closed under intervening normal service — P
**Mechanism.** Prove that a bounded set of ordinary interleaved A/C service actions maps both landing tubes into states still fixed by the opposite item and keeps its queue reservation valid. **Novelty / control.** Rely-guarantee reasoning already handles interference; this would give an opener permission that remains live while the learner serves old work, not just while blocked. **Cheapest first test.** Insert each of two normal stateful old reads between P1 items; make one read update a slot used by the second opener. Compare pausing service and fresh per-interleave proofs. **Old-task retention.** Measure ordinary service coverage and gaps during the interleave, after second arrival and on the invalidating path.

### 08. Three queues with a genuine higher-order obstruction — P
**Mechanism.** Store a minimal *set of consumed openers* that invalidates an otherwise valid swap, rather than declaring the pair compatible forever. **Novelty / control.** Contextual independence is known; a reusable teaching obstruction would identify which truthful earlier writes invalidate the core/landing proof and which do not. Compare pairwise independence plus full prefix search. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three finite gates with all pairs valid on the initial envelope but a third update enabling a coupled direction that invalidates one later swap; verify all six orders as oracle. **Old-task retention.** Report the first unsafe old slice on the triple and A/C on all newly routed orders, not only the pair tests.

### 09. A safe route if only one queue ever arrives — P
**Mechanism.** Pair the dual-order contract with an explicit timeout continuation for each unmatched opener prefix, priced as a right to serve the old tasks without inventing a missing example. **Novelty / control.** Sagas and staged commitments already have recovery; the candidate is a reusable prefix certificate for serving without undo or fictitious teaching when the reserved opposite tail stays live. **Cheapest first test.** Drop either queue in P1 and enumerate all suffixes after expiration. Compare serialize-until-both and one-opener plus recertification at matched wait budget. **Old-task retention.** Keep measuring A/C after cancellation and on recurring B arrivals, including the old slice sensitive to the untreated tail.

### 10. Repair budget charged to the queue that gets the gain — I
**Mechanism.** Match B gain separately for the two queues before scoring order flexibility; debit retries and dropped examples to the responsible queue. **Novelty / control.** Per-group accounting is known; this is a falsification control for branch claims that quietly buy independence with a stalled queue. **Cheapest first test.** Compare one perfect pair with a high-total-gain arm that starves the other queue and with a rate-matched resampling arm. **Old-task retention.** Compare old A/C on matched two-queue progress slices and at the deadline, on both accepted and refusal-heavy streams.

### 11. A verified bit only on a mixed collision fiber — P, #240#12 seed
**Mechanism.** Publish a bound predicate of trusted pre-clip evidence only for a post-clip class in which that predicate changes admission. **Novelty / control.** Minimal publication and state minimization are prior art; the proposed object is the conditional receipt at the many-to-one learning transition. Compare audit-first, fixed-width sketch and replay. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate P3, including a coarse trusted trace incapable of issuing the bit and a stale receipt from the opposite queue. **Old-task retention.** Evaluate both old suffixes for every admitted class and check A/C under fallback; record provenance false accepts separately.

### 12. Choose the cheapest *observable* separator — P
**Mechanism.** Search a frozen menu of trace predicates and bind the cheapest one that is constant on each side of the required binary partition in each mixed fiber; refuse when no accessible predicate separates it. **Novelty / control.** Feature selection and active diagnosis already select tests; this primitive would carry an exact proof that a specific observable substitutes for hidden history *after a specified clip*. Compare exact pre-audit and optimal one-feature decision trees. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate exact sign, coarse magnitude and an authenticated step flag, including two histories tied on all affordable features. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C for each chosen separator's admitted suffixes and measure old service lost to fallback in the unobservable pack.

### 13. Scope contraction on a fiber needing three answers — P
**Mechanism.** When two future admissions would require more than one bit, explicitly certify a smaller suffix menu whose decision signatures fit, and deny requests outside that menu until replay. **Novelty / control.** Rate-distortion and automaton minimization already trade information for expressiveness; this would certify *which legal continuations* may survive under a fixed receipt cap without permitting any false admit. **Cheapest first test.** Use the three-signature P3 extension and compare two-bit exact publication, blind one-bit truncation and exhaustive restricted menus. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C on the restricted route and on the fallback for each excluded suffix, charging lost old service separately.

### 14. A receipt that passes through the opposite opener — P
**Mechanism.** Certify that transporting a bit from a mixed first-landing fiber through the second opener preserves precisely the decision it encodes, or discards it if the later fiber is provably uniform. **Novelty / control.** Dataflow joins and incremental provenance propagate labels; the proposed operation is a checked push-forward through a lawful reciprocal teaching edge, with an explicit failure when new preimages change the decision partition. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate both orders with one unchanged mixed fiber and one merged class requiring an extra distinction; compare re-audit and append-only receipts. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C before and after receipt transport on both orders, plus fallback for the newly ambiguous class.

### 15. Bound the no-receipt path, not just the bit — P
**Mechanism.** Treat deliberate receipt absence as a certified uniform fiber, not an undocumented third symbol; authenticate the observation and bind the optional encoding so dropping a bit cannot create an admit. **Novelty / control.** Sparse encodings and authenticated omission are known; the reusable proof links intentional silence to the clip's full admitted preimage at declared precision. **Cheapest first test.** Compare P3's benign fiber with a rounded or packet-loss case making benign and mixed fibers indistinguishable; price presence markers and binding against fixed-length export. **Old-task retention.** Measure A/C after trusted silence, loss-triggered fallback and delayed verification; don't reward refusing all normal work.

### 16. Revoke only decision-changing fibers when contracts drift — P
**Mechanism.** Diff admission signatures after a named policy revision and invalidate only receipts whose prior resolution no longer suffices. **Novelty / control.** Proof-cache invalidation and schema migration are known; the target is precise re-audit after a provenance obligation changes *across many-to-one learning updates*. **Cheapest first test.** Tighten P3's provenance predicate on one formerly benign class; verify a negative case in which no retained observable can reissue the receipt. Compare global invalidation and exact incremental audit. **Old-task retention.** Test old gaps and availability during targeted revocation and on the unresolved class's fallback.

### 17. Receipt separation at the quantization boundary — I
**Mechanism.** Bound evidence rounding jointly with landing export rounding and identify cases where the proposed predicate is not a sound separator at actual precision. **Novelty / control.** Quantized verification and the parent's audit partitions already do most of this; use it to invalidate toy bit economies. **Cheapest first test.** Move P3's violating history from `5/2` to `2+1/16`; compare exact trace, coarse bins, direct trusted threshold and replay, charging their precision. **Old-task retention.** Test both rounded clip schedules A/C and the conservative path at each threshold tie.

### 18. Same decision today, different decision after a lawful continuation — P
**Mechanism.** Certify a bounded continuation signature, retaining only the first distinguishable future admission when possible rather than logging two histories with the same immediate decision. **Novelty / control.** Myhill–Nerode equivalence already defines the distinction; test whether a separating suffix can be captured with cheaper learning-side evidence than an exact bounded replay/minimization stack. **Cheapest first test.** Plant two histories with identical today decisions that split after a shared third B update; exhaustive bounded suffix enumeration is the oracle. **Old-task retention.** Measure A/C after the shared update, at the delayed admission and on the replay fallback if the bound is exceeded.

### 19. Buy order flexibility by moving the audit cut, not the threshold — P
**Mechanism.** Jointly choose a truthful compatible pair and a trusted point along each order at which the same predicate can still be issued before its evidence disappears. The clip threshold and optimizer remain fixed by the chosen items and learner. **Novelty / control.** Schedulers place checks and machine teachers pick items; the candidate interaction is a pair whose cross-landing compatibility *also* carries the last observable distinction past the first arrival. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-by-two queue pool with distinct evidence lifetimes; require a deadline win over separate best-pair selection stacked with earliest sound audit, and compare an exhaustive joint solver. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C in every delayed prefix and confirm neither the rejected receipt nor the waiting policy drops normal old service below the floor.

### 20. Provenance pressure changes the optimal pair — P
**Mechanism.** Rank reciprocal pairs by the cost of reconstructing only decision-changing fibers, rather than how many histories the final state merges. **Novelty / control.** The parent's audit-aware clip selection is the nearest precedent; the putative extension prices *both arrival orders* and the interactions between their intermediate fibers. Compare independently audit-optimized openers plus exact joint recertification. **Cheapest first test.** Build two pairs with equal B gain and common final state but different intermediate mixed fibers; an equal-size benign-collision pack should not affect selection. **Old-task retention.** Check both intermediate A/C panels, accepted suffixes and fallback; if the joint selection only serves easy old states, count it as a loss.

### 21. Refuse only the unsafe order of an indistinguishable pair — P
**Mechanism.** When a single bit cannot cover all fast-order requests, spend it on one declared suffix and route the rest to a certified slower order when that order is uniformly admissible. **Novelty / control.** Selective verification and fallback routing are known; the proposed object is an order menu specialized to a residual collision fiber rather than a single global deny. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate one fiber with a universally lawful slow schedule and three fast-schedule signatures across two suffix requests. Compare two-bit publication and reject-to-replay. **Old-task retention.** Check the fast admitted A/C slices and the slow route under its longer exposure; charge misses caused by the slower deadline.

### 22. Shared-state witness that is not an independence-by-design trick — P
**Mechanism.** Search a blind fixed pool for reciprocal openers sharing actual weights and a stateful read dependency; qualify only pairs where cross-landing proof, not disjoint storage, makes both orders legal. **Novelty / control.** Disjoint parameter routing and merge wrappers already give easy swaps; the target is a transferable certificate for truthful examples whose supports overlap but whose reachable repaired effects do not interfere. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a tiny shared ReLU model with measured truthful examples, compare split heads, control with equal B progress and an exact finite verifier, and publish an empty feasible set if exact core identity rules everything out. **Old-task retention.** Read held-out A/C on shared features at each prefix and after unrelated lawful updates that expose residual slot damage.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can truthful examples create a swap that an ordinary optimizer did not already provide?** Start with 01, 04 and 22. Freeze full proof state and queues before search; require one nontrivial shared-state pair under the same optimizer, both legal waits and a failing coupled control. If step counters, slots or service clocks break exact identity, report the counterexample rather than treating weight-only equality as proof reuse.
2. **Can the verified bit be cheaper than checking first?** Start with 11–12 and 15. Demand a real mixed fiber, a benign fiber with no receipt, a collision in the trusted observation and a priced binding. Exact decision bits are uninteresting if obtaining them costs an ordinary pre-audit or if a one-bit hash/refusal baseline matches the usable coverage.
3. **Is there value in solving pairing and evidence lifetime together?** Use 14, 19 and 20. Isolate a case with matched per-queue B gain in which both independent arrivals are useful, and in which an observable distinction lives through one opener but not the other. If the best serialized audit plus paired or cached recertification ties, keep the simpler stack.
4. **Does either certificate survive more than one seam?** Use 07–08 and 18. Add an ordinary interleaved service action or a third truthful example; look for the earliest A/C violation or future admission that distinguishes a merged history. Require a bounded scope and an explicit fallback before claiming reuse.

**Verdict:** Conditional primitive candidate, not a pilot nomination. A reciprocal certificate must open two useful arrival orders with unmodified A/C proof obligations, and its sparse receipt must close the decision-changing collisions at lower total cost than the matched stack. Otherwise keep the broken-landing and unobservable-bit counterexamples and prefer a single opener or clamp plus explicit pre-audit and ordinary recertification.
