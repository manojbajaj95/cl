# Continual verifiers 2: Retract what was taught, keep what was proved

Depth 1 · 26 idea cards · **Ambitious but doable** · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Parent:** [#411](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/411), [CV23: reward-premise attribution](root-continual-verifiers.md#cv23--attribute-skill-loss-to-the-reward-version-that-taught-it--ambitious-but-doable-p) and [CV16: process labels with an expiration condition](root-continual-verifiers.md#cv16--process-labels-with-an-expiration-condition--ambitious-but-doable-p). Both reached 3, neither reached 4. The reviewer found that most other cards fell into assumption-based truth maintenance, incremental SAT/verification, model-based diagnosis, consumer-driven contracts or reusable holdouts. This node does not claim novelty for a dependency graph, an invalidation flag, a score with a version tag, or retaining a proof unchanged.

**Why they fall short.** CV23 locates experiences that received a bad reward, but under on-policy learning the premise may also have changed *which experiences were collected* and how later clean updates acted. Deleting marked records, negating a local gradient, and changing the reward at deployment solve three different problems. CV16 sensibly stores validity and progress separately, but a validity bit plus a recomputed advantage is already available. What might be new is a *smaller object than a saved rollout panel* that transports signed credit under a specified solver change or returns a concrete comparison that would reverse it. The two lines meet when a reward correction changes both the sign of the original teaching signal and the learner that consumes it.

## Literature check

Checked alphaXiv searches and linked paper titles/abstracts. **P** means candidate primitive, not a novelty verdict; **I** means an improvement or assay. No promotion by analogy: reject a P claim if the strongest stacked, equal-budget controls tie.

| Already available | What would count as different here |
|---|---|
| [ATMS](https://dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf) tracks the assumptions supporting facts; [ProofPatch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01357) propagates changed Lean dependencies. | Dependency truth is not the causal effect of training. Use both as bookkeeping controls; claim novelty only for corrective writes or portable credit that changes a learner's behavior. |

| [Rewarding Progress](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) defines progress under a prover; [VinePPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679) and [Step-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18629) use step-level comparisons. A coupling reduces variance only under its assumptions; it does not itself make missing branches identifiable or confer a durable validity label. |

**First-test convention.** Use enumerable two-to-five-step toy MDPs (packing, sorting or arithmetic constructions) with exact statements and frozen latent dynamics. Keep unauthorized learning writes and correct deployment changes distinct. For A, define two references before collecting data: fixed-log training with v2 rewards and no other intervention, and online training from the same initial state with v2 from the start, averaged over prespecified coupled seeds. Fixed-log agreement is not agreement with the online target. Where appropriate include clean online retraining, continue under v2, checkpoint rollback plus v2, TracIn-guided approximate unlearning, ordinary reward relabeling, and a reward-factorized SF/GPI arm. For B, hold the proof statement fixed, and compare full fresh branching, separate proof/value heads, and #408-style revalidation on the same stored comparisons. Freeze unaffected old tasks A/B (including one that shares features or trajectories with the edited task); measure their live accuracy or return, not just similarity to the old checkpoint. At matched storage, independent oracle calls, interaction and update compute, report current correctness, old-task retention, uncertainty/coverage, and adaptation cost separately. Funding a counterfactual with extra fresh data is valid only if the controls receive the same budget. All tests are proposed, not run.

## A · Retract a teaching effect rather than delete its examples

### CV2-01 · A teaching write with a later-state inverse — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Learn a small transport rule that rewrites an early premise correction through later *clean* updates. Represent the correction by its action-logit contrasts **and** its effect on one future probe update. Train transport on matched short histories differing only in where the correction is applied. After each clean update rewrite those contrasts and fit the smallest local write that realizes them; if two corrections agree in current logits but react differently to the probe, they are not interchangeable.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Meta-gradient RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09801) traces learning sensitivities and [task arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089) negates static updates. The claim is a finite-edit transport operation that generalizes over unseen clean updates; first-order Jacobian pushforward and fixed-log replay must be strong controls, not straw men.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train on short fixed logs from a two-feature softmax bandit and hold out the longest sequences and update orders. Compare replay, Jacobian-vector products and static negation at fixed bytes and total update budget, including transport training; include a write that cancels logits but changes Adam momentum. Reject if it only works when the updates commute or cannot improve the compute–error frontier over replay.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B after the correction and another clean write, including a task sharing the erroneous feature.

### CV2-02 · First exposure, not first bad reward — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When switching a premise would alter a historical policy, locate the first fork at which that change would alter *exposure* under a declared coupling. Use limited simulator queries there to replace only the missing clean branch; reuse suffixes only after an observed state and learner-state recoupling, or estimate explicitly under weaker assumptions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Exact RL unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04182) couples removal under stability; [online correction removal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10280) exposes downstream feedback dependence. [Gumbel-Max OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05824) models counterfactual trajectories. The candidate is an exposure-targeted repair primitive for a retained reward-bearing record, not just replay from the first invalid checkpoint.
- **Cheapest first test:** In a short packing MDP the erroneous reward causes the learner to miss a second route with valid rewards. Compare fixed-log relabeling plus OPE, earliest-checkpoint replay and targeted fork resampling against the online clean reference. Drop if it cannot save simulator calls under matched residual error.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old tasks whose evidence lies before the exposure change and old tasks reached down the original route.

### CV2-03 · An update that isolates only the bad premise interaction — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** On a matched batch, evaluate four reward variants with premises p and q toggled. Use the mixed difference of resulting *policy changes* to learn a separate correction for their conjunction; do not retract q's standalone effect. Retain or retrain interaction channels only if the interaction recurs on unseen batches.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Reward-specific token credit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21439) separates reward components; [repo PL3-15](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/381) already makes source conjunctions detachable. This only advances beyond PL3-15 if verified reward interventions reveal conditional teaching effects that a source-gated adapter and a factorial loss do not isolate, e.g. normalization induces interactions on the same examples.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use shared packing trajectories where one interaction is caused only by within-batch normalization and one by real environment contact. Compare independent reward normalization, source-subset residuals and four-way replay at matched variants and rank.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain legitimate use of each premise alone and one unaffected joint behavior after retiring an interaction.

### CV2-04 · A correction that remembers the optimizer's response — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Make an undo record return a paired policy correction and a small optimizer-state correction. Accept the pair only if the *next independent clean lesson* elicits the right response, not simply if action probabilities now match v2.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [SISA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817) retrains stateful learners; [Form and Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17590) studies misaligned states in unlearning. The candidate operation is compressing the counterfactual *learning response* by premise rather than saving/restoring optimizer state or wiping moments.
- **Cheapest first test:** With a tiny Adam policy, construct v1/v2 histories whose logits agree before a held-out clean lesson but whose moments differ. Compare policy-only task arithmetic, zero moments, replayed moments and a paired compact correction on future learning. Require gains on held-out lessons not used to fit the response sketch.
- **Old-task retention:** Check live A/B before and after the independent update; report resets that only protect the first check.

### CV2-05 · Premise retraction by effect substitution — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Learn a replacement training signal from v2 whose on-policy action change cancels the v1-specific effect but substitutes a valid reason for behavior the two premises agree on. Use paired cases that decouple the two reasons; absent separable evidence, do not claim attribution.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Semantic strategy unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17917) targets a strategy rather than records; [Datamodels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00622) predict responses to dataset changes. The operation targets a causal *substitution* at equal retained behavior, not just forgetting a particular action or making a source marker unreadable.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train a bandit on two correlated packing cues, only one now valid. Compare continue-under-v2, reward-relabel replay, and separate unlearn-then-relearn against a paired replacement at equal new examples. Evaluate on fresh independent cue crosses.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain an old decision supported by the still-valid cue, including where that cue previously co-occurred with the bad cue.

### CV2-06 · The smallest repair commutator — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** For two pending premise edits, use a cheap clean-update test to compare doing them together versus in the two possible orders. Export a measured difference as a small joint correction or mark the pair inseparable. A recipient can reuse the correction only on an update class for which the difference is stable.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Meta-gradient RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09801) and [task arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089) are natural stacked baselines; the [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md) already covers generic edit ordering. Candidate only if a measured order discrepancy learns an actual corrective write that survives unseen pairs, not if it just predicts which order to choose.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use two overlapping erroneous reward channels with one order-sensitive policy update. Compare all orders, joint relabeling and a low-rank discrepancy correction on held-out continuations at equal budgets.
- **Old-task retention:** Check untouched A/B and side effects on a third clean premise after both edit orders and a later clean update.

### CV2-07 · Replaying the tutor, not the record — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Persist a small set of executable *query choices* for soliciting clean feedback, not the historical assessor's answers. After retraction, replay a query with the repaired learner and current context so that feedback downstream of the retracted premise can change. For deterministic feedback compare to replay of the exact tutoring function.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Online robot correction removal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10280) already distinguishes fixed demonstrations from policy-dependent corrections; [exact RL unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04182) targets counterfactual histories. The candidate is a reusable way to *regenerate* policy-dependent teaching from a bounded query interface; replaying a fixed label or simulator seed is insufficient evidence of novelty.
- **Cheapest first test:** A synthetic tutor picks a hint based on the learner's last action. Compare cached labels, full tutor rerun, ordinary active relabeling and stored query policies on two edits, with controls free to query the same tutor.
- **Old-task retention:** Check unaffected old skills learned with hints and the clean reuse of a hint in a novel context.

### CV2-08 · Prove there is no identifiable local undo from this log — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Pair two toy worlds that agree on all recorded transitions and rewards but give different v2-optimal choices on a missing branch. Report the range of clean-policy outcomes over both worlds and the minimal experiment needed to separate them.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Doubly robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722) depends on coverage or correct models; [robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) studies uncertainty over MDPs. This is an identifiability assay for premise repair, not a new learning primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare full replay, pessimistic OPE and a single independent fork. A locally confident undo before that fork is a failure; after the fork, compare recovery compute against retraining.
- **Old-task retention:** Keep ordinary A/B decisions unchanged while withholding a claim of repair on the missing branch.

### CV2-09 · Route replacement credit where its absence matters — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Select v2 relabels by the independently measured divergence between repaired and clean trajectories, with nonzero exploration for rare divergences. A strong baseline applies the same intervention-first budget to ordinary reward relabeling.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [TracIn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08484) allocates influence to examples; [Audit Where Hacks Pay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35716) allocates audit calls where reward errors matter. Improvement: use *post-retraction divergence* to spend checks, not a new credit operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Add one low-frequency state where locally accurate relabeling leaves the policy on the wrong route. Compare prioritized replay and impact audits at equal oracle calls.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old rare successes as well as common A/B behavior; score ordinary reward and oracle return separately.

## B · Replace expiring step scores with reusable comparisons

### CV2-10 · Portable signed credit from a paired occupancy difference — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** For two forced actions at one proved prefix, keep a sparse **difference** of continuation occupancies, including a bound on omitted mass. Transport a signed label to a new solver only if a measured correction for the changed occupancy cannot flip it. Keep the obligation that determines which future sample could invalidate the bound.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Successor features](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312) already represent occupancies and recombine rewards; [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) already cancels shared effects and accumulates debt. Candidate only if storing the signed difference admits composable corrections for policy changes with strictly less state or rebranching than compressed SF plus #408 on the same observations. Fixed-policy reward recombination is not new.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct two legal arithmetic branches with a common continuation and a rare clue the upgraded solver can use. Compare two successor vectors, their plain difference, and a bounded sparse correction under two held-out solver changes. Count incorrect sign carries at matched coverage and oracle calls.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain old sign orderings for unaffected policies and old prefix validity, including an invalid action leading to the same terminal state.

### CV2-11 · A comparison whose adversary is a feasible solver edit — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Label a step by the nearest permitted continuation change that reverses its order, measured in executable edits (e.g. adding one tactic at one state). A certificate holds for a declared edit family or returns a reproducible reversing solver; it is not a claim over all future models.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) bounds value under model ambiguity; [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) invalidates on a solver footprint. The candidate is a transferable *reversal witness* specified in solver operations rather than a generic perturbation bound or score margin.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate allowed tactics on a five-state proof game. Compare a robust min–max bound over the same solver-edit set, #408 and stored nearest reversing edits under predeclared held-out upgrades. Better edit discovery at equal search costs is required; a certificate being true by construction is not a result.
- **Old-task retention:** Check unaffected old progress orderings and verify the exact prefix verdict under each reversing edit.

### CV2-12 · A matched prefix that stays useful after reconvergence — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Export a pairwise step comparison with a stopping rule: stop scoring when both branches meet in a state carrying all outcome-relevant history, then reuse one continuation for both. Learn stopping rules that still apply after a solver swap; record when a latent history difference blocks the stop.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Bisimulation metrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1386) summarize behavioral equivalence; [Twin-World Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31544) couples luck; [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) already cancels common tails. Novelty requires learned *reuse of reconvergence conditions* under changed continuation structure while beating the stack of these baselines, not simply stopping at identical states.
- **Cheapest first test:** Let two prefixes meet at the same displayed inventory but differ in a latent deduction flag; new solvers encounter distinct merge points. Compare exact-state memoization, learned bisimulation plus #408, and a stopped comparison at equal bytes and samples.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old ordering where the flag matters and old equivalences where it does not.

### CV2-13 · Partial credit that is safe to train on — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** If a stored comparison leaves a set of possible step rankings under an upgrade, derive the intersection of their allowed update effects on a frozen probe set. Take a bounded update only if it helps the identifiable part without worsening the unresolved comparisons to first order, then independently check finite-step effects or abstain.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) estimates values under ambiguity; [CAGrad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14048) combines conflicting objectives. Candidate only if partial identification produces a *less conservative write* than interval abstention or generic robust multi-objective training given the same rankings.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three candidate arithmetic moves have exactly one identified ordering after a solver swap. Compare abstention, pessimistic step-DPO and robust optimization over the same constraints; score update benefit and wrong-way effects on held-out prefixes.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B before and after repeated partial updates, particularly an old action sharing the ambiguous representation.

### CV2-14 · A signed comparison with a capability that can be switched off — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Pair every stored progress comparison with a small, executable capability intervention on the continuation policy. A new solver can be evaluated both with and without a specific tactic while preserving the other continuation choices insofar as the interface allows; keep a contrast attributable to that tactic rather than all differences between model versions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Rewarding Progress](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) uses a separate prover; [#406](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/406) includes repair-barred continuation policies. The distinction is a held-fixed, cause-specific *toggle in the incoming solver*, not choosing two already divergent panel members. If toggling changes unrelated behavior, only the compound intervention is identified.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enable or disable an arithmetic shortcut via the allowed action set on three otherwise shared continuation policies. Compare a fixed panel, GPI and toggled credit under a fourth solver; match calls per forced branch.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old valid detours and progress orderings on unrelated tactics with the intervention both enabled and disabled.

### CV2-15 · A portable question rather than a portable answer — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Distill an obsolete preference into the smallest pair of successor-state queries whose answers recover it across a declared family of solvers. The query pair is persistent; its answers are live. Many old preferences may share the same two queries even when their signs differ.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [VinePPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679) branches for fresh step credit; [successor features](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10964) amortize reward changes. Candidate only if the query basis amortizes unseen *continuation* changes beyond a cached critic over the same states and fixed panel, not simply because a value cache shares evaluations.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use many legal prefixes feeding into rearranged subproblems. Compare memorized values, successor-feature probes, sparse fresh rollouts and learned query equivalence classes on a later solver with different state visitation.
- **Old-task retention:** Recover old progress comparisons and untouched validity judgments from the live solver with the same query budget.

### CV2-16 · Progress as a decomposition with an explicit remainder — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Split a step's progress into independently checked irreversibility, an explainable part conditional on named continuations, and an unassigned residual. Train on separate losses; do not score an unassigned residual as zero or invalid.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Rewarding Progress](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) separates prover progress from the solver and [Decision Models as PRMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27068) provide step-level decision signals. An extra head and an explicit residual are an improvement/control, not a new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare two heads with split lifetimes, a third residual head, and complete fresh branching on an upgrade that makes a previously poor but legal step useful.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old impossible-versus-merely-slow distinctions, including when aggregate success has improved.

### CV2-17 · A non-telescoping credit audit — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Under a declared fixed reference continuation and discount, compare the discounted sum of purported potential differences with the endpoint difference. After a solver update, use independently rescored path segments to detect incompatible shaping values, including when all segments were assigned the same version. This does not test whether a coherent potential is useful.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Calculus on MDPs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09570) treats potential shaping as a gradient. Only an audit for the potential-based component; do not assume arbitrary action advantages telescope or claim the identity is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** Take two legal routes to the same endpoint and fit separate estimates of the same potential on overlapping segments. Compare a version tag, direct sum audit and random label refresh at equal independent checks; include a consistently wrong potential.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve valid old path scores and avoid blocking changed routes whose credit was coherently recomputed.

### CV2-18 · A replayable obstruction to transport — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Store two continuation models indistinguishable at the probes used so far yet ranking the step differently. Reuse this obstruction to avoid the *same blind probe class* on other prefixes, and query only a differentiating observable if it is admissible.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) bounds possible values, and [interactive specification elicitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22382) queries unresolved intent. The candidate state is a transferred non-identification witness about *solver competence*, not an uncertainty flag or reuse of a spec counterexample.
- **Cheapest first test:** Introduce distinct hidden capabilities with identical success rates on a training panel and opposite effects on two step families. Compare uncertainty sampling, robust interval plus fresh search, and stored obstructions at equal checks on an unseen family.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve determinate credit on old supported families; count false abstentions as well as confident errors.

## C · Repair the lesson when both its reward and its student have changed

### CV2-19 · A fourth-corner witness for joint credit migration — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Let D(v, π) be the checked return difference of two forced actions under reward version v and continuation π. With transition dynamics held fixed, the interaction between a premise edit and a solver upgrade is the inner product of the reward change with the change in *paired* occupancy difference. Partition state-action events by which premise is paid; keep sparse paired-occupancy changes only for blocks learned to matter across solver swaps. For omitted blocks bound the error by the sum of each block's maximum absolute reward change times its upper bound on omitted occupancy-change mass. Query the contribution of the largest uncertain block first, or branch fresh if no mass bound is credible. Carry the old prefix proof only under the unchanged proof statement.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Successor features](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312) factor reward from occupancy; [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) considers solver and verifier changes. The identity is not novelty. The primitive would be a cheaper *joint* migration test than compressed SF plus #408, with a bound on the interaction based on only the affected premise and continuation, for unseen edits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use arithmetic action pairs where each single-axis repair preserves the preference but some joint changes flip it. Learn the block sketches on one set of edit pairs and sample new pairs after freezing. Compare full four-corner rebranching, SF on a policy bank, additive repairs and sparse overlap on cost versus false carries at the same coverage.
- **Old-task retention:** Keep old proof validity and old rankings whose reward/solver changes do not interact; report misses on independent held-out edit pairs.

### CV2-20 · Separate the cost of changed judgment from the cost of changed exposure — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** In a controlled simulator, replay four histories with reward evaluation and data-collection policy each independently set to v1/v2 regimes. Compare the effect of changing a grading rule on frozen data with the extra change when the learner collects different data. Leave unsupported combinations unidentified outside the simulator.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dynamic mediation analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13348) already separates pathways sequentially; [online robot correction removal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10280) demonstrates dependent exposure. This is a diagnostic for how badly fixed-log reward repair misses the on-policy target.
- **Cheapest first test:** Put a changed reward on one early route and delay its consequences until a later clean task. Compare exact four-history results against estimates from fixed-log relabeling plus OPE.
- **Old-task retention:** Independently score old tasks before and after training on each of the four histories; report which losses are actually tied to changed exposure.

### CV2-21 · A correction stencil that leaves a control branch untouched — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Recompute only the signed progress correction for action pairs whose legal continuations reach the revised premise. Build a policy update that applies the correction on the affected half of each matched pair and enforces zero change on the unaffected half. A replacement lesson can therefore change the decision without erasing valid progress evidence that happened to use the same features.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Reward-specific token credit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21439) localizes reward influence, and [Step-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18629) optimizes pairwise step comparisons. The proposed operation transfers a *corrective teaching contrast*, not a token mask or a positive/negative label pair. Compare projected retraining with the same control examples.
- **Cheapest first test:** In a tiny proof game, one early move is shared by two branches but the altered reward is paid in only one branch. Compare paired relabel replay plus constrained policy optimization, separate reward heads, and the correction stencil on new prefixes.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure neutral-branch effect and performance of old tasks relying on the same initial move after correction and one additional update.

### CV2-22 · Quarantine a step's credit without discarding the step — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a premise is retracted, strip just its disputed credit channel from affected steps. Allow the now-clean prefix to generate fresh v2 comparisons, but only resume policy updates from it once one independent branch outcome confirms the new signed comparison; do not let the learner use its own newly increased frequency of the step as confirmation.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Trajectory unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21111) removes whole records; [Better Policies, Worse Verifier Audits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29029) shows component audits can collapse as policy exposure changes. Candidate only if typed separation makes previously unusable steps teach correctly with fewer independent comparisons than relabeling plus a component audit.
- **Cheapest first test:** Build a game where correct prefixes were strongly reinforced by an erroneous later bonus, then remove it. Compare trajectory discard, full relabeling, and held-out fresh branching with and without using the preserved prefix proof, at equal sample counts.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify old legal but rarely executed prefixes and old component checkers independently of aggregate terminal success.

### CV2-23 · Correct for a bounded future learning response, not a single past state — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Specify a family of next lessons under which a repaired learner must respond like one trained without the bad premise. Use a handful of *chosen* clean lessons as probes to fit a counter-update; retain the first new lesson on which its response diverges as a boundary. The target is a local equivalence of learning operators, not equality of weights, optimizer, or all past behaviors.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Form and Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17590) distinguishes forms of unlearning misalignment; [meta-gradient RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09801) measures sensitivity through learning. CV2-04 repairs a specific hidden state; this variant asks whether the smaller behavioral quotient is durable across optimizer changes. It must beat probe-response distillation from the same clean reference, not just a policy-only repair.
- **Cheapest first test:** With a clean online reference, fit counter-updates for a tiny SGD learner and an Adam learner, using two common probe lessons. Test the same correction representation on new lessons and a changed learning-rate schedule; compare explicit state repair and direct response distillation.
- **Old-task retention:** Test both A/B immediately and the ability to relearn an old variant at the same rate after a further clean lesson.

### CV2-24 · Buy an experiment that distinguishes two corrective writes — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When two supported clean histories imply different policy corrections, rank experiments by which one disambiguates the *action change after repair*, not which most reduces reward or value error. Learn to translate recurring disagreements into a runnable fork and reuse that translator across verifier versions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Decision-focused experimental design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05340) already selects information for downstream decisions; [Twin-World Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31544) uses paired rollouts. Candidate only if a learned translator reuses repair counterexamples to beat decision-focused design over the same experiments with no more oracle calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hide one of two causes of a false step label; the best probe for explaining the reward is not the best probe for choosing the patch. Compare generic information gain, decision-focused design and transferred fork selection under a held-out third correction.
- **Old-task retention:** Confirm that the chosen correction leaves old skills with both harmless sources of ambiguity intact.

### CV2-25 · Separate version changes that commute in evaluation but not in learning — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Compare applying a reward migration and a continuation update in either order. Test agreement at three levels: checked prefix facts, pairwise credit, and the independently measured effect of writing that credit to the learner. Show where matching the labels is not enough.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Successor features](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312) factor evaluation and [meta-gradient RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09801) differentiates through learning. This is a falsification assay for claims of commuting updates, not a new operator.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct versions that agree on all final labels but take SGD steps in different orders on shared features. Compare label refresh and both update orders to a same-start joint replay; score the next clean learning step as well.
- **Old-task retention:** Track A/B at all release boundaries, not just after final replay.

### CV2-26 · A repair record that closes only after a new lesson — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Separate closure of a reward premise from closure of its teaching consequences. Each repaired step comparison can authorize one small corrective update; that write remains provisional until a held-out lesson tests its predicted interaction with the corrected step. Failed lessons reopen only the tested interaction and propose a new corrective comparison rather than reusing the stale reward.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Reusable holdout](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629) guards repeated validation; [Inspectable Graders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11464) separate verifier quality from policy improvement. Neither makes a post-repair lesson part of the editable credit object. Candidate only if closure evidence reduces *subsequent* repair cost beyond gated relabeling with the same holdout and independent audits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Plant a benign old lesson that interacts with revised arithmetic credit only after a second update. Compare immediate repair gates, end-of-stream auditing, and per-interaction closure on a third held-out edit at matched independent labels.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old unrelated and overlapping lessons after both releases; count latently bad repairs that pass the immediate reward check but fail after new learning.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can an early correction travel through later learning without replaying it? (CV2-01, CV2-04, CV2-06, CV2-23)** Start on a fixed log to remove exposure ambiguity. Require an effect transport that handles genuinely noncommuting, finite updates and predicts the response to a clean held-out lesson. Compare fixed-log relabel replay, static negation, exact local Jacobian pushforward, optimizer reset and response distillation at equal cost. If only logit agreement or infinitesimal edits survive, keep replay or meta-gradients.
2. **Can an on-policy correction recover the missing experience that the wrong premise prevented? (CV2-02, CV2-07, CV2-08, CV2-20)** Start with paired toy worlds identical on the observed log but different at the first unsupported branch. Match clean online retraining, rollback, continue-under-v2, reward relabeling, and TracIn-based repair; also give the strongest control the same counterfactual access. A claim needs both honest uncertainty before the distinguishing fork and lower interaction cost afterward at matched A/B retention.
3. **Can portable progress credit survive a simultaneous solver and reward change without a full continuation panel? (CV2-10, CV2-14, CV2-18, CV2-19)** Hold the validity proof constant. Compare signed occupancy corrections and fourth-corner witnesses against robust SF/GPI, #408-style revalidation and fresh VinePPO branching. Draw joint edits after freezing the object; demand fewer calls or bytes at matched false carries and coverage, including cases where neither individual change would flip the sign.
4. **What can be learned from credit that is only partly identified? (CV2-11, CV2-13, CV2-15, CV2-24)** Hide a solver capability that makes only some comparisons ambiguous. Test whether using the identifiable component or buying one discriminating fork beats interval abstention and robust policy optimization on the same evidence. Require gain under finite writes and retention after an independent follow-on update.
5. **Can a verifier retract a bad reason without undoing behavior that has a good one? (CV2-03, CV2-05, CV2-21, CV2-26)** Plant correlated cues plus a real conjunction and independently test both after a premise edit. Compete with factorized reward heads, conjunction adapters, same-probe constrained replay, unlearn-then-relearn, and clean online retraining. A portable correction must preserve valid learning under unseen cue crossings; simply keeping the old action score does not suffice.
