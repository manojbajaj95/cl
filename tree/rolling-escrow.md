# Rolling escrow across defaults

**Status:** Ideas only. The fixtures are paper checks; no pilot or GPU work. **Parent:** [Escrowed paired leases](escrowed-paired-leases.md) ([PR #214][parent]). [Flood triage, row 325][triage] promotes parent cards **18** (roll a finite escrow) and **10** (make a relation unnecessary with truthful prep) to score 3, not to established primitives. The [idea ledger][ledger] requires a retention check in every first test and a joint mechanism that beats the best stack of its parts.

**Verdict:** The two ideas can fail independently. Rolling escrow must let a useful first write survive recurring default, extinguish the abandoned right, and still admit a genuinely blind pair without accumulating live obligations. Query-free prep must leave unequal raw slots but make the difference irrelevant to **every** admitted continuation, not merely to one launch read. The strongest new claim is that training establishes a reusable continuation guarantee which lets the escrow retire proof dependencies and reopen capacity that neither part could free alone.

## Contract and comparison

- **Freeze the interface first.** A = original tasks, Bᵢ = useful installed writes, C = installed and incoming successor tasks, W = frozen paired witness. Fix held-out tests, per-skill floors (including rare slices), clocks, legal batch IDs and precedence, observable events, lawful state reads, scope of W and of ordinary future updates, and dropout boundaries before creating blind arrivals. Retention means A and installed C plus **every** installed Bᵢ, checked after the indicated event and after a normal suffix; also require incoming C's utility and W's actual service. Refusals, useful single arrivals and completed pairs are separate outcomes.
- **An escrow boundary is an inductive claim.** The live record holds unspent entitlements, resource backing, applicable proof fragments and their current scopes. If a partner defaults, the installed write remains usable, every abandoned right becomes unspendable, and a lawful path to another real pair remains. Acquire/release the backing and mark the entitlement in the same logical transition. A finite reservation count is not a byte bound: charge proof templates and instances, clocks and epochs, revocation state, historical reconstruction, routing and model state, pinned or idle support, peak scratch and waiting. Separately report per-cycle planning/verification work, cumulative cost and worst-case default streak. No fall-back to unbounded append, reread or checkpoint/replay work.
- **Treat equivalence as a continuation contract.** Let H(z) be all *reachable* full states matching the lawful public prefix; hidden slots, their clocks and data cursors matter. Freeze a public action language for the remaining lease, including normal updates, fallback/default events, and permitted extensions into the next lease. Two states are equivalent if the same admitted actions are enabled, give the same specified public/contract outputs (or satisfy predeclared paired error bounds), and take them to related states; the common continuation must actually deliver W by its deadline. A proof can be by closure under each enabled transition, or by complete finite-horizon enumeration with an explicit expiry. Do not fit a witness, drop hard histories, or ban inconvenient suffixes after seeing the blind demands. Distinct raw state is allowed, including divergence outside the declared interface; this is not an erasure or privacy guarantee.
- **The prep word cannot be a disguised query.** Use one open-loop order over a predeclared portion of the successor's already-planned truthful data, under the same optimizer, step clocks and example multiset. No added steps, target-derived examples, resets, donor data, slot reads, time-dependent branching on private events, or fitting to the delayed payload. Publish the same order/proof binding across the original H(z). On each history, compare the full weight endpoint with default order under the parent's declared tolerance; pay for meaningful departures rather than calling them slot-only. If the word misses the launch deadline, makes W agree at the wrong value, or causes even one required suffix to escape the equivalence, the relation was not removed for that scope.
- **Main test.** Cross coupled rolling escrow with independently renewed one-shot [JRO2][parent], and trained equivalence with [JRO4][parent] (timely paid relation), default/uniform/best open-loop ordering, [OSR11][parent] order-only recovery, [synchronizing batches][sync-batch], legal direct reset and no read. Include per-pair two-phase commit, ledger with safe garbage collection, [fixed-budget handoffs][handoff], and checkpoint + replay under the same total bytes, and exact same-information planning as a tiny-host ceiling. Include an oracle relation with no read latency as an explicitly privileged ceiling. Match lawful opportunities, C utility, A/B retention and hard clocks before comparing timely pairs, useful half-use, proof/entitlement high-water marks and total cost. Replay at least three default cycles with alternating first sides, ordinary training and a later successful blind pair; then widen the streak, overlap and target mix. The combined arm must beat the best non-interacting stack, not a stale-ledger strawman.

## Two paper checks

### F1. Same final weights, different slots, one continuation cell

Take heavy-ball with step size 1, momentum coefficient 1/2, `m′ = m/2 + ∇L(w)` and `w′ = w − m′`. Start the prep at `w=(x,y)=0, m=±(1,0)`, with the same clock. These aliases are reachable from zero in two earlier steps with gradients `∓eₓ, ±3eₓ/2`; both histories end at zero weights with opposite slots. The prep menu is three truthful quadratic C batches with common teacher `(x,y)=0, c=1`: each has loss `wᵀHᵢw/2 + (c−1)²/2`, with a separate momentum for c. Let `H₁=I`, and
`H₂=[[3/2,1],[1,1]]`, `H₃=[[7/4,1/2],[1/2,3/2]]`. Both latter Hessians are positive definite.

| Word | Step-2 weights for the positive alias | Final (x,y) weights, both aliases | Final (x,y) momentum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prep: 1 → 2 → 3 | (0, 1/2) | (0, 0) | ±(0, 1/2) |
| Default: 1 → 3 → 2 | (1/8, 1/4) | (0, 0) | ±(1/8, 1/4) |

In both arms the first step gives `w₁=∓eₓ/2`. Setting `Mᵢ=3I/2−Hᵢ` gives `w₃=(M_last M_middle−I/2)w₁`; `M₂M₃=M₃M₂=I/2` so both endpoints match exactly. Starting at `c=m_c=0`, both orders end at `c=5/4`, reducing C loss on c from `1/2` to `1/32`. The two prep slots really differ.

Freeze admitted suffixes with no y-to-observable coupling and no y-dependent guard, output, clipping, or stopping rule. Then equality of `(x,m_x,c,m_c)` is closed under those suffixes, even as raw y slots evolve. For a witness check, require one further ordinary training step and precommit to adding a calibration offset from `{−1/16,0,+1/16}` to its resulting x read; W requires output 0 and the action cannot inspect that read first. The default order needs opposite offsets for the two histories after that step (`x=∓1/16`); the prep order uses offset 0 for both. A timely bit about the sign of `m_x` suffices for the default arm. A legal slot reset before prep would also tie on this fixture. An A check `|x|≤1/2` passes at all the prep cuts and the illustrated W step; this is not a rich A/B retention result. Add two ordinary steps with H₂ to the admitted suffixes instead, and the prepared aliases have `x=±1/4` at the second step: the old proof is out of scope. The test distinguishes closed behavior from one-read agreement, not this fixed quadratic from optimized teaching or robust planning.

### F2. Constant live count is not constant memory

In a single reusable slot, the live token for pair j is canceled on default and the slot is reissued to pair j+1. If token validity uses only a wrapping generation and authentic old messages can be delayed arbitrarily, a late original token becomes valid again at wrap. A nonwrapping counter uses growing bits; a permanent revocation set or an off-ledger clock also has a cost. A constant-size recent-token window can be sound with bounded message lifetime and issue rate, checked expiry, and no reuse inside that lifetime; test just inside and outside the reuse boundary. If every installed Bᵢ also contains an independent arbitrary bit that must remain exactly retrievable by its fixed probe, N installed tasks require at least N bits somewhere: there are 2ᴺ different required answer strings. Fixed escrow metadata cannot make that capacity bound disappear. A constant-*total*-space claim needs a specified jointly representable task family (or bounded active tasks), with held-out retention rather than free archival storage. Neither result proves rolling service.

## Idea cards

## A. Bound what rolls, not just what is allocated

### 01. Reissue only after a proof-carrying close — inherited primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Close a default with one fragment that proves the abandoned right is dead, preserves the installed write, and establishes the exact assumptions needed for the next pair to reserve the released backing. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [JRO2][parent], escrow transactions and two-phase commit already handle a single reservation; the candidate is a renewable learner-side precondition, not reissuing an ordinary token. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three alternating defaults and a successful fourth pair against fresh one-shot escrow with safe garbage collection, including one permanently pinned backing. **Old-task retention check.** Test all earlier Bᵢ and A/C during closure, immediately after the next reservation, and after its first write.

### 02. Separate proof lifetimes by what can invalidate them — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use one invariant per shared carrier and an expendable fragment binding a pair's deadlines and useful reads to it; discard the latter at timeout only if no live obligation cites it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Safe order families][safe] and [order handoffs][order] compose cached certificates; the bet is reusing a carrier theorem across new learned uses even after all old lease bindings expire. **Cheapest first test.** Reissue the same carrier to three novel witness bindings; change one legal read in the third. Compare reference-counted proof caching and scoped instantiation at equal verification and bytes. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and Bᵢ after each discard, including a rare prior B that uses the changed read.

### 03. Spend only against a reserve that a real consumer can use — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify a potential over usable backing and fallback debt, not free handles. Each timeout must pay down debt enough to fit a named cold replacement demand, including its peak scratch. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint releases][release] proves joint admission and [successor-funded returns][funded] charges shared carriers; the new target is a reusable induction measure for alternating default and ordinary training. **Cheapest first test.** Compare free-handle and load-aware certificates on a two-carrier host with one hidden live reader; check the exact scheduler ceiling and a pattern that exhausts reserve. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise the old reader and A/C before and after the replacement actually consumes capacity.

### 04. Carry a fallback invariant through changes of first writer — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Transfer a public role-parametric theorem that supports either side publishing first without keeping two independent fallback allocations alive. Keep any semantic asymmetry as a checked, current assumption. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [JRO2][parent] already allows role reversal and [fixed-budget handoffs][handoff] recycles allocations; the distinction is renewability across side changes without importing old histories or reserving both role plans. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate left–right–left defaults in a smooth two-head host where slopes differ by role; compare two cached single-role proofs and a symmetric role-swap negative. **Old-task retention check.** Probe both sides' earlier writes and held-out A/C at each swap and final pairing.

### 05. Let a useful write change backing without changing its contract — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Before returning a right, have successor training make an already-installed B serve through a shared, younger carrier; validate the old task against its original acquisition floor before unpinning the old carrier. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Fixed-budget handoffs][handoff] recycles slots and [successor-funded returns][funded] uses successor work for return; the hypothesis is retiring one real resource dependency *while* B remains public during renewal. **Cheapest first test.** Force reuse by a fourth consumer after three defaults, with one B that cannot migrate; compare exact reallocation, separate adapters and equal-budget distillation. **Old-task retention check.** Test rare B probes at both switch boundaries and A/C during subsequent use of the reclaimed resource.

### 06. Use a deadline window instead of a permanent tombstone — improvement
**Mechanism.** Bind entitlement acceptance to bounded, predeclared delivery/retry lifetimes; recycle a token only after no scoped old message can apply. Count the time source and tokens still in flight. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Expiring leases, generation counters and safe reclamation already do this; it audits the claim that proof memory did not simply move into revocation state. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust F2 through a wrap with late and duplicate events, plus an unbounded-delay negative. Compare an ordinary generation counter and one-shot cleanups. **Old-task retention check.** Check A/C and every legitimately installed B after stale rejection and after the first valid reuse.

### 07. Prove a bound on overlapping fallbacks and arrivals — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Size a single pool for simultaneous fallback and new reservations by proving which cuts can overlap in reachable training traces; never lend mutually required scratch. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint releases][release] and [protective orders][protect] already certify joint cuts; the candidate object is an overlap certificate that survives a rolling change of useful writers and their proof scopes. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two adjacent leases with a default between two successor batches and a third arrival; compare serial, independent reservation and optimal scheduling. **Old-task retention check.** Test old Bᵢ and A/C at the interleaving that attains peak load and after the later paired write.

### 08. Make irreducible retention debt explicit — improvement
**Mechanism.** Separate fixed escrow state from task information. Freeze compressible task families, exact acquisition floors and live-task width; refuse new admissions when the required behaviors outgrow the host. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Information counting and [fixed-budget handoffs][handoff] already bound storage; the audit prevents an “unlimited pairs” claim from silently retiring old B tasks. **Cheapest first test.** Contrast F2's independent bits with tasks generated by one shared coefficient vector; compare a compact task store and a learned shared carrier at equal total bytes. **Old-task retention check.** Query every B probe in shuffled order after each reuse, plus rare A/C slices and blind inputs from previously installed B tasks.

## B. Make preparation remove the need to observe

### 09. Train for a closed continuation cell, not equal slots — inherited primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose one held-batch word that maps the entire reachable alias class into a relation closed under every admitted suffix transition, without resetting hidden slots. Bind a common W dispatch before observing the alias. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Synchronizing batches][sync-batch] already separates raw state and read equality; [JRO4][parent] buys a decision split. The stricter claim is eliminating the priced split over a future action language, not only making one launch read agree. **Cheapest first test.** Use F1 and its coupling negative; compare one-read alignment, paid sign relation, direct reset and exact common-word search. **Old-task retention check.** Check A/C and pre-existing Bᵢ across each prep prefix, W, and every allowed slot-sensitive suffix in the fixture.

### 10. Find the invisible slot directions from the legal future — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For a frozen switched linear host, compute the largest subspace inside the read kernel that is invariant under all admitted updates. Accept an order when its full state difference lands there; reject equality only at sampled reads. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Observability and invariant subspaces][kalman] supply the algebra; a candidate CL operation spends truthful examples to make a live query obsolete under a named scope, rather than predicting drift with a Gramian. **Cheapest first test.** Extend F1 by one reachable direction; compare exact invariant closure against a Gramian threshold, reset and exhaustive prefix tests. Drop any primitive claim if plain synthesis ties. **Old-task retention check.** Include A/B/C reads in the kernel test and run rare old slices after the longest reachable distinguishing suffix.

### 11. Make slot blindness nontrivial in a smooth shared host — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make nuisance variations tangent to a learned invariant set while both slots and W remain useful elsewhere on the same host; match C progress and preserve raw differences. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Synchronizing batches][sync-batch] considers merging through an inactive gate, and F1 isolates coordinates by construction. The new claim is closure under smooth, genuinely coupled updates without sealing an unused pathway. **Cheapest first test.** Search orders of three fixed true C batches in a tiny two-head tanh learner; verify closure or a certified tube on a held-out interval, with matched reset, projected update and exact tiny-host search. **Old-task retention check.** Test A/C and every Bᵢ on support-sensitive inputs for which shutting down the shared carrier would fail.

### 12. Match Adam clocks as part of the equivalence, not a nuisance covariate — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Include first and second moments, bias-correction age and arithmetic in the transition proofs; leverage ratios that keep an output direction identical through ordinary updates, not equality at one ratio snapshot. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Branch handshakes][branch] explores joint slot symmetries; the novelty bet is entering a closed cell by training alone without a slot transformation or a different optimizer clock. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate order permutations in one visible and one nuisance Adam coordinate, then introduce a nonzero subsequent gradient and an age shift; compare reset, optimizer projection and matched-clock query. **Old-task retention check.** Check A/C and Bᵢ before and after the nonzero-gradient burst, with small-margin tasks that detect an adaptive step discrepancy.

### 13. Certify a finite contract with a hard expiry — improvement
**Mechanism.** If only bounded-horizon agreement is provable, expire the fragment before the first unchecked transition and return to a certified fallback or a still-lawful relation. Never sell it as persistent equivalence. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Bounded model checking and [order handoffs][order] already give scoped guarantees; this is a lifetime and pricing control for the stronger closure claim. **Cheapest first test.** Add to F1 a suffix whose distinguishing read appears only after two steps; sweep expiry against query arrival and W's latest launch. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and Bᵢ on the last certified step and the first ordinary step after expiry, including the no-query fallback.

### 14. Demand a no-word certificate when a query still matters — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For every legal prep word, exhibit an attainable class and a required demand whose timely useful reply sets still have empty intersection; distinguishability alone does not imply a query is needed. Transfer a compact obstruction to a scheduler instead of retrying prep. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Group obstructions][obs] certifies failing reply intersections for a given information set; the proposed dual covers changes to that set and its reply sets induced by physical training. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate all truthful three-batch words on a host where a persistent sign forces opposite W bindings, plus a distinguishable-but-irrelevant sign negative and F1 as a positive. Compare exact no-word search and query-first policies. **Old-task retention check.** Verify A/C and Bᵢ during bypass and paid-query service, and reject any supposedly decisive word that wins by dropping a protected read.

### 15. Bound future behavioral spread with nonrenewable error — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For approximate equivalence, certify a forward-invariant error tube that pays for new perturbations and accumulates no hidden “fresh tolerance” at every renewal. Freeze deadlines and decision margins in advance. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Approximate bisimulation][approx] provides precision guarantees; the new object would be an optimizer-learned, spendable error budget conserved across lease renewals. **Cheapest first test.** Perturb F1's Hessians and compare a common Lyapunov tube with per-cycle tolerance resets and paid queries near the first decision boundary. **Old-task retention check.** Check near-margin A/C and earlier Bᵢ after the longest default streak, not just mean endpoint loss.

### 16. Precommit against a family of blind witnesses — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select prep before seeing which of two or more noninterchangeable W jobs arrives. Certify their joint continuation cell, then dispatch only on the legitimately public job type. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [JRO4][parent] already transports a relation across witnesses; [synchronizing batches][sync-batch] considers two witnesses. The improvement becomes a primitive candidate only if one closed cell amortizes multiple otherwise necessary queries without shrinking the real offer. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze two different W reads, hold one job draw blind, and include a third out-of-scope read. Compare one bit per job, shared relation, and exact joint common-word search. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and Bᵢ through both served branches and through lawful refusal of the third.

## C. Make the two mechanisms earn their coupling

### 17. Retire a proof dependency with already-needed training — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use prep to prove that a live fallback no longer depends on a hidden-slot obligation inherited from the defaulted pair; release the backing for that obligation only after the new closed proof holds. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Fixed-budget handoffs][handoff] reuses backing and [JRO4][parent] splits histories. The distinctive interaction is replacing a retained conditional obligation with a cheaper single live fragment *by changing* continuation behavior. **Cheapest first test.** Cross cleanup and prep in a two-carrier fixture with an actual third claimant; give the independent stack identical search/proof budgets and a lawful default pattern that leaves no releasable carrier. **Old-task retention check.** Run all A/C and Bᵢ at discharge, immediately after reuse, and one unrelated ordinary update later.

### 18. Make defaults determine when to prepare, not what hidden state to target — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reorder an already-scheduled C prefix selected by a public timeout stage so one class-wide prep word finishes inside the next W window; never condition the word on an unobserved default cause. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Order handoffs][order] caches partial-order policies; the bet is recovering otherwise unusable temporal slack from actual C progress rather than repeating warmup or borrowing another lease's time. **Cheapest first test.** Try early and late timeouts in a four-batch DAG with a real W deadline; compare cached scheduling, default-independent prep, and a latency-free query. **Old-task retention check.** Score A/C and every Bᵢ at the early prep prefix, the late-case fallback, and the next blind pair.

### 19. Reuse a quotient proof without assuming a stationary successor — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Admit a new successor law only after a public refinement check maps its transitions to the existing interface; invalidate rather than pile up exceptions when a new law can expose an old slot difference. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Refinement and [order handoffs][order] already port contracts; the distinctive bet is preserving query-free service through genuine learner-law drift without keeping old slot histories or a proof per successor. **Cheapest first test.** Replace the C objective twice, first within a verified invariant family and then with F1's coupling; compare full recertification, selective cache invalidation and query in each epoch. **Old-task retention check.** Probe A/C and every prior B on both law changes, including fallback at invalidation and the first admitted update under the new law.

### 20. Protect paired service without silently excluding hard continuations — improvement
**Mechanism.** Freeze requested action and payload coverage in a blind stream before proof synthesis; report which admitted continuations disappear under each gate, and charge each disappearance as lost service. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Safe order families][safe] already trades admission breadth for safety; this stress test detects a vacuous quotient built by refusing all slot-sensitive work. **Cheapest first test.** Interleave ordinary slot-coupling C and cold partners in a predeclared held-out mix; compare strict gating, paid queries, and proof widening at equal total budget. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate A/C and installed Bᵢ at both accepted and rejected cuts and after all ordinary work that the system promised to support.

### 21. Teach renewability rather than optimizing one lucky period — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** In separate offline design streams, seek a shared host where authentic C progress tends to establish the same small successor interface on each round; freeze the host and order synthesis rules before blind leases. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Successor-funded returns][funded] asks whether learning buys recovery; [absorbing cycles][cycle] studies repeated dynamics. The harder claim is a learned interface that keeps bounded renewal possible without requiring a particular cycle, task ID or extra per-pair supervision. **Cheapest first test.** Fit a tiny smooth multi-head host offline; evaluate unseen, irregular defaults with matched meta-training and same-width separate adapters, plus an adversarial order with a different partner mix. **Old-task retention check.** Keep all historical Bᵢ, A and C acquisition floors fixed and retest after every blind renewal and a common ordinary suffix.

### 22. Certify renewal against an adaptive default schedule — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Synthesize a strategy on public escrow states with one uniform choice per public history; prove it can return to a ready class after every allowed default and serve if a compatible pair arrives. A plan in hindsight for each dropout trace is insufficient. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Safety games and [fixed-budget handoffs][handoff] already provide fixed points and repetition; the candidate is a reusable learner-aware winning region under rolling retention, with proofs that compress without weakening cold admission. **Cheapest first test.** Build a two-entitlement game with a three-cycle greedy trap and a bounded task family. Compare the exact winning region with a stitched set of one-shot certificates, then give the adversary one additional legal dropout cut that invalidates the strategy. **Old-task retention check.** Require A/C and every installed B in the winning-state predicate and challenge it on the trap and escape traces.

### 23. Require the same public randomness to work for every alias — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Prove that the common prep places aliases in a cell closed under a shared, public randomness stream for ordinary mini-batch sampling or dropout. The certificate must not get a history-specific permutation of random draws that the runtime cannot implement. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Probabilistic bisimulation and [synchronizing batches][sync-batch] already cover stochastic abstraction or post-processing; the bet is query elimination with the actual shared training randomness, not equality in distribution under an unavailable coupling. **Cheapest first test.** Use two equally likely suffix batches whose scalar outputs are swapped across aliases; a marginal test passes but a common-draw test fails. Search for an order that instead makes both draws agree, against exact stochastic planning and a paid relation. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and earlier Bᵢ under each draw, especially the paired draw hidden by the equal marginals.

### 24. Break proof cycles before trying to reclaim their carriers — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Track simultaneous fallback dependencies: a proof may promise that a second proof will hold *after* release, but neither may use the other's not-yet-established release as its premise. Let one checked C training prefix discharge a cycle edge and authorize a finite joint close. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Rely–guarantee reasoning and deadlock detection cover the logical cycle; the distinct bet is breaking it through useful training, rather than paying for an extra carrier or withdrawing an installed task. **Cheapest first test.** Give two overlapping leases mutually conditional closes, and a held-batch C word that removes one edge. Compare a resource-graph scheduler, spare backing and a paid query; falsify with a cycle where no lawful word breaks either edge. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and each Bᵢ before either close, in the intermediate state after the first discharge, and when both freed resources are used.

### 25. Try to compile the joint mechanism into the incumbent stack — improvement
**Mechanism.** Give a transaction controller with safe garbage collection and a conformant curriculum planner the same proof interface, data, cached work and observables; instrument bytes, service and cost at each cut. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** The baseline is exactly [JRO2/JRO4/OSR11][parent] plus [fixed-budget handoffs][handoff] and [synchronizing batch synthesis][sync-batch]; if that stack reproduces the result at equal cost, rename it as composition, not a primitive. **Cheapest first test.** Solve the crossed factorial exactly on a finite host, then reuse its planning policy on withheld ordinary training laws with tightened memory. **Old-task retention check.** Require per-cut A/C and installed Bᵢ noninferiority before comparing service, including after expiry, refusal and actual backing reuse.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can ordinary training retire a real escrow dependency, making the joint system strictly better than independently stacking the two mechanisms?** Start with 01/09/17/24/25. Show that the same truthful prep that closes the slot distinction lets a blind next partner actually use released backing. Cross the two mechanisms and count timely pairs and useful single arrivals at matched retention, C utility, proof/learner bytes and clocks. Kill if safe cleanup plus independently synthesized prep achieves the same frontier.
2. **Can a slot distinction be made irrelevant to a genuinely shared, nontrivial future?** Use 10–12/14/16/23. Derive an attainable alias class and a lawful no-word negative. Require unequal raw slots, a common W that works and closure over a frozen family of normal updates and blind jobs. A one-read match, dormant dummy slot, hard-coded mask or a direct reset tie does not establish the stronger operation.
3. **What limits fixed-space renewal when defaults and valid tasks compound?** Use 02–08/22. Separate proof, entitlement, task-state and admitted overlap bounds; make reuse and freshness explicit. Map the smallest exhaustion pattern and check the next blind pair after three defaults and longer streaks. If safe bounded reclamation requires a service restriction, state its exact assumption and cost.
4. **When should the system choose prep, a paid relation or fallback under shifting deadlines and laws?** Use 13/15/18–20. Freeze the successor offers, query latency, action scope and verification cost before seeing blind jobs. Require a decision rule that anticipates expired proofs and open obstruction cores and improves timely protected service over the best precommitted or query-first schedule. Never trade old-task retention for an apparent reduction in reads.
