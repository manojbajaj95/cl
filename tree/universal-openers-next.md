# Universal openers next: cancel the hidden route, then return the write capacity

**Status:** Ideas only; the two paper examples below specify proposed falsifiers, not pilot results. **Parent:** [Universal openers](universal-openers.md) ([PR #284][parent]), cards [03](universal-openers.md#03-open-a-route-by-negating-a-dependency--primitive-candidate) and [13](universal-openers.md#13-use-expiry-to-return-capacity-to-an-incompatible-native-edit--primitive-candidate). The [reviewer's score-3 row][triage] singles out two mechanisms: a due update makes two shared reader paths respond oppositely to a hidden alias; expiry admits a second incompatible native write without erasing the first. The references below are nearest controls, not claims of exhaustive novelty. Cards 01 and 09 restate the two seeds as reference controls.

## What would count

- **Same blind write, all aliases.** A/B/installed C are the old tasks; B_d is the defaulted B slice (common and rare), W is paired service, and R_d is the defaulter's receipt. Before the opener seal the reachable aliases, due record menu, native optimizer including its slots, editor interface, independent arrival bounds, tolerance, and allowable continuations. Inherit the parent's fresh draw, trusted report, coverage accounting and independent clearing. The report stays with the verifier; it cannot pick the write, cap, slot, phase or route, and aliases giving other reports still count. Test both the individual paths and their sum under identical editor actions. An equality at one point, or cancellation only along the diagonal where both inputs move together, is not independent-input tolerance.
- **Capacity means a native write becomes feasible.** For fixed truthful E and F, show E is admitted and F excluded while open (also check F after E but before decay). Before spending E, certify F at its guaranteed later admission, with E's independently scored service still installed. Check that F wasn't simply sent away to a spare, and that an early attempt, late E, absent E and every declared no-result branch are handled on time. Ordinary updates move the phase; a clock or a certificate can observe it but cannot reset it. Claim only the deadline and continuation envelope actually proved.
- **Compare at equal delivered service and resource budget.** Match initial item margins, truth access, useful C gain, clocks, scheduler, executed update/probe work, and peak bytes and live byte-ticks (including proofs, tags and slots). Give the strongest control the same legal batch choice, native warm-up, projections if affordable, scheduler and independent diagnostic. Include the non-cancellation [carried-coverage](carried-coverage.md) opener, one opener per alias, freeze, [OGD][ogd], [GEM][gem], [DER][der], the single-editor window, two openers, a tuned fixed gate and dedicated units at matched total capacity. Following the [idea ledger][ledger], no win if the best stack of those parts ties. Report old-task floors on every prefix and refusal, meet W by its deadline, actually reuse R_d backing after lawful retirement, then run a blind old-task suffix including both B_d slices. Each card names an additional retention stress.

## Two paper probes to design the first tests

**X — identical endpoint, different write tolerance.** For h ∈ {−1,+1}, two live paths contribute `3/8 + h/4 + h·p·u` and `3/8 − h/4 + h·q·u` to an old reader. Both paths read the same two editor inputs u. An opener is to change slope vectors `p=(1/2,1/2), q=(0,0)` to `p=(3/4,3/4), q=(−1/2,−1)`. The idle read stays 3/4; its tolerance is 3/32. Each independent axis write of magnitude 1/4 gives error 1/8 before and 1/16 after; either path's post-opening response alone exceeds tolerance on either axis. The joint corner `(1/4,−1/4)` after gives error 1/8 and still fails. Thus the union of the two closed axis intervals is allowed after opening, not their full square. A third alias with the same idle read and magnitude-matched slopes `p=(3/4,3/4), q=(1/2,1)` fails both axis extremes; a report identifying it cannot save this opener. The coefficients prescribe the desired state change; a real first test must obtain it from a truthful native update and demonstrate positive held-out C gain on both axes.

**T — phase, not elapsed ticks.** Use one shared scalar x, starting at 0, and a gate g. A useful opener is to set g=3/4. Native E and F apply gate-scaled SGD to truthful squared losses with targets 1 and 3/8 respectively: `E(x)=x+g·(1−x)` and `F(x)=x+(1−g)·(3/8−x)`. Held-out service predicates are `x≥7/16` for E and `1/4≤x≤1/2` for F. At high g, E gives 3/4; F gives 3/32 if attempted first and 21/32 immediately after E, both outside its service band. An ordinary gate update `g←g/3` does not change x; F then gives 15/32, satisfying both predicates. After the same number of ticks a weaker legal record `g←2g/3` would give F output 9/16, so do not certify that branch for the same deadline unless another timely lawful update makes it safe. Late E from x=0 at g=1/4 gives 1/4 and fails; if E missed, F alone at g=1/4 gives 9/32, which passes F but confers no E credit. This checks only the proposed admission arithmetic. A tuned fixed gate can reproduce the favorable path, and neither example verifies A/B/C/W. A pilot must put real pressure on the resource-matched stack, not count the arithmetic as a win.

## A. Earn tolerance by composing two live paths

### 01. Train the opposing pair, not just the common write — primitive candidate (seed reference)
**Mechanism:** Make a due record change the two alias-response slopes jointly so either blind axis write can land while both paths remain in ordinary service. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [OGD][ogd] spends a projection at edit time; this is #284#03's proposed reusable interval before the editor arrives, not a new claim beyond that seed. **Cheapest first test:** Instantiate X with a menu of two genuine C batches and native momentum SGD, spending just one opener. Compare the opened axis intervals to the non-cancelling common opener, per-alias openers, OGD and reader freeze, including the same-sign third alias. **Old-task retention check:** Test both rare B_d path-dominant families at both axis extremes after reuse, not just the balanced aggregate.

### 02. Exchange a bad second-order response for two good first-order ones — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train the two paths so a due update assigns mixed curvature to the path with the opposite cross term. The signed interference area of a two-write rectangle shrinks even though its single-axis edges are unchanged. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Sobolev training][sobolev] matches derivatives to targets; the additional object is a natively acquired rectangle of edit sequences bought by transferring curvature between existing old-reader paths, without old derivative targets. **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a bilinear two-path host and all four held-out corners in both edit orders; compare frozen curvature, first-order cancellation, and OGD applied twice. Reject if only one order passes. **Old-task retention check:** Keep an old reader with both inputs active and check it between writes as well as at the rectangle corners after reuse.

### 03. Train away the rare alias's unpaired response without tagging it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Make the same due update bind a third alias's currently unpaired response to the other path through a feature shared with ordinary C; the original two aliases keep their prior paired response. No third path or alias-indexed state is added. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [IRM][irm] looks for a predictor shared across environments; the proposal changes a worst-alias derivative of a *later* native weight update while allowing the existing paths to remain alias-specific. **Cheapest first test:** Extend X to three aliases and enumerate a frozen truthful C menu, including a twin where the third alias has a structurally unreachable opposite sign. Compare invariant training, per-alias openers and a dedicated added path. **Old-task retention check:** Treat the third alias as a rare old B_d slice and require its floor on both the successful and refused openings.

### 04. Make cancellation survive path-specific optimizer memory — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** A due update creates opposing *effective* responses across two different momentum decays, so the accumulated response is safe through a short blind write word even when the raw gradients at each tick are not opposite. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [OGD][ogd] and [PCGrad][pcgrad] change gradients now; the proposed reusable object is a response bound for untouched slots and later native steps, not opposition in gradient space. **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two momentum slots with decays 1/2 and 1/4 and opposite initial carries. Compare two-step native updates with gradient-level cancellation, slot reset and optimizer-aware projected writes. Try delayed overshoot after equal first writes. **Old-task retention check:** Read the installed A/B/C tasks on the overshoot tick and on the ordinary no-editor suffix, including rare B_d.

### 05. Spend the same opened pair on two old readers with different tolerances — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Coordinate one due update so the paths cancel the nuisance for two readers with different path weightings without making their useful features identical; bounded residual goes to the reader with spare margin. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [GEM][gem] jointly enforces remembered-task inequalities for an edit. Here the inequalities constrain a reusable future menu, paid for by changing a live pair that both readers still use. **Cheapest first test:** Try two unequal readout vectors and two prospective native directions. Exhaustively compare coupled paired training, joint GEM plus independent warm-up, and a rank-one host where protecting one reader necessarily harms the other. **Old-task retention check:** Register separate floors and matched margins; never average the tighter old read with the looser one.

### 06. Switch the cancelling partner at an activation seam — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train two live paths so the sign opposition transfers to a different active unit at an existing activation boundary; no service regression is allowed on the seam or on legal jumps across it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Piecewise-linear verification such as [Beta-CROWN][crown] can certify branching regions; the claim is an ordinary update that makes a previously closed seam traversable for future native edits without retaining two complete paths on each side. **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate the exact regions of a three-unit ReLU reader plus its two boundary points; compare fixed-sign cancellation, an exact verified warm-up and a dedicated seam unit. Include a legal step skipping the seam at evaluation. **Old-task retention check:** Preserve old inputs immediately on either side and exactly at the seam after a crossing and after reuse.

### 07. Cancel the worst reachable difference, not the largest hidden mode — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train opposite responses only to alias differences a blind continuation can still activate; preserve a large alias difference useful to another old reader rather than collapsing the whole hidden state. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Conformant planning][conformant] can ignore distinctions irrelevant to a robust plan, and [IRM][irm] can suppress nuisance. The bet is a due update that edits the response operator while leaving a useful latent distinction and expanding the same native menu. **Cheapest first test:** Give a linear host one reachable small nuisance mode and one large unreachable mode that an installed reader needs. Compare exact conformant search, full invariance and rank-matched warm-up; add a lawful word that makes the large mode reachable. **Old-task retention check:** Test the installed reader that needs the large mode and clear promptly on the newly reachable twin rather than suppress it.

### 08. Keep the interval under adversarial rounding of the two paths — improvement
**Mechanism:** Carry separate rounding envelopes for both signed terms at serving precision, choosing dyadic amplitudes or rejecting the opener when independently rounded errors exhaust retention slack. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Fixed-point interval verification already treats rounding uncertainty; this checks whether X-style path cancellation implies an actual deployment advantage over widening or an earlier safe edit, not a new solver. **Cheapest first test:** Exhaustively round each path to a small signed format before summing, including opposite round ties. Compare floating-point-only proof, one extra precision bit and exact representable warm-up at equal bytes. **Old-task retention check:** Use actual serving precision for near-boundary rare B_d and for the post-reuse suffix, not an audit executed in higher precision.

## B. Return capacity without undoing the first editor

### 09. Spend, then decay into an incompatible spend — primitive candidate (seed reference)
**Mechanism:** A useful opener raises a shared gate that admits E and excludes F; ordinary decay switches which truthful native update can be spent, leaving E's acquired service in the shared learner. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Temporal-logic MPC][stl] and [rolling escrow](rolling-escrow.md) already encode timed release; this restates #284#13's native release premise, not novelty beyond it. **Cheapest first test:** Instantiate T with separately drawn E/F batches and one shared learner. Compare the single window, two openers, best fixed gate at equal work, separate units, and a decay that erases E. **Old-task retention check:** Check E after F, both B_d slices on a missed-E fallback, and W before the handoff deadline.

### 10. Let E's useful gradient shorten its own occupancy — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Along with useful E learning, the native write changes the live rate of subsequent decay, so F can be guaranteed one due record earlier than after a missed E. E itself must not directly grant F admission. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Option-Critic][options] learns state-dependent termination; the claim is a change to the learner's future feasible write menu caused by doing E, not a learned stop decision or an E-complete flag. **Cheapest first test:** Couple gate decay to a shared feature learned by truthful E; enumerate E, a loss-matched decoupled E, no E and a timer that sees the same public events. Include a larger E gradient that overshoots and delays release. **Old-task retention check:** Check E and installed C on the accelerated and overshoot branches and W on the default-length branch.

### 11. Give F capacity in one direction while E keeps another — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Ordinary service drains one mode of a shared optimizer gate faster than the other. F can use the drained mode while E's service still needs the residual mode, with neither editor receiving a locked unit. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Hard attention to the task][hat] protects capacity by task-specific masks; here both modes remain live under native optimization and admission changes by decay, not a per-task mask switch. **Cheapest first test:** Use two shared coordinates with two decay rates and two fixed candidate F directions; compare a scalar schedule, per-task masks, two openers and rotated F that requires the still-occupied mode. **Old-task retention check:** Score the old task loading both modes after F, including continued updates that finally drain the slower mode.

### 12. Release a gate only after E's service has moved into the ungated path — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** The ordinary record causing decay also transfers the installed E effect into an existing ungated shared read. F can reuse the gate only if the transferred E read stays good after F; late offload cannot repair a premature failure. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Net2Net][net2net] transfers a function through network reparameterization and [LwF][lwf] preserves it with distillation. The candidate is a native useful record that simultaneously frees future write capacity and passes an independent post-edit service test, not an explicit copy or new unit. **Cheapest first test:** Two live paths and two lawful due records with equal service value; use gate-only and transfer-only offline masks, plus explicit function-preserving transfer and separate decay at matched work. Include an E validation family the receiving path cannot express. **Old-task retention check:** Read E, original A and rare B_d during the transfer and after gate reuse; equality of summed E output at the transfer instant is insufficient.

### 13. Use the late editor's due data to finish only the decay it needed — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** The ordinary first record of F dissipates remaining occupancy through a shared read while serving its own supervised objective; F's independently supplied second record is the first allowed incompatible write. The first F record must be legal even on the slow-decay branch. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** The parent's [single-editor window](universal-openers.md) measures remaining slack and [rolling escrow](rolling-escrow.md) releases backing; here the consumer's own due service completes the state transition for a later native spend. **Cheapest first test:** Extend T with two fixed F records, one useful but gate-only in its effect on x, and compare passive decay, a separate decay batch and swapping record order. Reject if the first record is only useful on the favorable alias. **Old-task retention check:** Run A/B/C and W after the legal first F record even when the incompatible F write never arrives.

### 14. Transfer to either of two late editors without reserving both — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Let the lawful decay word, chosen before either editor arrives, release a menu covering two independently drawn rival F jobs, each safe as the sole successor on the full alias set. Certify at most one spend unless the pair is separately proved. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Conformant search][conformant] and the parent's packet of native directions already allow robust alternatives; the proposed extra object is a successor menu paid for by surrendering the same occupied capacity rather than holding two future slots. **Cheapest first test:** Freeze both arrival menus and compare a single reserved F slot, two openers and optimal native search with no releasable gate. Include two individually legal F jobs whose composition harms E. **Old-task retention check:** Exercise each winner and the unserved job's timely fallback; test E and the tightest old reader on every race outcome.

### 15. Make the release deadline depend on due service, not a clock proxy — improvement
**Mechanism:** Compute latest E and earliest F from worst legal optimizer prefixes, explicitly including idle ticks and a slow record that preserves gate occupancy at the same age. Refuse to promise a deadline unreachable by a declared word. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Temporal-logic MPC][stl] already checks bounded temporal properties; this is a falsifier for using gate age as a substitute for shared learner state. **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate strong, weak and idle continuations from T. Give a tuned clock scheduler the same observe/schedule budget; score admitted jobs by actual deadline and do not count safe but late F. **Old-task retention check:** Check installed E and all older floors through the worst delay; include W and independent clearing when the deadline is unsupported.

### 16. Hand back capacity that F can actually spend, not slack it cannot use — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Choose among equally useful decay records by the effect on F's held-out native gradient orientation rather than gate magnitude. The live state transition makes the same fixed F useful even when two candidate decay records lower gate occupancy equally. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [GradMatch][gradmatch] selects records to match a training gradient; this seeks opposite outcomes for later untouched F at equal observed gate and phase, with no target gradient from F at choice time. **Cheapest first test:** Two due records give equal g and matched C gain but opposite slot carries; exhaustively check a presealed F menu and compare conventional GradMatch and gate-only handoff. Include a menu in which neither handed-back direction is spendable. **Old-task retention check:** Match E and older endpoint margins before F, then check their ordinary service after F and residual carry on the next old update.

## C. Ask whether the two mechanisms need each other

### 17. Spend a cancellation direction, then recycle its partner — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** The two paths originally cancel an alias for E. Its useful update lowers the old reader's need for the second path, and ordinary decay releases that path's previous edit capacity to F. The first path still carries E and the original old service. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [PackNet][packnet] frees weights through pruning before reuse; here a native trace changes which live cancellation term is necessary without pruning, a per-task mask, or a saved copy. **Cheapest first test:** Build a two-path bilinear host with four interventions: both effects, cancelling only, decay only, neither. At equal updates and byte-ticks compare cancellation plus separately optimized pruning/release and cancellation plus a dedicated F slot. Reject if a passive frozen old reader suffices. **Old-task retention check:** Probe the old path-dominant slices and E after F, including a blind suffix in which the freed term would otherwise be used.

### 18. Let F inherit a new cancellation pair rather than a free unit — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Early E spends the pair {p,q}; due decay drives a third *existing* path r into opposition with q as p returns to its old role. F uses {q,r} and sees no alias tag or saved E record. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [PathNet][pathnet] reuses network paths by routing; the proposed object is a change in which already-live responses cancel, with all routes fixed and still serving. **Cheapest first test:** Three paths with signed coefficients, two sealed alias pairs and separate E/F tasks. Enumerate partner swaps and compare fixed pairs, global projection, hard routing at equal width and one alias whose q/r responses cannot oppose. **Old-task retention check:** Exercise r's former old workload and p's restored old workload during both admissions and after the second spend.

### 19. Release a write span but keep proof of the protected directions in live dynamics — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** The same cancellation learned for E makes retired alias details irrelevant for F only on a residual subspace. Derive F's eligibility from that subspace in the live learner after proof and receipt backing are reused, without carrying the original report. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) already tests backing reuse and [OGD][ogd] protects a stored subspace. The claim is a learned change to reachable interference that makes the old evidence unnecessary for a new certified native spend. **Cheapest first test:** Construct two pre-release histories with distinct receipt/report data and identical advertised residual spans. Compare stored basis, live exact recompute and one legal F whose optimizer delta escapes the span despite an in-span raw gradient. **Old-task retention check:** Actually overwrite the old backing before F, run rare B_d and E on both histories, and test W when recomputation declines admission.

### 20. Spend an edit whose safety depends on the two paths drifting together during expiry — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Decay attacks two signed responses, not just the gate. Pair the rates through a due feature so F becomes feasible before the pair decorrelates enough to harm an old reader; certify the common handoff interval over all aliases. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Horizon-dependent tube MPC][tube] propagates coupled uncertainty; here lawful continual work is proposed to create the overlap, not to exploit an existing overlap. **Cheapest first test:** Sweep exact per-path rates and arrival interleavings in a two-mode host. Compare separate cancellation plus a timer tuned on both rates, symmetric frozen decay, and a twin with identical gate traces but independent path drift. **Old-task retention check:** Read E and the old cancellation-sensitive slice just before and after the first F opportunity, plus after the interval closes.

### 21. Make one ordinary old-service update pay both margins — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Choose a due old record from a service-equivalent pool whose *executed* delta both shrinks the uncancelled alias response and advances the E→F release. Neither effect alone enables F by its deadline. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [GradMatch][gradmatch] selects training records and [PCGrad][pcgrad] mitigates local conflict; the claim is a naturally available shared update that substitutes for two individually necessary interventions at the same ordinary old-task service. **Cheapest first test:** Freeze a small due pool and enumerate all singletons and all equal-budget stacked candidates, with each effect masked separately and with the joint record withheld. Charge the search; if the best stack picks the same record at the same cost, report a tie. **Old-task retention check:** Match ordinary old service of selected and alternative records, then probe both rare B_d and E after F rather than crediting the selected record's training loss.

### 22. Bound the minimum number of truthful records needed for a handoff — improvement
**Mechanism:** Exhaustively find lawful instances where separate cancellation and release each cost a record, the joint record exists or does not, and deadline slack turns that difference into a delivered job. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Conformant planning][conformant] already measures reachability and minimum plan length; this is the counterexample audit for the proposed interaction, not a new planning operation. **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two aliases, two phases and length-two native batch words under the same ledger. Run an oracle that knows aliases only as a price bound; add a same-endpoint but different-continuation witness to catch an unsound certificate. **Old-task retention check:** A path counts only if rare/common B_d, other A/B/C, E and W pass from acquisition through real backing reuse; score refused or expired jobs, not just successes.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can one due record make both independent edit axes safe by changing the two live path responses?** Start with 01–04 and 06. First require X's endpoints to tie and the same-sign and joint-corner controls to fail; then replace the stipulated change with a truthful native step. The claim dies if a non-cancelling opener, freeze plus OGD, or native warm-up offers the same held-out E menu at equal cost and old retention.
2. **Does expiry return capacity to a real F that a tuned fixed gate or a second opener cannot serve as cheaply?** Start with 09, 10, 12, 15 and 16. Seal the slow and idle words and both deadlines in advance. Compare the best feasible fixed schedule and strongest state-aware stacked control using the same clock, due menu and native capacity. If either ties, report feasibility only; if E regresses after F, the handoff fails.
3. **Is there an interaction worth more than doing the two jobs separately?** Start with 17, 18, 20–22. For the same seal, use both masking interventions, a no-shared-term twin and exhaustive native planning at equal total budget. Look for a strict reduction in lawful records or backing byte-ticks at identical old floors and deadlines, not a win over two deliberately weak baselines.
4. **What evidence can disappear at the cut without losing either the guarantee or an old skill?** Start with 04, 07, 08, 14 and 19. Search for same-read histories that disagree on the next effective update after R_d reuse. If they exist, name the missing live information and charge it; if they do not on the sealed host, test widening the arrival, rounding and alias envelopes before promoting the contract.
