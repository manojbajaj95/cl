# Learner–world co-evolution (depth 2): rollback un-suppression shocks & learner-induced guard removal

Child node (depth 2) of [PR #364](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/364) (`L2W21` + `L2W22`) · 26 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Parent triage.** In `reviews/flood-triage.md` (row 502), PR #364 (`tree/learner-world-coevolution-2.md`) kept two score-3 interaction primitives:
1. **`L2W21` — check for an un-suppression shock before rolling back:** query the suppression record before cutover and test the restored checkpoint $\theta_{t-1}$ on the failure modes the current model $\theta_t$ was preventing, because the live stream the rollback certificate checks no longer contains them.
2. **`L2W22` — detect guard removal caused by the learner's own prevention:** when the learner reliably prevents a failure, users and downstream tools drop their own guards (risk compensation / automation complacency), so the world now depends on that prevention; record the removed guards in the suppression record and pin the preventing behavior against rollback and forgetting until the guards return.

**Thesis.** `L2W21` and `L2W22` expose a coupled failure mode unique to continual learners embedded in adaptive worlds: *a learner's own reliability strips away external safety nets, while simultaneously erasing the failure signal from live traffic—making a routine rollback unleash an unguarded failure wave.* In Section A (`L3W01`–`L3W09`) we deepen `L2W21`—differential suppression witnesses, latent blast-radius estimation, multi-failure cascades, delayed-latency ticking bombs, prevention-grafted partial rollback, and risky-fallback checks. In Section B (`L3W10`–`L3W18`) we deepen `L2W22`—benign-decoy guard heartbeats, causal attribution of guard decay, readiness-proportional weight pinning, guard-re-arming handshakes, anti-complacency friction, and transitive guard-chain collapse. In Section C (`L3W19`–`L3W26`) we isolate genuine `L2W21 × L2W22` primitives—guard-weighted blast-radius gating, temporary runtime shield synthesis to unlock pinned rollbacks, co-evolutionary ossification prevention, and adversarial rollback-exploitation defense—each required to beat `L2W21` alone, `L2W22` alone, and their independent union.

## A · Un-suppression shock certification before rollback (`L2W21` deepened)

### L3W01 · Differential-suppression witness (predicting $\text{Supp}(\theta_t) \setminus \text{Supp}(\theta_{t-1})$)
- **Mechanism:** Testing a rollback candidate $\theta_{t-1}$ against *every* historically suppressed failure mode wastes probe budget on modes $\theta_{t-1}$ already prevented. Compute the exact *differential suppression set* $\Delta\text{Supp}(\theta_t, \theta_{t-1}) = \{F : \theta_t \text{ prevents } F \text{ and } \theta_{t-1} \text{ did not}\}$ from stored minimal trigger prefixes, and concentrate pre-rollback certification exclusively on $\Delta\text{Supp}$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L2W21` queries the full suppression ledger; [Safe to Resume?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29381) checks execution continuity after rollback. Restricting pre-rollback shock testing to the *differential suppression set* between $\theta_t$ and $\theta_{t-1}$ isolates only the failures that cutover can actually unleash.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stateful world with 12 suppressed failure modes: 10 already prevented by $\theta_{t-1}$, 2 newly prevented only by $\theta_t$ (which also regresses on $C$), under a tight pre-rollback probe cap of 4 checks. Compare full-ledger uniform `L2W21`, `LW02 + LW03` independent, plain rollback, and differential-suppression probing. Kill if random 4-probe sampling of the ledger or full-ledger hashing ties on shock detection at equal probe budget.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $C$ restoration, zero un-suppression shock on the 2 newly prevented modes, and untouched $A/B$ retention after rollback.

### L3W02 · Post-un-suppression arrival-rate estimator (blast-radius forecasting)
- **Mechanism:** Knowing $\theta_{t-1}$ cannot prevent an unleashed mode $F \in \Delta\text{Supp}$ is insufficient without knowing *how often* $F$ will erupt once $\theta_t$ is removed. Run read-only shadow evaluations of $\theta_{t-1}$ on recent live $W_t$ prefixes to estimate the *latent un-suppressed hazard rate* $\lambda_F^{\text{latent}}(W_t)$, weighting rollback veto and repair decisions by expected immediate blast radius rather than binary vulnerability.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Performative Prediction with Selective Labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08272) estimates censored rates for current actions; `L2W21` checks binary failure capability. Estimating *live latent arrival rate under the counterfactual rolled-back policy* turns binary shock detection into quantitative blast-radius triage.
- **Cheapest first test:** World with two modes in $\Delta\text{Supp}$: $F_{\text{rare}}$ (latent rate $0.1\%$) and $F_{\text{flood}}$ (latent rate $25\%$), while $\theta_t$ suffers a $15\%$ regression on $C$. Compare binary-veto `L2W21` (blocks both), plain rollback (allows both), forward fine-tuning, and hazard-rate-weighted rollback triage at equal probes. Kill if binary `L2W21` or always-forward-fine-tune achieves equal cumulative utility across mixed rare/flood regimes.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $C$ recovery, $F_{\text{flood}}$ containment, and untouched $A/B$ accuracy across both rare- and flood-shock regimes.

### L3W03 · Multi-failure un-suppression cascade check (joint shock interference)
- **Mechanism:** Rolling back $\theta_t \to \theta_{t-1}$ can unleash two or more previously suppressed failure modes $F_1, F_2 \in \Delta\text{Supp}$ *simultaneously*. Even when $\theta_{t-1}$ recovers cleanly from $F_1$ alone and $F_2$ alone, their concurrent arrival in $W_t$ can exhaust shared recovery bandwidth or trigger conflicting recovery actions (un-suppression write-skew). Require pre-rollback certification to test *pairwise concurrent* unleashed shocks from $\Delta\text{Supp}$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L2W21` tests each suppressed failure mode independently; [SafeLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26313) checks single-fault rollback risk. Testing *concurrent multi-mode un-suppression cascades* captures nonlinear interaction failures invisible to single-mode checks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Resource-bounded agent world where $\theta_{t-1}$ handles $F_1$ alone (100%) and $F_2$ alone (100%), but concurrent $F_1 + F_2$ (both suppressed by $\theta_t$) deadlocks the recovery queue. Compare single-mode `L2W21`, `LW02 + LW03` independent, shadow twin at equal bytes, and pairwise-cascade certification. Kill if single-mode `L2W21` with a conservative capacity margin detects the deadlock equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $C$ recovery and zero deadlock under concurrent $F_1 + F_2$ exposure alongside normal $A/B$ tasks.

### L3W04 · Prevention-grafted partial rollback (keeping $\theta_t$'s prevention while reverting $C$)
- **Mechanism:** When `L2W21` blocks rollback because $\theta_{t-1}$ would unleash failure $F$, do not fall back solely to slow forward retraining. Construct a *prevention-grafted rollback* $\theta_{\text{graft}} = \theta_{t-1} \oplus \text{Prev}_{\theta_t}(F)$—restoring $\theta_{t-1}$'s behavior on the regressed task $C$ while grafting *only* $\theta_t$'s verified prevention boundary on $F$'s trigger manifold (via localized routing, subspace merge, or targeted distillation).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L2W25` distills $\theta_{t-1}$'s $C$ forward into $\theta_t$; here we invert the direction when $\theta_t$ has broad regressions and only a *narrow* prevention gain—grafting $\theta_t$'s prevention onto the clean $\theta_{t-1}$ base.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small MLP/adapter agent where $\theta_t$ gained a clean 1-slice prevention on $F$ but regressed across 4 old slices $C_1\dots C_4$. Compare plain rollback, forward fine-tuning of $\theta_t$ on $C_1\dots C_4$, `L2W25` forward distillation, and prevention-grafted rollback onto $\theta_{t-1}$ at equal gradient steps. Kill if forward fine-tuning of $\theta_t$ with $F$-replay ties on $C_1\dots C_4$ recovery speed and $F$ prevention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure restoration of all 4 slices $C_1\dots C_4$, preservation of $F$ prevention, and zero drift on untouched $A/B$.

### L3W05 · Shielded micro-canary for drifted-world un-suppression probing
- **Mechanism:** If world $W_t$ has mutated enough that archived $W_0$ triggers no longer predict whether $\theta_{t-1}$ will unleash $F$ in live $W_t$, run a *shielded micro-canary*: route a tiny fraction (e.g., 1%) of live $W_t$ traffic through $\theta_{t-1}$ wrapped inside a hard runtime veto shield (`VEPA`/`SafeCoEvo`-style) that intercepts any unleashed failure action before execution and logs the exact live trigger rate.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [VEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12813) and [SafeCoEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36580) shield live forward policies; `L2W15` runs read-only shadow dry runs that cannot observe multi-step state reactions. Combining a *hard runtime veto shield* with a *live micro-canary specifically for rollback un-suppression testing* measures real $W_t$ triggers at zero execution harm.
- **Cheapest first test:** Drifted multi-step tool world where offline $W_0$ triggers miss a new $W_t$ failure pathway unleashed by $\theta_{t-1}$. Compare offline `L2W21`, unshielded 1% canary rollback, read-only shadow dry run (`L2W15`), and shielded micro-canary probing at equal live episodes. Kill if read-only shadow dry run (`L2W15`) detects the drifted $W_t$ un-suppression pathway equally accurately.
- **Old-task retention:** Confirm zero live failure execution during canary screening and full retention of $A/B$ and $C$ after cutover decision.

### L3W06 · Delayed-latency un-suppression traps (ticking-bomb rollbacks)
- **Mechanism:** Some failures prevented by $\theta_t$ do not erupt on step 1 after rolling back to $\theta_{t-1}$—instead, $\theta_{t-1}$ silently accumulates latent state corruption (e.g., stale cache entries, unclosed handles, drifting queues) that explodes $H$ steps later. Record each suppressed mode's *incubation horizon* $H_F$ in the suppression ledger and require pre-rollback certification to roll out $\theta_{t-1}$ for $H_F$ steps (in sandbox or world model) before approving cutover.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Controller-Agnostic Runtime Safety Certificates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16865) certify multi-step reachability for forward controllers; `L2W21` checks immediate failure on trigger probes. Extending pre-rollback un-suppression checks to *multi-step incubation horizons* catches delayed ticking-bomb rollbacks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stateful queue/cache MDP where $\theta_{t-1}$ looks 100% healthy for 15 steps after rollback before an un-suppressed leak overflows the buffer at step 20. Compare 1-step `L2W21`, `CWX3` alone, shadow twin at equal bytes, and $H_F$-horizon rollback certification. Kill if 1-step `L2W21` on a state-delta proxy catches the delayed overflow equally reliably.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $C$ recovery, zero step-20 overflow after rollback, and untouched $A/B$ performance.

### L3W07 · Ramped un-suppression schedule (gradual reversion below recovery throughput)
- **Mechanism:** An instant step rollback $\theta_t \to \theta_{t-1}$ unleashes a synchronized spike of failure $F$ that exceeds $\theta_{t-1}$'s recovery throughput, whereas a *ramped traffic/policy interpolation* $\alpha(t): 1 \to 0$ caps the instantaneous un-suppressed arrival rate below $\theta_{t-1}$'s recovery capacity while $\theta_{t-1}$ re-acquires prevention online. Compute the maximum safe ramp rate $\dot{\alpha}_{\max}$ directly from `L3W02`'s latent hazard rate.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Local and Global Stability in Performative RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06467) analyzes gradual policy updates forward; gradual *reversion* paced specifically to *un-suppression recovery throughput* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Queue-constrained service simulator where instant rollback to $\theta_{t-1}$ causes catastrophic overload from unleashed $F$, while a 5-step ramp keeps $F$ within recovery capacity as $\theta_{t-1}$ adapts. Compare instant rollback, binary-veto `L2W21` + forward fine-tuning, and hazard-paced ramped rollback at equal horizon. Kill if a fixed 50/50 canary step ties across varying latent hazard rates.
- **Old-task retention:** Score cumulative $C$ utility, zero recovery-queue overload on $F$, and old $A/B$ retention throughout the ramp.

### L3W08 · Un-suppression-compatible checkpoint retention (smart rollback memory)
- **Mechanism:** Under finite storage, keeping only the *most recent* $K$ checkpoints can leave every stored checkpoint incompatible with rollback because all of them predate a critical prevention skill or share a broken world assumption. Replace FIFO checkpoint retention with an *un-suppression-compatibility selector* that keeps the minimal diverse set of checkpoints minimizing worst-case $|\Delta\text{Supp}|$ against current $\theta_t$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard checkpointing uses FIFO/LRU; `AgentRewind` ([2608.14380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14380)) checkpoints within a single episode. Managing *long-horizon continual checkpoint memory* by *un-suppression compatibility* is the new operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** 20-update continual stream with a 3-checkpoint storage cap, where update 8 introduced critical $F$-prevention and update 18 introduced a $C$-regression. Compare FIFO-3 checkpoints, best-val-3 checkpoints, and un-suppression-compatible checkpoint selection. Kill if FIFO-3 + `L2W21` forward-repair fallback matches recovery cost and $C$ restoration.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify clean recovery of $C$ and retention of $F$-prevention and $A/B$ after late-horizon regressions.

### L3W09 · Risky-fallback contagion check on unleashed failures
- **Mechanism:** When an unleashed failure $F$ hits a rolled-back agent $\theta_{t-1}$, $\theta_{t-1}$'s *fallback routine* (e.g., aggressive retries, destructive cache clears, or escalating to a deprecated API) can cause worse collateral damage in mutated world $W_t$ than $F$ itself. Extend `L2W21`'s pre-rollback probe to verify not only whether $\theta_{t-1}$ detects $F$, but that $\theta_{t-1}$'s *triggered fallback action* remains inside $W_t$'s safe envelope.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Recovery Gone Too Far: Risky Fallback in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19886) documents dangerous agent fallbacks on ordinary tool errors. Auditing *restored-checkpoint fallback actions* specifically under *rollback-unleashed failures in mutated $W_t$* is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Tool-agent simulator where $\theta_{t-1}$'s old fallback for $F$ calls a now-destructive/rate-limited endpoint in $W_t$. Compare standard `L2W21` (checks only whether $\theta_{t-1}$ flags $F$), `AgentRewind`, and fallback-envelope-certified rollback. Kill if a static tool-permission denylist on $W_t$ prevents all collateral damage without rollback-specific fallback probing.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero collateral damage to $W_t$ state upon $F$ recurrence while retaining $A/B$ and $C$.

## B · Detecting & governing learner-induced guard removal (`L2W22` deepened)

### L3W10 · Benign-decoy guard heartbeat (detecting dropped guards without real harm)
- **Mechanism:** While $\theta_t$ prevents 100% of real failures $F$, external human reviewers and downstream validators receive zero triggers—making it impossible to observe whether their guards are still active. Inject ultra-low-rate *benign synthetic decoys* (tagged, zero-impact canary anomalies designed to trip the external guard's check without causing real harm) to directly measure live external guard readiness $g_t(F) \in [0, 1]$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Maintaining Human Verification Capacity under Automation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38459) and [Looks Fine to Me](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27801) study human oversight decay retrospectively. Using *live benign decoy heartbeats* as a continual-learning sensor feeding `L2W22`'s guard-removal ledger is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Coupled simulator where an external guard's catch probability decays from $1.0 \to 0.0$ after 60 failure-free steps under $\theta_t$ (in half the runs, the guard stays at $1.0$). Compare `L2W21` alone, `LW02` alone, heuristic streak-length guessing, and benign-decoy heartbeat sensing at equal overhead ($\le 1\%$ decoy rate). Kill if a simple streak-length threshold predicts guard removal equally accurately across both guard-decaying and guard-stable worlds.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero real-world harm from decoys, intact $A/B$ utility, and retained $F$-prevention after unrelated updates $C, D$ when $g_t \to 0$.

### L3W11 · Causal attribution of guard decay (competence-induced vs. exogenous removal)
- **Mechanism:** An external guard can disappear for two opposite reasons: *competence-induced complacency* (users/tools dropped it because $\theta_t$ never failed) or *exogenous deprecation* (the underlying risk or workflow was retired independently). Compare guard-heartbeat trajectories across staggered user/environment cohorts (or small exposure variations) to causally separate *learner-caused guard erosion* from exogenous removal before pinning weights.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Partially Performative Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07890) studies partial population exposure; [Modeling AI Overreliance as a Complex Adaptive System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19616) models overreliance dynamics. Using *cohort-staggered causal attribution* to decide whether a missing guard warrants weight pinning prevents permanently freezing weights for exogenously retired risks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two-cohort simulator with 6 guards: 3 drop strictly after $\theta_t$ achieves 95% prevention (competence-induced), 3 are removed exogenously when a legacy endpoint is deleted. Compare pin-all-missing-guards `L2W22`, performative-RL retraining, and cohort-attributed pinning at equal probes. Kill if pinning every missing guard (without attribution) incurs negligible plasticity loss on later tasks $C, D$.
- **Old-task retention:** Score retention of prevention on the 3 competence-induced slices *plus* plasticity on new tasks $C, D$ and untouched $A/B$.

### L3W12 · Guard-dependency pinning operator (weight stiffness proportional to $1 - g_t$)
- **Mechanism:** Map the measured external guard readiness $g_t(F) \in [0, 1]$ directly into a continuous *continual-learning pinning stiffness* $\lambda_{\text{pin}}(F) \propto (1 - g_t(F))$ on $\theta_t$'s prevention subspace (e.g., weighted EWC/OGD projection or adapter freeze). When external guards are strong ($g_t \approx 1$), the learner keeps full plasticity; as external guards erode ($g_t \to 0$), prevention automatically hardens against forgetting from unrelated future updates.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard EWC/OGD sets static task importance from Fisher information alone, ignoring *external world redundancy*. Coupling *parameter protection stiffness dynamically to live external guard readiness* $1 - g_t$ is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Capacity-limited continual learner updating on unrelated tasks $C, D, E$ while external guard readiness $g_t(F)$ varies across $\{1.0, 0.5, 0.0\}$. Compare uniform EWC, static `LW02` rehearsal, binary `L2W22` freeze, and $1 - g_t$ adaptive pinning at equal compute. Kill if static Fisher-weighted EWC matches both $F$-prevention retention (when $g_t = 0$) and $C/D/E$ plasticity (when $g_t = 1$).
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $F$-prevention retention after $C, D, E$ updates under $g_t = 0$, plus normal $A/B$ retention and $C/D/E$ accuracy under $g_t = 1$.

### L3W13 · Guard-re-arming handshake (protocol for safe un-pinning)
- **Mechanism:** A world-pinned prevention slice cannot stay frozen indefinitely—eventually the learner must refactor, compress, or roll back. Define a *guard-re-arming handshake*: before releasing a pinned prevention slice, emit an explicit *de-pinning warning / visibility cue* to downstream users or tools and hold the pin until `L3W10`'s guard heartbeat confirms $g_t(F) \ge g_{\min}$, guaranteeing external safety nets are back online before the learner relinquishes responsibility.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [AI Agents Push Humans Out of the Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23642) highlights the difficulty of bringing humans back in; [SafeCoEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36580) co-evolves internal guards. Making *verified external guard re-arming* a formal precondition for un-pinning continual weights is the new operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Simulator where a pinned prevention slice must be refactored to make room for new task $D$, and external guards take a stochastic $k \in [5, 30]$ steps to re-arm after notification. Compare immediate un-pinning, fixed-delay un-pinning, and heartbeat-confirmed re-arming handshakes. Kill if a fixed 15-step delay ties on zero-failure safety and $D$ acquisition speed.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero $F$-failure spike during the transition, successful $D$ acquisition, and retained $A/B$ accuracy.

### L3W14 · Anti-complacency friction injection (preventing guard atrophy proactively)
- **Mechanism:** Rather than waiting for external guards to atrophy to $g_t = 0$ and locking the learner into permanent weight-pinning, maintain a *guard-exercise floor*: inject minimal, bounded *legible friction* (e.g., surfacing uncertain sub-decisions or periodic calibrated review prompts) whenever $g_t$ dips below a target band—keeping external verification alive so the learner preserves its own future plasticity and rollback freedom.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Maintaining Human Verification Capacity under Automation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38459) and [Unaccountable Delegation, Fading Skills](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08601) analyze skill atrophy conceptually. Turning *guard-exercise maintenance* into an active closed-loop controller inside a continual learner to protect *the learner's own future plasticity* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Long-horizon human–agent simulator where zero friction causes human verification to decay to $0$ by step 100 (pinning $\theta_t$ and blocking later adaptation), whereas $3\%$ calibrated friction keeps $g_t \ge 0.7$. Compare zero-friction `L2W22` pinning, constant random friction, and $g_t$-feedback friction injection at equal human-effort budget. Kill if constant random $3\%$ friction ties on steady-state cost and retained plasticity.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $A/B$ steady-state utility, $F$ safety, and ability to learn late tasks $C, D$ without hitting a pinning wall.

### L3W15 · Re-arming-latency taxonomy (software guards vs. atrophied human oversight)
- **Mechanism:** Not all dropped guards are equally hard to restore: a disabled software linter or API schema check can be re-enabled atomically ($\tau_{\text{rearm}} \approx 1$ step), whereas an atrophied human oversight habit or forgotten team runbook takes many cycles to rebuild ($\tau_{\text{rearm}} \gg 1$). Tag every guard in `L2W22`'s ledger with its estimated *re-arming latency* $\tau_{\text{rearm}}$, enforcing strict weight pinning on high-$\tau_{\text{rearm}}$ human guards while allowing faster re-arm-and-refactor on low-$\tau_{\text{rearm}}$ software guards.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Knowing Is Not Enough](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01976) and [Impact of Expertise and Trust](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14660) study human oversight barriers; `L2W22` treats all removed guards uniformly. Separating *instant software re-arming* from *slow human re-skilling* inside the pinning ledger prevents both over-pinning software and under-protecting human oversight.
- **Cheapest first test:** World with 2 dropped software guards ($\tau_{\text{rearm}} = 1$) and 2 atrophied human guards ($\tau_{\text{rearm}} = 40$). Compare uniform `L2W22` pinning, never-pin, and latency-indexed pinning + re-arming. Kill if uniform `L2W22` pinning ties on plasticity for new tasks $C, D$ while keeping human-guarded slices safe.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure zero failure spikes on human-guarded slices plus $C, D$ plasticity and untouched $A/B$ retention.

### L3W16 · Transitive guard-chain collapse (second-order Peltzman effects)
- **Mechanism:** When learner $L$ reliably prevents failure $F$, direct downstream consumer $T_1$ drops its validator, which in turn causes *second-order* consumer $T_2$ (which relied on $T_1$) to drop *its* safeguard too—creating an unguarded multi-hop chain invisible at $L$'s immediate boundary. Propagate *guard-dependency receipts* through downstream hops so `L2W22`'s ledger reflects *end-to-end* unguarded blast radius rather than only 1-hop checks.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Modeling AI Overreliance as a Complex Adaptive System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19616) notes network-level overreliance; `L2W22` monitors only direct users/tools. Tracking *transitive multi-hop guard removal* inside the continual learner's pinning ledger is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 3-node pipeline ($L \to T_1 \to T_2$) where $T_1$ keeps a weak check ($g_1 = 0.5$) while $T_2$ completely drops its guard ($g_2 = 0$) because $L$ prevented $F$ for 100 steps. Compare 1-hop `L2W22`, `L2W21` alone, and transitive guard-chain pinning. Kill if 1-hop `L2W22` (monitoring only $T_1$) prevents $T_2$ failures equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Score end-to-end $T_2$ safety after an unrelated update $C$ on $L$, plus untouched $A/B$ accuracy.

### L3W17 · Guard-preservation regularizer during policy selection
- **Mechanism:** Two candidate continual updates $\theta_t^{(1)}, \theta_t^{(2)}$ can achieve identical accuracy on new task $C$, yet $\theta_t^{(1)}$ produces overly confident, opaque outputs that accelerate human guard decay ($g_t \downarrow$), whereas $\theta_t^{(2)}$ preserves inspectable reasoning/uncertainty cues that keep $g_t$ high. Add a *guard-preservation term* to update admission so the learner prefers updates that do not silently erode external oversight.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Looks Fine to Me](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27801) shows plausible-looking agent outputs undermine human oversight; [Performative Policy Gradient](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20576) optimizes task reward under shift. Penalizing *update-induced oversight erosion* directly at continual update selection time is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Simulated reviewer whose scrutiny decays faster on opaque/high-confidence outputs than on structured/calibrated outputs; offer two accuracy-matched updates on $C$. Compare accuracy-only selection, KL-to-base regularization, and guard-preservation-regularized selection. Kill if standard calibration (temperature scaling) alone preserves $g_t$ equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $C$ accuracy, sustained $g_t$, and untouched $A/B$ retention across 10 sequential updates.

### L3W18 · Portable guard-assumption contract across model upgrades
- **Mechanism:** When a world-pinned learner $\theta_t$ is retired and replaced by a new base model or architecture $\theta_{\text{new}}$, the *world* still has dropped guards ($g_t \approx 0$). Export all world-pinned prevention obligations from `L2W22`'s ledger as a mandatory *pre-deployment guard-inheritance contract* that $\theta_{\text{new}}$ must pass before cutover—ensuring a model upgrade never silently removes a world-pinned safety crutch.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `root-learning-from-other-models` `LM30`/`LM32` triage old skills across model upgrades without knowing *which skills the world has dropped its own guards for*. Prioritizing *world-pinned* obligations in upgrade contracts bridges co-evolution to model replacement.
- **Cheapest first test:** Upgrade from adapted $\theta_t$ to a stronger fresh base $\theta_{\text{new}}$ that solves $C$ better but misses 1 of 4 old prevention slices—specifically the one where $g_t = 0$. Compare standard upgrade triage (`LM30`), full distillation, and guard-pinned upgrade contracts at equal probe budget. Kill if standard `LM30` triage (treating all 4 prevention slices with equal weight) catches the unguarded miss equally reliably.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero post-upgrade failure spike on all $g_t = 0$ world-pinned slices while unlocking $\theta_{\text{new}}$'s gains on $C$.

## C · Joint `L2W21 × L2W22` primitives: un-suppression shocks in a guard-stripped world

### L3W19 · Guard-weighted un-suppression blast-radius score
- **Mechanism:** Combine `L3W02`'s latent un-suppression hazard rate $\lambda_F^{\text{latent}}(W_t)$ with `L3W10`'s external guard readiness $g_t(F)$ into a single *unguarded shock risk* $R_{\text{shock}}(F) = \lambda_F^{\text{latent}}(W_t) \cdot (1 - g_t(F))$. Permit fast plain rollback when an unleashed failure lands on an *intact* external guard ($g_t \approx 1$), and block/graft *only* when rollback would unleash $F$ into a *stripped* guard ($g_t \approx 0$).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L2W21` alone blocks safe rollbacks that an intact external guard would harmlessly catch; `L2W22` alone pins prevention even when $\theta_{t-1}$ already prevents $F$ ($\lambda_F^{\text{latent}} = 0$). Multiplying $\lambda_F^{\text{latent}} \cdot (1 - g_t)$ eliminates both false vetoes and false-safe rollbacks.
- **Cheapest first test:** 2×2 world crossing $\{\text{rollback unleashes } F, \text{does not}\}$ × $\{\text{guard intact } g_t=1, \text{guard stripped } g_t=0\}$ under a $C$-regression. Compare `L2W21` alone, `L2W22` alone, independent `L2W21 + L2W22` (veto if either fires), and multiplicative $R_{\text{shock}}$ gating. Kill if independent `L2W21 + L2W22` achieves the same $C$-recovery speed and zero unguarded shocks across all 4 cells.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $C$ restoration latency across all 4 cells, zero unguarded $F$ failures, and untouched $A/B$ retention.

### L3W20 · Temporary runtime prevention shield to unlock a pinned rollback
- **Mechanism:** When $\theta_t$ suffers a severe regression on $C$ requiring immediate rollback to $\theta_{t-1}$, *and* rollback is blocked because $\theta_{t-1}$ would unleash $F$ into a guard-stripped world ($g_t = 0$), compile a *temporary runtime wrapper shield* from $\theta_t$'s prevention predicate on $F$ and wrap it around $\theta_{t-1}$—serving as an artificial external guard ($g_{\text{synth}} \to 1$) that unlocks immediate safe rollback until real guards re-arm or $\theta_{t-1}$ is patched.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [VEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12813) and [Probabilistic Option Shielding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09197) shield forward learning; `L2W22` alone simply *forbids* rollback when pinned. Synthesizing a *disposable rollback-unlocking shield from the bad checkpoint $\theta_t$'s own prevention slice* turns a deadlocked rollback into an immediate safe recovery.
- **Cheapest first test:** World where $\theta_t$ has a severe $C$-regression and $g_t(F) = 0$ (blocking plain rollback and `L2W22`), while forward fine-tuning of $\theta_t$ on $C$ takes 50 steps. Compare `L2W21` alone, `L2W22` alone + forward fine-tuning, `L3W04` weight grafting, and temporary runtime shielding around $\theta_{t-1}$ at equal latency. Kill if forward fine-tuning of $\theta_t$ or `L3W04` weight grafting recovers $C$ equally fast with zero $F$ leaks.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify immediate $C$ restoration, zero $F$ leaks while $g_t = 0$, and clean shield removal once real guards or weights recover.

### L3W21 · Two-stage un-pin-then-rollback schedule (re-arming before reversion)
- **Mechanism:** For non-emergency regressions where rollback to $\theta_{t-1}$ is blocked by a guard-stripped world ($g_t = 0$), execute a *two-stage coordinated rollback*: Stage 1 keeps $\theta_t$'s prevention live while triggering the guard-re-arming handshake (`L3W13`) and running `L2W21` recovery warm-up on $\theta_{t-1}$; Stage 2 cuts over to $\theta_{t-1}$ *only* once $g_t \ge g_{\min}$, completely eliminating the un-suppression shock window without needing a runtime shield.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Neither `L2W21` alone nor `L2W22` alone schedules *external guard restoration as Stage 1 of a policy rollback*. Comparing staged re-arm-then-revert against runtime shielding (`L3W20`) tests whether world-coordination beats software-wrapping.
- **Cheapest first test:** Simulator where external guards re-arm in 10 steps upon alert, and a moderate $C$-regression favors rolling back to $\theta_{t-1}$. Compare immediate rollback, `L2W22` permanent veto + forward repair, `L3W20` runtime shield, and two-stage re-arm-then-rollback. Kill if `L2W22` + forward repair matches cumulative reward over the 50-step window.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $C$ restoration, zero $F$-shock incidents during Stage 1 and Stage 2, and untouched $A/B$ retention.

### L3W22 · Guard-erosion early-warning trigger for rollback-checkpoint hardening
- **Mechanism:** Instead of waiting until a regression forces an emergency rollback into a guard-stripped world, monitor `L3W10`'s guard readiness velocity $dg_t/dt$. The moment an external guard starts decaying ($g_t$ crosses a warning threshold), proactively distill $\theta_t$'s prevention of $F$ into the *standby rollback checkpoint* $\theta_{t-1}$ offline—so if rollback is later triggered, $\theta_{t-1}$ is already immune to the un-suppression shock.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L2W21` discovers $\theta_{t-1}$'s vulnerability only *at rollback time* (too late for instant cutover); `L2W22` pins $\theta_t$ instead of hardening $\theta_{t-1}$. Using *live guard decay as an offline trigger to pre-harden the standby rollback checkpoint* keeps instant rollback permanently open.
- **Cheapest first test:** 500-step stream where guard $g_t$ decays over steps 50–150 and a sudden $C$-regression hits at step 200 requiring 0-latency rollback. Compare `L2W21` alone, `L2W22` alone, independent `L2W21 + L2W22`, and guard-decay-triggered standby checkpoint hardening at equal offline compute. Kill if periodic uniform checkpoint refreshing (ignoring $g_t$) achieves the same 0-latency safe rollback at equal compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that offline prevention-hardening of $\theta_{t-1}$ preserves 100% of $\theta_{t-1}$'s $C$ and $A/B$ behavior while eliminating the step-200 un-suppression shock.

### L3W23 · Counterfactual guard-presence audit after rollback (no false credit from guard luck)
- **Mechanism:** When a rollback $\theta_t \to \theta_{t-1}$ finishes with zero observed incidents, distinguish three hidden outcomes: (1) $\theta_{t-1}$ genuinely prevented $F$, (2) an intact external guard silently caught $F$ (masking $\theta_{t-1}$'s weakness), or (3) $F$ simply has not arrived yet. Record the *3-way attribution tuple* `(learner_prevented, guard_caught, unexposed)` using `L3W10`'s guard telemetry so future updates do not falsely certify $\theta_{t-1}$ as safe when the guard later drops.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Performative Prediction with Selective Labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08272) studies hidden outcomes of skipped actions; post-rollback monitoring usually treats "zero incidents" as proof of safe restoration. Disentangling *learner prevention* from *silent guard luck* closes a subtle multi-cycle blind spot.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two-cycle simulator: in Cycle 1, $\theta_{t-1}$ is restored while guard $g_1 = 1$ silently catches $F$; in Cycle 2, $g_2$ drops to $0$ and the learner re-uses $\theta_{t-1}$'s "passed" certificate. Compare naive zero-incident certification, `L2W21` alone, and 3-way counterfactual guard-presence auditing. Kill if re-running `L2W21` fresh on every rollback (ignoring historical certificates) ties at equal probe cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure zero Cycle-2 unguarded failures alongside $C$ and $A/B$ retention.

### L3W24 · Plasticity-vs-pinning equilibrium controller (preventing co-evolutionary ossification)
- **Mechanism:** If every successful prevention strips an external guard (`L2W22`), and every stripped guard permanently pins a weight subspace (`L3W12`), a long-lived continual learner eventually hits *co-evolutionary ossification*: so much of its capacity is pinned as external safety crutches that it can neither learn new tasks nor roll back. Enforce a *global pinning budget* $B_{\text{pin}}$—whenever total pinned capacity approaches $B_{\text{pin}}$, proactively schedule anti-complacency re-arming drills (`L3W13`/`L3W14`) on the highest-plasticity-cost slices to reclaim free capacity.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Classical plasticity loss is an internal optimization pathology; *co-evolutionary ossification* is a *world-induced plasticity lock* caused by accumulated external guard dependence. Managing a *global pinning budget coupled to external guard re-arming* is an ambitious-but-doable primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 20-task continual stream on a finite-capacity network where each task's prevention causes its external guard to drop within 50 steps. Compare unconstrained `L2W22` pinning (which freezes the network by task 10), no pinning (causes catastrophic shocks), and global-pinning-budget + targeted guard re-arming. Kill if standard parameter expansion / modular adapter allocation at equal total parameter cap avoids ossification without ever re-arming guards.
- **Old-task retention:** Score late-task plasticity (tasks 15–20), zero unguarded failure spikes across tasks 1–20, and untouched $A/B$ retention.

### L3W25 · Adversarial rollback-exploitation defense
- **Mechanism:** In a strategic world, an adversary who observes that downstream guards dropped ($g_t = 0$) can deliberately inject errors on task $C$ to trigger an automated rollback $\theta_t \to \theta_{t-1}$, then immediately exploit the unleashed, unguarded failure $F$. Couple the rollback trigger to a *joint $C$-regression + unguarded-$F$-exposure* security gate: whenever rolling back would open a high-$R_{\text{shock}}$ (`L3W19`) unguarded surface, refuse plain rollback and route automatically to shielded rollback (`L3W20`) or forward repair (`L2W25`).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard rollback triggers assume regressions are benign/accidental; `L2W21` and `L2W22` assume non-adversarial shifts. Treating *induced rollback into a guard-stripped world* as an adversarial attack vector closes a co-evolutionary security hole.
- **Cheapest first test:** Strategic attacker simulator that probes for $g_t(F) = 0$, then floods $C$ with adversarial regressions to force rollback and immediately fires $F$. Compare standard automated rollback, `L2W21` alone, `L2W22` alone, and adversarial-rollback-gated shielded recovery. Kill if `L2W22` alone (which unconditionally pins $\theta_t$) defends equally well without suffering denial-of-service on $C$.
- **Old-task retention:** Score resistance to $F$-exploitation *and* recovery of benign $C$ traffic alongside untouched $A/B$.

### L3W26 · Minimal joint state for un-suppression + guard-pinning (`(F, ΔSupp, λ_latent, g_t, τ_rearm, pin_mask)`)
- **Mechanism:** Compress the entire coupled `L2W21 × L2W22` apparatus into one minimal, checkpoint-attached 6-field state tuple per tracked failure mode—`(F, ΔSupp, λ_latent, g_t, τ_rearm, pin_mask)`—updated online by shadow probes (`L3W02`) and decoy heartbeats (`L3W10`). Test whether this tiny structured state strictly dominates both independent `L2W21 + L2W22` ledgers and a full frozen-world shadow twin (`LW29`) at equal memory bytes and probe budgets.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L2W28` bundled `LW02 + LW03` broadly; here we isolate the *minimal sufficient state* for the two specific surviving primitives (`L2W21` + `L2W22`) so any downstream continual-learning method can carry it cheaply.
- **Cheapest first test:** 1,000-step coupled benchmark combining delayed un-suppression shocks, mixed software/human guard decay, benign and adversarial regressions, and 15 sequential tasks under a strict byte and probe cap. Compare plain rollback + replay, `LW29` shadow twin at equal bytes, independent `L2W21 + L2W22`, and the unified 6-field state controller. Kill if independent `L2W21 + L2W22` or a compressed `LW29` shadow twin ties on worst-case unguarded shock regret and late-task plasticity.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure untouched $A/B$ accuracy, $C$ regression recovery, zero unguarded $F$ shocks, and late-task plasticity across the full 1,000-step horizon.
