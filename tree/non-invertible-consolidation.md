# Non-invertible consolidation: can designed singular steps forget on purpose and merge histories?

Parent: [Synchronizing consolidation (PR #108)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/108), feeding top-10 #2 (Nerode quotient of edits) in [flood-top10.md](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-top10.md). **Node question:** invertible gradient steps never merge edit classes, so classes merge only at non-invertible steps (decay to zero, dead units). Can deliberately designed non-invertible steps be a CL primitive for controlled forgetting and consolidation? These are raw research bets, not novelty verdicts. The only experiment is a small NumPy probe ([`tree/probes/noninvertible_consolidation_probe.py`](probes/noninvertible_consolidation_probe.py), about 10 s on CPU).

## Root cause: gradient descent hides history; it never deletes it

1. **The step map is a bijection.** For `θ' = θ − η∇L(θ) − ηλ_wd θ`, the Jacobian is `(1 − ηλ_wd)I − ηH`. It is nonsingular whenever `η(λ_max(H) + λ_wd) < 1`, the usual stable regime. So the full state after any finite run is an injective function of the history: two different pasts remain two different states. "Forgetting" under SGD is observational. The current readout stops showing the old information, but a later edit can still read it. Relearning savings, and relearning attacks that recover a forget set by fine-tuning only on the retain set ([2505.22310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22310), [2502.05374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05374)), are the empirical face of this fact.
2. **Contraction is slowest exactly where the protected loss is flat.** Per eigendirection, a history difference shrinks by `1 − ηλ_i − ηλ_wd`. In flat directions (`λ_i ≈ 0`) only weight decay contracts, at the same rate for every parameter. It cannot tell nuisance history from a flat direction that a future task will read. Probe, part 1: plain GD keeps a flat-direction gap of 1.27 forever. GD + weight decay needs 28,095 steps to reach 1e-6 and never becomes exactly equal (float64 or float32: the gap only underflows to denormals, about 1e-42).
3. **The singular steps we get for free choose the wrong kernel.** Decay to zero, ReLU death, magnitude pruning, quantization rounding and clipping merge states by magnitude or activity. They are blind to what is protected. Probe, part 2: 50% magnitude pruning costs task A +0.176 MSE and leaves the history gap unchanged (1.44 vs 1.48).
4. **Even a well-chosen kernel only erases what the protected readout cannot see now.** Let a linear consolidation step `N` keep a protected readout `O` (`ON = O`). Then the mergeable history differences are `ker N ⊆ ker O`: the differences that are invisible to the protected readout *today*. PR #104 showed that today's readout is the wrong object, because later edits expose unread tails. The safe kernel is the annihilator of the **edit-closed** protected span (PR #104's `B`), not of the current activations. A second residual sits inside the protected span: two histories can fit task A with different parametrizations (gauge, such as ReLU rescaling), and a projection keeps both. Probe: projecting onto the span read by task-A activations (anchor-reset of the complement) cuts the history gap from 1.48 to 0.61 at +0.002 A-MSE, and from 0.40 to 0.23 after 50 shared continuation steps on a new task. It does not reach 0; the remaining gap lives inside A's span.

**What follows.** A non-invertible step is the only way to make two pasts truly one. So it is the only way to delete information exactly (not just hide it), and the only way for consolidation to save state or tests. To be a primitive it must (a) pick its kernel as the complement of what the protected tasks *and their future edits* read, (b) fix the gauge inside the protected span, and (c) show the merge in future-edit traces and relearning savings, not just in today's outputs.

### Probe results (5 seeds, mean [min, max])

ReLU MLP 16-48-1. A shared task A, then a history-specific task B1 or B2 (each trained with A replay), then one consolidation operator. "Gap" = RMS output difference between the two histories on random probe inputs. "Savings" = steps a learner that never saw B1 needs to relearn B1, minus the steps for history h1 after the same operator (0 means no residual memory of B1).

| Operator | ΔMSE A | Gap now | Gap after 50 C steps | B1 loss / var | B1 savings (steps) | Steps to learn C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.000 | 1.48 [1.16, 1.88] | 0.40 [0.36, 0.44] | 0.07 | 3.6 [2, 7] | 14.4 |
| weight decay, 200 steps on A | +0.003 | 1.09 | 0.33 | 0.13 | 3.4 | 11.2 |
| magnitude prune 50% | +0.176 | 1.44 | 0.43 | 0.30 | 3.0 | 16.0 |
| unlearn B (random targets + A replay, 300 steps) | −0.012 | 1.26 | 0.47 | 1.08 | 0.6 [0, 2] | 15.2 |
| span projection (A activations, tol 1e-3) | +0.002 | 0.61 [0.43, 0.87] | 0.23 [0.20, 0.29] | 1.34 | 1.8 [0, 4] | 13.4 |
| span projection (tol 1e-2) | +0.081 | 0.32 | 0.14 | 1.48 | 0.8 [0, 1] | 12.2 |

Reading: the designed singular step is the only operator that halves the history gap at near-zero retention cost. Its savings are no better than invertible unlearning, though: all savings are a few steps, within seed noise, so this probe cannot show relearning-proof forgetting. It also gives no plasticity gain (weight decay learns C fastest). This is a toy and the probe is not a CL result.

**Shared first-test protocol.** Extend the probe: CPU, a width-16 to 64 MLP or a 3-coordinate linear learner, 5+ seeds. The standing controls are no-op, matched weight decay, magnitude pruning, shrink-and-perturb, random-target unlearning with A replay, retrain-from-scratch without B, and Re-Basin alignment where two checkpoints exist. Always report: A loss right after the step and after continuation edits of A; the history gap after shared continuations, both on held-out suffixes and on an adversarial suffix; relearning savings of the erased task against a never-saw-it learner under the same operator; and new-task acquisition. Kill a card if it wins only on current outputs, only against no-op, or ties retrain-from-scratch at lower cost only because it forgets A too.

## Raw ideas

### A. Design the kernel: what exactly is erased

1. **Edit-closed annihilator reset.** **Mechanism:** compute the protected span closed under allowed edits: the span of task-A activations and Jacobian rows after `k` sampled continuation edits (PR #104's `B` in the affine case). Project weights onto it and reset the complement to a canonical anchor. **Why it might be new:** GPM/COP/OBU use current activations to protect or unlearn. The kernel here is defined by what *future* edits read, which the root cause says is the right object. **Cheapest test:** the probe plus PR #104's exact affine oracle, with a planted tail that a continuation edit exposes. Compare current-span vs edit-closed-span projection on post-edit A loss. **Retention:** A after the projection *and* after continuation edits on A; current-span projection should fail exactly there.
2. **Gauge-fix, then project.** **Mechanism:** before the projection, map each history to a canonical representative inside the protected span, such as ReLU rescale balancing to a fixed norm ratio and sorting units by a canonical key. This map is many-to-one on parameters and the identity on function. **Why it might be new:** spin-echo was killed because symmetries don't change training, but PR #104's `(1,1)` vs `(2,½)` shows that rescaled twins *do* diverge under later edits. Canonicalizing erases that hidden channel; it does not store anything in it, unlike the killed Noether memory. **Cheapest test:** PR #104's uv toy, then the probe's in-span residual gap (0.10 on A inputs); does it drop to the A-function gap alone? **Retention:** function unchanged to float error by construction; A after continuation.
3. **Coarse quantization in the kernel only.** **Mechanism:** round coordinates in the unprotected complement to a coarse grid, and keep the protected span at full precision. Rounding is many-to-one, so histories inside one cell merge. **Why it might be new:** quantization is used for compression and quantized statistics for exact unlearning ([2610.07197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07197)). Grid resolution as a per-subspace *forgetting resolution* is not. **Cheapest test:** the probe with a coarse grid in the kernel vs uniform quantization at equal total bits. **Retention:** A loss; savings of B.
4. **Apoptosis step.** **Mechanism:** kill and re-initialize units whose output weights carry the most between-history variance and the least A-readout, not dormant ones. **Why it might be new:** ReDo and continual backprop reset by dormancy or utility for plasticity; the selection score here is history divergence for consolidation. **Cheapest test:** the probe, against ReDo and random resets at equal unit count. **Retention:** A loss and A-margin floors per group.
5. **Importance-shaped proximal threshold.** **Mechanism:** a soft-threshold step whose per-parameter threshold is inverse Fisher of the protected tasks. Probe part 1 shows that a soft threshold merges two histories *exactly* in finite time (2,609 steps), unlike weight decay. **Why it might be new:** FADE learns per-parameter decay rates, which are invertible; this is the singular counterpart, with a finite merge time that can be predicted. **Cheapest test:** the probe vs FADE-style adaptive weight decay at matched A loss: steps to exact merge, and gap. **Retention:** A loss through the whole schedule.
6. **Truncate the history delta, not the weights.** **Mechanism:** at each consolidation, SVD-truncate `θ − θ_last_commit` to rank `k` along protected-span-weighted singular directions. This erases low-energy history-specific components. **Why it might be new:** low-rank decay and LoRA shape the whole weights or the update; truncation as a history merge with a commit point is different. **Cheapest test:** two histories differing in low-energy deltas; the gap after truncation vs the same-rank LoRA retrain. **Retention:** A and the high-energy B (if it is meant to be kept).
7. **Saturation sinks.** **Mechanism:** use bounded units (hard-tanh). Consolidation drives history-specific units into saturation, where they become constants; the gradient is 0, so the state is absorbing. **Why it might be new:** saturation is normally a failure; here it is a designed, non-invertible absorbing state with an explicit entry rule. **Cheapest test:** hard-tanh MLP probe; the gap and A loss vs pruning those units. **Retention:** A, and new-task acquisition (do absorbed units cost plasticity?).

### B. Controlled forgetting with a certificate

8. **Exact-merge unlearning certificate.** **Mechanism:** if the difference between "trained with B" and "trained without B" lies in `ker N`, then `N(θ_B) = N(θ_noB)` exactly. The certificate is the measured residual `‖N(θ_B) − N(θ_noB)‖` against a cheap reference run without B. **Why it might be new:** projection unlearning claims robustness empirically; SISA retrains. An equality certificate on the state itself rules out relearning attacks by construction. **Cheapest test:** the probe with h1 vs never-saw-B under `N`: residual, savings, and a retain-only fine-tuning attack ([2505.22310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22310) protocol). **Retention:** A loss; the A-utility cost vs retrain-from-scratch.
9. **Landauer ledger for continual learning.** **Mechanism:** count the bits each step destroys (log preimage volume for prox and rounding; 0 for invertible steps). Predict forgetting and consolidation gain from bits spent. **Why it might be new:** it makes "invertible steps forget nothing" quantitative and gives a conserved-information budget. It may connect to the rate–drift law (top-10 #4). **Cheapest test:** an exactly enumerable quantized learner; count distinct reachable states before and after each step, and correlate with B savings. **Retention:** the fraction of the bits budget spent on A-read coordinates must be 0.
10. **Reversible between commits, singular at commits.** **Mechanism:** all ordinary updates are invertible (exact undo is cheap: reverse the step or keep seeds), and a periodic designed commit is the only lossy step. The result is database-like semantics: exact rollback inside a window, irreversible consolidation at commit. **Why it might be new:** reversible CL exists, and so do resets; making invertibility *the* boundary between undo and commit, with the commit kernel chosen by idea 1, does not. **Cheapest test:** the probe with float64 and exact inverse steps; undo error before and after a commit; memory per window. **Retention:** A across commits; old commits never touched again.
11. **Selective savings deletion.** **Mechanism:** include "speed of relearning A-variants" in the protected readout, and choose the kernel so that B's savings vanish while A-variant savings stay. **Why it might be new:** the inverse of top-10 #5; savings become a protected or erasable quantity, not only a side effect. **Cheapest test:** the probe with an A-variant task (rotated teacher); savings for both after `N`. **Retention:** A and the A-variant relearning speed.
12. **Forgetting that pays for plasticity.** **Mechanism:** use consolidation merges as the plasticity reset, since a canonical state may be easier to train from than any single history. **Why it might be new:** plasticity resets are random or utility-based. **Cheapest test:** steps to learn C after `N` vs shrink-and-perturb at equal norm. **Retention:** A. *The probe already says no at this scale: 13.4 vs 11.2 steps for weight decay. Keep only if a larger stream shows otherwise.*

### C. Consolidation as a many-to-one map onto canonical states

13. **Canonical fiber library.** **Mechanism:** keep a few canonical states and consolidate by projecting onto the nearest canonical fiber: vector quantization of history in edit-closed coordinates. Many pasts collapse to few classes in *one* step, unlike PR #108's words. **Why it might be new:** model routing picks experts; this is a learned quantizer over history classes whose cells have equal future-edit traces. **Cheapest test:** four histories over two curricula; the number of classes after `N` vs k-means of weights. **Retention:** each history's protected tasks after snapping.
14. **Learned idempotent consolidator.** **Mechanism:** train a small hypernetwork `N(θ)` with losses `N∘N = N`, `O∘N = O` on edit-closed probes, and gap minimization across histories. **Why it might be new:** IDER and the idempotent generative network work in output or input space; this is a weight-space idempotent operator that is learned, not derived. **Cheapest test:** linear learners where the analytic projector is known; does the learned `N` recover it, and generalize to held-out histories? **Retention:** A after `N` on held-out histories.
15. **Branch merge by common projection.** **Mechanism:** apply the same `N` to every branch of a federated or multi-run learner. If the branches differ only in `ker N` they become identical, and averaging is trivial. **Why it might be new:** TIES and Re-Basin merge distinct weights; here a shared singular map makes them equal first. **Cheapest test:** four probe branches; `N` then average vs Re-Basin then average vs TIES. **Retention:** every branch's shared protected tasks.
16. **Coarse-graining by unit fusion.** **Mechanism:** fuse units whose incoming weights in the protected span agree (two units become one, with summed outputs). This is non-invertible, and it merges histories that split one feature across units in different ways. **Why it might be new:** neuron merging (ZipIt-style) compresses one model; using fusion as a history-quotient step is new. **Cheapest test:** the probe with duplicated-feature histories; the gap after fusion vs pruning at equal width. **Retention:** A.
17. **Kernel reset of optimizer state.** **Mechanism:** reset Adam or momentum moments to canonical values only along `ker N`, and keep them on the protected span. **Why it might be new:** PR #108 #2 resets velocity with a word; this is a one-step singular reset scoped by the protected span. **Cheapest test:** heavy-ball probe; future-edit gap with full velocity reset vs scoped reset. **Retention:** A, and the A step response after the reset.

### D. Make the singular step itself the object of study

18. **Adversarially learned kernel.** **Mechanism:** pick `ker N` to minimize the worst-case history gap under a separating suffix found by an adversary, subject to `ON = O` on the edit-closed span. **Why it might be new:** PR #108 #24 trains a reset *word*; this trains one singular map, which is cheaper to deploy. **Cheapest test:** a bilinear toy with a planted read-after-write distinction. **Retention:** held-out A tests that the adversary did not see.
19. **Deterministic low precision as a free consolidator.** **Mechanism:** late-training bf16 or int8 with deterministic rounding merges histories whose difference is below grid resolution in flat directions. Measure this precision-induced coalescence rate. **Why it might be new:** stochastic rounding is studied for accuracy, not as a known rate of history merging. **Cheapest test:** emulated bf16 in the probe: merge counts vs grid size, against stochastic rounding. **Retention:** A at each precision. *Probe part 1 with float32 and fine weight decay merges only at denormal underflow, so this needs coarse grids.*
20. **Deadband optimizer.** **Mechanism:** drop parameter updates below a threshold `ε` (a non-invertible dead zone). Tiny nuisance drifts never accumulate, so histories differing only by sub-ε pushes stay equal. **Why it might be new:** sparse or clipped updates are used for compute; a deadband as a consolidation guarantee is not. **Cheapest test:** the probe with `ε` swept; gap vs A and C acquisition. **Retention:** A, and whether small A refinements get dropped.
21. **Order-invariant consolidation by commuting projectors.** **Mechanism:** design the consolidation steps for tasks A and B to commute (`N_A N_B = N_B N_A`, e.g. projectors onto nested or orthogonal spans), so consolidation order cannot matter. **Why it might be new:** O2 commutative learners (open, ledger) target gradient updates; commuting *singular* steps is a narrower, testable law. **Cheapest test:** linear spans with overlapping protected sets; the order gap with and without commuting construction. **Retention:** A and B under both orders.
22. **Singular consolidation for edit order.** **Mechanism:** after sequential MEMIT/ROME edits, project the edited layer onto the key-covariance span of the live edits plus protected keys, and reset the rest. Edit sequences that differ only in overwritten or retracted edits then merge. **Why it might be new:** sequential editing work regularizes interference and does not merge edit histories. The ledger's capacity-law kill gives the baseline to beat. **Cheapest test:** a linear associative memory with 20 edits in 2 orders; the gap after `N`. **Retention:** live edits and protected keys.
23. **Commit-triggered forgetting schedule.** **Mechanism:** trigger the singular step when the measured history gap (from shadow continuations) exceeds a budget, rather than on a clock. **Why it might be new:** reset schedules are periodic or utility-triggered; this one is triggered by history distinguishability. **Cheapest test:** a stream of 10 tasks; gap budget vs periodic commits at equal commit count. **Retention:** worst-group A floors across the stream.
24. **Irreducible-memory detector.** **Mechanism:** when no `N` with `ON = O` can merge two histories, the residual difference *is* protected information. Report it as the minimal live memory a learner must carry. **Why it might be new:** it turns non-invertibility into a measurement of how much history is really needed. It overlaps PR #108 #23 (obstruction certificate), but for one step. **Cheapest test:** the probe; the residual gap at the maximal safe kernel vs the A-function gap between histories. **Retention:** by definition, the A-read part must stay.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Is the edit-closed annihilator, after gauge fixing, the maximal safe kernel, and does resetting it make two histories exactly equal at zero retention cost in a nonlinear learner?** Highest: it would be a one-step consolidation operator with an exact correctness condition (1, 2, 14, 24). First check: does the probe's residual gap (0.61 now, 0.23 after continuation) fall to the A-function gap alone when the span is edit-closed and the gauge is fixed? If retain-only continuation re-separates the histories, stop.
2. **Can a singular step certify unlearning by state equality and beat relearning attacks at lower cost than retraining?** (8, 11, 3) A clean certificate would differ from 2505.22310 / 2508.15449 (empirical robustness). The probe's savings are too small to tell (0.6–3.6 steps), so the first test needs a task with large savings.
3. **Is there a Landauer-style ledger, with history entropy equal to retained protected information plus bits destroyed, that predicts forgetting?** (9, 19, 3) Novel as a law, but risky: it may only restate data-processing inequalities. Test on an exactly enumerable quantized learner.
4. **Does reversible-between-commits, singular-at-commit give exact undo plus bounded memory in a way neither reversible CL nor resets do alone?** (10, 17, 20, 23) Lower: it is likely a systems combination unless the commit kernel needs idea 1.
5. **Do designed merges buy anything beyond equality: plasticity or amortized testing?** (12, 13, 15) Lowest: the probe shows no plasticity gain (weight decay learns C fastest). PR #108's amortization question applies unchanged.

## First discriminating CPU experiment (proposed)

Extend `tree/probes/noninvertible_consolidation_probe.py`. Build the edit-closed span from task-A activations and Jacobians after 8 random continuation edits, fix the ReLU gauge by per-unit norm balancing, project, and reset the complement to the initialization anchor. Compare against current-span projection, pruning, weight decay, random-target unlearning and retrain-from-scratch without B, over 10 seeds. Use a B task with large savings (at least 30 steps in the no-op arm), so the certificate claim can fail. Pass: the history gap after continuation falls below the strongest control's CI and below the A-function gap plus 10%; B savings are within the CI of never-saw-B; A loss stays within 1% after the step and after continuation edits on A. Kill: the gap stays at the current-span level, or the savings gain disappears against retrain-from-scratch at matched compute.
