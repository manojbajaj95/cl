# Learner–world co-evolution (depth 4): cyclic manifold re-anchoring & strategic non-learning under performative debt

Child node (depth 4) of [PR #366](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/366) (`L4W03` + `L4W15`) · 26 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Parent triage.** In `reviews/flood-triage.md` (latest row, PR #366 / `tree/learner-world-coevolution-4.md`), two score-3 primitives were kept while routine EWC stiffness tweaks (`L4W01`, `L4W02`, `L4W05`–`L4W07`) were pruned:
1. **`L4W03` — re-anchor protection when it cycles (`0→1→0`):** when external guard readiness rises, protection relaxes and the learner drifts freely to master new task $C$; if the guard later drops again, re-centering protection on a *re-validated prevention point near current weights* avoids dragging the learner back to a stale historical anchor $\theta^*_F$ and erasing $C$.
2. **`L4W15` — price pinning debt before admitting a prevention:** before learning to prevent a new failure $F$, estimate the long-term plasticity cost of the external guards that mastering $F$ will cause downstream teams/tools to drop, and admit $F$ only if its value exceeds that debt against `L3W24`'s budget—meaning the learner may *deliberately decline to master a low-value skill* because of the world's performative response.

**Why this node pushes beyond EWC tweaks.** Both kept ideas break foundational assumptions of classical continual learning:
- **`L4W03` breaks monotonic parameter protection:** protection is *cyclic* because the world toggles whether it needs the learner to hold an invariant. Once $\theta_t$ drifts far away to master $C, D, E$, the right primitive is not tweaking a quadratic EWC penalty—it is *anchorless functional/behavioral re-anchoring*, *mode-connectivity ridge projection*, *meta-learned 1-step hibernating attractors*, and *representation-grafted readouts*.
- **`L4W15` breaks "more competence is always better":** in a performative world where competence dismantles external infrastructure, mastering a low-value failure can be *net-negative* over a lifetime. The right primitives are *strategic non-learning*, *bounded-competence ceilings below the reflexivity threshold*, *active Learning-to-Defer that strengthens external guards*, *reverse-performative curricula*, and *real-options debt pricing*.

## Section A · Beyond EWC anchors: cyclic manifold re-anchoring & hibernating attractors (`L5W01`–`L5W09`, deepening `L4W03`)

### L5W01 · Anchorless behavioral-manifold projection (zero parameter anchor)
- **Mechanism:** Abandon parameter-space anchors $\theta^*_F$ altogether. Store only a tiny *behavioral invariant oracle* (a set of $k$ safe-boundary probe inputs $\mathcal{B}_F$ and required safe margins). When an external guard cycles $0\to 1\to 0$, project the current weights $\theta_t$ onto the nearest point in the *functional safe set* $\mathcal{M}_F = \{\theta : m_F(\theta; \mathcal{B}_F) \ge \gamma\}$ via a single KL/margin-constrained proximal step around $\theta_t$—anchoring the *behavior* locally without pulling parameters back toward any historical coordinate.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Beyond Parameter Protection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37145) and [Convergence of Continual Learning in Homogeneous Deep Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30559) analyze functional/margin projections over *monotonic* task sequences; `L5W01` applies anchorless margin projection specifically to *cyclic* $0\to 1\to 0$ re-entry after arbitrary parameter drift on $C$.
- **Cheapest first test:** 2-layer MLP on a `0→1→0` guard cycle where $C$ rotates internal hidden representations by $90^\circ$ during $g_t=1$ (destroying coordinate alignment with $\theta^*_F$). Compare fixed-anchor online EWC, naive unvalidated re-anchor at $\theta_t$, `L3W12` alone, and anchorless functional projection `L5W01`. Kill if parameter-space `L4W03` or online EWC matches $C$-retention and $F$-prevention after representation rotation.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $C$ accuracy stays intact after $g_t: 1\to 0$, $F$-prevention is 100% restored, and old tasks $A/B$ do not regress.

### L5W02 · Mode-connectivity ridge search (bridging across $C$'s basin after drift)
- **Mechanism:** If learning task $C$ during $g_t=1$ carried $\theta_t$ out of $\theta^*_F$'s local quadratic basin, local gradient steps from $\theta_t$ either fail to recover $F$ or tear down $C$. Search for a *low-loss piecewise-linear mode-connectivity ridge* between $\theta_t$ and the $F$-safe manifold, selecting the point $\theta_{\text{ridge}}$ along the ridge that maximizes $\min(\text{Acc}_C, \text{Prev}_F)$ as the new cyclic re-anchor.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Mode connectivity in continual learning is typically used to interpolate *static* task endpoints; using ridge search as a *cyclic re-anchoring operator* finds a joint $(C \cap F)$ intersection even when $\theta_t$ and $\theta^*_F$ are separated by a loss barrier.
- **Cheapest first test:** Non-convex continual benchmark with an explicit loss barrier between $\theta^*_F$ and $\theta_t$ (trained on $C$ during $g_t=1$). Compare fixed-anchor EWC, local-gradient `L4W03`, unvalidated re-anchor, `L3W12`, and ridge-search `L5W02`. Kill if local-gradient re-anchoring from $\theta_t$ reaches the same $(C, F)$ Pareto point without ridge search.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify both $C$ and $F$ sit inside a single connected basin after re-anchoring, with intact $A/B$ retention during subsequent task $D$.

### L5W03 · Meta-learned 1-step hibernating attractors (plasticity-free dormancy)
- **Mechanism:** Why pay *any* consolidation penalty while $g_t(F)=1$? Instead of freezing weights during the `1` phase, add an ultra-cheap Reptile/MAML-style *hibernation regularizer* during $C$-training: keep $\theta_t$ within *1 gradient step* of an $F$-safe point ($\min_\theta \mathcal{L}_C(\theta) + \beta \mathcal{L}_F(\theta - \alpha \nabla \mathcal{L}_F(\theta))$). While $g_t=1$, $\theta_t$ itself need not prevent $F$ (maximizing $C$ plasticity); the moment $g_t$ drops to $0$, a single gradient step snaps $\theta_t$ onto a fresh local re-anchor $\theta_F^{\text{re}}$ with near-zero $C$-damage!
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Online meta-continual learning (OML/ANML) meta-learns representations to resist *monotonic* forgetting; `L5W03` meta-regularizes *dormant* skills specifically to guarantee 1-step cyclic re-entry when an external guard relapses.
- **Cheapest first test:** `0→1→0` cycle with 3 conflicting tasks $C_1, C_2, C_3$ trained during the $g_t=1$ window. Compare fixed-anchor online EWC, `L3W12` alone, post-hoc `L4W03`, and 1-step hibernating attractor `L5W03` (at equal compute). Kill if post-hoc `L4W03` recovers $F$ in 1 step with equal $C_1$–$C_3$ retention without the hibernation term.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $C_1$–$C_3$ accuracy is higher during $g_t=1$, $F$ snaps back in 1 step on $g_t\to 0$, and $A/B$ remain above floor.

### L5W04 · Cycle-drift bifurcation check (when the relapsed world's $F'$ differs from old $F$)
- **Mechanism:** During a long $g_t=1$ vacation, the external world evolves: when the guard eventually drops, the live failure mode $F'$ in world $W_{t_2}$ may have drifted from the original $F$ in $W_{t_0}$, so re-anchoring to *either* $\theta^*_F$ or old trigger witnesses would protect the *wrong* invariant! On every $1\to 0$ transition, run a fast counterfactual discrepancy test comparing old $F$-witnesses against live $W_{t_2}$ probes; if $F' \neq F$, abort re-anchoring and route to fresh $F'$-learning (subject to `L4W15` debt pricing).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `L4W03` assumes the failure definition $F$ is stationary across the `0→1→0` cycle; in a co-evolving world, guard vacations are precisely when upstream schemas and failure modes drift.
- **Cheapest first test:** Cyclic world where $F$ mutates into $F'$ (shifted trigger boundary) during $g_t=1$ in half the runs and stays stationary in the other half. Compare stationary `L4W03`, fixed-anchor EWC, unvalidated re-anchor, and bifurcation-checked `L5W04`. Kill if stationary `L4W03` prevents $F'$ equally well on mutated cycles.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero false protection of obsolete $F$, rapid prevention of $F'$, and untouched $C$ and $A/B$ retention.

### L5W05 · Representation-grafted readout re-anchoring (convex re-anchor in $C$'s coordinates)
- **Mechanism:** When task $C$ heavily rewrites shared backbone features $\phi_{\theta_t}(x)$ during $g_t=1$, trying to re-anchor the whole network fights the backbone. Freeze the newly learned $C$-backbone $\phi_{\theta_t}$ and solve a *convex readout-only* (or narrow side-adapter) re-anchoring problem on $\phi_{\theta_t}(\mathcal{B}_F)$ to restore $F$-prevention natively in $C$'s new coordinate system—anchoring only the tiny readout/adapter while leaving the backbone 100% plastic for $C$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dually Constrained Parameter Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29269) constrains full-network updates in Jacobian null spaces; `L5W05` exploits representation invariance to decouple cyclic re-anchoring from backbone plasticity completely.
- **Cheapest first test:** Deep network trained on $C$ during $g_t=1$ with heavy feature drift, followed by $g_t\to 0$. Compare full-network `L4W03`, fixed-anchor online EWC, `L3W12` alone, and readout-grafted `L5W05` (with a linear separability check on $\phi_{\theta_t}(\mathcal{B}_F)$). Kill if full-network `L4W03` matches $C$-retention and $F$-prevention when backbone features shift substantially.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify 0% $C$-degradation upon $F$ re-anchoring whenever $\phi_{\theta_t}$ remains linearly sufficient for $F$, plus intact $A/B$.

### L5W06 · Proactive basin-escape alarm (re-anchoring *before* the manifold disconnects)
- **Mechanism:** Waiting until $g_t$ drops to re-anchor (`L4W03`) fails if task $C$ already crossed an irreversible representational cliff during $g_t=1$ (destroying linear separability of $F$ in $\phi_{\theta_t}$). While $g_t=1$, cheaply monitor the *re-anchoring distance* $d(\theta_t, \mathcal{M}_F)$ every $K$ steps; if $d$ approaches a basin-escape threshold, apply a brief *soft-tether* projection to keep $\theta_t$ on the $F$-reachable side of the ridge—paying tiny cost during $C$ to guarantee cheap re-anchoring later.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Learning Dynamics of Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33620) characterizes when feature drift causes irreversible forgetting; `L5W06` turns that boundary into an early-warning *reachability tether* for dormant skills whose guards may relapse.
- **Cheapest first test:** `0→1→0` cycle where task $C$ includes a feature-collapse step that irreversibly erases $F$'s linear features if unchecked. Compare reactive `L4W03`, fixed-anchor EWC, `L3W12`, and proactive-tether `L5W06`. Kill if reactive `L4W03` recovers $F$ equally well after $C$'s feature-collapse step without destroying $C$.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $C$ achieves full accuracy (no premature freezing), $F$ remains reachable upon relapse, and $A/B$ hold.

### L5W07 · Multi-guard simultaneous-relapse alternating projection (Dykstra re-anchoring)
- **Mechanism:** Real-world shocks (e.g., an on-call staffing crunch or CI outage) cause *multiple* external guards $F_1, F_2, F_3$ to drop simultaneously after a shared $g_t=1$ window. Sequentially re-anchoring $F_1$ then $F_2$ lets $F_2$ undo $F_1$ or crush $C$. Run *Dykstra's alternating projection* (cyclic sequential margin projection, cf. `2606.30559`) onto the intersection $\mathcal{M}_C \cap \mathcal{M}_{F_1} \cap \mathcal{M}_{F_2} \cap \mathcal{M}_{F_3}$ initialized at $\theta_t$, finding the unique minimum-displacement joint re-anchor (or certifying infeasibility immediately!).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Convergence of Continual Learning in Homogeneous Deep Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30559) proves sequential projections converge onto intersections of margin sets; `L5W07` operationalizes that geometry for simultaneous multi-guard relapses.
- **Cheapest first test:** 3-guard simultaneous `0→1→0` relapse after learning $C$, where $F_1, F_2, F_3$ have conflicting gradients. Compare sequential `L4W03`, summed online EWC, unvalidated re-anchor, and alternating-projection `L5W07`. Kill if sequential `L4W03` or summed EWC reaches the same joint $(C, F_1, F_2, F_3)$ satisfaction.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify all 3 relapsed preventions hold simultaneously with minimal $C$-drop and intact $A/B$.

### L5W08 · Synthetic boundary-sketch distillation (architecture-invariant cyclic re-anchoring)
- **Mechanism:** Any parameter-space anchor (`L4W03`, EWC, online EWC) breaks if the model undergoes pruning, quantization, LoRA merging, or width expansion during the $g_t=1$ vacation. Replace parameter anchors with a *10-example synthetic boundary sketch* (distilled counterfactual pairs straddling $F$'s decision boundary). On $g_t: 1\to 0$, re-anchor by distilling $\theta_t$ on the boundary sketch under a $C$-KL trust region—surviving arbitrary architectural mutations between cycles.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Dataset distillation / boundary sketching is normally used for static compression; using it as an *architecture-invariant cyclic re-anchoring primitive* decouples guard-cycle protection from fixed parameter topologies.
- **Cheapest first test:** `0→1→0` cycle where the network is pruned by 30% or has a LoRA merged during $g_t=1$ while learning $C$. Compare parameter-anchor `L4W03`, online EWC, random-replay buffer at equal bytes, and boundary-sketch `L5W08`. Kill if equal-byte random replay matches $F$-recovery and $C$-retention after pruning/merging.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $F$ re-anchors cleanly even after structural model edits during $g_t=1$, with intact $C$ and $A/B$.

### L5W09 · Reversible skill-folding (migrating prevention between weights and dormant micro-wrappers)
- **Mechanism:** Instead of keeping $F$-prevention in shared weights across noisy $0\to 1\to 0$ guard cycles, *fold* $F$ out of the main weights into a dormant, zero-runtime-cost external micro-wrapper (a tiny side classifier/filter) whenever $g_t$ rises to $1$. If $g_t$ briefly relapses, activate the wrapper instantaneously (zero weight interference with $C$!); only *unfold* $F$ back into main weights via `L5W01` if the guard stays down past a persistence horizon $T_{\text{fold}}$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Turns `L4W03` into a two-timescale architectural primitive: short guard blips never touch main weights at all, while permanent guard extinctions trigger clean behavioral unfolding.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stream with 5 brief guard blips (2–5 steps) and 1 permanent guard extinction while training $C_1 \dots C_5$. Compare weight-only `L4W03`, fixed-anchor EWC, always-wrapper (memory/latency cost), and reversible-folding `L5W09`. Kill if weight-only `L4W03` suffers zero cumulative $C$-degradation across 5 rapid cycles.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero weight disruption during short blips, bounded wrapper count via unfolding on permanent drops, and intact $A/B$ + $C_1 \dots C_5$.

## Section B · Beyond greedy learning: strategic non-learning, performative admission & guard-preserving competence (`L5W10`–`L5W18`, deepening `L4W15`)

### L5W10 · Strategic non-learning via bounded-competence ceilings (staying below the reflexivity threshold)
- **Mechanism:** `L4W15` makes a binary choice: admit $F$ or decline $F$. Push further: in performative systems, external guards disappear only when the learner's prevention rate $p_F$ crosses a *reflexivity threshold* $p_{\text{crit}}$ (`2610.29577`) where humans/tools stop seeing failures and dismantle their checks. Deliberately *cap* the learner's competence on low-value failure $F$ at $p_F^* = p_{\text{crit}} - \delta$ (e.g., preventing 90% of routine noise while leaving 10% benign/low-cost triggers visible to the external guard), capturing 90% of the immediate utility at **zero pinning debt**!
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [The Reflexivity Threshold](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29577) identifies phase transitions in performative multi-agent systems; *deliberately capping a continual learner's competence just below the guard-extinction phase transition* is a genuinely bold anti-ossification primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Lagged guard-removal world where downstream guards switch off whenever $p_F > 0.95$ for 20 steps, with 10 sequential tasks $C_1 \dots C_{10}$. Compare always-admit ($p_F\to 1.0$), binary `L4W15` (decline $F$ completely, $p_F=0$), post-hoc `GSX6`, performative-prediction retraining, and bounded-competence `L5W10` ($p_F^*=0.90$). Kill if binary `L4W15` or always-admit + `GSX6` achieves higher joint cumulative value + $C$-learning speed.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify external guards stay alive ($g_t \approx 1$), zero pinning budget is consumed, and $C_1 \dots C_{10}$ + $A/B$ retain full plasticity.

### L5W11 · Active guard-exercising Learning-to-Defer (learn to *route* $F$, not to *prevent* $F$)
- **Mechanism:** When `L4W15` declines to learn $F$-prevention because its pinning debt exceeds its value, passively ignoring $F$ still hurts user utility. Instead, train a lightweight *deferral head* (`2611.26440`, `2611.05814`) that detects $F$-triggers and routes them cleanly—with structured diagnostic context—to the external guard! Routing $F$ consumes negligible subspace rank, *actively exercises* the external guard (reversing expert/guard drift!), and avoids absorbing $F$ into pinned weights.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Learning to Defer under Expert Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26440) and [Learning When Human Decisions Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05814) treat expert drift as an exogenous shift to adapt around; `L5W11` uses L2D *performatively* as a continual-learning substitute for weight absorption, keeping external guards sharp while saving internal plasticity.
- **Cheapest first test:** World with 8 low-value/high-rank failures and drifting external guards alongside 10 high-value tasks $C_1 \dots C_{10}$. Compare always-admit, passive-decline `L4W15`, standard L2D (which defers only on uncertainty, not pinning debt), `GSX6`, and guard-exercising deferral `L5W11`. Kill if passive-decline `L4W15` or always-admit achieves equal cumulative value + $C$-learning speed.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify external guards stay calibrated ($g_t \ge 0.9$), zero internal prevention pinning on the 8 deferred failures, and high $C_1 \dots C_{10}$ + $A/B$ accuracy.

### L5W12 · Performative elasticity probing (empirically measuring $\partial g_t / \partial p_F$ before admission)
- **Mechanism:** `L4W15` assumes the learner knows in advance which failures will trigger downstream guard removal. In reality, guard elasticity $\eta_F = -\partial g_t(F) / \partial p_F$ varies wildly across teams and tools. Before admitting $F$ globally, deploy $F$-prevention on a *small canary slice* (or short micro-horizon) and directly measure whether downstream guard activity drops ($\hat{\eta}_F$); admit globally only if $\text{Value}(F) > \hat{\eta}_F \cdot \text{Rank}(V_F) \cdot \mu_t$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Stabilizing Performative Feedback Loops with Minimal Model Deployments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14065) uses minimal deployments to estimate performative map sensitivity; `L5W12` uses micro-deployment elasticity probes specifically to *price continual-learning pinning debt* before committing weights.
- **Cheapest first test:** 12-failure world with unknown heterogeneous guard elasticities (half inelastic $\eta_F=0$, half elastic $\eta_F=1$). Compare always-admit, oracle-$\eta$ `L4W15`, value-only admission, `GSX6`, and elasticity-probed `L5W12`. Kill if value-only admission or post-hoc `GSX6` matches `L5W12`'s cumulative value + plasticity without probing.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify all inelastic failures are safely admitted, elastic low-value failures are rejected before causing ossification, and $A/B$ stay intact.

### L5W13 · Reverse-performative curriculum (shaping the world's guard portfolio by selective mastery)
- **Mechanism:** Flip `L4W15` from passive self-defense to *active ecosystem design*. Suppose external guards have wildly different real-world operating costs (e.g., congested human doctors/reviewers `2610.15128` vs. free deterministic linters). Deliberately *admit and master* failures guarded by congested high-cost humans (intentionally letting the world retire those expensive guards!) while *declining* failures guarded by cheap deterministic tools—using selective continual learning to reshape external infrastructure toward maximum joint human–AI throughput.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [To Defer or Not to Defer? Online Learning with Congested Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15128) and [Strategic Decision Focused Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14907) optimize routing for a *fixed* expert pool; `L5W13` optimizes *which capabilities to internalize vs. refuse* so the external guard ecosystem self-reorganizes beneficially.
- **Cheapest first test:** Coupled world with 5 failures guarded by a congested expensive human pool and 5 guarded by free regex validators, under a tight `L3W24` pinning budget. Compare value-only `L4W15`, always-admit, `GSX6`, and reverse-performative curriculum `L5W13`. Kill if value-only `L4W15` (ignoring external guard operating cost) achieves the same joint system throughput + plasticity.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify pinning budget is spent exclusively on relieving human bottlenecks, free software guards remain untouched, and $A/B$ + new tasks stay fast.

### L5W14 · Transparent abstention contracts (preventing user churn when declining to learn $F$)
- **Mechanism:** A practical failure mode of `L4W15` is *silent non-learning*: if the learner declines to master $F$ and simply fails silently on $F$-inputs, users lose trust and abandon the learner (destroying value on high-value tasks $A, B, C$!). Pair every `L4W15` rejection with an explicit, calibrated *abstention contract* (`2610.27258`, `2610.04542`): on $F$-inputs, output a structured abstention naming the external guard responsible, preserving user trust and adoption while keeping the external guard active.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [The Differential Effects of AI Abstention on Advice Acceptance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27258) shows explicit calibrated abstention preserves human trust far better than silent errors; coupling it to `L4W15` makes strategic non-learning viable in user-facing deployments.
- **Cheapest first test:** User-retention simulator where silent errors on $F$ cause user churn at rate $5\times$ higher than explicit calibrated abstentions, alongside 10 tasks $C_1 \dots C_{10}$. Compare silent-decline `L4W15`, always-admit, `GSX6`, and contract-abstention `L5W14`. Kill if silent-decline `L4W15` retains equal user adoption and total value.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero user-churn collapse on $A, B, C$, active external guards on $F$, and full plasticity on $C_1 \dots C_{10}$.

### L5W15 · Scratch-branch interference pricing (measuring true marginal debt, not raw rank)
- **Mechanism:** Static `L4W15` debt estimates ($\text{rank}(V_F) \cdot \mathbb{P}(\text{drop})$) misprice failures that *share* features with existing tasks $A, B$ (whose true marginal plasticity cost is near zero!) vs. failures that collide directly with future-task subspaces. Before committing $F$, train $F$ in a fast *disposable scratch copy* and compute its actual marginal gradient interference against a held-out proxy stream of upcoming tasks; admit $F$ iff `Value(F) > Empirical_Interference_Debt(F)`.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Understanding Model Selection For Learning In Strategic Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07588) studies model selection anticipating strategic response; `L5W15` measures *parameter-space interference* in a scratch branch before paying the irreversible performative cost of deploying $F$.
- **Cheapest first test:** 12-failure stream where 6 failures reuse existing $A/B$ features (low true interference despite high raw rank) and 6 collide with upcoming $C_1 \dots C_6$. Compare rank-heuristic `L4W15`, always-admit, value-only admission, and scratch-priced `L5W15`. Kill if raw rank-based `L4W15` matches `L5W15` on cumulative value + $C_1 \dots C_6$ speed.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify feature-sharing preventions are admitted cheaply, colliding low-value ones are rejected, and $A/B$ + $C_1 \dots C_6$ stay high.

### L5W16 · Real-options hurdle pricing under irreversible guard extinction
- **Mechanism:** Once mastering $F$ causes an external guard to be permanently dismantled (e.g., a team deprecates its legacy validator), the pinning commitment is *irreversible*. By real-options theory, irreversible capacity allocation under uncertain future high-value tasks $C_{\text{future}}$ should not admit at $\text{Value}(F) > \text{Debt}(F)$ (NPV $= 0$); it requires a strictly higher *option-value hurdle* $\text{Value}(F) > \text{Debt}(F) + V_{\text{opt}}(B_{\text{rem}}, \sigma_{\text{future}})$, growing steeper as remaining pinning budget $B_{\text{rem}}$ shrinks.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard `L4W15` uses static break-even pricing; real-options hurdle pricing prevents early mediocre preventions from permanently crowding out rare, ultra-high-value future tasks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stochastic task-value stream (heavy-tailed future task values arriving over 40 steps) with irreversible guard deletion and finite budget $B_{\text{pin}}$.
