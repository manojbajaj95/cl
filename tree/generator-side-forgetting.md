# Generator-side forgetting: can self-play still ask the old question?

Parent: [PR #119, agent self-curriculum forgetting](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/119), cards 15 and 18; direct seed: [FCU9 in PR #36](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/7a445eeabd310aafb994d169bf71f8f2b4ce1151/ideas/flood/cross-pool-17.md#L21).

**Node question.** A solver can still pass previously learned tasks on independent inputs while its task generator loses the ability to generate fresh instances of those same task types. What should persist in the generator so that old families remain available without freezing the curriculum? This is an idea node: no experiments, pilots, or GPU work.

## Root cause: passing an old question does not mean you can ask a new one

FCU9 proposes a frozen executable goal witness and separate matched generator and solver probes. If the generator cannot emit a valid old question but the solver still answers externally supplied ones, repair the missing grammar production; if only the solver fails, practice the skill instead. This is a proposed failure mode and discriminating test, **not** an empirical finding from PR #36. The parent node's CPU result concerns selection from available tasks, not loss of their generation.

A frontier reward can starve mastered families of signal. That by itself reduces exposure, not necessarily ability. The hypothesis is that subsequent training overwrites shared productions, old goal cues drift, or the validity/acceptance path drops the remaining viable outputs. The loop cannot reliably tell: it observes solves on what it admits, and a missing family supplies neither a wrong answer nor a learning-progress signal. Two generators with identical admitted frontier distributions can have different old-task capabilities. Conversely, a solver that passes an archive may simply be remembering it. Use fresh external items to test it.

For each old semantic family, distinguish four quantities: **exposure** (its share among normal admitted tasks), **reachability** (probability of at least one fresh valid instance within a fixed number of raw proposals when asked via an old goal cue), **breadth** (coverage of hidden semantic subcases at that same budget), and **solve** (accuracy on independent fresh old-family instances). Report raw validity and admission separately: a generator can reach a family that its gate rejects. In a diagnosis-only arm, swap the old cue encoder, proposal head, or gate one at a time; charge deployed methods for any components they retain. Classify low exposure with intact reachability as disuse, not generator-side incapacity. Low reachability with intact solve is the FCU9 case; low solve with intact reachability is solver-side forgetting. A diagnostic ceiling with a larger search budget can distinguish rarely reachable from structurally unreachable in the toy, but finite failed sampling does not prove zero support in general.

## Common first-test protocol

- Use a tiny categorical or linear-softmax proposer and tabular or linear solver over two enumerable toy domains, e.g. symbolic string transformations and small arithmetic expression puzzles. Specify families by independently executable semantics, not prompt words. Pretrain on old families, measure all four quantities, train on interfering new ones, then test midstream, after the shift, and after a further shift without repair. Use paired worlds and hold out seeds, witnesses, semantic subcases, and transition orders. Include generator-only, solver-only, gate-only, and shared-state shifts; include a no-forgetting arm that only changes the sampler. Planted faults test localization, not whether natural forgetting happens; require separate ordinary-update runs, and drop the main premise if those show only disuse. Match initial reachability, solver difficulty and margins across families before interpreting forgetting order.
- The first tests below can each use the smallest applicable slice of that protocol. Each **old-task check** adds a stressor; it still includes fresh, independent solver accuracy on all old families, plus worst-family generator reachability and breadth on unseen instances. Never credit archive recitation or a paraphrase of a seen instance as fresh. Probe with hidden evaluator-only witnesses and report invalid, duplicate, rejected, and abstained attempts in the denominator. Include new-family learning and proposal quality; freezing a competent old proposer is a control, not a win if nothing new can be learned.
- Budget-match bytes (including witnesses, validators, checkpoints, learned state and optimizer state), generation attempts, verifier/solver calls, training updates and search work. Add ordinary self-play, uniform old-family archive prompts, fixed archive replay, a frozen old checkpoint when it fits, and FCU9-style localized repair as applicable. Include generative replay in which the previous generator supplies old-instance training data to **both** roles. Under oracle family IDs, compare a quota or round-robin scheduler; where IDs are hidden, do not lend them to one arm alone. Use PLR-style revisits and R-Diverse-style skill diversity at the same cost where they are plausible ties. Compare utility and retention on a cost frontier when costs cannot all be matched.

## Prior-art and kill screen

Abstract-level checks (HF Papers and arXiv), not novelty clearance:

- [Absolute Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335) uses one model in proposer and solver roles with executable validation; [Propose, Solve, Verify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18160) uses formal verification for task generation and solver training; [R-Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004) trains a separate challenger toward the solver's frontier. Neither dual roles nor executable checks are new.
- [R-Diverse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13103) distinguishes semantic diversity from surface variation and discourages recycling across rounds. [SESA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29468) closes a loop from the solver's skill memory to the future task distribution. Semantic coverage and curriculum–memory feedback are not novelty claims by themselves.
- [Deep Generative Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690) maintains an old-data source alongside a solver; [Lesort et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09111) already study forgetting in continual generators under external streams. The question here is loss of *conditional, novel task generation* coupled to self-play while an independently tested solver still passes.
- [PAIRED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02096) uses two policies' return gap to drive environment design; [POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753) evolves environment–solver pairs and transfers solutions; [Prioritized Level Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03934) schedules previously seen levels; [Teacher–Student](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00183) and [ALP-GMM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07224) select practice for learning progress.

The [idea ledger (PR #6)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/bcc59e7ea5ecf4b96dca83b7d4fb9dc716dd2804/reviews/idea-ledger.md) already kills **Learning-progress self-curricula**, **Replay-deadline option**, and **Delay-compensated retention gating**: no new scheduler here. **Self-quizzing replay (#11)** found no edge from who writes matched questions; none of these cards rests on generator identity. **Functional syndromes** found diffuse damage and no advantage over a dense sketch or raw replay: no sparse-decoding assumption. **Composition contracts** and **Counterexample-guided lesson preconditions** were killed: the objects below are executable generation or diagnostic operations, not compatibility metadata or revised lessons. **Reverse grokking** and **Cooldown facts** make matched margin/frequency mandatory for claims about the order of loss. The cards are primitive candidates; when a same-budget control ties, keep the simpler method.

## Raw ideas

### A. Recover the ability to ask, not a copy of the question

1. **Inverse-witness synthesis.** **Mechanism:** Turn a checker for an old behavior into constraints on the current proposer's production rules: search for a rule edit that satisfies a positive witness and rejects near-misses without seeing any old task text. **Why it might be new:** FCU9 calls for reteaching a missing production; CEGIS synthesizes programs against specs, but the proposed operation is witness-to-*edit* on a live task generator that must keep its new semantics. **Cheapest first test:** In an enumerable string grammar, hide historical examples and search for small edits from partial input/output witnesses after an ordinary conflicting update. **Old-task check:** Generate fresh old strings from held-out starts without the witness at inference; test unedited old families too. **Control that could tie:** Direct constraint enumeration to supply synthetic replay, with the same witness and search budget.

2. **Freshness algebra.** **Mechanism:** Store meaning-preserving rewrite operators on a family and a separate set of meaning-changing operators that preserve family membership. Require fresh instances to leave the old example's semantic equivalence class, not just its text form. **Why it might be new:** R-Diverse distinguishes surface from skill diversity; this makes the distinction executable and allows a family to be transported by operators rather than remembered as a cluster. **Cheapest first test:** Arithmetic puzzles with variable renaming versus changes to dependency graphs; check whether learned rewrites can reach unseen graphs. **Old-task check:** Count distinct held-out graph classes per old family and check solve on externally generated graphs. **Control that could tie:** Skill-aware deduplication plus conventional grammar fuzzing with the same examples, executions and stored bytes.

3. **Counterexample basis for missing productions.** **Mechanism:** When multiple single- or multi-production losses explain the same validity failures, actively search for goal requests whose acceptance signatures separate the rival loss sets; output an ambiguity set when they cannot be separated. **Why it might be new:** Grammar debugging and FCU9 can detect failure; the object here is a set of discriminating requests that identifies which repair is justified, without assuming sparse damage (ledger: Functional syndromes). **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a small grammar with overlapping derivations, compare localized repairs after same-size probe sets and include diffuse multi-rule changes. **Old-task check:** Apply only identifiable repairs; measure recovery of unseen old subcases and collateral loss on old families not used by the probes. **Control that could tie:** Greedy failure-coverage requests plus direct rule ablations, charged for every fork.

4. **Deleted-symbol migration on derivations.** **Mechanism:** When the latent task language changes, learn a partial translator from old derivation trees to new derivations that preserves executable behavior and can explicitly report unrepresentable old cases. **Why it might be new:** The parent's goal-code anchoring holds codes fixed; grammar migration instead carries the generating relation across a changed vocabulary, not just a stable address or compatibility label (ledger: Composition contracts). **Cheapest first test:** Split and merge production symbols in a small compositional grammar with some expressivity genuinely removed; fit translation from a few behavioral anchors. **Old-task check:** Translate unseen old derivations into fresh instances and score false claims of preservation, plus intact old families. **Control that could tie:** Keep the old decoder under the same total memory cap, or reparse every anchor in the new grammar and retrain on those parses.

5. **Task-family residues.** **Mechanism:** Factor a retired family relative to the live generator: save the smallest *executable patch* that supplies old distinctions the live grammar cannot express, and regenerate the rest from current productions. **Why it might be new:** Generative replay stores a generator or its samples; the candidate object is the residual task language, which can be recomputed as the base grammar improves. **Cheapest first test:** Remove two shared operators in small string grammars, then sweep the byte cap and amount of real overlap, including zero overlap. **Old-task check:** Hide whole combinations of old operators; require reconstruction without stored instances and track native reachability separately from fallback service. **Control that could tie:** A compressed frozen old grammar plus shared dictionary/Huffman coding and a normal versioned fallback.

### B. Separate the failure mechanisms

6. **Train-time role seam.** **Mechanism:** For shared-model self-play, fork the *same* update into proposer-only, solver-only and combined gradients; use nonadditive damage on matched old tasks to choose a separable role interface rather than merely turning down the learning rate. **Why it might be new:** Absolute Zero shares weights and R-Zero separates models; this seeks a measured interaction that specifies which interface must be separated and when, not a fixed split. **Cheapest first test:** Two heads on a one- or two-dimensional bottleneck with shared semantics, sweeping role overlap and matched initial margins. **Old-task check:** Test both roles after subsequent jointly trained tasks, including old families absent from the fork probes. **Control that could tie:** Fixed role adapters or gradient projection with the same parameters, compute and probe labels.

7. **Admission counterfactuals.** **Mechanism:** Shadow-score raw proposals through both the old and live verifier, then use disagreement on independent semantic witnesses to distinguish validator drift from generator drift and to repair only the failing boundary. **Why it might be new:** Absolute Zero uses executors to verify proposed tasks and FCU9 separates generator from solver; the extra object is a version-crossed observation mapping that can explain apparent loss even when both roles still work. **Cheapest first test:** Insert verifier version changes, including one valid spec evolution and one regression, while some proposer runs independently drift. **Old-task check:** Recover admission of fresh, semantically valid old tasks without readmitting genuinely invalid ones; report raw generation separately. **Control that could tie:** Direct semantic unit-test replay and a fixed verified fallback gate with the same number of checks.

8. **Forced-prefix tomography.** **Mechanism:** Intervene at different points in a derivation with externally provided prefixes to distinguish inability to enter the right branch from inability to finish it; train the smallest upstream or downstream component consistent with both observations. **Why it might be new:** Constrained decoding typically imposes validity at use time; this uses graded intervention to diagnose a broken stage, then requires unconstrained recovery. **Cheapest first test:** Use a two-stage sampler with matched raw old-family success but different entry and completion faults; hide fault locations and include natural shifts. **Old-task check:** Evaluate from the usual goal cue with no supplied prefix on hidden tails, before and after one further shift. **Control that could tie:** Random prefix-completion drills plus uniform end-to-end replay under equal tokens and calls.

9. **Generator credit with exposure pinned.** **Mechanism:** Train a proposer with a shadow evaluation that contrasts solver improvement under a generated practice block against a block with the same family histogram and initial difficulty, but different semantic subcases. Attribute only the residual improvement to the generator. **Why it might be new:** PAIRED contrasts policies, and learning progress can reward revisiting the same lost skill; this contrast isolates the generator's choice *within* a fixed exposure schedule. **Cheapest first test:** Tabular learners with benign and shortcut-heavy instances sharing family labels; replay paired blocks from identical solver snapshots. **Old-task check:** Require independent accuracy on withheld shortcut-breaking subcases and retained generation of them, not just higher gains on the practiced block. **Control that could tie:** R-Diverse-style skill sampling plus family-balanced, difficulty-matched uniform subcase selection with the same shadow rollouts.

10. **Model-free separability witnesses.** **Mechanism:** Generate task pairs whose public cues are identical but whose verifiers require different old reasoning, then seek the minimal additional cue that causally restores conditional generation. Otherwise label the families unaddressable under the current interface. **Why it might be new:** Goal-code anchoring keeps an address stable; this tests whether a valid address can exist at all under the allowed interface and discovers information the generator must be given. **Cheapest first test:** Two old query languages with colliding cues and different latent semantics; allow a one-bit repair, including a negative control in which no permitted bit distinguishes them. **Old-task check:** Test on unseen alternate phrasings and both sides of each collision; score mistaken cross-family substitutions. **Control that could tie:** Supervised family classification with a learned or human-supplied tag under the same cue-bit and label budget.

### C. Make preservation a property of the task language

11. **Conservative task-language extension.** **Mechanism:** When adding a new production, search for an extension of the grammar that preserves the meanings of all old reachable derivations up to an explicit bound; return a counterexample if none fits the capacity cap. **Why it might be new:** POET adds new environments, and grammar extension is familiar in programming languages; the candidate operation makes semantic noninterference a learned curriculum action while the live generator must still find the derivations. **Cheapest first test:** Add operators to tiny bounded grammars with overloads that can shadow old ones; compare exact bounded checks to empirical ones. **Old-task check:** Check full live reachability separately from formal validity, including old cases beyond the proved bound. **Control that could tie:** Separate versioned namespaces with a shared decoder, plus random validation at equal cost.

12. **Reversible novelty operators.** **Mechanism:** Learn paired transformations that extend an old task into a new one and recover its old core from a fresh extension, with operationally checked meaning on both legs. Keep the recovered old core callable without replaying the source. **Why it might be new:** POET uses forward stepping stones and solution transfer; the backward map makes continued exploration a possible source of old tasks. Round-trip consistency alone is not a win (ledger: Cycle-consistency lesson scoring). **Cheapest first test:** Add independent nuisance constraints to arithmetic puzzles, then introduce a shift that reuses their structure; hold out extension forms. **Old-task check:** Recover distinct old semantic instances from unseen extensions without task history, and measure native cue-conditioned service separately. **Control that could tie:** A standard generative replay grammar plus augmentations, with the same operators, state size and calls.

13. **Semantic fracture maps.** **Mechanism:** Maintain distinguishability of family pairs under transformations by tracking which minimal edits flip the verifier. If a generator merges families, train it on the boundary edits rather than on easy representatives of their interiors. **Why it might be new:** R-Diverse distinguishes semantic clusters; a fracture map preserves the local counterfactual that separates two clusters even when marginal examples and success rates look fine. **Cheapest first test:** Build near-identical string puzzles whose solutions differ only with an order-sensitive condition; hide the critical boundaries during the shift. **Old-task check:** Generate and solve fresh examples on both sides of unseen boundary edits, with matched difficulty. **Control that could tie:** Standard hard-negative mining and semantic contrastive replay with the same number of labels.

14. **Composable generation obligations.** **Mechanism:** Preserve not just a primitive's own task family but its ability to generate under an independently supplied context, e.g. requiring an old constraint to be embedded inside a new expression without becoming vacuous. **Why it might be new:** Test composition itself is familiar in property-based testing; this targets generator competence under unseen composition, not the killed Composition contracts card's compatibility metadata. **Cheapest first test:** Train a bounded generator on atoms, then on new pairs that reuse atoms in shortcut contexts. Search for a use that forces the old atom to matter. **Old-task check:** Ask for unseen old–old and old–new combinations; require verifier sensitivity when the old atom is removed, plus standalone old competence. **Control that could tie:** Grammar-based combinatorial testing and balanced replay of atomic tasks at equal solver and verifier calls.

15. **Proof-carrying negative space.** **Mechanism:** Carry small constructive counterexamples to proposed *equivalences* between families, and train a generator to produce paired instances that witness the difference even after task aliases merge. **Why it might be new:** Semantic deduplication (R-Diverse) prevents redundant output; here a refutation is a retained task-generation object, used to stop false semantic deduplication. **Cheapest first test:** Use predicates identical on shallow expressions but inequivalent at higher depth; hold out which pair is merged and learn from the refutations. **Old-task check:** Generate fresh paired witnesses at unseen depths and check individual old families, including genuinely equivalent aliases that should not consume distinct budget. **Control that could tie:** Store discriminating example pairs and train a contrastive semantic classifier using the same bytes and calls.

16. **Independent validity witnesses that can expire.** **Mechanism:** Keep multiple disagreeing executable specs for an old family, retain test inputs where they agree, and expose ambiguous requests when independent interpretations diverge after a tool or language change. Revise the generation interface only after a separate semantic adjudication. **Why it might be new:** Executable reward (Absolute Zero) presumes an authority; here the task-generation object includes a set-valued semantics under verifier uncertainty, not a single old verifier to replay. **Cheapest first test:** Simulate two independent checkers and both shared and independent spec bugs in a small arithmetic DSL; use a hidden reference. **Old-task check:** Report verified old breadth and solve on hidden-reference tasks, counting abstention against service and false agreements against spec accuracy. **Control that could tie:** A frozen reference verifier, or majority-vote verifier ensemble with fixed abstention, at matched implementation and execution cost.

### D. Change what the generator can borrow or spend

17. **Solver-to-proposer construction bridge.** **Mechanism:** Extract reusable, typed intermediate proof states from solving externally authored old tasks, then learn inverse constructors that assemble *new* old tasks around those states. Use the solver as a source of constraints, not as a teacher that rewrites replay. **Why it might be new:** Hindsight relabeling and self-quizzing turn existing traces into examples; the candidate is a constructor that transfers surviving solver knowledge to unreachable generator branches without preserving the starting questions. **Cheapest first test:** A tabular symbolic solver exposes proof traces for a few old puzzles; rebuild task generation after a shift and match access to traces in all arms. **Old-task check:** Generate valid tasks with unseen premises and proof shapes; verify solver accuracy on a separate old test set. **Control that could tie:** Answer-first problem synthesis and trace-conditioned replay with the same trace tokens and verifier calls.

18. **Cross-host task transplants.** **Mechanism:** Export an old family by compiling its operational constraints into a different toy host, run a new generator there, then import the *construction recipe* rather than host-specific outputs. **Why it might be new:** POET transfers solutions between environments; this transfers task-producing operations and tests whether the generator's old competence was bound to its native representation. **Cheapest first test:** Encode the same small relations as list programs and grid puzzles, shift the source host, and test a transplant through the other encoding. **Old-task check:** Generate fresh old-source instances through the current source interface, including held-out relation combinations and families with no analog in the donor. **Control that could tie:** Direct cross-host example translation followed by ordinary replay, with the same translators and executions.

19. **Physically separate blind spots.** **Mechanism:** Give two small proposers distinct observation routes to old-family evidence; choose routes by whether one can construct a valid old task when the other route is removed, not by disagreement in their usual outputs. **Why it might be new:** POET and ensemble diversity give multiple generators; here the preservation target is complementary access to evidence, as in FCU7, conditioned on an explicit outage model rather than independence by assumption. **Cheapest first test:** Give two toy generators different admissible construction inputs; remove a local channel and, separately, a shared source. Include a centralized implementation allowed all the same legal observations. **Old-task check:** Report fresh old-family service during each outage and false success when both channels share a blind spot. **Control that could tie:** A single generator with both channels and dropout, or replicated generators at identical total capacity and observation cost.

20. **Proposal search as a disambiguation currency.** **Mechanism:** Spend extra raw proposals only on goal requests for which more samples could distinguish low conditional mass from a missing constructor; spend structural probes instead when additional samples cannot resolve the ambiguity under the cap. **Why it might be new:** FCU10 buys a canary to choose practice; here the experiment chooses between a probabilistic explanation and a structural one with different generator repairs. No theorem of zero support from samples alone. **Cheapest first test:** Use tiny grammars where very rare and impossible old subcases have similar observed failures; reveal ground truth only for scoring. **Old-task check:** Compare post-treatment fresh service for rare and structurally lost old families and count harmful repairs of merely rare ones. **Control that could tie:** Uniform budget splits between extra sampling and direct production probes, plus standard sequential probability testing.

21. **Amortized cold-start contract.** **Mechanism:** Let the system deliberately remove a generator branch only if a bounded reconstruction procedure from a witness can recover fresh breadth without the solver becoming the task author. The contract specifies latency, retained information and what is lost under another shift. **Why it might be new:** PLR preserves levels and checkpoints preserve generators; the candidate is a service guarantee for reconstructing a family after deleting its implementation, not a replay deadline or a prediction of forgetting. **Cheapest first test:** Generate small families with different compressibility, retire some branches under a memory cap, and measure worst-case recovery on unseen retirement orders. **Old-task check:** Score reachability before repair, service within the stated cap, novel breadth after repair and after another shift, plus solver retention throughout. **Control that could tie:** Save/restore or per-family compressed grammar with the same byte and billed recovery limits; abandon the contract if it just repackages those.

22. **Exchangeable elementary tasks as a bottleneck test.** **Mechanism:** Replace a subset of old tasks by a different generating basis only when each basis can reconstruct the other's *fresh* semantics under a bounded interpreter; use the replacement to free capacity for new operators. **Why it might be new:** POET keeps stepping stones and FCU11 uses surrogate practice to preserve solver obligations; this exchanges generating bases, testing correspondence in both directions instead of positive transfer to a solver. **Cheapest first test:** In finite algebraic puzzle families with alternative bases, hide necessary compositions and include a noninvertible substitution. **Old-task check:** Verify each old family's fresh reachability under the new basis, not just new-basis validity, and test pre-exchange solver competence independently. **Control that could tie:** Ordinary grammar compression and compiler minimization with the same interpreter size and synthesis work.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Which observation makes generator-side incapacity identifiable apart from disuse, a bad cue, or gate drift?** Seek a minimal separating set of interventions under a fixed call budget; benchmark the no-forgetting and indistinguishable negative controls before attributing anything to a missing production. Cards 3, 7, 8, 10, 20.
2. **How much of a task family can an executable witness reconstruct without task examples?** Characterize when witnesses underdetermine held-out breadth, and when a small residual or bridge closes the gap over equal-budget replay. Cards 1, 5, 17, 21.
3. **Can the task alphabet change without silently changing what old goals mean or how they compose?** Separate valid migration from preserved conditional reachability and require unseen compositions, not just a finite suite of restored prompts. Cards 2, 4, 11–15, 22.
4. **Which parts of co-training cause irreversible loss, rather than merely moving the curriculum frontier?** If proposer–solver interaction or shared evidence matters, an intervention should preserve old generation under ordinary later updates while the new-task curve stays competitive. If a fixed split or quota ties, keep the simpler baseline. Cards 6, 9, 16, 18, 19.
