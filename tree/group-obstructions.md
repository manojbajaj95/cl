# Group obstructions and slot relations: break higher-order conflicts without restoring raw state

**Status:** Ideas only; no experiments, code, or GPU work. **Parent:** [Deletion licenses](deletion-licenses.md) ([PR #190](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/190)), cards 09 and 14. **Review seeds:** [Flood triage row 297](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/433078891ef76ae1040c9c539cd48c3b5eaf5aba/reviews/flood-triage.md#L297). **Ledger read first:** [Idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/433078891ef76ae1040c9c539cd48c3b5eaf5aba/reviews/idea-ledger.md). The two seeds are starting points, not new contributions: store a proof of the smallest group of histories with no shared low-regret reply when every pair has one, buying only observations that break the group; and reacquire one certified relation between optimizer slots instead of their values, with a safe fallback.

## What would make this a continual-learning primitive?

**Not finding an unsatisfiable core or evaluating a relational abstract domain.** Minimal unsatisfiable subsets ([MUS enumeration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09001)), minimal hitting-set diagnosis ([first-principles diagnosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16180), [ASP hitting sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09194)), equivalence-class active learning ([EC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091)), [CROWN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00866) / [relational neural verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13118), [Craig interpolation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08532) and [counterexample-guided neural repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11342) are established. The candidate is a **portable higher-order obstruction and slot-relation recovery interface** that works *after* raw histories and reply tables are deleted: a blind editor knows *which* minimal `k`-tuple (`k ≥ 3`) of compatible histories defeats every shared low-regret reply, buys only a lawful observation—or a single certified coupling predicate over live optimizer slots, never raw slot values—that shatters that `k`-tuple, and requires a fresh proof in every resulting answer cell.

### Why pairs miss higher-order obstructions

- Let `H` be the live histories still compatible with retained state, and for each `h ∈ H`, let `R(h)` be the set of replies that satisfy hard old-task A/C obligations at every served prefix and meet the declared regret bound on `h`. A common disposition exists iff `∩_{h ∈ H} R(h) ≠ ∅`.
- Pairwise counterexample search checks only pairs `(h_i, h_j)` with `R(h_i) ∩ R(h_j) = ∅`. When the Helly number of `{R(h) : h ∈ H}` exceeds `2`, every pair can share a valid reply while a minimal `k`-tuple (`k ≥ 3`) satisfies `∩_{i=1}^k R(h_i) = ∅` and `∩_{i ∈ S} R(h_i) ≠ ∅` for every proper subset `S ⊂ {1, …, k}`.
- Carrying a compact separation proof of that minimal `k`-obstruction lets a blind editor reject observations that only split non-obstructive pairs and buy only an observation (or adaptive bundle) whose answer partition strictly breaks the `k`-hyperedge. Breaking one `k`-obstruction never certifies completeness: every resulting answer cell must independently earn a fresh common-reply proof or take the prechecked safe fallback, because an apparent split can leave a second overlapping `k'`-obstruction intact.

### What a certified slot relation can and cannot replace

- When a later edit introduces an optimizer-mediated coupling across deleted history, reacquiring raw momentum, second-moment, or preconditioner slots defeats deletion and leaks unused state. Instead, the editor queries a lawful shadow checker for one certified coupling predicate `P(s_1, …, s_m)` over **live** optimizer slots (with declared cost, bit budget, freshness binding, and soundness scope).
- Keep only `P` if it discharges the active obstruction proof and forbid access to raw slot values; if `P` is ambiguous, stale, or unavailable before the deadline, execute the prechecked safe fallback (or a certified minimal slot reset). A predicate computed on a cached pre-step slot is invalid even when current model outputs agree, because the next optimizer step can still diverge on a protected C slice.

**Paper case.** Let three compatible histories `h_1, h_2, h_3` admit safe low-regret reply sets `R(h_1) = {a, b}`, `R(h_2) = {b, c}`, and `R(h_3) = {a, c}`, where each excluded reply violates a distinct protected old task (`C_1, C_2, C_3`). Every pair shares a safe reply (`b`, `c`, or `a`), so pair-guided recovery sees no blocking pair, yet the triple shares none. Realize `h_1, h_2, h_3` by two live optimizer slots `(m_1, m_2)` whose individual signs are uninformative (or forbidden to export), while one certified coupling predicate `P(m_1, m_2) := (m_1 + m_2 > 0)` separates `{h_1}` from `{h_2, h_3}`: cell `P = 1` admits `a` (or `b`) and cell `P = 0` admits `c`, both independently verified on A and C without exposing `(m_1, m_2)`. Now add a fourth history `h_4` with `R(h_4) = {a, b}` so `{h_2, h_3, h_4}` forms a second 3-obstruction: an observation that isolates `h_1` breaks the first triple yet leaves the `P = 0` cell obstructed, proving why cell-level recertification is mandatory. Finally, replace the live shadow checker with one reading a one-step-stale momentum copy: `P` passes while the next update breaks `C_2`.

### Controls and verdict

Give every arm the same frozen jobs, reply menu, verifier, fallback, deadlines, and byte/query caps. Compare: (1) pair-guided recovery, (2) exact MUS/core-guided recovery with full table access (ceiling), (3) exhaustive diagnosis, (4) an apparent-split trap leaving a second obstruction, (5) MNS6 coalition search, (6) raw slot reacquisition, (7) exact CEGAR, (8) CROWN-style affine bounds on the slots, (9) `#185#20` impossibility witnesses, (10) a stale-moment negative, and (11) the strongest stack of those baselines at matched query bytes and retained bytes. Score retained bytes, peak bytes, query bits, checking cost, admitted utility, refusal rate, false approvals, and independent ordinary A/C (including rare slices) at every served prefix.

**Conditional verdict:** Keep the primitive claim only if compact `k`-obstruction certificates and/or certified slot-relation queries beat the combined baseline (pair-guided + exact core-guided + CROWN/raw-slot + CEGAR) at matched admitted utility, query bytes, and A/C retention after real deletion. A faster MUS solver, a tighter affine box, or a refusal-heavy fallback is not enough. **P** = conditional new reusable object or operation; **I** = improvement or audit, not a pilot recommendation. All tests below are paper or tiny CPU proposals, not results.

## Idea cards

### Higher-order group obstructions

#### 01. Store the smallest non-pairwise obstruction, not the reply table (P; parent 09)
- **Mechanism:** Retain a compact separation proof of a minimal `k`-tuple (`k ≥ 3`) of compatible histories with empty joint reply intersection while every pair intersects. Buy only observations that strictly split the `k`-tuple, and require an independently checked reply or fallback proof in every resulting answer cell.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [MUS enumeration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09001) and [counterexample-guided repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11342) already extract minimal conflicts. The candidate is a portable obstruction object used to choose lawful observations *after* the original reply table is deleted, with no pairwise-completeness assumption.
- **Cheapest first test:** The 3-history `{a,b}, {b,c}, {a,c}` paper case plus the 4-history apparent-split trap. Compare pair-guided recovery, exact core-guided recovery, exhaustive diagnosis, and MNS6 coalition search at matched query bytes.
- **Old-task retention check:** Bind each forbidden reply to a distinct C violation (`C_1, C_2, C_3`); verify ordinary A and all three C slices in every resulting answer cell and on the refusal path.

#### 02. Certify Helly rank as a deletion invariant (P)
- **Mechanism:** Attach to a deletion license a proved upper bound `h` on the Helly number of the live feasible-reply family (e.g., `h = 2` when feasible reply sets are intervals/trees, authorizing pairwise checks; `h = 3` when triples can obstruct). A new edit checks whether its constraints preserve `h` before paying for higher-order search, escalating arity only when `h` jumps.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Combinatorial Helly theorems characterize intersection families and [MUS enumeration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09001) searches conflicts directly. The candidate turns the Helly number of feasible repairs into a checkable deletion invariant that gates pairwise vs `k`-wise recovery.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two finite edit families: one with interval-structured feasible replies (`h = 2`) and one adding a single non-convex constraint (`h = 3`). Compare always-pairwise, always-triple, exact core-guided recovery, and Helly-gated switching at matched checking cost.
- **Old-task retention check:** Plant an edit that silently breaks interval structure by excluding a middle reply on a rare C slice; verify the invariant check catches the `h: 2 → 3` jump before any C-unsafe reply ships.

#### 03. Minimal hitting-set queries over overlapping obstruction hyperedges (P)
- **Mechanism:** When several `k`-obstructions overlap, maintain their active minimal `k`-hyperedges and select one lawful observation whose answer partition cuts *every* active hyperedge simultaneously (a 2-coloring/hitting split of the hypergraph), instead of resolving one core per round.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [ASP minimal hitting sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09194) and [EC2 active learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091) cut pairwise edges between equivalence classes. Here the target is `k`-ary hyperedges (`k ≥ 3`) whose pairwise projections are all feasible, avoiding multi-round apparent-split traps.
- **Cheapest first test:** Four histories with two overlapping 3-obstructions `{h_1,h_2,h_3}` and `{h_2,h_3,h_4}` and two candidate 1-bit probes (one breaks only the first triple; one breaks both). Compare EC2 pairwise weighting, sequential core-guided recovery, and hyperedge-hitting selection.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A and all four C slices in both answer cells of the winning 1-bit probe, and confirm that the sequential baseline either pays a second query or fails C in the residual cell.

#### 04. Obstruction-lifting certificates across a new edit (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a new edit updates the reply menu or cost plane, run a compact lifting check on the stored `k`-obstruction to certify whether it shrinks to a `(k-1)`-obstruction (unlocking cheaper pairwise probes), persists at arity `k`, or inflates to `k+1`, without reconstructing the deleted history table.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Dynamic implicit hitting sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24214) update transversals as constraints arrive. The candidate certifies *arity transitions* of a stored conflict across continual updates using only the obstruction proof and the new edit's local delta.
- **Cheapest first test:** A 3-obstruction subjected to three successive edits: one tightens a single history's reply set (collapsing to a 2-obstruction), one is neutral, and one splits a reply into two guarded variants (inflating to 4). Compare full core recomputation and lifting checks at matched bytes.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check A/C after each transition; include a deceptive tightening that drops a reply only on A while leaving the C-induced 3-obstruction intact.

#### 05. Asymmetric 1-vs-(k-1) splits exploiting minimal-core geometry (P)
- **Mechanism:** By minimality of a `k`-obstruction, removing *any single* history leaves a `(k-1)`-tuple that already admits a common safe reply. Prefer a cheap asymmetric observation isolating one spoiler history `h_i` over a pricier balanced partition, even when the asymmetric probe has strictly lower Shannon entropy.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [EC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091) and information-gain active learning favor balanced splits. Exploiting minimal-core geometry shows a low-entropy 1-vs-(k-1) test can resolve a `k`-obstruction in one step where balanced probes leave `k` intact or cost more.
- **Cheapest first test:** A 4-obstruction (every triple shares a reply; the quartet does not) with one cheap 1-vs-3 probe (0.81 bits) and one pricier 2-vs-2 probe (1.0 bit) that leaves a secondary conflict. Compare entropy sampling, EC2, exact core-guided recovery, and spoiler isolation.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A/C for the singleton cell and for the common reply of the `(k-1)` cell; include a trap where a fifth history hides inside the `(k-1)` cell and must be caught by cell recertification.

#### 06. Pairwise-blindness audit: isolate when pair-guided repair loops or over-refuses (I)
- **Mechanism:** Build a matched synthetic benchmark of `k = 2` versus `k = 3, 4` reply hypergraphs with identical reply-set cardinalities and pairwise overlap densities, measuring false common-reply approvals, non-blocking query loops, and excess refusals under pair-guided CEGAR versus `k`-core recovery.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [First-principles diagnosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16180) and MNS6 coalition audits already note non-pairwise conflicts. This is a controlled ablation isolating whether `k ≥ 3` obstructions actually separate methods at equal byte and query budgets.
- **Cheapest first test:** 20 generated 6-history finite games matched on `|R(h)| = 3`, half with `h = 2` and half with `h = 3`. Compare pair-guided CEGAR, exact MUS recovery, and random-probe baselines.
- **Old-task retention check:** Attach distinct rare-slice C failures to every reply outside `R(h)` so any false approval by pair-guided CEGAR registers immediately as a C drop.

#### 07. Fractional obstruction duals as online query-budget impossibility gates (I)
- **Mechanism:** Store a fractional packing of disjoint or weakly overlapping `k`-obstructions (the LP dual of minimal hypergraph hitting) to certify a sound lower bound on the minimum observation bits needed to eliminate all obstructions; refuse immediately without querying when the remaining budget or deadline falls below the dual bound.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Minimal-transversal lower bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28012) analyze offline hypergraph complexity. This audit uses the dual packing number as an online zero-query refusal gate after deletion.
- **Cheapest first test:** Six histories containing two vertex-disjoint 3-obstructions under a 1-bit query cap (dual bound requires `≥ 2` bits if probes only split one core at a time). Compare greedy querying, exact exhaustive search, and dual-gated early refusal.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A/C remains untouched on the zero-query refusal path, and test a positive control where one global 1-bit probe hits both disjoint cores simultaneously so the dual must account for probe fan-out.

#### 08. Pierce a k-obstruction by synthesizing one new reply instead of querying (P)
- **Mechanism:** Treat a `k`-obstruction dualistically: instead of paying for an observation to split `{h_1, …, h_k}`, check whether synthesizing *one* new verified reply `d` (e.g., a guarded blend or abstention-free composite of `{a, b, c}`) satisfies `d ∈ ∩_{i=1}^k R(h_i)` and dissolves the obstruction with zero observations, choosing between synthesis and querying by total certified cost.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Counterexample-guided neural repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11342) repairs a single failing trace and [coverage sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0590) assume a fixed policy menu. Here the `k`-obstruction certificate directly specifies the exact `k` constraints a zero-query piercing reply must simultaneously satisfy.
- **Cheapest first test:** The 3-history `{a,b}, {b,c}, {a,c}` paper case with a bounded synthesis operator that can build a fourth reply `d` at cost `s`, swept against observation tariff `q`. Compare query-only recovery, synthesis-only repair, and dual cost-optimal choice.
- **Old-task retention check:** Independently verify `d` on A and all three C slices (`C_1, C_2, C_3`), rejecting any synthesized `d` that passes the three history losses by silently degrading an unqueried rare C slice.

### Certified optimizer-slot relations without raw values

#### 09. Reacquire one certified coupling predicate over live optimizer slots (P; parent 14)
- **Mechanism:** Ask a lawful shadow checker for a single certified coupling predicate `P(s_1, …, s_m)` over live optimizer slots. Retain only `P` if it discharges the active proof and forbid access to raw slot values; if `P` is ambiguous, unavailable, or fails freshness, take the prechecked safe fallback.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [CROWN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00866) certifies properties via affine bounds over inputs or activations. The candidate is a source-restricted relational query at a blind editor's proof boundary that discharges deletion-license obligations without exporting raw optimizer state.
- **Cheapest first test:** A tiny two-slot momentum learner where `sign(m_1 + m_2)` determines whether a second update lands safely. Compare raw slot reacquisition, exact CEGAR, CROWN-style affine bounds on the slots, `#185#20` impossibility witnesses, and a stale-moment negative at matched query bytes.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check A and a held-out C slice after both optimizer steps, where the C slice fails under the wrong branch even though current model outputs and solo slot signs agree.

#### 10. Interpolant-derived slot predicates from failed deletion-license proofs (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a blind edit fails a deletion-license proof, compute a Craig interpolant `I(s)` between the live optimizer step semantics and the safety/regret violation formula of the incumbent, then query the shadow checker *only* for the truth value of `I(s)` on live slots—synthesizing the minimal task-specific relation rather than picking from a fixed predicate table.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Craig interpolation in program verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08532) extracts loop invariants in static code. Here interpolation runs across the proof-vs-optimizer boundary to mint a 1-bit lawful shadow query that hides raw slot values.
- **Cheapest first test:** A 4-slot linear/ReLU momentum update where only one linear combination `m_1 - 2 m_2 + m_3 > 0` separates safe from C-unsafe next steps. Compare a fixed sign-predicate library, CROWN box bounds, raw slot export, and interpolant-minted 1-bit queries at equal query bits.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check A/C on both branches of `I(s)` and include a C-sensitive direction orthogonal to the regret objective so the interpolant must cover both safety and regret.

#### 11. Gauge-invariant slot relations surviving parameter rescaling and rotation (P)
- **Mechanism:** Restrict certified slot predicates to reparameterization-invariant relations—such as cosine alignment signs between momentum and gradient, or spectral-ratio inequalities under Adam/Muon preconditioning—so function-preserving weight rescaling or neutral transport between edits never invalidates the predicate.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Muon spectral interference analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27518) and [CLAD constrained abstract domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34628) study structured representations. This directly fixes the gauge-dependence failure of raw-weight and raw-slot checks under function-preserving rescaling.
- **Cheapest first test:** A two-layer ReLU learner with Adam/momentum slots; apply a function-preserving layerwise positive rescaling (`W_1 → α W_1, W_2 → W_2 / α`) between two edits. Compare raw-slot affine bounds (CROWN), coordinate-wise sign predicates, and gauge-invariant angle/ratio predicates.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A/C is identical before and after the rescaling and after the subsequent edit; reject any predicate whose truth value flips under the no-op rescaling.

#### 12. Multi-step decay envelopes for a certified slot relation (P)
- **Mechanism:** Pair each certified slot predicate `P(m_t, v_t)` with a sound persistence horizon `τ` computed from the optimizer's decay factors `(β_1, β_2)` and declared per-step gradient norm caps. Later edits within `τ` steps reuse `P` at zero query cost, while any step exceeding the norm cap immediately expires `P` and forces re-query or fallback.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [STABLE drift-controlled CL optimizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10655) and [differentiable restart policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08883) regulate optimizer dynamics directly. The candidate turns decay dynamics into a sound time-to-live lease on a relational predicate, closing the stale-moment gap.
- **Cheapest first test:** A 5-step momentum stream with one small-gradient regime (predicate persists 3 steps) and one spike step (predicate flips at step 2). Compare per-step re-query, unguarded reuse (stale-moment negative), and envelope-leased reuse at matched query budget.
- **Old-task retention check:** Plant a C failure right after the gradient spike; verify the envelope expires `P` before the spike-induced bad step is served, preserving A/C.

#### 13. Expressiveness-vs-bits audit: sign couplings vs octagons vs affine slot predicates (I)
- **Mechanism:** Compare three restricted shadow-checker predicate families—qualitative sign/orthant couplings (`sign(m_i · m_j)`), octagonal difference bounds (`±m_i ± m_j ≤ c`), and general affine inequalities (`a^T m ≤ b`)—at equal query bit budgets against CROWN relaxations and raw slot export.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Fit-LRP polytope relaxations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16003) and [relational B&B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13118) trade domain tightness for compute in offline verification. This is an accounting audit measuring how much predicate expressiveness continual-learning recovery actually needs per bit.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three tiny momentum worlds whose true safe boundary is (a) a sign orthant, (b) an octagon, and (c) a skewed hyperplane. Score query bits, proof-check time, false refusals, and admitted utility across all three predicate classes plus CROWN and raw slots.
- **Old-task retention check:** Keep A/C fixed across all predicate classes; confirm tighter predicate classes never trade away C safety to reduce query bits.

#### 14. Shadow-checker freshness proofs against stale-moment aliasing (I)
- **Mechanism:** Bind every certified slot-relation answer to a monotonic optimizer step index and a lightweight state-transition digest of the last committed update, rejecting any predicate evaluated on a lagged or pre-step shadow buffer even if current network outputs are unchanged.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Pacti](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17751) checks static assume–guarantee interfaces. This is the dedicated freshness audit for row 297's stale-moment negative, isolating silent optimizer-state lag from output drift.
- **Cheapest first test:** Replay the two-slot momentum test with three shadow checkers: live, one-step lagged (matching current outputs), and checkpoint-restored. Compare unchecked predicate acceptance against freshness-bound acceptance and exact CEGAR.
- **Old-task retention check:** Construct the lagged state so current A/C outputs pass identically while the next step breaks C; require the freshness check to force fallback and preserve C.

#### 15. Certified partial slot reset as a constructive fallback when relations fail (P)
- **Mechanism:** When the shadow checker returns `UNKNOWN` or refuses a coupling predicate `P(s_1, …, s_m)`, instead of aborting the edit or exporting raw slots, apply a verified partial reset to the minimal subset of coupled slots (e.g., zeroing one momentum buffer so `m_1 · m_2 = 0`) that makes `P` hold by construction, paying only the bounded adaptation slowdown.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Differentiable optimizer restart](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08883) resets momentum to accelerate optimization, and `#185` slot splices inject target states. Here a zero-information partial reset acts as a *proof-discharging fallback* when relational observation is denied.
- **Cheapest first test:** A 3-slot momentum learner where two slots are coupled and the shadow checker is disabled on half the edits. Compare pure refusal, global optimizer reset, raw slot export, and minimal coupled-slot reset at equal update steps.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A/C immediately after the partial reset and after the subsequent edit; ensure resetting the coupled slot does not destabilize a protected C trajectory that depended on momentum damping.

### Where group obstructions and slot relations meet

#### 16. Break a k-obstruction with a single k-ary slot predicate (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a `k`-obstruction (`k ≥ 3`) arises from `k` coupled optimizer slots, every unary or pairwise slot relation can be uninformative (e.g., cyclic sign order or 3-way parity), whereas one 1-bit `k`-ary coupling predicate over live slots shatters the `k`-core in a single query without exposing any individual slot value or pairwise difference.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Relational B&B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13118) verifies fixed relational specs and [inconsistent-constraint diagnosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09005) does not match predicate arity to conflict arity. Matching shadow-predicate arity to the Helly arity `k` of the obstruction beats both pairwise probes and raw multi-slot export.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three optimizer slots `(m_1, m_2, m_3)` whose 3-way sign product induces the `{a,b}, {b,c}, {a,c}` 3-obstruction, with every unary and pairwise slot marginal uniform. Compare unary probes, pairwise probes, raw 3-slot export, CROWN bounds, and one 1-bit ternary predicate.
- **Old-task retention check:** Map each excluded reply to a distinct C slice (`C_1, C_2, C_3`); check A and all C slices on both answer cells of the ternary predicate.

#### 17. Arity-impossibility witnesses: prove no low-arity slot predicate breaks the k-core (P)
- **Mechanism:** Extend `#185#20` impossibility witnesses to relational arity: export a compact proof showing that every lawful slot predicate of arity `< k` induces identical answer sets across the `k`-obstruction histories, authorizing immediate escalation to an arity-`k` shadow check or safe fallback without wasting low-arity queries.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#185#20` certifies impossibility for sample-only actuators and [transversal lower bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28012) bound hitting-set size. The candidate separates *arity-insufficient* shadow menus from genuinely unobservable states.
- **Cheapest first test:** The 3-slot parity/cyclic-order obstruction under two shadow menus: pairwise-only (impossible) and pairwise-plus-ternary (feasible). Compare exhaustive low-arity search, `#185#20` witnesses, and arity-impossibility receipts across repeated blind edits.
- **Old-task retention check:** Confirm A/C holds throughout early fallback under the pairwise-only menu, and independently verify A/C once a newly authorized ternary predicate invalidates the receipt.

#### 18. Compile a k-obstruction into a bounded-disclosure adaptive binary predicate tree (P)
- **Mechanism:** When the shadow checker forbids `k`-ary predicates and allows only binary predicates, compile a short adaptive sequence of binary slot relations that shatters the `k`-core while proving an explicit residual entropy/non-reconstructibility bound on the raw slot vector, refusing if the sequence would leak raw values.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Counterexample-guided branching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14823) and [EC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091) build adaptive trees for accuracy/verification without a disclosure ceiling. Here the adaptive binary tree is jointly constrained to shatter the `k`-hyperedge *and* keep raw slot values unrecoverable.
- **Cheapest first test:** A 3-slot continuous/quantized momentum space with a 3-obstruction and only binary difference predicates (`m_i - m_j > 0`) lawful. Compare raw slot export, unrestricted binary CEGAR, and disclosure-bounded adaptive binary trees.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A/C in every leaf cell of the adaptive tree and confirm that a single-step interruption mid-tree falls back cleanly without breaking A/C.

#### 19. Shape optimizer-slot couplings during training to cap future Helly arity (P)
- **Mechanism:** During ordinary training before deletion, apply a lightweight relational regularizer (or update filter) that steers live optimizer slots away from orthants known to generate `k ≥ 3` reply obstructions for a declared edit family—collapsing future obstructions to `k ≤ 2` at write time without seeing held-out edit payloads.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [FOGO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10406) and [STABLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10655) stabilize gradients/drift generically. The candidate targets *relational slot geometry* specifically to reduce the Helly arity of future obstructions, making later deletion licenses cheaper to discharge.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two matched-utility training trajectories on a tiny multi-task MLP: unconstrained Adam vs slot-relation-regularized Adam, evaluated on a held-out family of blind edits. Compare future `k`-core frequency, recovery query bits, and exact dual control at equal training budget.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify ordinary A and rare-slice C before and after training and after held-out edits; include a sham regularizer that reduces `k`-cores by collapsing plasticity on a rare C slice.

#### 20. Cell-level recertification carrying the acquired slot predicate as an assumption (P)
- **Mechanism:** After a certified slot predicate `P` splits a `k`-obstruction into cells `C_0, C_1`, inject `P` (or `¬P`) directly as an explicit premise into each cell's fresh completeness proof to test whether a secondary hidden `k'`-obstruction survives inside `C_0` or `C_1`; refuse to issue a cell license until the cell is proved obstruction-free or its residual core is recorded.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [CEGAR](https://doi.org/10.1007/10722167_15) and [Craig interpolation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08532) refine abstractions iteratively. Binding the acquired slot relation into the child cell's *completeness* check directly closes the apparent-split failure mode from row 297.
- **Cheapest first test:** The 4-history two-triple trap (`{h_1,h_2,h_3}` and `{h_2,h_3,h_4}`) where `P` isolates `h_1` into `C_1` and leaves `{h_2,h_3,h_4}` in `C_0`. Compare unchecked post-split dispatch, full replanning, and predicate-carrying cell recertification.
