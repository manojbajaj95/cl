# Sealed random checks: draw useful work after the claim is fixed

**Status:** Ideas only; two paper fixtures, no learner runs or GPU work. **Parent:** [Check as work](check-as-work.md) ([PR #253][parent]), cards **06** and **15**. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 368][triage] scores both 3: seal the uncertainty claim and its backing before a public draw of the checking C opener, with declared detection probability; prefer equally useful C items that move a shared feature used by the defaulter's rare slice, without training on its labels. The [idea ledger][ledger] requires normal old-task retention, matched margins, and a merged mechanism that beats the strongest stack of its parts. Its rare-event distillation and gradient-selection results make replay and gradient matching required controls, not straw baselines.

**Verdict:** Randomizing a useful batch buys a check against a claimant that must choose its history before the draw; it does not make a missed lie safe. Prefer rare-feature work only within the draw laws that meet the declared detection floor, and retire the receipt only once a common continuation serves the rare slice under actual reuse. The open question is whether the useful work can buy both benefits more cheaply than a separate check plus the best safe migration.

## Contract

- **Seal the input, not just the prose.** A = original tasks, Bᵢ = installed writes including the defaulter's B_d, C = installed and successor work, W = paired service, R_d = the defaulter's live receipt. Freeze the claim, obligation, actual pre-state and backing (including relevant slots and clocks), lawful opener menu and timed fallback before using the first eligible unpredictable public draw. Specify who prevents state substitution, clock or menu changes, skipping draws and replaying a seal. Sealing fixes what will be tested, not whether the claim is true. A trusted execution boundary can supply this in a toy; real binding or extra observation is an explicit cost and assumption. Use only independently trustworthy observations already emitted by ordinary C work.
- **Declare power against a fixed harmful set.** Let L contain the reachable pre-states falsifying the claim and breaking a named backed obligation under the declared continuation. Publish the opener law q and decision rule, including report precision, observable nuisance and timeout semantics. Against L, declare β = inf over h in L of Σᵦ q(b) Pr(reject on b | h, b), under a stated noise model (or take worst case over allowed nuisance). Here rejection means the observed ordinary check refutes the claim; a timeout diverts to clearing but is not counted as detection. Also declare an upper bound on honest rejection and report no-result frequency separately. These are conditional guarantees for the frozen menu and a claimant who cannot choose h after the draw. A prior-average rate, nominal menu, or detection rate calculated only among completed draws does not qualify. No retry-until-pass.
- **Protect service on every draw, even a miss.** Each opener must be genuinely needed for C, lawful on all relevant pre-states and within the same A/B/installed-C floors and deadlines. Reserve independent clearing for rejection or no result. The conditional right cannot be spent if any unresolved history can make service unsafe; a pass may suffice for *diagnosis* without authorizing W or release. Do not shrink or discard evidence before the ordinary check reads it. Consume live claim/draw state at the decision boundary; no bespoke durable draw record, including reconstructible historical tags. A pre-existing public beacon or live entitlement store is not a private check log, but charge access and any live pinning. A later consumer needing the historical result is outside the receipt-free variant unless its obligation is discharged by the current service invariant.
- **Test retirement by use, not by deletion alone.** Choose among legal, C-utility-matched items using only lawful pre-draw feature information; no B_d labels, held-out rare examples, receipt replay, or oracle scores during the write or draw. The proxy must name a feature already shared with genuine C duties. After resolving the claim or independently clearing it, require one public continuation to serve A/B/installed C and W across unresolved histories without R_d or its old backing, even when the backing is actually reused. Test actual reuse, installed commonplace and rare B_d slices anchored to acquisition, and a blind lawful suffix including later defaults. If the suffix is finite, state its scope and what safely takes over. A held-out offline intervention may test the claimed feature; it is not an online service step.
- **Match the strongest joint control.** Include the best announced opener, a passive checker with all the same ordinary reads, authenticated state binding, a separate randomized check or [segment verification][pol], carrier transfer without rare targeting, [GEM][gem] on rare examples, same-budget rare [replay][der], constrained shared [distillation][distill], and keeping R_d. Stack the cheapest lawful check with the strongest migration/retention method and let it optimize the order, reuse ordinary reads and collect dead records. If the stack can pick the same joint policy with the same information and resources, it ties; a new objective or name is not a win. Match C utility, menus, per-item starting margins, deadlines and useful pairs. Compare worst-class detection, honest refusal, timeouts, peak/live byte-ticks (including metadata and reserved backing), planning/check computation, optimizer steps, clocks and after-reuse retention. Check at least three adaptive defaults before claiming a renewal benefit.

## Two exact paper fixtures

### R1. A public draw can buy detection, but rare preference spends it

Take a hidden, sealed momentum vector m in {0, e₁, e₂, e₃}, with one honest history m = 0. All other histories falsely claim zero to back an idle step requiring x′ = 0 from x = 0. On that idle step x′ = −m/2. For the useful C opener, take the same heavy-ball update x′ = −m/2 and y′ = 1, starting at y = m_y = 0 with gradient −1 on the C coordinate y. Three required C queues have interchangeable first items in this toy and the same y teacher. Their ordinary post-step serving outputs include a queue-specific auxiliary channel x′ᵢ not used in that item's loss. The i-th queue's observable check rejects iff x′ᵢ ≠ 0. A requires every |xᵢ| ≤ 1/2; C's held-out y loss drops from 1/2 to 0 on every draw. The threatened exact-zero obligation is not authorized by this check. The table is detection only, not a completed B/W clearing model.

| Sealed history | Opener 1 rejects | Opener 2 rejects | Opener 3 rejects |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 (honest) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| e₁ | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| e₂ | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| e₃ | 0 | 0 | 1 |

Every announced deterministic opener has β = 0; a uniform draw has β = 1/3 and zero honest rejection. If only opener 1 has rare-feature value (an assigned payoff here, not an observed learning effect), fixing β ≥ 1/4 lets one put at most 1/2 on that opener, attained by q = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4). Dropping either other opener breaks the bound. A claimant allowed to swap m after the draw defeats every draw. An observation of all three coordinates, if ordinary at the same cost, detects with certainty and removes the need for this randomization. This is a coverage witness under a stipulated interface, not a claim that the three openers are the only possible checks.

### R2. Equal new-task utility does not imply rare-slice migration

Start a unit-step SGD host with old backing o = 1 and young shared weights (s,t) = (0,0). Two due C examples, on separate feature inputs, each truthfully target 1 under the same teacher: their losses are (s−1)²/2 and (t−1)²/2. Either is a legal first batch. The hidden C panel has balanced squared loss L_C = ((s−1)² + (t−1)²)/4. Rare B_d inputs read max(o,s) as their signed margin, with acquisition floor 1; common B_d, A and installed C read separate untouched coordinates initially on their floors. At the reuse cut, a required other job runs o²/2 and takes o to 0. No old label is read.

| Required C item before reuse | (o,s,t) before reuse | (o,s,t) after reuse | L_C at both cuts | Rare B_d margin after reuse |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| s item | (1,1,0) | (0,1,0) | 1/4 | 1 |
| t item | (1,0,1) | (0,0,1) | 1/4 | 0 |

Both arms start at L_C = 1/2 and meet the rare floor before reuse. If both C items can legally run before the reuse deadline, the distinction vanishes. Even after the s-first transfer, a new truthful C context with loss (s−1/2)²/2 lands s at 1/2; if that context must be served with this update before a B_d read, the longer suffix fails. The fixture shows a matched-C, deadline-specific distinction and a falsifier, not that an actual shared feature can be located without labels. The shared route and rare dependence are stipulated; a C-only same-order scheduler and a privileged choice using the toy feature map tie the s-first arm.

## A. Spend randomness only on useful work

### 01. Draw after the state is sealed — primitive candidate (parent 06)
**Mechanism.** Consume a hidden-state claim by executing one fresh, publicly sampled C opener, then expire the live check state. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Delayed challenge selection and [Proof-of-Learning][pol] already inspect committed training; the proposed operation is one checking write that would have been due anyway, without a stored transcript. **Cheapest first test.** R1 against fixed, uniform paid challenge, full lawful read, and post-draw state substitution. **Old-task retention check.** Serve A/B/installed C at each draw and exercise the independent B/W fallback.

### 02. Optimize the least-detectable lie rather than average power — improvement
**Mechanism.** Solve for a mixture over lawful openers using one coverage constraint per harmful history; replay the binding worst case when the menu changes. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Minimax experimental design already fits this matrix game; the gain would be utility at fixed detection, not the solver. **Cheapest first test.** Duplicate a diagnostic column in R1 and compare uniform-by-item, uniform-by-coverage and exact mixing. **Old-task retention check.** Exclude any opener violating an acquired floor on any alias; score all refused paths with their fallbacks.

### 03. Buy conditional detection before accepting an order restriction — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Grant a live scheduling right only if every allowed availability branch before the deadline has a useful draw law with the promised detection. Otherwise reserve clearing, not a fictitious rate. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust scheduling and [rolling escrow][escrow] insure capacity; here the resource owed at arrival is a mixture of distinguishing actions. **Cheapest first test.** Two shifts: an unrelated duty may remove queue 2 from R1. Compare nominal random sampling, reserved paid check and exact robust schedules. **Old-task retention check.** Service for the duty displacing queue 2, rare B_d and old A/C in both branches.

### 04. Bind hidden state across ordinary service before the draw — improvement
**Mechanism.** Bind a version of the actual backing and the only public intervening transitions, so an A service event cannot be used to swap a lie alias before opening. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Snapshot isolation and authenticated checkpoints solve binding; the question is whether existing execution metadata suffices without a dedicated audit log. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate legal and substituted A interleavings in R1; compare a frozen checkpoint, authenticated chain and proof from the live version. **Old-task retention check.** A is still served on time during the wait, and B/installed C remain intact after the draw.

### 05. Make a skipped draw consume the opportunity, not the skill — improvement
**Mechanism.** Fix the first eligible beacon round and make silence or expiry invoke independent clearing, rather than let a claimant try another round or kill already useful C work. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Challenge-response timeout and [verified renewal][renewal] clearing already cover aborts; the gain would be lower interruption cost. **Cheapest first test.** Simulate a claimant that responds only on misses and compare fixed-deadline clearing against resampling and rollback. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C, particularly W and B_d at the clearing deadline; do not score silence as a detected lie.

### 06. Carry coverage, not a list of missed challenges — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** In a two-opener word, let the live public state give a lower bound on the next detection probability *conditional on every surviving harmful history*. Expire that state at the decision cut. If the per-step bounds are β₁, β₂, overall detection is at least 1−(1−β₁)(1−β₂), without independence. A reachable no-detection branch that skips the second check has second-step power zero and must enter the bound. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Anytime-valid inference][anytime] already handles adapted evidence; the candidate is a finite useful training word with a compact survivor guarantee. **Cheapest first test.** Compare repeated and complementary missed-history masks under the same C deadline. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C after each prefix, early exit and late clearing.

### 07. Compile precision into the draw law — improvement
**Mechanism.** Reject a nominally diagnostic opener if its claimed separation falls in the same lawful report bin, or redistribute its mass to other safe openers. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Quantized fault diagnosis and parent 05 already address rounding; only the power of the *whole mixture* under changing serving precision is new here. **Cheapest first test.** Round R1's 0 and −1/2 to one bin on queue 2 and compare conservative detection, mean-rate calibration and a paid wider report. **Old-task retention check.** Preserve the least-margin A and B slices at every report precision and its fallback.

### 08. Separate a reject from a license to spend — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let the sampled update eliminate some histories, but authorize W only when the residual set admits a common safe continuation. A high β with a dangerous miss must not unlock unsafe service. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Set-membership diagnosis plus robust [escrow][escrow] are the stack; the candidate contract couples the ordinary training transition to both residual uncertainty and the backing it may release. **Cheapest first test.** Add two misses to R1, one harmless after the batch and one requiring continuing coverage, and compare full refusal, independent audit plus safe spending, and acceptance on a pass. **Old-task retention check.** Deliver every previously admitted B/W obligation at the deadline on both misses.

### 09. Price the entire frontier of rare preference — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish the feasible laws q before the draw, then favor carriers by maximizing ordinary-C rare-feature progress under fixed detection, honest rejection and service constraints. Quote a coverage-to-retirement frontier, not just a preferred score. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust design and [example reweighting][reweight] already optimize mixtures; the candidate interface prices verifiability in the same useful actions that could discharge an old service dependency. **Cheapest first test.** R1's biased law against uniform plus rare transfer, an independently paid check and the exact joint optimizer; repeat with the only aligned item illegal. **Old-task retention check.** Match common and rare floors and verify against actual reuse for each admissible terminal branch.

## B. Move the feature, then prove the receipt is dead

### 10. Prefer truthful work on the feature the rare slice uses — primitive candidate (parent 15)
**Mechanism.** Break ties among C-useful items toward changes on a shared feature used by B_d, using only information lawfully present before drawing. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GEM][gem] and [DER][der] consult old data; the candidate substitution comes from successor data and releases old backing. **Cheapest first test.** R2 extended to held-out rare contexts with matched C utility, against untargeted transfer, GEM, equal-cost rare replay and a world with no shared feature. **Old-task retention check.** Rare/common B_d, other Bᵢ, A and installed C after destructive reuse and a blind C suffix.

### 11. Learn an agreement across successor contexts, not a shortcut — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Prefer C items whose updates agree on a feature across two environments where their common shortcut reverses. Do not use B_d labels to define the agreement. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Invariant risk minimization][irm] tries to identify stable predictive structure; the added requirement is a learned shared route that suffices under reuse, not only better domain generalization. **Cheapest first test.** Two feature factors, with shortcut sign reversal in C and a held-out third rare B_d context; compare IRM, joint C fitting and same-byte replay. **Old-task retention check.** Match B_d margins before training and test both shortcut signs after reuse alongside old A/C.

### 12. Reject feature movement that never reaches the rare reader — improvement
**Mechanism.** Test the proposed carrier offline by removing the old backing and intervening on the learned feature, with a sham feature intervention matched in scale. A high feature-gradient score alone cannot justify release. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Concept interventions][concept] and parent 13 already localize effects; the gain is a sharper false-retirement test in the presence of redundant readers. **Cheapest first test.** Two ReLU features with tied update norms but only one causally read by rare B_d after reuse. **Old-task retention check.** Score unperturbed A/B/installed C and rare B_d on lawful reuse separately from intervention damage.

### 13. Find two C items that protect different rare factors — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve an ordered pair of required C items that supplies both factors of a rare B_d conjunction, with the first leaving enough margin for the second. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Gradient matching and multivariate distillation already assemble training sets; the proposed operation discharges a conjunctive backing dependency through distinct useful writes before expiry. **Cheapest first test.** Two independent deficits, with tied individual gains and one pair updating the same factor twice; compare exact budgeted subset selection, replay and a single transferred route. **Old-task retention check.** Score each rare sub-context and their alternating stream at both prefixes and after reuse, plus A/C.

### 14. Trade safe reuse for a proved later cut, not a better average — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** If the first targeted item improves a feature but leaves an unsafe slot, carry the old receipt only until a scheduled ordinary C item brings the full state into a reuse-safe set. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Barrier certificates and delayed release are standard; the candidate is a finite learned path whose final landing removes the receipt without a recovery write. **Cheapest first test.** Add heavy-ball to R2 and enumerate orders with tied endpoint weights and unequal momentum; compare reset, receipt retention and constrained sequence fitting. **Old-task retention check.** Verify rare/common B_d through the delayed cut, actual reuse and next C step; report the added receipt byte-ticks.

### 15. Demand a learned margin against the next lawful eraser — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select the item that makes the young route robust to the most damaging admitted next write, not the one with the biggest rare transfer at reuse. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust distillation and parent 16 already protect later learning; test whether a useful C item can *earn* the needed headroom without a custom training target. **Cheapest first test.** Replace R2's eraser with two equally likely lawful C suffixes and compare greedy rare gain, robust replay and a kept receipt. **Old-task retention check.** Worst suffix, every rare context, other Bᵢ and ordinary A/C after old support is gone.

### 16. Protect against a false feature hint — improvement
**Mechanism.** Require agreement between two pre-existing public views of proposed feature relevance, or use the untargeted draw and retain R_d. Do not treat a claimant's own feature report as verification. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust example weighting and [public consistency][consistency] cover unreliable hints; this tests the added cost of a false deletion. **Cheapest first test.** Swap the genuine and nuisance feature hints on one held-out defaulter; compare blind bias, two-view scoring and no targeting. **Old-task retention check.** Both correct and false hints, including rare B_d after fallback, plus ordinary A/B/C when the proposal is refused.

### 17. Retire a reader, not just an old tensor — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Release R_d only after teaching makes every admitted rare-service reader runnable on shared state, including a later reader that formerly used the receipt for routing rather than the output itself. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Witness replacement][witness] and ordinary garbage collection already clear unreachable state; learned retirement would remove the last live dependency without replacing it with a per-defaulter routing table. **Cheapest first test.** Give two late readers equal immediate B_d outputs, but one still needs an old route ID on reuse; compare same-byte constrained distillation and precise liveness tracking. **Old-task retention check.** Both readers after actual reuse plus A, other Bᵢ and installed C; count bytes for any new routing support.

## C. Make the check and the transfer compete for the same budget

### 18. Let one opener reveal the alias and open the shared feature — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Look for an ordinary opener whose trusted C output is diagnostic and whose live update opens the feature on which a later C write can land rare retention. Safety cannot depend on learning which alias it was through hidden reads. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Dual control and parent 19 share control with observation; this adds that post-seal randomization must keep enough useful transfer paths across the full support. **Cheapest first test.** Two ReLU cells with equal C utility, one enabling a second truthful batch before reuse; compare a separate random challenge plus best sequence. **Old-task retention check.** Every draw and every legal second step, including the rare fallback when the channel never opens.

### 19. Make a detected lie change the transfer route without erasing C — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** On rejection, use the current clearing status to select a C continuation that does not rely on the refuted backing; on a miss, use only a continuation valid over remaining aliases. Do not store a draw-specific historical decision record. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Saga compensation and [verified renewal][renewal] already route failures; the candidate is a useful sampled update that preserves C progress and narrows which *future* ordinary words can migrate B_d. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a default after the opener and before reuse; compare reset, an independent audit plus contingent fitting, and one universal fallback. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/C at interruption and restart, rare B_d on both continuations and W by its original deadline.

### 20. Test the hardest harmful class enabled by retiring the receipt — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Before the draw, include in L aliases that fail an independently specified post-release obligation, even if today's skill passes. Recompile when the admitted suffix changes, never after observing the draw. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Parent 22 anticipates renewal failures; the extension assigns each failure class a coverage constraint in a distribution over useful training. **Cheapest first test.** Two harmless-now aliases, only one breaks a blind paired use of freed backing. Compare immediate-loss coverage, a separate continuation-aware check and paid refusal. **Old-task retention check.** Old per-slice floors before release, after reuse and at the blind pair for both aliases.

### 21. Keep checkability when the carrier becomes next cycle's backing — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve useful opener diversity for the next blind claimant by avoiding transfer words that make all next-cycle observations alias, even if current deletion is safe. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Persistent excitation and [rolling escrow][escrow] insure future signals and rights; this asks if a shared learned carrier can preserve the option of a fresh sealed check while doing C. **Cheapest first test.** Three defaults where two equal-utility transfers leave different next-menu coverage matrices; compare a greedy draw policy, a paid next check and exact sequence planning. **Old-task retention check.** All installed A/B/C, especially the oldest rare B after each reuse and the final paired service.

### 22. Give the independent stack the same optimizer — improvement
**Mechanism.** Enumerate lawful policies over the same inputs and draws, first with freely composable checking and migration, then with a price for any unavoidable extra read, action or pinned dependency. Look for a reachable service-and-release point the stack truly cannot achieve. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Factorial ablation and constrained policy search already do the bookkeeping; this is a kill test, not a learner. **Cheapest first test.** Cross two menus varying coverage and feature transfer independently, including a case with the same action implementing both policies. **Old-task retention check.** Compare identical per-history A/B/C floors and timely W before crediting any resource advantage.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can one genuinely due C word beat the strongest separate-check-plus-transfer stack?** Start with 09, 18 and 22. Compare at matched detection, false refusal, C gain, old-task floors and deadlines, with the same information and legal scheduler. If no new point appears on the cost/retirement frontier, keep only a scheduling improvement or a negative result.
2. **How much worst-case detection survives realistic binding, supply and precision?** Start with 01–07. Give the claimant adaptive defaults, queue starvation, post-seal substitutions and selective silence; estimate the coverage guaranteed for every harmful history, not the average over cooperative runs. If lawful ordinary observations already catch everything or cannot separate anything, randomization has no job.
3. **Can C-only feature selection predict after-reuse retention on blind rare contexts?** Start with 10–16. Hide B_d labels and the rare panel, align acquisition margins, and vary whether C contains the causal shared feature or a spurious proxy. Compare against same-budget rare replay and ask whether gains persist through an unannounced lawful suffix.
4. **What live state is necessary for safe repeated checking and retirement?** Start with 08, 17 and 19–21. Find the minimum service state needed to handle a miss, timeout or later default without accidentally keeping a check history or a substitute R_d. If release ever relies on the chance a lie was caught, keep independent backing.
