# Continual verifiers 3: Repair the next lesson, renew the reversing witness

Depth 2 · 24 idea cards · **Ambitious but doable** · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Parent:** [#412](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/412). The [review in #6](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6/files) ([triage row at the reviewed commit](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/1515213b13f62caad9e06227f3da31128667ef4a/reviews/flood-triage.md#L574)) kept two lines at 3, neither at 4: **CV2-23+CV2-04**, retraction until the learner responds to future clean lessons like a v2-trained learner; and **CV2-11+CV2-14**, capability-indexed progress comparisons whose nearest reversing intervention is an executable solver edit. These are not the same as restoring an earlier checkpoint and recomputing a progress score after every upgrade.

**Where novelty has to move.** In the repair line, optimizing post-update scores on a panel is not new. Could repair instead produce an editable interface for *how* a lesson is taken up, reusable on later premises and learner versions without copying either learner's state? A good repair must not merely resist the old proxy: it must allow the corrected rule to be learned or legitimately reinstated with fresh authority at the clean learner's rate. In the capability line, a nearest reversal is ordinary recourse or robust search until it can distinguish “can run the tactic” from “uses the tactic differently on these two branches,” and be renewed across changes of solver implementation. An old witness's failure to transfer does not certify that the old sign survived.

## Literature check

Checked targeted alphaXiv results and linked titles/abstracts. **P** means candidate primitive, not a novelty verdict; **I** means improvement, known operator or assay. A new dashboard, gate, or more expensive adversarial inner loop does not earn a primitive claim.

| Available already | What must be different here |
|---|---|
| [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) and [MUDMAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12484) are adaptation-aware defenses. More directly, [Update-Matched Unlearning (UMU)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) gives the same simulated update to the defended model and an undefended reference initialized from the same unlearned model, to avoid penalizing useful learning. [Optimizer-state alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17590) also measures counterfactual update directions. | Merely matching a post-update clean target is insufficient. Compare to a TAR-style meta-objective with the *same* clean reference and to UMU-style update matching; require a reusable correction of finite lesson effects, not suppression plus preservation or recovery of the original optimizer state. |
| [Relearning attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01318), [sharpness-aware defenses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05374), [representation results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11685), [benign syntax-driven recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03379), and [limits of information-only certificates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09004) make recovery speed an unreliable stand-alone readout. The repo's [top-10 #5](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/1515213b13f62caad9e06227f3da31128667ef4a/reviews/flood-top10.md) deliberately uses relearning as a read head; [#114](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/114) found a raw-logit shortcut and [#336](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/336) already proposes finite-response patches and two-step writes. | Use learning-only reads to *identify and repair* divergence under authorized lessons, not to propose another hidden channel or a frozen recall score. Give no-update readers the same lessons and follow-up queries. |
| [Interventional recourse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06278), [robust counterfactual explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04842), [interventional separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247), and [robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) find or validate intervenable witnesses under uncertainty. [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) already has change-impact invalidation, common tails and cumulative debt; [VinePPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679), [PAV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) and [SF/GPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312) cover credit recomputation. | Ask whether a witness can be *re-executed at the causal interface* of an incoming solver, and either prove its scope under a declared toy model or return a specific new contrast. A new robustness radius, counterfactual search, or dependency graph is not enough. |

**First-test convention.** Use enumerable packing processes and arithmetic games with fully specified valid proof statements and reward versions. Fix clean from-scratch v2 reference trajectories (report matched-seed and across-seed error), and define a lesson as an authorized batch **plus** its update and any observable feedback it may request. Score live service, learning response on unseen lesson sequences, and untouched A/B after each prefix, including one old skill that shares features with a repaired rule. Give every method the same clean references, lesson/branch access, FLOPs, retained bytes, and independent checker calls. For a learning-only claim, compare frozen-output and transcript readers, raw gradients, Jacobian/Hessian probes, and actual single-update endpoints at matched access. For solver transitions, freeze the validity checker and evaluate false credit carries on held-out upgrades at matched coverage. “Equivalent” means only the declared probes, horizon, edit grammar and tolerance; none of these is certified deletion. No tests below have been run.

## A · Make repair reusable across future lessons

### CV3-01 · Repair the meaning of a lesson at its input port — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Keep the repaired learner's core updater unchanged. Learn a small translator that writes two signed residual terms into the loss for each *clean, ordinary lesson*: one keyed to the retracted premise's effect on the lesson, and one keyed to a matched context where the lesson should remain unchanged. Fit on the clean-reference differences in immediate response and response to a second lesson. For a held-out retraction, fit only the premise binding using one witnessed disagreement; the translator cannot query the reference at use time.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) matches the outcome of the same update, while [dataset distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10959) synthesizes training inputs. Candidate only if reusable **lesson translation** beats per-premise distillation plus a generic hypernetwork adapter on new sequences or a new updater, not if it stores clean answers.
- **Cheapest first test:** A two-feature packing learner under SGD and Adam, with a bad premise binding shared by otherwise valid lessons. Rebind one fresh premise and hold out lesson order and optimizer. Compare stored counter-updates, learner-state repair, TAR/UMU-style matching, and a budget-matched synthetic-data generator.
- **Old-task retention:** Probe A/B and an old valid correction after every translated update; raw old lessons unrelated to the retraction must still work.

### CV3-02 · Authorized relearning without inherited savings — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Pair a revoked rule with a fresh-rule control of matched difficulty. A repair exports a correction that removes only the revoked rule's *excess savings* over a clean v2 learner. Under an authority-approved later lesson, let both relearn at the clean rate instead of teaching permanent resistance to the rule's shape.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) and [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) separate persistence and plasticity; [top-10 #5](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/1515213b13f62caad9e06227f3da31128667ef4a/reviews/flood-top10.md) reads savings directly. Novel only if the paired saving residual supplies a transportable **corrective write** that outperforms update matching given the same authorized reintroduction episodes; this is not a hidden-memory proposal.
- **Cheapest first test:** Revoke a synthetic boundary rule, continue teaching an unrelated boundary at the same sample difficulty, then reintroduce a legitimate revision. Compare first-hit learning curves and non-reinstated controls after a held-out change of optimizer; give TAR/UMU arms the same clean reintroduction target.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve learning speed on two old authorized exceptions, one with the same syntax as the revoked rule.

### CV3-03 · Repair from the fork where learning first became distinguishable — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Learn a probe sequence that splits two candidate residual effects that agree in current output; record the suffix that isolates the offending effect. Use that suffix to train a repair localized to the separated subfamily, then reuse the split on a new premise pair without memorizing the original labels.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adaptive distinguishing sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01139) and [apartness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05419) separate state machines; [#336](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/336) already proposes a multi-update read. Candidate only if the split supports a better selective repair than the same discriminating sequence fed to ordinary response distillation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct two benign nonlinear learners identical on old outputs and single-update endpoints but diverging after a two-lesson ordering. Compare a frozen discrimination tree, direct two-step meta-training and split-conditioned repair on a held-out premise pair; shortcut readers get the same inputs.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old ordered lessons whose first update is shared with the diagnostic sequence.

### CV3-04 · Undo a change to what a lesson asks next — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Compare clean and repaired learners under the same feedback policy, not just the same fixed lesson log. When the learner asks a different next question, return a small correction for that divergence and carry forward only the branch of supervision the corrected learner actually requested.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Exact RL unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04182) couples data collection and [online robot correction removal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10280) shows how feedback inherits earlier effects. This is only different if the repair changes a faulty *query response* with an independently reusable local operation and costs less than rerunning the feedback policy from the first divergence.
- **Cheapest first test:** A synthetic tutor answers one of two learner-requested sorting questions. Match fixed-log updates first, then hold out a tutor that switches its follow-up after one answer. Compare exact rerun, first-exposure repair from CV2-02, and a query-response correction at equal tutor calls.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old questions asked for valid reasons and downstream A/B when no tutor is available.

### CV3-05 · A repair that can be transplanted between updater implementations — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Export a correction in coordinates of *two observed lesson transitions* (e.g. change on a packing decision before/after a clean follow-up), not parameter or moment coordinates. Fit a small local implementation in the recipient using target-blind calibration lessons, then bind the correction to the addressed premise once. A recipient that cannot realize both transitions reports a concrete infeasibility witness.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MAML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400) optimizes post-update adaptability; [Model Stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) tests interoperation between representations. The proposed transfer carries a change in **learning effects**, not a pretrained initialization or a model-to-model activation map. Ordinary cross-optimizer response distillation is the key control.
- **Cheapest first test:** Move one premise repair from a linear learner to a factored nonlinear learner with different optimizer state. Fit calibration maps on unrelated lessons. Compare independent refits, shared meta-optimizer and transported corrections on unseen premises at equal labels and model-state bytes.
- **Old-task retention:** Test both recipients after the same unrelated lesson and an old skill-specific lesson, not just immediately after transport.

### CV3-06 · Close the repair under neighboring legal lessons — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Alternate replay of clean, ordinary lesson sequences and bounded search for the clean sequence on which repair diverges most. Evaluate separate effects of larger tests, a better search routine, and a stronger objective.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761), [MUDMAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12484), and [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) are already inner-loop defenses; this is an explicit clean-target control with adaptation, not a new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Grow the held-out lesson horizon from one to four without changing the synthetic curriculum. Compare fixed-panel response matching and adaptive matching at the same inner steps.
- **Old-task retention:** Check ordinary transfer and A/B on the same sequences that separate the repaired and clean learners.

### CV3-07 · Distinguish selective repair from slowing the entire learner — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** For every bad-premise relearning curve, require an equally difficult, authorized comparator and a direct estimate of first-step and finite-step response. Include a dose-matched low-learning-rate learner as the negative control.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) explicitly measures new-task learning; [information-only certificate limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09004) show why parameterization changes matter. This is an assay, not a new way to repair.
- **Cheapest first test:** Match three small learners by current outputs, then change step size or rescale an equivalent parameterization. Report how often the proposed selectivity score spuriously prefers the globally slow learner.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify both old outputs and adaptation rates on legitimate examples where no premise was retracted.

### CV3-08 · Return the smallest clean lesson family the repair cannot serve — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Compare alternative repairs on a declared curriculum grammar. When no allowed repair realizes all requested lesson transitions, return a minimal pair of conflicting lesson programs and a *specific* added repair coordinate that resolves them. Retest that coordinate on an unseen conflicting pair before treating the interface as expanded.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Apartness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05419) furnishes distinguishing witnesses; [Model Stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) tests compatibility. The candidate operation grows a writable lesson interface from constructive incompatibility, beyond automaton learning plus generic rank growth; “infeasible” is relative to an exactly searched toy repair class.
- **Cheapest first test:** Give a rank-one repair two clean sequence constraints that need independently controllable effects. Compare adding random rank, gradient-conflict-triggered rank, and the returned coordinate on a new premise at matched rank.
- **Old-task retention:** Check both formerly conflicting old lesson families and an untouched A/B pair after expansion.

## B · Make a reversing capability witness travel across solvers

### CV3-09 · Split a tactic into availability, use, and outcome — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Store a reversal as two interventions at the same legal continuation state: make the tactic available, then force or prevent its execution with availability unchanged. Record separate changes in voluntary use and checked outcomes on both original branches. Rebind a successor only where both interventions implement the same contrast; otherwise refresh progress and keep validity.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional recourse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06278) and [dynamic mediation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13348) already separate interventions; CV2-14 already toggles tactics. The candidate is the **portable difference of intervention responses**, not the decomposition. It must beat causal mediation plus #408 when a successor supports the tactic but applies it differently.
- **Cheapest first test:** Set up two arithmetic solvers with identical answers and tactic menus but different voluntary activation. Compare presence-only witnesses, full branching and availability/use witnesses on a third policy; include invalid forced steps that must remain rejected.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve unaffected progress orderings, and verify invalid old steps even when allowing the extra tactic would solve the final problem.

### CV3-10 · Rebind a witness to a different implementation of the same tactic — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Represent the witness as the signed difference between the toggle's effects on the two branches, with a separate bound on changes *without* the toggle. Find a local replacement intervention in the new solver, including a composition of primitives. Common-mode changes are allowed: match the signed effect and test a negative-control branch, then carry the sign only if the combined error bounds leave its margin intact. Otherwise return the branch where correspondence is unresolved rather than asserting tactic identity.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Causal abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20161) and [interventional agreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) check behavioral correspondence; [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) already cancels common tails. The proposed quotient is narrower than interventional equivalence of solvers and reusable across multiple competing branches. It must beat learned causal alignment plus #408, including when that baseline uses the same signed observables.
- **Cheapest first test:** Refactor one arithmetic shortcut into two composed moves and rename an unrelated shortcut to the old name. Compare name matching, causal-abstraction alignment plus revalidation, and negative-control rebinding on a held-out third implementation.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain old validity and correctly scoped step rankings on both solver implementations.

### CV3-11 · A witness that survives the loss of its tool — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Store two semantically distinct ways to *intervene* on a tactic's effect (e.g. toggle availability, or use a legal state construction that isolates the same adoption decision). If the new solver cannot be toggled directly, allow the indirect witness only when it excludes the same alternative explanations in both branches.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) selects interventions among surviving causal hypotheses; [robust counterfactual explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04842) handle changed models. Candidate only if a learned replacement intervention maintains label transport under missing controls better than fresh intervention search at equal access.
- **Cheapest first test:** Remove an enable/disable API from a synthetic solver but allow a lawful alternative prefix that blocks the tactic. Compare fresh forced branching, restored-interface oracle and stored alternative interventions; include a prefix that also alters another option and must fail.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve unaffected old path rankings and old legal moves for solvers that still expose the original toggle.

### CV3-12 · A two-sided switch bound, not the nearest flip — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Enumerate minimal reversal edits and minimal restoring edits, then keep the nearest of each rather than treating reversal as monotone in capability. Keep unsupported edit combinations unknown; do not infer a safe region from a lone negative test.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) and [recourse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06278) already optimize over alternatives. Improvement only: compare paired executable boundaries against a one-sided expiry rule. The edit frontier is not novelty.
- **Cheapest first test:** One added tactic reverses a preference; a second restores it. Compare nearest flip, robust min–max and two-sided boundaries on unseen ordered subsets at equal calls.
- **Old-task retention:** Check unchanged rankings and reject any validity flip inferred solely from the new progress frontier.

### CV3-13 · Rescue a usable comparison after its reversal witness expires — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** If a full step comparison is no longer defensible, extract the largest sub-comparison still causal under the same forced prefix, such as “before either branch invokes tactic t.” Expose this as a smaller training operation and attach the specific contrast still needed for the full comparison; never use the smaller conclusion as the full preference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [VinePPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679) branches at intermediate states; [PAV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) scores local progress; [robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) bounds outcomes. Candidate only if a reusable *decomposition of valid teaching comparisons* outperforms simple segmentation plus partial-label masking under the same constraints.
- **Cheapest first test:** A packing shortcut changes the sign after a clear branch boundary, while an earlier independently checkable local goal remains informative. Compare whole-label refresh, segment PRMs and recovered sub-comparisons for held-out upgrades.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain old prefix judgments and old task gains after training on the shortened comparison.

### CV3-14 · A reversal that identifies a substitution rather than a missing tactic — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When disabling t no longer reverses the preference, compare which allowed replacement actions actually fulfill t's role on each branch. Rebind to a joint intervention only if it isolates the verified substitution; otherwise allow the old label to lapse without calling the successor less capable.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [SF/GPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312) recombines policies, and [interventional agreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) distinguishes counterfactual models. Candidate only if learning reusable role substitution contrasts reduces rechecks beyond policy-bank comparison and generic minimal new reversal search.
- **Cheapest first test:** Replace an arithmetic shortcut with either of two lawful tactics, each used on a different branch. Compare one-tactic ablation, joint robust search and role-based substitution on another solver with both implementations.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old rankings on paths where the original tactic remains uniquely effective and exact old validity on all paths.

### CV3-15 · Test capability witnesses against action redistribution — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Report both the controlled intervention effect and the full policy response. Force one action on paired starts, then let the continuation redistribute probability; compare against a successor that shifts probability with no capability change. If the results disagree, retain only the controlled claim.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Counterfactual OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05824) and [PAV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) already evaluate alternatives under defined policies. This is an attribution control, not a new index.
- **Cheapest first test:** Match success at both versions but change where a known arithmetic tactic is selected. Compare menu-only expiry, forced-effect expiry and independent reranking under live policies.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify old comparisons with unchanged traffic and report old valid-but-not-preferred steps separately.

### CV3-16 · Amortize only the experiment, not its verdict — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Group progress labels by a *shared minimal intervention program* that contains separate falsifiable predictions for each label. Run that program once on a successor and update only the labels its observations identify. Learn grouping from previous upgrades and require it to help on new causal layouts, not merely deduplicate identical traces.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) actively distinguishes hypotheses and [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) reuses shared changes. Novelty needs query savings over joint experiment selection with the same hypothesis set, from transferable grouping learned across upgrades.
- **Cheapest first test:** Many arithmetic prefixes have distinct validity proofs but share two tactic transitions; later compose the tactics differently. Compare cache deduplication, optimal experiment selection on each version, and transferred groups at fixed calls and false-carry ceilings.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain old isolated comparisons that don't share the experiment, including a superficially similar prefix whose transition differs.

## C · Repair how capabilities are learned, not just whether they are present

### CV3-17 · A crossover lesson that distinguishes residual teaching from new competence — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** For a disputed progress flip, cross the **same clean lesson** with the presence or absence of its independently verified continuation tactic. Store the difference of the lesson's effects on the two forced branches, not its final score. If the repaired learner differs from the clean learner only in that interaction, fit a correction to the interaction while leaving ordinary tactic execution untouched.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) matches an update against a reference and [dynamic mediation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13348) decomposes causal paths. The factorial contrast is known; a candidate primitive must turn a localized interaction witness into a *portable correction to learning* that works without replaying all four cells on every successor. Compare those methods stacked, not separately.
- **Cheapest first test:** Match two sorting learners on outputs and one-step lessons, then let an authorized tactic interact with the second update of a clean sequence. Fit from one retraction and reuse the repair on a second; compare fixed-log repair and factorial response distillation with the same four observations.
- **Old-task retention:** Confirm legal use of the tactic, A/B without it, and ordinary acquisition of an unrelated tactic after correction.

### CV3-18 · A reversal with a teaching program instead of a manual toggle — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a deployed solver cannot expose a direct tactic switch, represent a prospective reversal as a short **authorized, ordinary lesson sequence** that would teach the tactic, plus a proof-checked test for where it is actually used. Run the teaching witness on a scratch copy only; transport the credit boundary if the measured causal effect generalizes under an independently chosen solver update.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dataset distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10959) teaches through small datasets, [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) optimizes over changes induced by fine-tuning, and [interventional recourse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06278) finds actionable changes. The sought object is a portable *future label-expiry experiment* whose teaching effects decouple from incidental changes better than distilled lessons plus fresh branching.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two proof solvers expose different interfaces but can learn from the same legal tactic examples. Compare a named toggle, shortest fresh lesson search, and a transported sequence with held-out controls for other tactic effects; count learnability and correct reversals separately.
- **Old-task retention:** Check unchanged credit and independently proved steps on the live learner, and test the scratch branch for unrelated capability loss.

### CV3-19 · A correction with a right-to-learn counterexample — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Couple a retraction with an explicit, ordinary lesson that the repair must *not* neutralize. If a second valid correction sharing the old feature becomes unlearnable, reuse that lesson as a counterexample that carves out an allowed uptake direction in the repaired learner. Preserve that direction through later premise corrections without allowing it to revive old behavior on its own.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Conflict-Averse Gradient Descent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14048) constrains multi-objective descent; [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) preserves plasticity. Candidate only if counterexamples improve later repairs across premises better than CAGrad or projected UMU on the same lessons. Protecting one gradient direction or setting a new threshold is not new.
- **Cheapest first test:** A retracted baggage rule and a permitted exception share features. Record the first old exception lesson that the naive repair suppresses, then require it to protect transfer after a second overlapping retraction. Compare stored lesson replay, update projection and the witnessed uptake direction.
- **Old-task retention:** Keep the exception and a disjoint A/B task teachable while verifying that no additional authority-free proxy recovery occurred.

### CV3-20 · Test a taught capability at the first divergence in its use — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** After a repair, replay only the first prefix where the clean-trained and repaired learners acquire different tactic-use preferences under identical authorized lessons. The verifier returns a pair of corrections: one for the update's treatment of that prefix, and one for progress labels that used the divergent continuation. Verify each is independently needed before applying it to the appropriate consumer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Online correction removal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10280) detects feedback dependence, and [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) invalidates preferences on policy changes. Candidate only if the *paired local repair* recovers both counterfactual learning and valid retained labels better than exposure-corrected unlearning followed by ordinary expiry at equal budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use a four-step sorting curriculum where the first divergent preference precedes any changed terminal reward. Compare repair-only, relabel-only, stacked repair plus revalidation, and learning both corrections from the same witnessed split.
- **Old-task retention:** Keep old valid rankings on paths that pass through the divergent prefix without using the disputed tactic.

### CV3-21 · Do not mistake suppressed learning for a preserved comparison — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Pair each observed progress sign with a held-out clean lesson check. If a repaired learner preserves the old sign only because it no longer acquires a valid tactic, classify the repair as failed even though old credit looked stable.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) measures plasticity, and [Better Policies, Worse Verifier Audits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29029) separates verifier quality from policy-induced observations. This is a two-consumer assay, not a new repair.
- **Cheapest first test:** Create a slow-learning impostor and a selective repair with matched current rankings. Distinguish them on clean tactic acquisition, then rerank with fresh branches.
- **Old-task retention:** Require old output and learning-rate retention on both unrelated and overlapping authorized tasks.

### CV3-22 · A learning-response seam that keeps unrelated lesson orders free — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Learn to translate two authorized lesson streams separately until they reach a specific premise-dependent tactic conflict. At the conflict, use a witnessed pair of orders to learn a shared correction; otherwise allow both lesson orders without a global replay or predetermined curriculum. The seam is observable in the finite **response**, not a rulebook dependency alone.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MAML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400) can optimize over orders and [CV2-06](continual-verifiers-2.md#cv2-06--the-smallest-repair-commutator--ambitious-but-doable-p) already tests correction commutators. Candidate only if the seam learned from one problem transfers to novel orderings and yields an actual localized correction beyond meta-training on the same orders.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two sorting lesson streams commute on three prefixes but change tactic uptake in opposite directions on a fourth. Compare full sequence replay, per-order adapters, and learned seam corrections on longer, interleaved streams under equal compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Test both original lesson sequences and retain independently valid old decisions after an interleaving through the seam.

### CV3-23 · Prove a control is too weak before trusting a transported label — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Construct two candidate solvers that agree on visible actions, the tactic menu, and a designated set of passive progress probes, but disagree on a verified intervention. Mark exactly which witnesses cannot distinguish them.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional agreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) formalizes this distinction and [apartness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05419) learns distinguishing experiments. This is the matched-world falsification test for portable witnesses, not a new equivalence operator.
- **Cheapest first test:** Draw a held-out proof game where two lawful solvers differ only on a branch that becomes reachable after a lesson. Compare passive upgrade fingerprints and executable witnesses, with matched observations until the separating check.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify old proofs and preserve rankings on branches that both candidate solvers treat identically.

### CV3-24 · A repair that can hand off to a genuinely new verifier — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** After premise repair, hand a successor verifier a small pair of lesson-response contrasts and independently executable tactic tests. The successor uses its own criterion to choose which progress labels to check and must reproduce the same clean learning response without querying old verifier scores. If that is impossible, return the smallest lesson on which the successor's correction differs.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Optimizer-state alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17590) compares learning directions, [ProofPatch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01357) maintains statement review validity, and [CV34](root-continual-verifiers.md#cv34--rebuild-a-verifier-from-signed-obligations-not-from-old-weights--ambitious-but-doable-p) already rebuilds verifier state. Candidate only if the handoff reconstructs **future policy update effects** with fewer new checked comparisons than rebuilding the verifier and then redoing response distillation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Replace a learned trace scorer with a finite reference checker plus a different progress model after two earlier retractions. Freeze old scores; compare carried contrasts, ordinary relabeling and rebuilding on equivalent-sized evidence, then give all arms the same unseen clean lessons.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old valid/invalid distinctions and measure adaptation to both A/B and one corrected exception after the handoff.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can a repair travel as a translation of clean lessons rather than a new defended initialization? (CV3-01, CV3-03, CV3-05, CV3-08)** Start on the SGD/Adam toy from #412, but reserve a new premise, new lesson order and changed step schedule. Give TAR/UMU-style meta-training, direct response distillation and synthetic-data generators the same clean reference and budgets. Require fewer new checked lessons to reproduce *finite sequence responses*, with A/B intact. If endpoint matching or state repair ties, retain the simpler method.
2. **Can a reversing witness survive replacing the implementation, not merely toggling a tactic's name? (CV3-09–CV3-12, CV3-14, CV3-16)** Swap a tactic for a two-step equivalent and independently vary voluntary use on the two branches. Compare #408, robust min–max over the same edit grammar, causal alignment, fixed panels, SF/GPI and fresh VinePPO calls. Require fewer false carries or fewer calls at the same coverage on held-out upgrades, with unproved correspondence going to unknown.
3. **Can a corrected learner regain a legitimately retaught rule with no excess savings and no plasticity penalty? (CV3-02, CV3-06, CV3-07, CV3-19)** Reserve explicit authorization for reintroduction; measure old residue without authority and the learning response with it. Compare clean retrain, update-matched objectives, syntax-matched fresh lessons and a matched slow-learning impostor. If the effect is explainable by lower learning rate, harmless syntax, or a frozen read of the same transcript, discard the read-head claim.
4. **Can a learning-dependent reversal witness repair both a learner and the step preference it consumes? (CV3-17, CV3-18, CV3-20, CV3-21, CV3-23)** On a held-out proof game, use a clean lesson sequence and branch intervention that identifies where an update deviates from the clean learner. Compare independent state repair plus credit invalidation, a four-cell response fit, and the joint witness at equal feedback. Require genuine gains in both clean adaptation and verified label transport rather than moving error from one subsystem to the other.
5. **Which failed transport tells us how to extend the interface? (CV3-04, CV3-08, CV3-11, CV3-13, CV3-24)** Hide which of two conflicts breaks the handoff—a new requested lesson or a missing capability intervention. Compare adaptable interpreters and fresh search given the same examples. A counterexample should enable cheaper subsequent repair on a different held-out premise, not merely explain the current failure; carry forward verified A/B and treat unsupported credit as unknown.
