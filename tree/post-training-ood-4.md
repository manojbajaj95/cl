# Post-training OOD 4: teach what survives a rebind, buy what changes a decision

**Status:** Ideas only; all tests below are proposals. No implementation, learner runs, or GPU work.

**Parent:** [Post-training OOD 3](post-training-ood-3.md) ([PR #319][parent]), child of [Post-training OOD 2](post-training-ood-2.md). **Review seeds:** [Flood triage, lines 434–437][triage] on [PR #6][review]; only line 437 is the #319 verdict. Extend its two score-3 cards: **#319/02**, one fixed relational hint across fresh public rank-to-action bindings, with decisions following evidence rather than action names; and **#319/09**, learn where the student's own public query program first reaches a target decision risk within budget.

**Ledger read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger]. Do not revive private shorthand, generator-identity replay, rare-prefix splitting, effect-class replay, or sequential teacher refresh. These proposals teach from public evidence, not from an answer encoded in a hint, an archived trace, or an executable teacher tool. The ambitious-but-doable and primitive labels below are candidates, not reviewer verdicts; a tie with the stacked control leaves an improvement or an audit, not a new primitive.

## What is allowed to change?

- A binding maps relational choices to actions in an ordinary public interface. Under a faithful renaming, the *decoded* decision should stay fixed while the emitted action may change. With ties, aliases, or partial menus, score the set of acceptable executable actions, not a chosen canonical spelling. An apparent rebind that changes effects or stakes is a different task, not a symmetry.
- Use a declared loss and a frozen population or explicit set of test populations. Evaluate the learner's stopping decision on its own public transcript, never on the teacher's private state. In enumerable worlds compute conditional risk exactly; for sampled tests use independent evaluation. The risk of a specific action can rise after a surprising read. No affordable crossing is an outcome, not a reason to drop that episode. When timing or cost is compared, name the unit: calls, time, or the full resource vector.
- A short hint, a stopping label, or the choice of lesson can still be the answer channel. Freeze the hint and teaching schedule before drawing bindings and answer-changing evidence within each matched block. Charge any per-instance supervision to the training budget, never expose it at evaluation, and audit transcript metadata as well as text. Hold out generators, not just fresh permutations of familiar examples.

## Common CPU test contract

- **Compare the seeds directly.** For #319/02 use #312/01 same-hint pairs, fixed bindings, matched relational augmentation, SKiC-style examples and an explicit equivariant model with the same public inputs; include an unsupported binding. For #319/09 use #312/13 payload withdrawal, active feature acquisition (including oracle distillation), value-of-information ordering, and expert-query imitation at equal cost; include a world with no affordable distinguishing query. For hybrid cards stack the strongest applicable controls. Use exact finite search as a feasibility bound; also compare it as a method if its total cost fits the cap. Include matched filler and entropy-matched temperature for stochastic learners.
- **Bill the whole stream.** First enumerate tiny worlds and use a tabular or small shared-feature learner where a learning claim is at stake. Reserve future families before setup; evaluate unaided Pass@1 and decision risk, costs to reach a predeclared risk target, the censored fraction, and cumulative setup plus later learning and inference cost. Count all attempted reads, failures, retries, verifications, train steps, persistent bytes, and hint bits including scheduling. No extra panel, decoder prompt, or teacher scaffold at zero-hint evaluation; the task's ordinary public interface remains available.
- **Retention on every card.** In addition to the slice named on each card, evaluate five prelearned families on their ordinary zero-hint tasks before the new stream, at each consolidation boundary, and after later transfer. Compare at matched new-task progress with the pre-update learner and best matched control; report per-family accuracy, rare-path accuracy and public work. Reject a mean old-family drop over 2 pp or retention below matched GRPO where applicable.

**Literature check.** Searched Hugging Face Papers for symbolic equivariance, compositional prompting, policy abstractions, observation experiments and active feature acquisition; checked cited abstracts in the indexed records or on arXiv/PMLR. [SE-RRMs][serrm] enforce symbol-permutation equivariance and [SKiC][skic] composes demonstrated skills. [OracleImitator][oracle] already distills privileged rankings of which feature to buy; [Near Optimal Bayesian Active Learning for Decision Making][hec] already acquires tests until consistent hypotheses fit a decision region, including overlapping regions. [Active sequential hypothesis testing][active] already optimizes sensing versus declaration cost. Neither renaming symbols, asking useful questions, nor stopping on an action region is a novelty claim. The cards name narrower differences to test; this is a targeted prior-art screen, not a claim of priority.

## A. Learn the relation without freezing an action dictionary (extends #319/02)

### 01. Teach a partially defined action, not a default action — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach a rule with an unbound outcome: when none of its relational targets are executable, the rule yields no action and hands control back to the ordinary policy. Admit the lesson only if it distinguishes an empty image from a nonempty image under fresh bindings, without naming a fallback. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SE-RRMs][serrm] handle symbol permutations, and [Predictable MDP Abstraction][pma] restricts abstract actions to predictable effects. Compare a masked equivariant policy with the same fallback; the candidate is transferring a rule's *domain of authority*, distinct from action legality. **Cheapest first test:** Omit the preferred target from one public menu; the remaining legal action can be unsafe to choose by default. **Old-task retention:** Include a prior family where that same remaining action is the correct ordinary fallback.

### 02. Teach a set, then vary who gets to break its ties — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train the hint to preserve the whole set of relational optima. An independent public caller chooses a tie-break objective after training; score whether the learner resolves ties for that caller without giving a non-optimal target a vote. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Near Optimal Bayesian Active Learning for Decision Making][hec] represents overlapping acceptable decision regions; [SKiC][skic] composes demonstrated skills. Compare direct set-valued supervision plus SKiC-style caller examples; the claim is retention and later reuse of unresolved choices without supervision on the new tie-break. **Cheapest first test:** Two objects have equal rank but differ in public duration and energy; alternate which cost a new caller minimizes, with a strict-rank negative. **Old-task retention:** Score the old family where ties must still use its original deterministic rule.

### 03. Teach what can survive two objects sharing a button — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** For a many-to-one binding, teach whether all effects that one button can produce satisfy the relational rule; never invert the binding to a convenient representative. Vary the public dispatch policy independently of the button mapping. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Multi-Agent MDP Homomorphic Networks][hom] exploits state-action symmetries and [Predictable MDP Abstraction][pma] restricts actions with unreliable outcomes. Compare exact effect masks plus abstract-action supervision; the new candidate is a reusable *rule-relative* check of mixed fibers. **Cheapest first test:** One button randomly selects either a rank-1 or a rank-2 object; the same mixed-rank pair is safe for “rank at most 2” but not for “rank exactly 1.” **Old-task retention:** Check old tasks where a lottery over multiple targets is the intended behavior.

### 04. Teach local coordinates without promising a global dictionary — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Learn relational decisions in local menus and only transport distinctions agreed on by their public overlaps. When different valid routes of translation yield different concrete IDs, retain any action that is invariant to that ambiguity and leave the others unresolved. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Practical Equivariances via Relational Conditional Neural Processes][rcnp] builds equivariance through relational inputs; [Multi-Agent MDP Homomorphic Networks][hom] shares experience across global symmetries. Compare an equivariant model plus explicit partial-map composition; the candidate is teaching *path-independent decisions* even when global identities cannot be recovered. **Cheapest first test:** Three menus share only partial keys; the two paths disagree on identical-rank objects but agree on the unique worst object. **Old-task retention:** Include old menus with a real global key where individual identities must still be tracked.

### 05. Teach precisely which comparison is independent of the ruler — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Use a shared hint to teach different transport rules for ordinal and cardinal predicates: order survives arbitrary strictly increasing rescalings, a fixed gap need not. Learn the boundary jointly, with the same rescalings and opposite evidence for both predicates. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Practical Equivariances via Relational Conditional Neural Processes][rcnp] derives equivariance from invariant relations. Compare an equivariant ordinal encoder plus a separate calibrated numeric head; the candidate is learning *which* transport to apply to a held-out predicate composition rather than building in one scale treatment. **Cheapest first test:** Warp four ranks without reversing them; “lower than the reference” must survive while “two units lower” can flip. **Old-task retention:** Recheck exact old numerical comparisons as well as order-only tasks.

### 06. Teach how to hand off a choice when the next interface renames it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach a two-step rule whose first action decides which menus can appear later. Assign credit to its ability to leave a valid relational continuation under bindings drawn only after the commitment, not to matching one demonstration path. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Multi-Agent MDP Homomorphic Networks][hom] carries symmetries through sequential decisions; [SKiC][skic] composes shown skills. Compare equivariant planning plus independently rebound follow-up demonstrations; the candidate is *transfer of a continuation obligation*, not equivariance of the final label. **Cheapest first test:** Two initial selections pay equally now, but only one leaves any lower-ranked legal target across all feasible next menus. **Old-task retention:** Check old tasks where the seemingly fragile choice opens a specifically needed continuation.

### 07. Separate permission to rename from permission to reverse — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach the ordinary object-name map separately from a public orientation bit supplied by the caller. Treat order reversal as a lawful transport only when the caller reverses the relation too; otherwise preserved choices are evidence of a wrong rule. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Subequivariant Graph Reinforcement Learning in 3D Environments][sube] restricts symmetries to those compatible with physical asymmetry; [SKiC][skic] supports composition of named rules. Compare known subgroup augmentation plus explicit orientation conditioning; the test is whether a shared hint teaches an admissible transformation of a new rule composition. **Cheapest first test:** In a line-navigation puzzle, independently mirror positions and flip the caller's “upstream” convention; include a fixed external reference that may not be mirrored. **Old-task retention:** Verify old absolute-left instructions do not become symmetric.

### 08. Teach invariance to aliases without erasing votes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach a distinction between duplicating a display label and adding a new contributing object. Require stable decisions when a duplicate is certified as another view of the same object, and require evidence-sensitive decisions when it is a distinct contributor. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SE-RRMs][serrm] handles permutations of symbols and [SKiC][skic] composes skills. Compare identity-deduplicated preprocessing plus equivariant augmentation; the candidate is transferring a rule for *when multiplicity is semantic* even when the interface changes how it signals identity. **Cheapest first test:** Draw the same three ballots with one repeated row; a public provenance mark distinguishes an alias from a fourth ballot that can change the result. **Old-task retention:** Include old histogram tasks where equal-valued, distinct entries both count.

### 09. Teach effects separately before a caller combines them — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach the relational effects an action can satisfy without ranking them on the student's behalf. Train under caller objectives where two effects always agree on the winner; provide public contrasts that distinguish the effects on the rejected actions. After rebinding, let new callers make either effect mandatory, and withdraw the contrast supervision. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Decision-Focused Learning][dfl] trains predictions for downstream decisions; [Predictable MDP Abstraction][pma] learns action effects. Compare a multi-objective effect predictor with matched public labels and an exact selector. The possible gain is retaining distinct effect evidence despite training decisions that never reward separating the effects. **Cheapest first test:** Both latency and reversibility favor one action during training; when that action is absent, the two remaining actions trade one effect for the other. **Old-task retention:** Recheck old single-criterion choices where optimizing any additional effect would be wrong.

## B. Teach the budget boundary, not the expert's shopping list (extends #319/09)

### 10. Teach the cheapest exit from each risk range — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** From learner-reachable histories, supervise a small frontier of cost-to-reach-risk targets instead of one first crossing. Withdraw the frontier labels and set a new public tolerance and price table; the learner must choose a route and stop accordingly. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Estimating Conditional Mutual Information for Dynamic Feature Selection][cmi] already handles variable budgets and nonuniform costs; [Active sequential hypothesis testing][active] optimizes speed and error jointly. Compare budget-conditioned acquisition trained on the same oracle work. The candidate is a transferable frontier learned from the student's occupancy, not any one price-conditioned policy. **Cheapest first test:** Use three sensors, one fast/noisy, one slow/accurate and one cheap only after a setup read; test unseen combinations of tolerance and prices. **Old-task retention:** Check original low- and high-precision callers at their original tariffs.

### 11. Teach the cheapest way to beat the actual fallback — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Supervise a first crossing in *excess decision loss relative to an independently predeclared, publicly available fallback*, not absolute confidence. Reassign the fallback in held-out tasks; the same observed risk can justify stopping or more sensing depending on what declining an action would accomplish. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Value of Information][voi] weighs clarification against the stakes of proceeding; [Decision-Focused Learning][dfl] optimizes downstream decisions. Compare a full value-of-information rule with fallback as a competing action and matched estimation work. The teaching candidate is a reusable crossing target when the fallback changes but sensor evidence does not. **Cheapest first test:** The same ambiguous register supports a tentative move in one mode but a cheap safe default in another; evaluate achieved utility as well as coverage. **Old-task retention:** Check both legitimate old abstentions and old low-stakes tasks where deferring is costly.

### 12. Teach a read pair for its joint certificate, not two lucky margins — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train on the shortest affordable collection whose joint outcomes can settle the decision, including queries that have no standalone expected risk reduction. Transfer the dependency between reads, not fixed sensor tokens or the revealed terminal answer. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Adaptive Submodularity][asub] gives greedy guarantees under diminishing returns, not for unrestricted complementary measurements; [Hyperedge Cutting][hec] can search for decision regions. Compare finite lookahead and direct imitation of optimal bundles at equal oracle cost. The candidate is retained knowledge of complementarity under sensor replacement. **Cheapest first test:** Give two cheap XOR shares and one expensive direct read; rename or replace either share, then test under a cap where the pair just fits, plus a world where the pair is redundant. **Old-task retention:** Include old one-read tasks where pairing checks wastes the entire budget.

### 13. Teach which confirmations share a source — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Attach a public lineage test to a learned crossing rule: a certificate needing two independent witnesses cannot be earned by buying two outputs from the same underlying source. Teach how the dependence adjustment changes the *next* affordable query. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Active sequential hypothesis testing][active] chooses sensing from an information state and [Asymptotically Optimal Sequential Testing with Markovian Data][markov] accounts for dependent observations. Compare a dependency-aware exact belief update with acquisition trained at equal work. The scoped gain would be transfer of a source-checking procedure when correlations change but public lineage remains available. **Cheapest first test:** Two sensors either resell one noisy bit or sample independently, with both cases observable through a public source tag; measure false crossings on the shared-source case. **Old-task retention:** Check old tasks in which repeated reads from one device really are fresh measurements.

### 14. Teach the reserve for a query that changes the menu — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Allocate part of the remaining budget to replacement reads before issuing a state-changing query that can remove later observations. Transfer the reason for the reserve across cap changes, rather than copying the expert's first read. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Active sequential hypothesis testing][active] and dynamic programming already handle cost-aware contingent sensing given a model. Compare exact planning with coverage-matched policy distillation; the candidate is a small, reusable instruction for the *opportunity cost of an early observation*, especially on new prices and menus. **Cheapest first test:** Opening a sealed register both reveals a noisy state and destroys a cheap calibration when it jams; prepaying a backup is useful only at some caps. **Old-task retention:** Include previously safe destructive reads that reach the target immediately and should not incur a reserve.

### 15. Teach risk relative to a declared caller mix, not the teacher's prior — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach how a query changes evidence for the two rival actions, then require the learner to recompute whether evidence is enough under a newly supplied public range of caller mixtures. Supervise crossings under several training mixtures without declaring which will be used later. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Blackwell comparison][blackwell] formalizes comparisons of observation experiments, including prior-dependent garbling; [Towards Robust Active Feature Acquisition][robustafa] uses partial-input OOD detection. Compare likelihood-model transfer plus robust acquisition at equal data. The candidate is retaining only the evidence distinctions needed for a family of worst-mixture decisions, not prior correction itself. **Cheapest first test:** Reweight the frequencies of two known register types while holding sensor likelihoods fixed; arrange for a formerly unnecessary read to become indispensable. **Old-task retention:** Check old confident decisions under their original mixtures and old rare cases that legitimately need a longer trace.

### 16. Teach a crossing that survives the way we looked for it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach a policy to choose among rival public evidence-gathering routes, with stopping credit only when a bound is valid for the adaptive route and stopping time actually chosen. Audit its error rate on independent rollouts, including rare route switches. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences][cs] already provide time-uniform inference under stated assumptions; compare an anytime-valid acquisition baseline and exact controlled sensing. The novel bet is whether supervision on route-conditioned certificates transfers to a new query library without bringing the teacher's route optimizer. **Cheapest first test:** Two binary sensors are available under a cap; a public switch determines which sensor's readings depend on the hidden bit and which is pure noise. Compare an error bound valid under adaptive sensor selection with fixed-horizon intervals reused at every read. **Old-task retention:** Verify old one-shot decisions still take one read rather than paying a sequential-test overhead.

### 17. Teach the cheaper question the expert had no reason to ask — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Pick training branches where a query is useful only on the student's public history, not on the privileged trajectory. Supervise the risk reduction on that branch, then remove the branch selector so the learner must find it from public evidence. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [OracleImitator][oracle] distills oracle feature rankings from labels and hidden features; [Privileged Self-Practice][psp] already moves privileged information into sampling. Compare both stacked with cost-aware learner-rollout labels from the same verifier. The candidate is transferring *which deficits require sensing* when later students enter through different prefixes. **Cheapest first test:** The expert starts with a known parity; the student can disambiguate by comparing two visible registers. Alter which preliminary read students usually omit; do not count a crossing on a supplied trace as learned. **Old-task retention:** Check old episodes where parity is supplied normally and an extra comparison is unnecessary.

### 18. Teach a budget boundary without choosing a currency — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Label which public histories support a crossing under componentwise caps on reads and time, without fixing a conversion rate between them. Rebind the resource caps independently across later callers and teach only the non-dominated ways to acquire enough evidence. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Estimating Conditional Mutual Information for Dynamic Feature Selection][cmi] handles varying budgets and feature costs; [Decision-Focused Learning][dfl] optimizes downstream constrained decisions. Compare multi-budget acquisition with matched oracle labels. The candidate is amortizing an unpriced crossing frontier that includes alternatives no linear scalarization selects. **Cheapest first test:** Give query routes with costs (1 read, 6 ticks), (3, 5) and (4, 1), all reaching the same risk; a cap of (3, 5) needs the middle route. **Old-task retention:** Verify old single-currency tasks keep their original cheapest route.

## C. Learn what to bind and what to read in the same decision (bridges #319/02 and #319/09)

### 19. Buy only the part of the dictionary the decision needs — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Maintain candidate bindings consistent with public observations. Stop once they share a sufficiently safe concrete action; otherwise choose an affordable query that separates binding-and-evidence pairs which actually disagree on that action. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Near Optimal Bayesian Active Learning for Decision Making][hec] already solves decision-region acquisition: joint hypotheses over bindings and hidden states are an immediate reduction. The narrower claim is amortized transfer of how to factor those hypotheses across novel schemas; compare Hyperedge Cutting on joint hypotheses plus a learned equivariant parser at the same setup budget. **Cheapest first test:** One calibration read identifies the best button while leaving permutations of two unused actions unresolved; then use a new family where one formerly unused action matters. **Old-task retention:** Run old tasks whose ordinary objective really requires the full mapping.

### 20. Let a later caller decide which distinction was worth saving — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** With a budget shared by successive decisions, teach an acquisition policy not to discard information that is irrelevant to the first answer but can cheaply distinguish a future caller's admissible actions. The caller is drawn after the first action from a published class, never revealed by the hint. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Blackwell comparison][blackwell] already compares reusable information across decision problems, and [Decision-Focused Learning][dfl] trains for downstream utility. Compare dynamic programming for that published caller distribution and multi-task acquisition; the bet is a compact transferable *continuation value of evidence*, not general-purpose information ordering. **Cheapest first test:** Two first reads settle the immediate winner, but only one records the losing objects' relation for a later budgetless audit; include a future-caller class that never asks for it. **Old-task retention:** Check old isolated decisions where keeping the extra evidence is wasted.

### 21. Teach a public anchor that moves with the evidence — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** When a relational rule lacks a reference frame, teach a query procedure to discover a currently valid anchor, not a reserved token. Validate that changing the anchor changes the decision only when it changes the intended relation, and count unsuccessful calibration reads. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Practical Equivariances via Relational Conditional Neural Processes][rcnp] uses invariant relations, and [OracleImitator][oracle] teaches feature acquisition. Compare a public anchor parser plus actively acquired features, not a fixed token baseline alone. The novelty candidate is reuse of an anchor-discovery procedure across interfaces where the cheapest valid anchor changes. **Cheapest first test:** Two zones each give a possible public origin, but one is invalid for cross-zone queries; replace the valid zone after training and include a cap where neither can be established. **Old-task retention:** Give old global-coordinate tasks with a valid native origin and price needless recalibration.

### 22. Buy an observation that changes which rebind is lawful — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Some public evidence tells whether a relabeling is a symmetry at all. Teach an inexpensive applicability check preceding action transport; use the same apparent permutation with a different answer when the check reveals altered effects. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Subequivariant Graph Reinforcement Learning in 3D Environments][sube] restricts symmetry by physical asymmetry, and [Towards Robust Active Feature Acquisition][robustafa] detects OOD from partial observations. Compare explicit context-conditioned symmetry plus robust acquisition. The possible reusable object is a *budgeted test of symmetry applicability*, not a new symmetry architecture. **Cheapest first test:** A public switch swaps two commands in one mode and changes one command's effect in another; only an affordable diagnostic toggle distinguishes the modes. **Old-task retention:** Confirm old intrinsically asymmetric commands still take their normal route.

### 23. Teach query equivalence in effects, not in returned strings — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Reuse a query step only if an available replacement preserves the remaining reachable risk-cost frontier relevant to the rule, even if the two queries encode their observations differently. Train on both true substitutes and plausible copies whose extra noise makes a crossing unaffordable. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Blackwell comparison][blackwell] already formalizes garbling and comparisons across decision problems; [Near Optimal Bayesian Active Learning for Decision Making][hec] can plan with the new query library. Compare exact decision-specific substitution plus replanning. The bet is a compact, learner-usable substitution test that costs less than relearning on every library. **Cheapest first test:** Two query channels reveal the same public order via different encodings; add one cheaper copy with erasures that only fails under tight deadlines. **Old-task retention:** Test old tasks requiring the raw reading, for which the order-only channel is not an acceptable substitute.

### 24. Buy the smallest calibration that still permits a legal act — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach the learner to separate evidence about which *target* it wants from evidence about whether an act can realize that target. Transfer a conditional acquisition program that buys legality last when it can, but first when failure would strand the remaining budget. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Predictable MDP Abstraction][pma] learns restrictions on action effects and [Active sequential hypothesis testing][active] plans what to measure. Compare an explicit legality model plus exact contingent acquisition. The candidate is amortized transfer of a *separation between target uncertainty and realization uncertainty* across changed actuator menus. **Cheapest first test:** A rank test is free in one family, while the read that checks the only viable action's precondition expires early in another. Permute which target needs certification. **Old-task retention:** Include old interfaces where actions are always legal and a verification step would consume the only useful read.

### 25. Teach an evidence-only strategy that survives a late rebind — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train the learner to compress its acquired evidence before the concrete action dictionary is drawn; after the rebind, it must choose correctly from that compressed state without reopening the evidence stream. Withhold new binding families whose legal menus force distinctions absent from the training bindings. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SE-RRMs][serrm] enforces symbol equivariance and [Decision-Focused Learning][dfl] retains features for downstream decisions. Compare a sufficient-statistic baseline for the published target-menu family plus an equivariant actor. The transferable candidate is acquiring the *minimal information sufficient for late-bound action sets*, not storing a low-dimensional feature vector. **Cheapest first test:** In a three-object puzzle, the full order is unnecessary if the best target will always be available, but the runner-up matters when one public menu drops the best. **Old-task retention:** Check old routes where exact ordering of all three objects is required.

### 26. Teach where one more read buys the right to specialize — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Start with the strongest shared rule that is safe across currently compatible interfaces. Offer a narrower rule only when the learner can afford a public read that predicts either a meaningful improvement or the need to stay general. Score the choice of whether to specialize, not which branch the teacher took. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SKiC][skic] composes foundational skills, and [Value of Information][voi] already prices asking before acting. Compare a mixture of known rules with value-of-information routing on the same features. The candidate is reusable evidence about the *payoff of specialization* when the specialized rule denotes different concrete actions after rebinding. **Cheapest first test:** A rule of “choose any safe object” succeeds on every interface, but one affordably identifiable subfamily rewards choosing its rarest safe target; in a negative family the specialization has no gain. **Old-task retention:** Exercise old callers where higher reward for rarity is absent or reversed.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **What must be transported when a new menu is not a permutation?** Start with #01–04 and #25. Cross fresh menus (including aliases, missing targets and noninvertible bindings) with opposite-evidence worlds. Use exact masks and set-valued supervision with an equivariant learner as the stacked control. If they account for the transfer, keep the explicit interface rule rather than adding a teaching object.
2. **Can first-crossing supervision beat oracle query distillation on the learner's own costly trajectories?** Start with #10, #12 and #17. Separate observation prices from evidence likelihoods, and compare OracleImitator, exact finite lookahead and expert-query imitation where each can afford the same work. Demand a cumulative cost crossover on withheld families at equal risk and old-task retention, including no-crossing cases. A gain on privileged traces alone does not count.
3. **Does factoring binding uncertainty buy anything over known decision-region acquisition?** Start with #19, #22 and #24. Use Hyperedge Cutting on joint hypotheses as the primary control, with the same public parser and equal setup. The proposed extra object earns its place only if it reliably transfers across new interfaces at lower total work without turning unsupported bindings into confident guesses.
4. **What evidence is worth acquiring before anyone names the next action set?** Start with #20, #23 and #25. Vary the later caller distribution separately from the later action vocabulary. Compare replanning plus multi-task acquisition and an oracle sufficient statistic. If the extra evidence helps only when the future menu was effectively announced by the teacher, drop the lifetime claim.
