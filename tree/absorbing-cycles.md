# Job-absorbing stable cycles

**Status:** Ideas only; no training runs, code, or GPU work. **Parent:** [Two-address teaching cycles](teaching-cycles.md) ([PR #192](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/192)), cards 12 and 04. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 299](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/1c3db4db61fb5088def38b8efcff34e9b8262a7f/reviews/flood-triage.md#L299) gives both cards a 3: compose two non-attracting ReLU phases into an attracting cycle; serve a held-out job in one off-phase step and keep its effect as a fixed offset. **Read first:** [Idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/1c3db4db61fb5088def38b8efcff34e9b8262a7f/reviews/idea-ledger.md): reject wins by construction, stack the controls, match margins, and check ordinary old tasks in every first test.

## Can a frozen training cycle gain a job without losing what makes it stable?

**Verdict: neither stability by switching nor a displaced fixed point is new.** [Switched-systems stability][switching] and Floquet multipliers already describe a stable composition of unstable phases. [Edge-of-stability oscillation][eos], [cyclical learning rates][clr], and [finite-radius ReLU branch sensitivity][branches] are close comparisons, not evidence of job service. [Return corridors (#168)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/168) ask for one safe re-entry; [GRACE][grace] and [OGD][ogd] preserve edits by a separate store or protected directions. The candidate here is a learned batch whose own dynamics renew useful work, with a single unseen-job update selecting a new attracting orbit of that **same** batch, not an unchanged orbit with an unaccounted memory alongside it. Novelty would require reliable service or better cost at matched retention, not a spectrum or a picture of a loop.

## Three hand checks before proposing a first test

**W1 — mandatory coordinate-swap null (from the parent).** One squared-loss GD step on input `(1, −1)`, label 0 and step size 1 swaps the two coordinates. It gives `(1, 0) → (0, 1) → (1, 0)`, with return derivative `I`. There is no attraction, no learning, and every old linear reader `q` with `q₁ ≠ q₂` alternates its output. A surviving perturbation here is neutral memory, not a job offset in a stable direction. Run W1 for every cycle claim, including merged claims; score it on service, same-phase perturbation decay, and those non-invariant readers.

**W3 — two unstable phases can be honest GD on the same frozen loss.** Let `[z]₊ = max(0,z)`. Use a two-parameter host with trainable biases `x,y` and fixed linear/ReLU readouts, and minimize the sum of squared residuals

`L = (5/8)((x + 4/5)² + (y − 4/5)²) + (7/8)([−x]₊² + [y]₊²)`.

Unit-step GD sends `a = (−3/2, −2)` to `b = (2, 3/2)` and back. In the negative quadrant the step is `(−2x − 1, −y/4 + 1)`; in the positive quadrant it is `(−x/4 − 1, −2y + 1)`. The phase Jacobians `A = diag(−2, −1/4)` and `B = diag(−1/4, −2)` each have spectral radius 2; `BA = I/2`. Perturbations of sup norm less than 1/2 about either phase stay in the intended masks on the first step (phase gain ≤ 2 and opposite phase's distance to either axis ≥ 3/2), then halve every two steps. “Unstable phase” means one locally expanding eigendirection of the frozen-mask map, not expansion in every direction. This witness even has commuting Jacobians: noncommutativity is not necessary. It earns **no** service or retention credit. By contrast, a single affine update `T(z)=Mz+c` with a distinct 2-cycle must have `M²(b−a)=b−a`, so no such cycle attracts in the full space.

**W4 — what a fixed stable offset must mean.** In a common affine return chart, a one-shot perturbation `d₀` obeys `dₖ=Mᵏd₀ → 0` when `ρ(M)<1`. Thus there is no nonzero asymptotic offset in the same contracting affine chart with unchanged forcing; a long transient is not persistence. A *different*, locally attracting itinerary of the **same** frozen map can instead have a different affine return map. For a phase pair with the same slopes but job-selected intercepts differing by `c₀,c₁`, the two same-phase displacements satisfy `d₁=A d₀+c₀`, `d₀=B d₁+c₁`, hence `d₀=(I−BA)⁻¹(Bc₀+c₁)`. These are offsets between attracting orbits, not non-decaying perturbations of either orbit. With W3 slopes, a stable return can still amplify intercept error by a factor of 2. The algebra is only a target: the step must actually reach the new itinerary and both displaced points must satisfy its mask inequalities. A job-conditioned intercept supplied every period is repeated forcing, not one-shot absorption; a separate selector slot is a charged memory baseline, not evidence for a coupled mechanism. If the orbit in the full state really is unchanged up to phase, a fixed phase-aware readout cannot retain new information about that job.

### Shared contract for first tests

- Freeze the truthful batch, loss, optimizer, hyperparameters, and ordinary execution before the stream. Only the single off-phase gradient step may see the new job; use a sealed test split of that job for readout. Retire the job data afterward. Do not install a job-specific readout, phase clock, residual, or optimizer slot without charging it as state. Changes of activation region (or a live address) must be determined by the host.
- For switching, report real distinct phases, mask margins, both single-phase spectra, the ordered product, and an actual invariant two-step neighborhood. Measure held-out per-half service after source retirement versus a no-step trace. Compare W1, the single affine obstruction, switched-system synthesis, [trajectory matching][mtt], one-contracting-half batches, and a scheduler plus [ReDo][redo]. Same gradients, examples, extra scalar state and audit cost where applicable; show the tradeoff rather than claiming a budget-free control.
- For absorption, measure job gain immediately and after 1, 8 and 64 returns, at both phases; verify attraction to the displaced orbit after a second small disturbance. Compare return corridors, plain fine-tuning plus re-entry, self-seeding without attraction (#184 card 11), an equal-norm write into the expanding phase direction, projected fine-tuning, and a size-matched residual/cache. Include an identical final-state/job-label shuffle or an uninformative write as a no-service control where applicable. Repeated job inputs, readout changes and slow decay do not pass.
- **A** is the frozen suite of ordinary old tasks (include phase-sensitive and near-margin items); **C** is the previously served job suite. Match pre-write performance and per-item margins, then report worst-phase A and C during the excursion, on return and in the tail. Report absolute task scores. For a merge, beat the best switching control **stacked with** the best absorb-and-store control, with both allowed the same budget.

## Idea cards

### 01. Learn complementary unstable masks — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Search frozen batches for masks that expand different directions and contract each other's expansion while each half renews a different job carrier. **Novelty / control.** W3 and [switched synthesis][switching] give stability for free; the object to earn is a batch that also performs blind work, beyond MTT and an external ReDo schedule. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two-unit ReLU batches with rational inputs and truthful labels; require two distinct masks, both unstable, bounded return, and a held-out per-half loss reduction versus all shared controls. **Old-task retention.** Test A off both masks' common invariant readout, and C after 64 returns.

### 02. Make the job choose the attractor, not the next minibatch — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** One blind-job gradient step crosses a basin boundary; the unchanged batch settles into a translated two-cycle that implements the job. **Novelty / control.** Basin entry is [reachability][reach]; only fixed, measurable service on the new orbit beyond a return corridor plus size-matched GRACE is interesting. **Cheapest first test.** Search a tiny ReLU host for two cyclic basins with the same period and a fixed output distinction; present one labelled example of each blind job and no more. Reject if returning to the original itinerary explains the trace. **Old-task retention.** Score A in the boundary-crossing step and A/C at both new phases.

### 03. Couple the offset to the unstable carrier — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let the job alter the intercepts along the same weights that generate phase expansion, rather than append an inactive register. **Novelty / control.** [OGD][ogd] protects a spare subspace; coupling counts only if it saves state or service relative to the best separated cycle plus residual. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a three-coordinate host; constrain the job displacement to the carrier coordinates, use W4 to predict it, then clamp each coordinate in turn. Tie to the split model kills the merge. **Old-task retention.** Compare A readers of the shared weights and rerun C after each clamp.

### 04. Make each phase repair the other's error — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use noncommuting phases to rotate a job-induced error away from the next phase's expanding axis, and use its predictable transient to serve that phase's job. **Novelty / control.** Ordered products are standard Floquet analysis; held-out work tied to the rotation rather than just a small multiplier is the claim. **Cheapest first test.** Compare ordered, mask-frozen, and commuting W3-like controls at matched return rate and update norm; toggle only the cross-coordinate terms. **Old-task retention.** Track old mixed-coordinate readers at peak excursion and tail.

### 05. Spend a kink crossing to earn a safe return neighborhood — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose the batch so a thin region routes incoming writes into a wider certified two-phase tube. **Novelty / control.** [Hybrid regions of attraction][hybrid] and [provable repair][aprnn] already certify sets; here the batch creates a useful entrance around a self-renewing carrier. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate mask cells in two dimensions; compare certified safe write area, including useful jobs, against a return corridor plus ordinary fine-tuning. **Old-task retention.** Test old near-margin readers at the tube's worst intermediate points, not just its center.

### 06. Compile different jobs to the same safe itinerary — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Teach a family of truthful one-step job gradients to land in a common itinerary when their required answers agree, without fitting separate adapters. **Novelty / control.** GRACE already groups edits by key neighborhood; a basin is only an improvement in storage if it generalizes to untrained keys without a router. **Cheapest first test.** Hold out transformations of two job families. Compare sealed-test generalization and collisions to GRACE at equal bytes; falsify on opposite-answer jobs sent to one basin. **Old-task retention.** Check A lookalikes and earlier C jobs near each collision boundary.

### 07. Keep the displacement useful in both phases without a clock — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Shape the two intercept shifts so one frozen task readout makes the correct decision at both displaced points even when their offsets differ. **Novelty / control.** The parent protects a contrast across overwrites; here a new job must remain readable at both phases. Beat an oracle phase bit plus phase-specific adapter at charged state, not just phase-naive inference. **Cheapest first test.** Fit the batch before revealing one of two job labels; compare both-phase margins to W1 and the charged adapter. **Old-task retention.** A/C must pass both phases with no phase metadata at read time.

### 08. Teach order-resilient absorption without a write log — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Arrange basin transitions so two compatible off-phase writes compose to the same service contract in either order; incompatible writes should be distinguishable. **Novelty / control.** Commuting updates and replay already remove order sensitivity; the gain would be two lasting commitments on a cycle at less stored state. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust the two-job, two-order table plus a conflict pair; compare projected updates and a two-item replay buffer, not a Lyapunov proxy alone. **Old-task retention.** A throughout and C after each job in each order, not just the shared endpoint.

### 09. Trigger a job step from either phase — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Give both phase-specific job gradients a route into the same displaced orbit (possibly at opposite phases), so admission does not have to wait. **Novelty / control.** [Reachability][reach] can synthesize a controller; the useful object is a one-example write with no phase-dependent extra action or phase bit. **Cheapest first test.** Vary arrival phase for each sealed job; compare waiting one step or applying a phase-indexed projected update with wait and state charged. **Old-task retention.** Worst A during the two entrance paths and C after return from each.

### 10. Reuse the unstable half as a write amplifier — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Enter just before the expanding phase so one small truthful step reaches a useful alternate itinerary that an equally sized direct update cannot reach. **Novelty / control.** [Edge-of-stability][eos] and [cyclic LR][clr] already amplify updates; rule out larger effective LR and threshold crossing alone. **Cheapest first test.** Match peak update norm to a larger-LR direct write, flip the arrival phase and compare lasting service; discard if only initial job loss differs. **Old-task retention.** Test A for the amplified transient and C for collateral basin switches.

### 11. Store an interval of job values, not just a basin bit — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use many distinct attracting itineraries to quantize a continuous target; recover its value with one fixed readout and count resolvable offsets. **Novelty / control.** Quantized attractor memory is known; it must buy more useful precision per host byte than GRACE, a lookup table, or a scalar adapter. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate reachable stable branches on a one-dimensional slice; test unseen target values between training values, with measured safe basin widths. **Old-task retention.** Keep A near quantization cutoffs and test C after adjacent-bin writes.

### 12. Make overwriting an absorbed job reversible by a truthful counterexample — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A single corrected example moves the state to an attractor that implements the correction, while other jobs persist. No snapshot or restore operation. **Novelty / control.** Rollback and replaceable adapters are already practical; displacement earns its keep only if revision is local in job behavior, not a basin-wide reset. **Cheapest first test.** Do two compatible jobs and one correction; compare direct fine-tuning and saved residual replacement at equal bytes and gradient calls. **Old-task retention.** A and the uncorrected C job remain intact; score the corrected job against its new target.

### 13. Move a job effect between two certified carriers — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Find two stable itineraries whose phase offsets implement the same job, then let one new write move the effect to the other before the first is overwritten. **Novelty / control.** [Backward-compatible representations][bct] and the parent's overwrite compensation preserve reads; here relocation must trade no extra copy for a newly usable carrier. **Cheapest first test.** In a three-address host, force a later job to overwrite the first itinerary's carrier; compare two copies plus ordinary re-entry at equal bytes. **Old-task retention.** A in each address and C before, during, and after the relocation.

### 14. Learn a basin that rejects nuisance writes but absorbs job writes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Put equal-norm job-relevant perturbations across the basin boundary and nuisance perturbations safely inside the original contracting tube. **Novelty / control.** [OGD][ogd] and robust reachability already separate safe directions; benefit requires the distinction to transfer to off-phase gradients not used to design the tube. **Cheapest first test.** Interleave real and label-shuffled job gradients of matched magnitude; compare a projected updater plus residual, measuring false promotions and missed jobs. **Old-task retention.** Score A under rejected writes and A/C after accepted ones.

### 15. Earn protection through phase cancellation, not a frozen subspace — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let old logits move on each half but stay inside their decision margins, and make the disturbances cancel on return. **Novelty / control.** [OGD][ogd]/[GPM][gpm] suppress old directions per update; phase cancellation is only useful if it leaves more room for held-out service at the same worst-phase retention. **Cheapest first test.** Vary old-reader margins with fixed old-task accuracy; compare substep projection to full-period cancellation at equal job gain and worst-phase error. **Old-task retention.** A includes both small-margin decisions and absolute loss; assess C without averaging over phases.

### 16. Admit work by the whole excursion, not the return multiplier — improvement (certificate)
**Mechanism.** Export a per-phase bound on unsafe transient growth and allowable entry perturbations alongside the two-step contraction. **Novelty / control.** [Hybrid reachability][hybrid] already checks trajectories; improvement is an audit cheaper than replay, not a new stability primitive. **Cheapest first test.** Perturb W3 towards the expanding axis and towards a gate; compare the certificate with an endpoint-only test and exhaustive two-step rollout. **Old-task retention.** Count A violations an endpoint-only check misses, and rejected safe C writes.

### 17. Carry a write across a corrupted batch execution — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make a batch with an attraction tube that recovers after one evaluation drops or duplicates a single example's gradient, without replaying the held-out job. **Novelty / control.** Switched-system robustness is known; the claim is recovery of the job displacement under an execution fault, not just recovery of period. A whole-step no-op or duplication is only a clock shift under autonomous execution and is not evidence. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate single-example faults at both phases and in the first post-write return; compare a scheduler with a charged checkpoint. **Old-task retention.** A during fault and recovery, C after both phases recur.

### 18. Learn the cheapest batch that still works when the source is retired — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Distill the useful return structure into examples whose cyclic job service survives removal of all source jobs from training access. **Novelty / control.** [MTT][mtt] already matches finite trajectories; test whether certifying the indefinitely repeated map buys held-out service beyond matching the same two-phase sequence. **Cheapest first test.** Choose batches with equal two-step loss and example count; compare attraction and service after retirement over the shared horizon against MTT and refill. **Old-task retention.** A/C after retirement, with no examples from either suite in the tiny batch.

### 19. Predict offset sensitivity without fitting a rollout — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** Use W4's inverse to predict when a small intercept change creates unacceptable displacement, then check that the predicted shifted orbit stays in its masks. **Novelty / control.** This is fixed-point sensitivity and [ReLU branch analysis][branches], not a new memory. It must beat a cheap short rollout as an admission check (the ledger's Lyapunov warning). **Cheapest first test.** Compare predictions for held-out perturbations to two-return and 64-return outcomes at matched probe calls, including chart exits. **Old-task retention.** Report false approvals measured by worst A/C loss, rather than correlation with offset size.

### 20. Separate job capacity from a slowly leaking mode — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** Bound the tail analytically inside the certified chart and count jointly serviceable jobs after comparing against a near-unit return multiplier. **Novelty / control.** Multi-timescale traces and W1 can appear persistent at fixed horizons; this is a falsifier, not a new memory method. **Cheapest first test.** Fit equal 8-return job scores to a shifted attractor, a slow-decay control, and a W1 register; compare tail predictions and check with one perturb-and-recover test. **Old-task retention.** A and every prior C after the near-unit mode has had time to decay; flag any phase-only retention.

### 21. Give an independent editor a one-step job admission contract — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Export allowed write sets with predicted displaced phases, service readout margins, and transient A bounds; an editor uses only the contract and a new example to choose whether to write. **Novelty / control.** [Certificate entry ports (#171)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/171) inherit proofs on entry; this port must predict where a *new* lasting behavior lives without an editor-specific cycle. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze the contract on one job generator; compare another generator against a two-return trial and return-corridor admission at the same query budget. **Old-task retention.** Check A and prior C on every admitted write and report false acceptances separately.

### 22. Ask whether two unstable halves actually buy absorption — primitive candidate (merge)
**Mechanism.** Match the job-selected offset and return rate across hosts, then use complementary phase expansion to make an otherwise unreachable stable itinerary accessible in one step. **Novelty / control.** Neither [switched stability][switching] nor an attractor offset proves an interaction; beat the best scheduler/one-contracting-half cycle **plus** the best equal-state residual, not either part alone. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate paired two- and three-coordinate hosts and ablate expansion while matching margins and write norms; require extra useful one-step jobs, not a different spectral radius. **Old-task retention.** Worst A and C across job orders, excursions and both asymptotic phases.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can one honest blind-job step select a different attracting itinerary without smuggling in a memory slot?** Start with 02, 03 and 20. Establish the common-chart no-go, then demand a nonzero fixed phase-aligned offset, mask feasibility, job gain after retirement, and recovery from a second perturbation. If the offset is neutral, decays, or ties a size-matched adapter, stop the strong absorption claim.
2. **Can two unstable ReLU phases earn work that one stable phase or a scheduler cannot?** Start with 01, 04 and 18. W3 settles existence without settling utility. Demand held-out per-half service and A/C beyond W1, switched synthesis, MTT, and a ReDo schedule; stop if a one-contracting-half batch matches the frontier.
3. **Does switching enlarge the safe one-step job set, or do the two seeds merely coexist?** Start with 09, 14, 16 and 22 after the first two questions pass. Compare matched return rates and margins under the stacked control; a gain in basin reachability that disappears when transient A is checked does not count.
4. **How many commitments remain independently usable after later jobs arrive?** Start with 08, 11, 12 and 21. Measure simultaneous C, order sensitivity, safe revisions and absolute A per byte, not nominal basin count. If the certificate cannot predict blind admissions better than trial returns, or later jobs erase earlier ones at the same rate as plain fine-tuning, keep the single-write result only.

These are nearest comparisons, not an exhaustive novelty claim. The first gate is a tiny exact or enumerated CPU witness that beats simple controls on work and retention, not another oscillation plot.
