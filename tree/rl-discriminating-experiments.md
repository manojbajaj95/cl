# RL on OOD → learners that design discriminating experiments

**Parent:** [RL-on-OOD node, PR #51](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/51), branch 3 ("identifiability curriculum"). RL can expand or shrink strategy coverage, and OPSD token scores can be unrelated to verifier outcomes. Neither fixes the identification failure: two worlds with the same source action–reward table can rank actions oppositely after a shift.

**Question:** Can a learner generate the cheapest experiment that tells a transferable rule apart from a shortcut, and learn continually from those experiments?

**Working diagnosis (short):** Selecting a discriminating input from a given pool is mostly solved. Three things are missing: (1) *naming the rival*: every working method needs the shortcut as an explicit hypothesis, but a trained learner holds it only implicitly in its weights; (2) *a pre-shift reward* for experiment design, since the value of a discriminating experiment is realized only after the shift; (3) *keeping the result*: experiments are consumed as ordinary training data and their discrimination is erased by the next task. Our CPU probe shows (3) clearly. Even oracle-chosen cue-conflict queries leave rule A at chance after learning rule B.

## Why current approaches can't do this

| Family | What it does | What it lacks for this question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bayesian experimental design with LLMs: [BED-LLM 2508.21184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21184), [BMD 2610.25747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25747), [Model Discovery Agent 2608.09696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09696) | Choose experiments by expected information gain over an explicit hypothesis pool (BMD: the LLM proposes model families and a numerical engine fits them and runs EIG) | The hypotheses are *about the world*, written out in the open. The learner's *own* shortcut is never a member of the pool. Learning lives in a numerical engine or the context, not in weights; there is no continual retention across tasks. |
| Amortized BED by RL: [2610.20832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20832), [2607.03426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03426) | RLVR with EIG reward under a generative model *specified independently of the LLM's beliefs*; transfers from Twenty Questions to Medi-Q | The reward needs a known hypothesis space and likelihood. The skill learned is "ask good questions about an external target", not "find the input that separates my rule from my shortcut". |
| Active learning for spurious correlations: Tamkin et al. 2022; [CAML 2605.20771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20771) | Uncertainty acquisition picks cue-conflict points; CAML notes that appended queries are "diluted by the much larger labeled set and can be easily memorized", and meta-learns the inductive bias from them (+27.8% Dominoes, +29.9% Waterbirds minority accuracy) | Selection from a *fixed pool* that already contains conflicts; no generation, no cost model. The inductive bias is meta-learned per benchmark; it is not retained across a task stream. |
| Diverse-hypothesis ensembles: [DivDis 2202.03418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03418), [D-BAT 2202.04414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04414), multi-hypothesis TTA [2607.00259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00259) | Force heads to disagree on unlabeled data, then disambiguate with a few labels | They need an unlabeled pool where the rule and the shortcut come apart. They do not generate experiments or price them, and the head that loses is thrown away. |
| Self-play curricula: [SOAR 2601.18778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18778), [MagicBeanStalk 2610.12659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12659), [Skill Self-Play 2607.22529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22529), [Self-Play Pretraining 2609.30063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30063) | A teacher generates problems; SOAR grounds the teacher's reward in student progress on hard real problems | Reward is *progress on a target set*, and that set inherits the source shortcut, so nothing pushes the teacher to break it. Learnability rewards prefer exactly the problems the shortcut makes easy. |
| Counterfactual RL / shortcut mitigation in LLMs: [CFPO 2606.23206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23206), [GC²PO 2602.06475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06475), [DynaCF 2606.09043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09043), [CF test-time augmentation 2610.33904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33904), [Spurious Tool Use 2609.16268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16268) | Hand-designed counterfactual perturbations, or ones from a known nuisance list | A human names the nuisance. The learner does not discover which perturbation is the discriminating one, or the cheapest. |
| Falsification in LLM agents: [Failing to Falsify 2604.02485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02485), [FalsifyBench 2606.04751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04751), [2605.05851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05851), [FGSE 2611.14326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14326), [EvoBelief 2610.12789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12789) | LLMs show confirmation bias in 2-4-6 rule discovery. Counterexample prompting lifts discovery from 42% to 56%, and distilling it generalizes to the Blicket test. FGSE gates memory writes on transfer, falsification and regression probes | The falsifying tests are about rules *stated in text*, not about the shortcut the weights picked up. FGSE's probes are fixed per lesson rather than designed by a cost-aware learner, and nothing is written to weights. |

### Root cause

A discriminating experiment is defined relative to a **pair** (rule, rival): the cheapest `x` where `f_rule(x) ≠ f_rival(x)`, divided by its cost. Current learners lack all three parts of that object:

1. **No rival.** Gradient training leaves one function. The shortcut is not a separable hypothesis the learner can query or design against. Every working method (BED, BMD, DivDis, counterfactual RL) gets the rival from outside: a hypothesis pool, an unlabeled conflict pool, or a human-named nuisance.
2. **No pre-shift reward.** The value of a discriminating experiment shows up only after the shift. Progress, learnability and EIG-on-an-external-model rewards are measured on the source world, where the shortcut is optimal (the PR #51 exact counterexample: a 92.62% source optimum gives 13.33% on the target).
3. **No durable record.** An experiment's outcome enters weights as one more labeled point, so the discrimination is diluted and then overwritten by the next task. The learner keeps neither the experiment nor the fact that the rival was refuted.

### CPU probe (this PR)

`tree/probes/discriminating_experiments.py` (NumPy, 5 seeds, 97 s, results in `discriminating_experiments_results.json`). Source: label = rule on 8 rule bits; cue bit equals the label 98% of the time. A small MLP is trained, then 1–32 labeled queries are taken from a pool with about 10% cue-conflict points and the model is fine-tuned on them. Retention: then learn a new rule B and re-test rule A.

| Acquisition | Conflict share of 32 queries | Queries to 90% on cue-flipped A (per seed; * = >32) | A flipped / aligned @32 | A flipped / aligned after learning B | B accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| random | 8.1% | *,*,*,*,* | 45.3% / 100.0% | 49.9% / 54.0% | 95.5% |
| entropy | 100.0% | *,*,32,*,* | 80.8% / 91.2% | 53.8% / 53.9% | 93.8% |
| committee (5 seeds) | 98.8% | 30,*,16,22,* | 92.1% / 96.1% | 54.7% / 53.4% | 94.0% |
| DivDis-style 2-head | 29.4% | *,*,*,*,* | 61.9% / 99.7% | 51.1% / 54.4% | 94.6% |
| oracle (conflicts only) | 100.0% | 21,24,28,*,23 | 95.5% / 95.0% | 54.2% / 53.1% | 94.4% |

What it says, and its limits:
- **The confidence-collapse story is wrong here.** The base model is wrong on 67.9% of conflicts, but its entropy there is 0.547 nats vs 0.121 on aligned inputs, and entropy sampling picks conflicts every time (consistent with Tamkin et al.). *Selection from a pool is not the bottleneck.*
- **Even perfect experiments are expensive.** The oracle needs 21–28 conflict points to reach 90% (1 seed never does): each experiment is diluted by ~thousands of source points (CAML's point).
- **Retention is the clear failure.** After learning B, every arm, oracle included, falls to chance on A (50–55% on both splits). The discrimination was not kept anywhere.
- No ensemble member was rule-like or a pure cue follower: the "rival" exists only as a blend inside one function.
- Limits: a fixed-pool synthetic probe with an MLP. It does not test experiment *generation*, costs, or LLMs. The DivDis arm uses a two-head MI penalty, not the full method.

So the open problem is not "which point to query". It is: **make the rival explicit, reward discrimination before the shift, and keep what the experiment proved.**

## Raw ideas (unfiltered)

Each: mechanism / why new / cheapest first test (all first tests include the retention check: learn rule B afterwards, re-test A on cue-flipped and aligned splits, vs. equal-budget replay).

1. **Shadow-rival head.** Alongside the learner, train a deliberately lazy twin (early-stopped, low-rank, or simplicity-penalized) that fits the source using the easiest features. The experiment is `argmax_x |main(x) − rival(x)| / cost(x)` over generated inputs. **New:** the learner keeps its own shortcut as a named, queryable object rather than receiving one from outside. **Test:** probe world with generated inputs (bit flips from source points, cost = Hamming distance); queries to 90% and Hamming cost vs. entropy over the same generated set.
2. **Witness sets as memory.** Store every experiment that once split rule from rival as a *witness* (input, verified label, which rival it killed). Before any later write, check the witnesses and project the update away from witness violations. **New:** a minimal certificate of a rule, kept as the unit of continual memory instead of replayed data. **Test:** after A, keep 8 witnesses; learn B; compare A-flipped retention with 8 random replay points and with 8 oracle conflicts used as plain replay.
3. **Witness-to-constraint compiler.** Turn a witness set into a weight-space constraint: the subspace in which outputs on the witnesses stay fixed (Jacobian null space on witnesses only), with a rank budget. **New:** constraints selected by discrimination value, not by task gradients (unlike OGD/GEM). **Risk:** may tie OGD on the same points. **Test:** OGD on all A data vs. OGD on 8 witnesses vs. EWC; A-flipped retention per parameter of constraint rank.
4. **Minimal-edit experiment synthesizer trained by RL.** A generator edits a source instance; reward = verifier-confirmed disagreement between main and rival minus λ·edit cost. **New:** "cheapest discriminating experiment" as a learned operator with a pre-shift, verifiable reward (fixes root cause 2 without a target set). **Test:** symbolic DSL from PR #51; edits-per-discrimination vs. random edits and vs. BED over a hand-written pool.
5. **Shortcut vectors from paired training.** Fine-tune twice from the same checkpoint: once on source data and once on source with the cue shuffled. The weight difference is a *shortcut vector*. Design experiments by input-gradient ascent on sensitivity along that vector. **New:** a task vector for a shortcut, used to design experiments rather than to subtract. **Test:** probe; cosine of the designed inputs with true conflicts; queries to 90% vs. entropy.
6. **Self-split environments, then fill the cross cells.** Partition the source data to maximize an IRM penalty (EIIL-style), read off the inferred nuisance, and generate inputs in the empty cue × rule cells. **New:** generation into the cells the data never filled, not reweighting. **Risk:** EIIL exists; only the generation step is new. **Test:** cross-cell coverage and queries to 90%.
7. **Discrimination-grounded teacher (SOAR with a different reward).** Reward the problem-generating teacher by the number of student rival-hypotheses refuted per verifier call, measured on held-out *generated* conflicts, not on a fixed target set. **New:** grounding by discrimination instead of progress, so the shortcut no longer rewards the teacher. **Test:** small DSL, 0.5B student; compare with SOAR-style progress reward on post-shift accuracy.
8. **Hypothesis market across tasks.** Keep K hypothesis heads that bet on experiment outcomes (log score). Choose the experiment by payout spread per cost; bankrupt heads are archived, not deleted, and re-enter if a later task pays them. **New:** a persistent, priced population of rivals spanning tasks, in which a refuted shortcut is kept and can win again. **Test:** A→B→A′ stream where the cue becomes valid in A′; reacquisition speed vs. fresh training.
9. **Refutation self-play.** A Conjecturer writes a candidate rule program; a Refuter must find the cheapest input where the student's behavior differs from the program *and* the verifier sides with the program. The student trains on refutations only. **New:** self-play whose reward is falsifying the learner's own behavior at minimal cost, not solving or proposing problems. **Test:** 2-4-6-style triples with Qwen2.5-0.5B; confirmation ratio (2604.02485 metric) and held-out rule discovery, plus retention on an earlier rule family.
10. **Learned experiment-cost model.** Learn the real cost (tokens, verifier latency, environment risk) of each experiment type online, and optimize EIG per unit cost. **New:** "cheapest" made a learned quantity that transfers across tasks. **Test:** three experiment types with hidden costs; total cost to 90% vs. EIG alone.
11. **Identifiability-debt ledger.** After each task, record which feature pairs the data could not tell apart: train two learners with opposite inductive biases and log where they agree on the source but split off it. When a later task's data would resolve a debt, run that experiment first. **New:** unresolved underspecification as a continual-learning state carried across tasks. **Test:** 5-task stream where task 4 data resolves task 1's ambiguity; final accuracy on task 1 cue-flipped vs. no ledger.
12. **`do()` action tokens.** Give the policy an action `do(feature := value)` that re-runs the environment on a perturbed instance at a cost; RL decides when to use it before answering. **New:** experimentation as part of the policy's own action space, learned from outcome reward and amortized into weights. **Test:** probe world as an RL environment; post-shift accuracy, `do()` count, and whether the habit survives learning task B.
13. **Experiment templates that transfer.** Shortcuts recur across tasks (position, length, lexical overlap). Store each experiment that killed a shortcut as a parameterized template (perturbation type + slot) and instantiate it on new tasks. **New:** an archive of *experiment designs*, not data or edits (compare PR #51 idea 18, which learns an experimenter over weight writes). **Test:** 3 tasks sharing a position cue; queries to 90% on task 3 with templates vs. cold start.
14. **Symmetry-violation search.** A transferable rule should be invariant under some group of transforms. Search for the cheapest transform under which the learner's prediction changes but the verifier's label does not; that transform is the experiment, and the group found is stored across tasks. **New:** the experiment is a transform rather than an input, and the learned symmetry group is the memory. **Test:** probe with cue-bit permutations as the hidden symmetry; transforms needed vs. random augmentation.
15. **Internal experiments before external ones.** Run activation-patching experiments (patch the cue representation from a conflicting input) to predict which external experiment will discriminate. Spend verifier calls only on predicted splits. **New:** internal interventions as cheap substitutes for verifier calls, scored by how well they predict external outcomes. **Test:** MLP probe; verifier calls to 90% with and without a patching pre-screen.
16. **Two-world LoRA posterior.** Keep a rule adapter and a rival adapter with posterior weights updated by experiment outcomes. Inference mixes them, and a refuted adapter is frozen, not deleted. **New:** the rule/shortcut ambiguity held in weight space as a posterior over adapters, reversible if the world flips back. **Test:** A→A′(cue valid)→A stream; reacquisition steps vs. one adapter.
17. **Minimal-pair compiler as a skill.** An LLM writes a program that perturbs one attribute of a prompt while holding the rest fixed. Pairs the verifier confirms become a skill library of "experiment programs", reused on later tasks. **New:** experiment generators as persistent, executable agent skills, rather than task-solving skills. **Test:** two text tasks with a lexical shortcut; discriminations per LLM call on task 2 with the library vs. without.
18. **Coverage–confidence mismatch trigger.** Experiment only where the learner is confident but the source data in that region is narrow (low feature diversity). **New:** a trigger for "confident because of a shortcut" that does not depend on entropy. Entropy works in our probe, but it may fail in LLMs. **Test:** probe plus a calibrated-shortcut variant in which entropy is low on conflicts; trigger recall vs. entropy.
19. **Differentiable experiment design.** Relax the input space (Gumbel-softmax for discrete inputs) and take the gradient of the main–rival disagreement minus cost with respect to the input to synthesize experiments directly. **New:** one backward pass replaces search. **Risk:** gradient-based BED exists for continuous designs. **Test:** probe; synthesis steps vs. idea 4's RL synthesizer.
20. **Experiment-weighted consolidation.** During later tasks, weight the replay of each past experiment by the number of rivals it refuted, which concentrates retention on witnesses. **New:** retention priority set by discrimination value, not by loss or recency. **Test:** same as idea 2 with a 32-point mixed replay buffer.
21. **Rival resurrection test.** Periodically re-fit the rival from scratch on current data. If it fits, the old discrimination has been forgotten; re-run the stored witnesses. **New:** detecting forgetting by checking whether the shortcut can return, not whether accuracy has dropped. **Test:** detect the A collapse in the probe at a lower verifier cost than periodic A evaluation.

## Ranked deeper sub-questions

1. **Can a witness set (the few experiments that refuted a rival) keep a rule through later learning better than equal-size replay or OGD?** The probe's clearest failure: every arm, including oracle queries, drops A to chance after B. Ideas 2, 3, 20, 21. Cheapest test: the existing probe plus witness storage; dead if 8 witnesses do not beat 8 random replay points by ≥10 pp on A-flipped after B.
2. **Can a learner keep an explicit rival hypothesis of its own, cheaply, so experiments are designed against its own shortcut rather than a given pool?** Ideas 1, 5, 8, 16. Cheapest test: shadow-rival head with generated bit-flip inputs. It must match oracle queries-to-90% without any conflict pool.
3. **Is there a pre-shift, verifiable reward for experiment design that transfers across task families?** Reward = refuted rivals per unit cost, versus progress (SOAR) or external EIG. Ideas 4, 7, 9, 10. Cheapest test: DSL with a 0.5B generator; transfer of the trained experimenter to a held-out shortcut family.
4. **Can internal interventions (activation patching) stand in for verifier calls when choosing the experiment?** Ideas 15, 18. Cheapest test: MLP probe; dead if the pre-screen does not cut verifier calls to 90% by ≥2×.
5. **Can unresolved identifiability be carried as continual-learning state and paid off by later tasks?** Ideas 11, 13, 14. A longer-horizon bet; needs a multi-task stream with planted delayed resolutions.
