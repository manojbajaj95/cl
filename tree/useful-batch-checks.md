# Useful-batch checks: decide before clipping erases the difference

**Status:** Ideas only; one exact paper fixture, no learner runs or GPU work. **Parent:** [Check as work](check-as-work.md) ([PR #253][parent]), especially cards 01, 03, 05, 06 and 08. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 378][triage] scores round-56 [TCM5][tcm5] at 3: use a required C batch to distinguish harmless from harmful claim violations before a prior opener clips away the evidence. TCM5 joins the parent's truthful check with [#249 cards 14/17/19][clips] (transport the decision, respect actual precision, move the audit cut) and [#232 card 18][shrink] (detect before shrink). **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger]: measure normal old-task service, match margins and budgets, and make a merge beat the strongest stack of its parts.

**Verdict:** A batch can safely do useful work on both sides of a disputed admission and still reveal which side the admission is on. That is only a joint advantage if it opens a legal schedule that the best budget-matched stack of batch selection, early checking and scoped evidence cannot match. A truthful batch, an authenticated claim and a safe post-clip model do not establish that a pre-clip right was safe to grant.

## Contract and falsifier

- **Decide the right, not whether every word of the claim was true.** Pin the requested right, backed obligation, deadline, allowed suffixes and acquisition-anchored floors before blind arrivals. For a frozen candidate prefix, distinguish compliant histories, harmless violations (out of claim but the named right remains backed), and harmful violations (spending that right can break the obligation in a declared continuation). Use the native optimizer, including slots, waits and clip order. A history can be harmless for one right and harmful for another. An inconclusive observation does not establish harmlessness.
- **Use only due work.** A = original tasks, B = installed work and backed obligations, C = installed and incoming successor tasks. Freeze the truthful, independently required C menu, legal reorders, reportable statistics and published precision. The checking step must serve C and preserve the required old floors on *all* plausible histories, including the ones on which the requested right will be refused. No fabricated examples, hidden state reads, diagnostic-only steps or sandbox forwards in the proposed operation. Charge any planning, verification or telemetry cost. An ordinary read must be trusted or independently available, not supplied only by the claimant.
- **Decide before reliance, observe before erasure.** For batch b and public prefix p, group reachable histories by the verifier's whole observable view after b, using actual quantization, rounding and nuisance ranges. An observation containing both allowable and harmful histories cannot authorize the right. For full coverage, the allowable and harmful observation sets must be disjoint; a partial checker may refuse mixed cells and report lost service. If an earlier legal opener merges the classes, move to a still-due separating batch before that opener, or obtain verified evidence *before* the loss for only the affected right. Bind any retained evidence to the right, cut, claim and optimizer/precision version; drop it only after the decision and dependent obligations no longer need it. If neither option works, refuse the right or use an independently backed fallback; do not discard the useful C update merely because the claim failed.
- **Make the strongest stack compete.** Compare each part alone, passive earliest sound check plus best legal C ordering and selective evidence, a separate audit at equal compute, [Proof-of-Learning replay][pol], authenticated claims without semantic verification, shrink-then-check, and an exact same-information planner. Let the stack reuse ordinary observations and choose its best lawful order. Include an opener that aliases at actual published precision, a margin-matched harmless lie, and a safe failed check that still serves C. Hold C progress per queue, information, old starting margins, retained views and clocks fixed or report a Pareto tradeoff. Report false admits, lawful refusals (split compliant and harmless), useful arrivals, peak and cumulative bytes, checks/compute, and timely backed service. Run normal held-out A, earlier B, the implicated B route or independent fallback, and installed C at every exposed prefix, after refusals and after a later blind arrival. Do not count a saved audit as a win if the fallback stopped serving or the right simply expired.

## Paper fixture: one due order checks, another erases

For one x block use unit-step heavy-ball with the **clipped** velocity stored: `u = clip(m/2 + g_x, -1, 1); x' = x - u; m' = u`. Start at x = 0 with m in {0, 1/4, 1/2, 1}; each is reachable from zero using gradients −m, 3m/2 under this same cap. The submitted claim is m = 0. A requested one-use B right at the E-first cut promises that an idle-x step from that cut will leave x ≥ −3/16. At other cuts the E-first right cannot simply be reused. The old A read in this toy requires −3/2 ≤ x ≤ 0.

There are two independent, due C batches: E fits y to 1 and has no x loss; L fits z to 1 and includes the truthful x target −1 with unit quadratic curvature. Start y = z = 0; both use unit squared-error steps on their own independent C coordinate. The ordinary x telemetry is the integer bin ⌊8|u|⌋; the verifier never reads m. E first gives:

| Entry m | E velocity u | E bin | x after the idle step promised by the B right | Admission class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | compliant |
| 1/4 | 1/8 | 1 | −3/16 | harmless violation |
| 1/2 | 1/4 | 2 | −3/8 | harmful violation |
| 1 | 1/2 | 4 | −3/4 | harmful violation |

E is A-safe even when it exposes a harmful violation, and its ordinary y gain is identical on all paths. L first has g_x = 1 and clips every history to the same (x, m) = (−1, 1), so even exact observations of the later E cannot recover the entry class. Coarsening E's publication to ⌊2|u|⌋ already merges the harmless tie with m = 1/2 without a preceding L; that cell must be refused or backed by independently verified pre-loss evidence. In the C-only schedules, E→L ends at x = −1−u/2, and L→E at x = −3/2; both fit y and z and respect A on all prefixes, but their x losses can differ. The idle-x step is a separate B read at the named cut, not an interposed step in those C-only schedules. This demonstrates separation, erasure and a precision failure; it does not show matched total C gain, full B fallback service or a win over a lawful passive early read. A real test freezes that fallback and tries the strongest passive read first.

## A. Make the ordinary batch decide something useful

### 01. Select for a right, not for disclosure — primitive candidate (TCM5 baseline)
**Mechanism.** Use the report of a due C step to admit both compliant and harmless histories while blocking harmful ones for a particular right, then release its clip dependency. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] already picks truthful separating examples and checks before shrinking; [#253][parent] already uses the opener as the check. This is the inherited benchmark, not a priority claim: compare useful right coverage, not factual lie detection. **Cheapest first test.** Reproduce the fixture with a blind harmless threshold tie and a same-cost passive read; score safe useful work on a refused claim. **Old-task retention check.** A and installed C after both orders; add an independent B fallback and test it on all refused histories before calling the fixture operational.

### 02. Admit a weaker backed right from a mixed bin — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Predeclare nested B rights with real deadlines or service levels. When the batch's observation is mixed for the requested level, grant only the strongest level backed by every member, without asking a new question or letting the claimant choose the downgrade after seeing the answer. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] already keeps scoped decisions; the action here is an executable weaker right from the same observation, not extra evidence for the original right. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three service levels and two quantized cells, one mixed for every nontrivial level; compare binary refusal plus separately reserved service. **Old-task retention check.** Serve normal A, B and installed C at the downgrade and at the next arrival; count unserved original B demands separately from successful weaker service.

### 03. Let safe work change which violation matters — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A due step can repair one suspect fallback while its ordinary output separates a different, unrepaired hazard. Relabel only rights not yet relied on, using the frozen obligations evaluated at the post-step cut. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] preserves detectability through contraction; this tries to remove one decision distinction by genuinely delivering service while checking a second, rather than merely discarding history. **Cheapest first test.** Use two quadratic risk directions and two due orderings; hide a suffix where the supposedly repaired direction matters again. Compare separate repair plus early audit. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise both B fallbacks on admitted suffixes and check A/installed C before and after repair, including the hidden suffix as a limit to scope.

### 04. Compile a menu of safe replies from one observation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Check a vector of backed rights with one C report but authorize only a jointly feasible set, not the union of individually safe rights whose reservations overlap. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] already discusses signatures with multiple decisions; the proposed operation compiles a compatible bundle at the due step under the shared capacity constraint. **Cheapest first test.** Three aliases, two individually safe rights competing for one reserve; compare independent per-right checks plus a capacity allocator with equal access. **Old-task retention check.** Service both B consumers at their true deadlines when jointly allowed, and exercise A/installed C and the losing consumer's fallback.

### 05. Separate around the clip knee without creating a probe — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose among equal-C-progress due batches so the harmful tail saturates at a different published scale from allowable histories, while every actual update remains inside the old-service envelope. Use the knee only when the published statistic exposes it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Iterative Machine Teaching][imt] selects examples for learning and [#249][clips] accounts for actual rounding; the bet is that the native saturating transition can expose a right, not that selecting examples or clipping is new. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate gradients on two sides of a fixed knee including the case where both classes saturate in one bin; compare unclipped ranking plus the best early audit. **Old-task retention check.** Match starting margins and run rare A, affected B and installed C just below and above the knee.

### 06. Use a native per-block skip inside a useful commit — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Where the optimizer already exposes a per-block skip/commit outcome, use that outcome only if the batch still commits independently measured C progress on every history. No global skipped step or audit retry counts as serving C. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] reads sizes from ordinary updates; this tests whether an existing discrete optimizer branch on one block can distinguish hazards without new telemetry or abandoning C. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two native masked blocks, one that delivers C in both cases and one whose mask can flip at the same visible weights; include a harmless mask flip and a negative where the C block also skips. Compare a passive mask read plus the usual scheduler. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C on both mask outcomes and refusal; include the old slice served by the skipped block.

## B. Put the check where evidence still exists

### 07. Find the last usable cut over partial orders — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Track maximal completed sets where a due C separator is still safe and available. Two independent openers may each preserve the distinction but erase it together, so don't assign each edge a fixed expiration. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] moves an audit cut over two reciprocal orders; this makes the cut depend on the reachable completed set rather than an individual opener. **Cheapest first test.** Three due batches, each pair of prefixes legal but one joint prefix mixed; compare greedy earliest check, edge-local rules and exhaustive schedules. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/B/installed C after each admitted prefix, including the delayed separator and a missed-cut fallback.

### 08. Make the order switch implementable at its own cut — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Build a public decision tree that chooses the next due batch from prior ordinary observations; at an aliased node it must choose one order safe for the entire node or refuse, even if an oracle could choose two better orders. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GraB][grab] optimizes data ordering; [#249][clips] chooses among feasible orders. The new candidate is a contingent schedule whose branching information can only come from its own useful steps before the deadline. **Cheapest first test.** Two hidden lanes needing opposite orders, plus a harmless alias; compare fixed order, earliest passive audit plus reorder and hidden-state oracle. **Old-task retention check.** All public branches preserve A/B/installed C, including the ambiguous lane and its fallback.

### 09. Use a pair of ordinary reads with no retained transcript — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When neither due batch alone separates, keep only a transient decision accumulator between them and consume it before reliance; abort if an interruption could outlive that accumulator. Charge it as scratch, not as zero state. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Proof-of-Learning][pol] verifies segments; [#253][parent] checks the first batch. This asks whether a short safe, already-due word resolves a right without replay or a durable receipt. **Cheapest first test.** Two binary reports whose pair separates, followed by a clip before the second report in the negative control. Compare earliest passive check plus a two-report audit at equal storage and latency. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C at both steps and after interruption; check B is never spent while the pair is incomplete.

### 10. Certify a wait as well as an opener — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish how long a separating due update stays valid while native idle decay, other work or queue delays change reachable aliases. Switch to an earlier due batch or a scoped decision record if the wait crosses that boundary. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] includes waits in landing sets; the object here is the expiration of a *particular report's ability to decide* even when the batch stays safe and eligible. **Cheapest first test.** Two wait lengths straddling a quantization boundary with equal C progress; compare check-on-arrival and audit-at-entry. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C in every allowed wait and after a missed arrival; include ordinary service while holding the right.

### 11. Recheck the counterfactual order without replay — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When a right depends on whether a second queue can jump ahead, use a due batch's report to eliminate just the histories that make the proposed alternate prefix unsafe. Do not use success of the observed order as evidence that the alternate order is safe. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] certifies reciprocal openings; this checks the alternate order in the observation fiber of the order actually executed, rather than certifying unconditional interchangeability. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two orders and one hidden slot where both final losses match but the alternate intermediate B spend fails. Compare separate order certification using the same reports. **Old-task retention check.** Check A/B/installed C at the alternate intermediate cut as well as the observed prefix and shared endpoint.

### 12. Move the evidence boundary under a precision change — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Derive which decisions lose separation under a declared lower-precision reader; spend the remaining high-precision observation on those live rights before transition, leaving rights still separable to their ordinary future batch. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] checks a fixed rounding boundary. Here a precision change creates a migration of *checking obligations*, not a migration of model state or blanket high-precision logs. **Cheapest first test.** One reporting-format change and two rights, only one of which becomes mixed; include double rounding, compare full retention and stop-on-change. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C on both sides of the format change, including refusal of the mixed decision when timely evidence is unavailable.

## C. Keep only the decisions the batch will not settle

### 13. Keep evidence only for unresolved fibers — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Before a destructive cut, issue decision evidence only where the predicted ordinary view remains mixed, charging any metadata needed to locate the fiber. On a homogeneous view, the observation itself is sufficient. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] already gives the per-fiber payload bound; the additional bet is that choosing the due batch also reduces the set of fibers that require costly verification and binding. **Cheapest first test.** Choose between two equally useful batches, one splitting many irrelevant bins and the other splitting one costly mixed bin. Compare the best separate batch choice plus scoped evidence. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C on recorded and record-free paths and when the binding is missing.

### 14. Route a refusal before the record disappears — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Consume a harmful report by handing the job to an independent fallback before a scheduled clip, so after the handoff no live consumer relies on the rejected right. Retain the fallback obligation, not a historical proof that the first right failed. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#253][parent] permits useful failures and [#232][shrink] orders detect before shrink; here the testable delta is whether fallthrough completed by the same useful schedule makes a refusal receipt unnecessary. **Cheapest first test.** Two defaults with handoff, one forced late; compare append-only refusals and a separate paid challenge window at equal service. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise the affected B task through its alternate route and usual A/installed C through both defaults and a subsequent blind pair.

### 15. Carry one fact across opposite openers only while it stays sufficient — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Transport a pre-loss decision through the opposite queue using a verified transition on its public fiber; invalidate or refine it if the transported class gains new possible continuations. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249 card 14][clips] already carries a bit across the opposite opener; the extension tests a live right whose validity depends on the other queue's actual ordinary C gain and not just its identity. **Cheapest first test.** Same opener ID, two realized gains, one rendering the old evidence insufficient; compare index-by-opener receipts and re-auditing. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C on both realized gains and the discrepancy fallback, not only on the nominal opener.

### 16. Make replay pay for a decision, not a whole trace — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** If no useful separator is available, locally replay only enough from an existing legal checkpoint to establish the affected decision before reliance. Account for checkpoint access, reconstruction and any persistent payload. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Proof-of-Learning][pol] is the segment-replay rival, and [#249][clips] already narrows record scope; this is a strong verifier control, not a new verification primitive. **Cheapest first test.** Compare exact full replay, partial replay and the due check on a three-unit prefix where the earliest separating read moves by one step. **Old-task retention check.** Keep A/B/installed C service live through verification delay and score usage of a fallback when replay misses the deadline.

### 17. Drop evidence that can no longer change any future decision — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After the decision is consumed, delete evidence only if every declared legal suffix has identical operational admissions and service requirements with or without it. A future consumer that can ask for the historical answer keeps the evidence live. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] already drops uniform decisions and [#253][parent] forbids forgotten downstream audit needs; test whether a due C step can advance the last *dependency* without widening the suffix contract. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two expiries and a late issued right that still consults the record; compare fixed TTL and reference counting under the same suffixes. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise ordinary A/B/installed C after actual deletion, including the earliest legal reuse and a blind request at the expiry boundary.

### 18. Bound selective evidence under repeated defaults — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve a capped number of unsettled decision slots. Spend them on mixed live rights for which no due batch can check in time; otherwise prefer the useful check or refuse new claims without revoking existing obligations. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) rolls finite escrow; this reserves verification capacity rather than backing capacity, with a failure mode when mixed obligations overlap. **Cheapest first test.** Four adaptive arrivals targeting the same cap, including two simultaneous hard mixed rights; compare append-only evidence and a fixed earliest-audit policy with the same peak bytes. **Old-task retention check.** Preserve A/B/installed C through slot exhaustion and demonstrate a later blind success without silent revocation.

## D. Try to kill the merge

### 19. Select against an adaptive harmless cover — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Bind the claim and its backing before the parent's public opener draw; choose the distribution to minimize harmful histories that imitate *harmless* violations. Grant deterministic safety only in pure observed cells; report chance of informative resolution separately from false-admit probability. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#253 card 06][parent] already randomizes after binding. This tests robust coverage under mixed semantic classes and pre-clip deadlines rather than claiming randomization authenticates a right. **Cheapest first test.** Finite menu game with one harmful history that aliases a different harmless history on each due batch; compare fixed check, uniform draw and a claimant who selects after the draw. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C on every draw, with refused branches and their service cost included.

### 20. Use the real optimizer to hunt false separators — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Enumerate reachable momentum or Adam states from cheap prior histories at identical visible weights; measure the actual exported scale and its rounding through unmodified due steps. Treat an abstract separator that collides in execution as a refusal. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] checks hidden-slot envelopes and [#249][clips] checks precision; this is their implementation-level falsifier, not an optimizer replacement. **Cheapest first test.** Tiny CPU linear model with identical entry weights, different slot histories, and a denominator or cap near a reported tie; compare exact shadow arithmetic and the native export. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate A/B/installed C per reachable history, not only averages; check acquisition margins before judging refusal rates.

### 21. Charge the normalization channel before calling the check free — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Give the passive verifier every lawful existing statistic, including the optimizer's unrounded scale only if it is already visible at that boundary. Sweep report precision and charge any new access. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] and [#253][parent] already warn that higher precision can kill the gain; this is a same-access control for the whole TCM5 frontier, not a new telemetry scheme. **Cheapest first test.** Compare the same finite queue under coarse, native and exact publication, with C ordering chosen independently and jointly. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C under each matched order; if faster checking changes waiting, run the old tasks at those earlier cuts too.

### 22. Produce an obstruction, not a weak positive — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** On a finite declared menu, return a harmful and an allowable history sharing every allowed pre-reliance observation, or a tree showing each still-separating due continuation is unsafe or late. This gives a reason to pay for evidence or narrow the right instead of tuning the classifier. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#253 card 10][parent] already identifies a receipt-free boundary; extend the obstruction to partial-order schedules with erasure, where no single fixed collision need defeat every strategy. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaustively solve a tiny observation game where each batch separates some pair but no public safe policy separates all; validate the obstruction against a reachable negative and a menu with one legal winning order. **Old-task retention check.** Verify the obstruction's fallback really serves A/B/installed C rather than treating abstention as retention.

### 23. Make two waiting claims share a check without granting both — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use one due C report to rule out harmful combinations of *simultaneously submitted* uncertainty claims, even when either claim alone admits an alias. Apply only a joint decision backed across the whole observed class; never assume the claimants' deviations are independent. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] groups decision signatures for one history and [#253][parent] checks one incoming claim; the proposed object is a batch check on correlated histories from two independently backed handoffs, not an evidence pooling vote. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two overlapping reservations, including adversaries whose slot errors cancel in the reported sum; compare selective joint checking against independent early checks plus the old allocator. **Old-task retention check.** Serve both earlier B tasks, normal A and installed C even on joint refusal, then exercise the reservation granted by a later separated observation.

### 24. Select a separator that survives allowed aggregation orders — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** With an ordinary reduced statistic and no control over reduction order, choose due microbatches for which allowable and harmful observation sets are disjoint across the *union* of permitted reduction trees. If they collide only on some trees, use an earlier check or verified evidence; do not pick a favorable tree after seeing the claim. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][clips] handles rounded publication and [GraB][grab] picks training order; here ordinary batch choice buys decision robustness against a distinct, fixed arithmetic nondeterminism without enforcing a new reduction order. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two legal reductions in a toy low-precision accumulator, where one due batch separates under each tree separately but produces an inter-tree collision. Compare a fixed deterministic reducer (charge latency) and a separate early audit. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C under every permitted reduction and after fallback, holding C gains and starting margins fixed.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can joint choice of a due C batch and its check cut serve more backed rights than the strongest separate stack at matched old service and C progress?** Start with 05, 07 and 13. Require a blind queue swap where the losing stack was allowed to optimize its order, reuse lawful statistics and retain scoped evidence. If the exact same-information optimum cannot improve service, stop.
2. **How many harmless violations remain distinguishable from harmful ones at actual optimizer and reporter precision?** Use 01 and 19–24 to measure pure-cell coverage, find reachable collisions and see whether any result survives adaptive claimants, a hostile tie and matched starting margins. Do not infer truth or utility outside the frozen language.
3. **When does moving the check beat keeping evidence, and when is neither lawful?** Use 07–12 and 15–18. Price bytes, binding and planning separately from optimizer passes, test a wait that deletes the distinction, and demand a decision-ready obstruction when all safe early cuts are blocked.
4. **Can refusal remain useful across repeated defaults without transferring the cost to old tasks?** Use 02, 04, 14 and 18. Exercise independent B service, rare A and installed C during refusals, then a later blind successful pair; otherwise an apparent check win may be lost service or serialization.

Prior-art comparisons are scoped, not priority claims: the closest in-repo precedents are [#232][shrink], [#249][clips] and the parent. HF Papers or arXiv abstracts verify the references for teaching, order selection, segment replay and the [GEM][gem] retention baseline.
