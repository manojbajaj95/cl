# Weight-gap handoffs: plant the discrepancy the successor's curvature damps

Child of [Gap handoffs](gap-handoffs.md) ([PR #258][parent]). The [score-3 review][triage] points to #04, where a required P batch leaves a bounded *weight* discrepancy between two histories so that Q's ordinary curvature produces a gradient difference opposing the leftover momentum, and #06, one P landing that every already-scheduled successor accepts without a route tag. Ideas and exact arithmetic only; no learner run or GPU work.

**Bet.** Momentum differences between histories cannot be edited lawfully, but weight differences become gradient differences through the successor's curvature. A small planted weight gap can therefore act as a delayed brake on the slot gap, and one landing may serve a whole successor family. **Verdict.** Untested. The fixtures below show that the landing is real in the scalar case, but there it equals tuning P's effective step size. The literal criterion of "zero the momentum gap at Q's first step" is the worst landing, and the obvious modal landing ties doing nothing. The claim is a candidate primitive only in hosts where the *direction* of the planted gap matters: multi-coordinate curvature, ReLU cells or Adam. There it must beat a tuned-step-size P and a tagged per-successor oracle at matched C progress.

## What would count

- **Same contract as the parent.** A and B are established skills; two legal writeback orders produce paired incoming states `(θ,m,v,t)`. P and Q are ordinary, required C routes over truthful records. Freeze delivery times, permitted orders, C progress floors, prefix A/B readers and the declared tail. No slot edits, resets, idle waiting or synthetic targets in the candidate. Resets and crossed slots are diagnostic arms only.
- **Score what readers see, including the landing tick.** The planted gap `Δθ` is itself a reader-visible difference at the moment P lands. Score the summed reader error from the first post-P read through the tail. Report the momentum gap separately, because it does not count as service. Zeroing `Δm` once is not damping (W1).
- **Separate direction from scale.** In a scalar quadratic, any landing reachable by P is a choice of P's step length on the gap coordinate (W2). A result needs a matched P that leaves the same `‖Δθ‖` and `‖Δm‖` in the wrong *direction*, and a P whose step size is tuned for the same C progress.
- **Tag-free means frozen successors, no oracle.** For #06, freeze the successor family and its schedule before fitting P. Compare against one P per successor with an oracle tag (the upper bound) and against no landing. Report the worst-case ratio to the tagged oracle on held-out successors, not the mean on fitted ones.
- **Retention floor in every test.** Score held-out A/B and rare old inputs on both trajectories at the landing tick, at Q's first sensitive step and over the declared tail. Count refusals and fallback cost. As in the [ledger][ledger], a same-budget tie is a negative result. Primitive candidates name reusable operations; improvements are audits. Prior-art comparisons are local, not clearance.

## Three exact fixtures (not evidence of learning)

All three use scalar heavy-ball on quadratic losses with `β = 1/4` and learning rate `1/6`. The pair difference `(Δθ, Δm)` under a loss of curvature `h` evolves as `Δm' = βΔm + hΔθ`, `Δθ' = Δθ − Δm'/6`. Reader energy is `E = Σ_{k≥0} Δθ_k²`, solved exactly from the discrete Lyapunov equation in `Fraction` arithmetic. Incoming momentum gap is `Δm = 1`. These are gap maps on prescribed curvatures, not truthful C workloads.

**W1 · The landing that zeros momentum is the worst one.** Q has `h = 1`; P lands a weight gap `d` just before Q. Then `E(d) = 151/56·d² − 37/168·d + 5/672`. With no landing (`d = 0`), `E = 5/672`. The best landing `d* = 37/906` gives `E = 4/1359`, 2.53 times lower. The modal landing `d = 1/12` puts the pair on Q's fast eigenvector, so both gaps decay exactly as `3^−k`. Its `E = 1/128` is *worse* than no landing, because the planted gap is read at the landing tick. The literal "curvature cancels momentum" landing `d = −β/h = −1/4` makes `Δm` exactly 0 after Q's first step, but `E = 207/896`, 31 times worse than doing nothing: the momentum is gone and the weight gap stays. For Q with `h = 3` (underdamped) the best landing gains only 1.19 times (`5/1728 → 4/1647`). For `h = 1/4` it gains 8.8 times (`5/177 → 4/1251`), and the momentum-zeroing landing is 369 times worse.

**W2 · One tag-free landing, and the acceptance test that hides it.** For successors `h ∈ {1, 3}`, the tag-free landing `d = 151/4800` is within 8.1% of each tagged optimum (`37/906`, `7/366`). No landing is 2.53 and 1.19 times worse. Adding `h = 1/4`, the best tag-free landing `d = 101/2400` is within 27.6% of every tagged optimum, against 8.8 times for no landing. A one-step acceptance rule `|Δm after Q's first step| ≤ 1/8` (with `|d| ≤ 1/2`) admits only `d = −1/8` for `{1, 3}`, which is 10.4 times worse than no landing under `h = 1`. For `{1, 3, 1/4}` it admits nothing. So a successor family can share a landing that a first-step acceptance rule declares impossible, and the rule's one admitted point is harmful.

**W3 · Reachability: the right landing needs a stiff P, and in one dimension that is just step size.** From equal weights, one ordinary P step lands at `d/Δm = −1/6`, which has the wrong sign: every optimum in W1 and W2 has `d/Δm > 0`. Two P steps with curvature `h_P` reach a positive ratio exactly when `1/4 < h_P/6 < 5/4`: the momentum gap flips sign before the weight gap does. Counting the P ticks in `E`, ordinary `h_P = 1` gives `5/672` before Q₁ (`h = 1`) and `17/3888` before Q₃ (`h = 3`). The stiff `h_P = 31/5` gives `1/540` before Q₁, 4.0 times lower, and `h_P = 33/5` gives `1/540` before Q₃, 2.36 times lower. One common `h_P = 127/20` lands at `(Δθ, Δm) = (−23/2880, −97/480)` and is within 0.3% of both tagged bests. The negative: here `h_P` only enters as the product `h_P/6`, so this is tuning P's effective step on the gap coordinate. A tuned-step-size P at matched C progress is the control the whole node must beat. The scalar case can show a handoff exists, but not that it is a new operation.

## Idea cards

### 01. Plant a weight gap the successor converts into momentum damping — primitive candidate (#258 #04)
**Mechanism.** Train P so the reachable post-P pair difference sits where Q's ordinary curvature generates a gradient gap opposing the leftover momentum gap, scored by total reader energy from the landing tick on. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Nesterov momentum][sutskever] evaluates the gradient at a weight offset proportional to momentum inside one optimizer. This plants a real, cross-task, history-paired offset through a required batch, and W1's optimum has the *opposite* sign to Nesterov's lookahead. **Cheapest first test.** Two-coordinate regression with a frozen anisotropic Q; enumerate P batches from a truthful menu and compare the best directional landing to a same-norm landing on the wrong axis. **Same-budget control.** Tuned P step size (W3), the #258 slot-gap handoff, waiting for decay, a slot reset as a diagnostic. **Old-task retention.** A reader aligned with the planted gap, read at the landing tick.

### 02. One landing every scheduled successor accepts, with no route tag — primitive candidate (#258 #06)
**Mechanism.** Fit one P whose landing minimizes the worst ratio to each successor's tagged optimum over the frozen family. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Simultaneous stabilization][simstab] and [co-stabilization][costab] seek one *controller* for many plants; [MAML][maml] seeks one initialization for many tasks. This seeks one *initial pair difference*, produced by a mandatory upstream batch, that all successor dynamics damp. **Cheapest first test.** W2 with held-out successor curvatures drawn from the scheduled family's range; report the worst-case ratio on held-out members. **Same-budget control.** One P per successor with an oracle tag, no landing, and #248 #08 joint training. **Old-task retention.** Read A at the landing tick under every successor, since the tag-free landing may plant a larger gap than any tagged one.

### 03. Score landings by reader energy from the landing tick, not momentum zeroing — improvement
**Mechanism.** Replace first-step slot-gap criteria with summed reader error that includes the planted gap's own read. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** This is a standard LQR-style quadratic cost on the [heavy-ball][polyak] linear system; it is an audit of #258 #04's acceptance rule, not a new method. **Cheapest first test.** W1/W2: rank the momentum-zeroing, modal and energy-optimal landings under both criteria and on a held-out successor. **Same-budget control.** No landing. **Old-task retention.** The landing-tick read is the retention check.

### 04. Make P stiff on the gap coordinate so its own steps flip the momentum gap — improvement
**Mechanism.** Choose P's truthful examples to raise P's curvature on the gap coordinate so two ordinary P steps reverse the momentum gap before the weight gap (W3). **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** In one dimension this equals per-coordinate step-size tuning (Adam-style preconditioning, learning-rate schedules), hence improvement. **Cheapest first test.** Scalar and diagonal regressions: compare data-selected stiffness against a tuned per-coordinate learning rate at matched C loss. **Same-budget control.** Tuned P learning rate, [OptiSelect][optiselect]-style data scoring. **Old-task retention.** A examples sharing the stiffened coordinate, read during P.

### 05. Use Q's cross-curvature to turn a gap on one coordinate into a brake on another — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Plant `Δθ_x` where Q's Hessian couples `x` to the coordinate `y` that carries the momentum gap, so `H_yx Δθ_x` opposes `Δm_y` while the reader on `y` sees nothing at the landing tick. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** No scalar step-size choice can produce this, which escapes W3's negative. Nearest are [GraB][grab]'s order balancing and [OGD][ogd], neither of which uses successor cross-curvature as the actuator. **Cheapest first test.** Two-coordinate quadratic Q with off-diagonal curvature and `Δm` only on `y`. Compare the cross landing against the best same-energy landing on `y` alone and against tuned step sizes on both coordinates. **Same-budget control.** Tuned diagonal P step sizes, best `y`-only landing, #258 H1 rotation. **Old-task retention.** An A reader on `x`, which the cross landing disturbs.

### 06. Let a ReLU cell give the two histories different successor curvature — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Plant the weight gap across a kink so the history carrying more momentum lands in Q's stiffer cell and the other in the flatter one. The successor's history-dependent curvature then does the differential damping. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #258 #09 uses Q's clipping branch to cap effects; [Effect commits][effect] opens ReLU cells for a shared effect. Here the cell choice *is* the damping gain. **Cheapest first test.** One hidden ReLU unit with a frozen Q; enumerate landings on both sides of the kink. **Same-budget control.** The best linear landing with matched gap norm, and tuned step size. **Old-task retention.** A rare old input near the kink, which the landing may push across.

### 07. Land a weight gap whose gradient repairs both Adam moments — primitive candidate (joint with #258 #11)
**Mechanism.** Under [Adam][adam], a planted gap makes `Δg = HΔθ`, which enters `Δm` linearly and `Δv` as `(g₊+g₋)Δg`. Choose the landing so one gradient gap reduces both, rather than splitting the work across P and Q. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #258 H2 shows that numerator and denominator targets can conflict for prescribed gradients. Here the conflict is resolved, or shown infeasible, through landing geometry. **Cheapest first test.** Scalar and two-coordinate Adam with frozen Q; solve for landings that satisfy both moment targets exactly, including a case where none exists. **Same-budget control.** The #258 #11 split P/Q repair, a joint native-update objective, a v reset as a diagnostic. **Old-task retention.** A coordinate with small v, read immediately after Q's first adaptive step.

### 08. Weight successors by schedule probability, not worst case — improvement
**Mechanism.** If the scheduler publishes arrival probabilities, minimize expected reader energy across successors, and report the worst-case ratio separately. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** This is stochastic programming or [robust MPC][hybrid] over a scenario set. **Cheapest first test.** W2 with skewed probabilities; check how often the expected-optimal landing violates a worst-case floor. **Same-budget control.** Minimax landing (card 02) and tagged oracle. **Old-task retention.** The rare successor's A read.

### 09. Certify the minimal number of landings a family needs — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** When no single landing is within a tolerance of all tagged optima (W2's `h = 1/4` stretches the ratio to 27.6%), compute the smallest cover of the successor family by landings. Commit to one only at a public boundary the scheduler already reveals. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Set-cover and k-center over successor optima; it audits when #06 must refuse. **Cheapest first test.** Sweep curvature spread in W2 and plot the covering number at tolerance 10%. **Same-budget control.** Tagged oracle; one landing per successor. **Old-task retention.** A read under each committed landing.

### 10. One landing for unknown successor arrival time — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Fit the landing to be damped for every lawful number of intermediate steps (A/B service or other C) before Q arrives, not only to Q's identity. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Sealed park entry][sealed] certifies arrival-time windows for parks; this needs a damped landing across delays under each intermediate curvature. **Cheapest first test.** W1 with 0, 1 or 2 intermediate steps under `h ∈ {0, 1/4}` before Q; compare the delay-robust and delay-tagged landings. **Same-budget control.** A delay-tagged oracle and no landing. **Old-task retention.** Reads during the intermediate steps.

### 11. Land in the subspace every successor damps fast, when Hessians share eigenvectors — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** If the successors' curvatures share an eigenbasis (common features, different scales), land the gap along a shared fast direction so the tag-free landing loses nothing. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Feedback rectification][rectify] constructs a shared eigenbasis for switched systems with controllers; here the basis is measured from frozen successors and used only to place a landing. **Cheapest first test.** Two diagonal Q's with matching eigenvectors vs two Q's rotated 45°; compare tag-free/tagged ratios. **Same-budget control.** Tagged oracle; minimax landing ignoring structure. **Old-task retention.** A reader in the shared fast direction.

### 12. Keep the planted gap in the A-reader null space — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Constrain the landing to directions old readers do not see at the landing tick, so the planted gap costs no retention while Q's curvature still damps it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [OGD][ogd] projects *updates* off old gradients; this projects a *paired difference* that is meant to be damped later. **Cheapest first test.** Three-coordinate quadratic where A reads one coordinate; compare unconstrained and A-null landings by B/C reader energy and A error. **Same-budget control.** Unconstrained landing plus replay at equal compute. **Old-task retention.** The A reader is the test; add one rare old input that reads the null direction late.

### 13. Publish a landing ellipsoid, not a point — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** P certifies only that the reachable pair difference lies in a declared set; successors are certified against the whole set, so either side can change without re-fitting the other. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Set-membership estimation (Milanese and Vicino) bounds states; [Loan commits][loan] uses set membership for confirmation. The new object here is a handoff contract between two optimizers. **Cheapest first test.** W2 with P landing noise; compare the set certificate against point fitting on held-out histories. **Same-budget control.** Point landing with a margin (#258 #08). **Old-task retention.** A read at the worst point of the set.

### 14. Test whether Nesterov's own offset already does the handoff — improvement (falsifier)
**Mechanism.** Run Q with Nesterov momentum, which evaluates `g` at `θ − lr·βm`, and ask whether the planted landing still helps. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Sutskever et al.][sutskever]; [EMA-Nesterov][emanest]. W1's positive optimal ratio suggests that the lookahead offset points the wrong way for reader energy, which makes this a cheap falsifier. **Cheapest first test.** W1/W2 with a Nesterov successor. **Same-budget control.** Nesterov successor with no landing. **Old-task retention.** Landing-tick A read.

### 15. Hand off the gap in output space — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Plant the discrepancy in the network's outputs on Q's inputs, so Q's Gauss-Newton curvature, not raw parameter curvature, does the damping. This puts the handoff on the coordinates Q's loss actually reads. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Backpropagated output momentum][bom] moves optimizer history into task space; this moves the *handoff target* into it. **Cheapest first test.** A linear-softmax model with two parameterizations of the same function; check that the output-space landing transfers across them while the parameter landing does not. **Same-budget control.** Parameter-space landing (card 01). **Old-task retention.** A outputs at the landing tick.

### 16. Make the planted gap the lagging history's missing C progress — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** The two histories differ in how far they have learned C. Plant the weight gap along C's useful direction, so the lagging history's extra P movement is real C learning that also brakes its momentum under Q. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Check as work][check] reuses one batch for checking and learning. Here the gap the handoff needs is itself useful progress. **Cheapest first test.** Two histories with different C progress, then compare a C-aligned landing with an orthogonal landing of equal energy. **Same-budget control.** Extra plain C steps for the lagging history at equal compute. **Old-task retention.** A reader correlated with C's direction.

### 17. Choose the landing from Q's curvature interval only — improvement
**Mechanism.** If only bounds `[h_min, h_max]` on successor curvature are public, pick the landing minimizing the worst ratio over the interval, as Chebyshev semi-iteration (Golub and Varga) does for spectra. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** That classical polynomial minimax makes this an improvement. **Cheapest first test.** W2 over `[1/4, 3]` continuously; compare with the three-point minimax. **Same-budget control.** Three-point minimax, tagged oracle. **Old-task retention.** A read at the interval ends.

### 18. Require the common landing to survive successor order — improvement (joint audit)
**Mechanism.** If two successors run in either lawful order, the landing must be damped by `Q₁Q₂` and `Q₂Q₁`, not only by each alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #258 #22 audits orders between P and Q; here the order is among successors. **Cheapest first test.** W2 with both orders; report whether one landing serves both. **Same-budget control.** An order-tagged oracle. **Old-task retention.** First A read in each order.

### 19. Refuse when a successor pushes the same way — improvement (falsifier)
**Mechanism.** Include a successor with negative or near-zero curvature on the gap coordinate, or one that turns the planted gap into more momentum. Certify that the tag-free fit refuses instead of averaging it in. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** This is the negative control #258 #04 names; [hybrid reachability][hybrid] already supplies refusals. **Cheapest first test.** W2 plus `h = −1/8`; check whether the minimax landing still beats no landing on the good successors. **Same-budget control.** No landing. **Old-task retention.** A read under the refusing successor.

### 20. Exhaust what a truthful P menu can reach — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** Before fitting, compute P's reachable pair-difference set from its finite truthful menu (W3 shows ordinary one-step P has the wrong sign). Intersect that set with the successors' good landings. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #258 #10's directional counterpart for weight gaps. **Cheapest first test.** A finite menu of six P batches in a two-coordinate host. **Same-budget control.** Brute-force two-route replay. **Old-task retention.** Report landings that exist only by breaking the A floor.

### 21. Size the gap so both histories stay on Q's unclipped branch — improvement
**Mechanism.** Gradient clipping caps Q's effective curvature for large gaps, so bound the planted gap to keep both histories' Q gradients unclipped, where the damping is linear and certifiable. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 B2 and #258 #09 show the clip branch alone does not certify updates; this is a margin rule. **Cheapest first test.** W1 with a clip threshold that one history crosses at the optimal `d`. **Same-budget control.** Unbounded landing. **Old-task retention.** A read when the clip binds.

### 22. Stack against the parent's slot-gap handoff before merging — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** Compare the weight-gap landing with #258 #01's slot-gap redirection and with their best independent stack at equal compute. Call the merged result a primitive only if it beats the stack. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** This is the ledger's merge rule. **Cheapest first test.** A two-coordinate host where each part alone yields a measurable gain. **Same-budget control.** The best stack of the two parts, tuned step size. **Old-task retention.** All A/B reads of both parts.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does a directional weight-gap landing beat tuned-step-size P at matched C progress?** Start with 05, 20 and 04, then 06. W3 shows the scalar case reduces to step size. The question lives in cross-curvature or kinks. Stop calling it a handoff if a tuned diagonal learning rate ties at matched C loss and A/B retention.
2. **Does one tag-free landing stay within a small factor of tagged oracles on held-out successors?** Use 02, 11, 17 and 09. Report the worst ratio as curvature spread grows (W2: 8.1% for `{1, 3}`, 27.6% with `1/4`) and the covering number at a fixed tolerance. If held-out members fall off, the result is a fitted average, not a landing.
3. **Under Adam, can one planted gap repair both moments, or does #258 H2's conflict force a split?** Use 07 with 21. Compare against #258 #11's split repair and a joint native-update objective. A feasibility map is useful even if the answer is no.
4. **Can the planted gap be useful C progress and invisible to A at once?** Use 16 with 12 and 15. If C-aligned and A-null directions never intersect in the host, report that trade-off rather than a primitive.
