# Escrowed paired leases

**Status:** Ideas only. The tests are proposals for paper analysis or tiny CPU fixtures; no pilot was run and no GPU is needed.

**Parents:** [Cross-pool round 48][r48] and [round 49][r49] ([PR #36][pool]). In [flood-triage rows 313–314][triage], the reviewer kept OSR11 (order-only recovery), JRO2 (a paired lease that survives half-use), and JRO4 (one relation that breaks the blocking coalition) at score 3. None yet beats its best parts. The [idea ledger][ledger] requires the combined mechanism to beat the stacked controls and each first test to check ordinary old tasks.

**Verdict:** The candidate primitive is a reusable lease that lets a useful write stay live when its partner defaults, and restores a frozen witness by ordering the successor's own batches rather than importing state. A live optimizer relation is worth buying only if its timely answer opens otherwise blocked useful service, and all answer paths have certified continuations. The lease earns primitive status only if this coupling buys more service or lowers total cost over the best equal-budget stack; if save/restore, cached policy dispatch, or separate reservations explain it, keep the narrower improvements.

## What the lease promises

- **Scope before demand.** Freeze the model/optimizer and clocks, ordinary A/C tasks, witness W (including its required slot relation and latest launch), lawful batch IDs, precedence, public events, deadlines, capacity, and blind payload/arrival/dropout distribution. A history is compatible if it is reachable under that scope and consistent with public evidence; do not drop a hard history because it fails. B₁/B₂ denote genuinely useful newly installed writes. Check every reachable prefix, including first publication, timeouts and subsequent unassisted updates. Keep useful single arrivals, successful pairs and refusals as separate counts.
- **OSR11: order only.** The recovery action payload is a fixed permutation of named truthful batches the successor already holds. Proof metadata can certify it, but cannot carry a donor slot, new example, gradient, or hidden dispatch hint. Check exact multiset equality, legality, weight endpoint tolerance relative to the successor's default order on the *same history*, all protected prefixes, and W's slot condition and timely launch on *every* compatible history. Actually execute W on blind jobs; a satisfied predicate alone is not service. Accumulation and a clock-matched optimizer are separate controls: the number and timing of momentum/Adam updates matter. Constant-LR heavy-ball with frozen gradients can lock momentum to the weight endpoint, as [batch-order interventions][bo] notes; SGD with frozen gradients and no slot is a negative, not a claim that curved SGD is order invariant.
- **JRO2: early service without early ownership transfer.** Put shared support and fallback scratch under a single capacity ledger before either write publishes. A first arrival may be published only if its useful behavior is already safe and there is a certified continuation that retains it on timeout. The paired allocation matures only after both blind writes clear utility and resource checks. While waiting, neither writer may spend or lend the other's reserved share. At timeout or rejection, route the installed write through its fallback, then return only the genuinely unused entitlement. Cancellation is forward-only: no model-wide rewind and replay in the candidate. An expired or duplicate arrival cannot consume released capacity. Bill support even while idle, proof/verifier work, buffer residence, waiting, scratch and foregone uses.
- **JRO4: decide, don't diagnose.** For a public prefix `z`, define `S(h)` as the set of replies meeting the useful service, safety, time and capacity requirements on compatible history `h`. A blocked prefix has no common useful reply, even though every pair may intersect; check that the live fallback still works. Buy at most one authorized relation over live optimizer slots only if it cuts every blocking core for the advertised service, independently recertifies a common reply in each promised cell, and improves affordable timely service over the no-query incumbent after charges. If an answer cell is declared fallback-only, charge lost service and do not call the obstruction fully broken. Missing, stale or late evidence uses the prechecked fallback, not a guessed cell. Recheck on receipt against the relevant optimizer epoch and lease state. A fixed order can be precommitted per cell; that is a JRO4-conditioned policy, not a standalone OSR11 success on the original information set.
- **Compare the whole stack.** Use each seed alone, then the best combination of [batch-order interventions][bo], [successor recovery][sr], [safe order families][so], [order handoffs][oh], [joint releases][jr], [joint branch moves][jb], [group obstructions][go] and [deletion licenses][dl]. Match truthful data, margins, useful arrivals, authorized actions/evidence, buffer and scratch budgets, hard clocks, and query/proof bytes. Include donor packet, legal direct slot write, reset, default/uniform/greedy orders, paid checkpoint + replay, atomic commit, independent reservations, best serial plan, [sagas][saga]-style compensation, raw slot retrieval, hitting-set queries, reply synthesis, and exact CEGAR. Exhaustive same-information planning is a small-fixture ceiling; look for cheaper reuse, not a claim to outperform optimal planning on the same action space. Replay dropouts at every publication boundary. **A/C** below always means held-out ordinary tasks, including rare support-sensitive slices; also retest every installed Bᵢ at its acquisition floor. Check after the specified transition and again after an ordinary suffix.

## Paper sanity check (not a learner result)

Take four equally observed histories with timely paired-reply sets `{a,b}`, `{b,c}`, `{a,c}`, `{a,c}`. Assume the first write is already useful and a separate common fallback preserves it but does not complete the pair. Every two histories intersect; the minimal blocking cores are `{1,2,3}` and `{1,2,4}`. A relation partition `{1,2}|{3,4}` admits `b` in the first cell and either `a` or `c` in the second. An equally sized response that isolates history 3 still leaves `{1,2,4}` blocked. If the answer arrives after the latest launch or invalidates an in-flight reservation, even the first relation cannot authorize paired service. This table tests the checker, not whether such a relation is lawful or such order replies are realizable by training. The first substantive fixture must derive the table (or a comparable obstruction) from reachable optimizer states and actual held-batch orders.

## Idea cards

### 01. A recovery receipt with no learner-state payload — inherited candidate (OSR11)
**Mechanism.** Freeze one order of the successor's own batches and use its resulting optimizer relation to run W across the entire public history class. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Batch-order interventions][bo] already gives checked order keys and [successor recovery][sr] restores the same witness without reset; the inherited claim is portable recovery at a live deadline with only the batch order as its action payload. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate permutations in a tiny rational two-head host under a predeclared nonconstant learning-rate schedule; include an aliased receiver where the same permutation is legal but yields the wrong slot. Compare the parent stack, gradient accumulation and a clock-matched optimizer. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and the installed B at every reordered prefix, after W and after one ordinary batch that reads momentum.

### 02. Keep the first live write, not the first reservation — inherited candidate (JRO2)
**Mechanism.** Certify a forward fallback that keeps B₁'s useful behavior on a surviving read if B₂ misses admission; release B₂'s entitlement only after clearing all live readers. Symmetrize for B₂ first. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint releases][jr] already handles a half-used release and [joint branch moves][jb] escrowed serial moves; the inherited object couples a useful early write with a non-spendable partner entitlement. **Cheapest first test.** On a two-head host with one shared scratch unit, compare atomic, separate and best serial release with complete, late, rejected and absent second writes. Allow a third real consumer to try freed support. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and whichever B arrived first at publication, fallback and after the third consumer writes.

### 03. Buy the split that opens paired service — inherited candidate (JRO4)
**Mechanism.** Offer one lawful optimizer relation with a checked action for each timely feasible answer cell; reject offers that leave a blocking core for the claimed paired service. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Group obstructions][go] already gives core-splitting predicates; JRO4 prices them at a live paired-release decision where late or blocked answers have a useful fallback, not another free query. **Cheapest first test.** Use the overlapping-core fixture, then derive the sets from a two-slot rational optimizer. Compare an equally priced decoy, raw values, hitting-set search and reply synthesis. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C and B₁ for both answers, for the decoy's residual core, and for no answer, checking B₂'s floor only where installed.

### 04. Make the booked order role-reversible — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve one common recovery suffix before either partner arrives. Give each first-arrival role its own certified prefix, but require both to reach a state where the same held-batch suffix serves W. Neither role gets to consume a batch needed by the other role's continuation. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Order handoffs][oh] carries a residual poset and [joint releases][jr] checks both spend orders; the new object is a shared order suffix across *alternative first-owner roles* with useful fallback on either branch. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust four held batches and two arrivals; compare distinct cached orders, a single conformant schedule and the shared suffix. Plant a role that used a required batch already. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C, the actual first B and any admitted second B at the convergence point and after W.

### 05. Stop at a useful insurance cut — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Find a cut in one physical batch order where the prefix is already a viable standalone outcome on timeout and continuing the suffix upgrades it to paired service. Choose the cut before knowing whether the partner arrives; reserve the suffix until the public decision. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Order handoffs][oh] preserves feasibility at a cut and [sagas][saga] compensates committed steps; here the paid prefix does ordinary useful learning on *both* exits without compensation or redundant replay. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate all cuts of a five-batch window and compare no-overlap serial, paid compensation, and independent fallback/commit orders at matched utility and latency. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C/B₁ at all public cuts, after stopping or continuing, and after the common downstream batch.

### 06. Reclaim by future read, not by elapsed time — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After timeout, issue a checkable reuse grant stating exactly which operations on the released support leave the survivor and W unaffected. A late arrival cannot change the grant; a new writer can consume it without learning the failed partner's state. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint releases][jr] already donates unused support; the candidate object is a restricted, transferable grant valid *through next use* even when some readers of the nominally freed address remain. **Cheapest first test.** Give a next writer two equal-utility writes, one clobbering a fallback feature and one in its verified null direction; compare whole-slot hold, whole-slot release and exact recertification. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C/B₁ before and after each independently authorized reuse, including inputs that exercise the residual read.

### 07. Transport a relation, not an answer — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify that a live relation acquired before a designated held-batch update implies the required post-update cell on every compatible history. Transfer the proof only across those update prefixes under which the implication was proved; otherwise expire it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Group obstructions][go] already uses decay and freshness bounds; the bet is a reusable relation-to-cell transport that allows an earlier cheap read to serve a later expensive lease boundary without weakening the fallback. **Cheapest first test.** In a rational two-slot learner, search for one preserving prefix and one sign-flipping prefix with the same public weights. Compare fresh query, timestamp-only expiry and implication transport at equal deadlines. **Old-task retention check.** Verify A/C and installed B on the transported path, withhold the stale authorization on the flipping path, and check its fallback too.

### 08. Let the query and the order compete for the same clock — improvement
**Mechanism.** Select a query only if the worst-case verified answer latency plus cell check and cell order still fits after charging the earlier writer's spent budget. Offer a no-query fallback by its own earlier cutoff if needed. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Deletion licenses][dl] and [decision-relevant queries][dq] already price diagnosis; this isolates the scheduling error of choosing an informative relation that consumes the exact launch window it was supposed to buy. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a schedule with a fast coarse relation, slow decisive relation and fast nonblocking decoy; compare exact budgeted diagnosis and information gain. **Old-task retention check.** Test A/C/B₁ when the policy waits, launches, or times out at each cutoff, plus B₂ when admitted.

### 09. Reserve continuation capacity by outcome, not twice — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Bind a single entitlement to the maximum feasible footprint across the mutually exclusive certified query answers, including verifier and fallback scratch. Allow unrelated work to use verified slack before the answer; never count room from two outcomes together. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint branch moves][jb] already tracks shared credit and [successor-funded returns][sf] has contingent reservations; this adds pre-answer spendable slack conditioned on a live optimizer partition whose cells name concrete order continuations. **Cheapest first test.** Use two time-varying scratch profiles with disjoint peaks and one background job. Compare sum, pointwise max with reachability, separate reservations and exact scheduling; introduce a nonexclusive event that invalidates the max. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C/B₁ under both cells with the background job present; check retention of that job if installed before W.

### 10. Make a truthful prep order render the query unnecessary — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use an already-needed part of successor training to make distinct hidden slot states fall into one continuation-equivalence cell while leaving their raw slots distinct. No additional examples, and no fitting to the delayed partner's payload. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Fixed-budget handoffs][fh] seeks synchronizing batches and [successor-funded returns][sf] buys service with learning; this tests *ordering the same* held examples to eliminate the demand for a specific priced relation on a live lease. **Cheapest first test.** Check two reachable optimizer histories, two lawful held-batch orders and a frozen obstruction; compare query plus default order, exact order search and resets, matching successor utility. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C, the successor and the first B before and after the prep order, then after the formerly blocked continuation.

### 11. Make fallback independent of a failed purchase — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify a single forward fallback after any partial query/check execution and any legal in-flight prefix, even when the issuer disappears. Pre-reserve the resources the fallback consumes so tentative pair work cannot exhaust them. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Sagas][saga] and [order handoffs][oh] cover compensation and abort continuations; the bet is a history-oblivious *common* fallback with live optimizer dependence and no added checkpoint. **Cheapest first test.** Drop the answer or checker at each of four boundaries on a momentum host; compare saved-state compensation and per-boundary cached fallback. Include a prefix that spent the last needed batch. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate A/C/B₁ immediately after every fallback and after a fresh ordinary update reads the resulting slots.

### 12. Swap only obligations with distinguishable identities — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Remap shared support after a lawful regime change only if both the early write's protected read and the pending partner's independently useful right have separately checked images. Reject a remap that aliases the rights despite equal solo outputs. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint branch moves][jb] preserves deployment choices across rebases; the new target is transporting *spent versus pending* rights through a rebase without cancelling early service or issuing a duplicate credit. **Cheapest first test.** Compare a full-rank change with one collapsing two equal-looking supports; allow timeout and on-time completion after each. Compare expiry/reissue and full verification. **Old-task retention check.** Test A/C/B₁ before and after rebasing, including a rare reader of the aliased support, and B₂ if completion is allowed.

### 13. Charge the first write only for its irreversible part — improvement
**Mechanism.** Split the resource record into live reads that fallback must keep, spent work that cannot be refunded, and reserved capacity safely available to the next job. Require a witnessed next use for any claimed useful refund. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint releases][jr] and [successor-funded returns][sf] already separate capacity and paid costs; the audit targets the false gain of returning an accounting token while the installed first write still pins the resource. **Cheapest first test.** Match two timeout traces with equal outer allocations but a hidden reader in only one, then offer a third write with the supposedly freed footprint. Compare token-only accounting and exact liveness. **Old-task retention check.** Check A/C/B₁ after refund and after the third write, not just when the ledger is updated.

### 14. Preserve real choice after the first public write — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Issue a lease only if its first publication leaves a common fallback and at least two partner-relevant continuations with different observable utility or range; an explicit public demand, not the hidden history, selects the continuation. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Order handoffs][oh] preserves distinct schedules; the bet is preserving economically different second-writer rights after a useful first write using no extra retained learner state. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-band release with a cheap order that silently destroys one band. Compare atomic wait, one cached policy and exact multi-policy planning with frequencies hidden at preparation. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C/B₁ on both branches and fallback, plus B₂ on held-out requests from each distinct band.

### 15. Optimize the lease against an attainable obstruction — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Construct a minimal set of reachable histories and dropout boundaries that jointly exhaust every order-only policy capable of the requested utility. Use the counterexample set to choose one specific preparation change before the next blind lease, not to tune the current hidden histories away. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Group obstructions][go] and [batch-order interventions][bo] already provide negative certificates. The new object is a failure certificate spanning both useful service and interruption safety, whose proposed value is selecting a remedy that transfers to fresh leases. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate products of three attainable histories and two interruption cuts; try a lawful window extension versus an equal-cost batch-order change on a held-out partner class. **Old-task retention check.** Score A/C and installed B at the formerly unsafe interruption cuts and throughout the repaired path.

### 16. Learn a smooth host where fallback does not need its own adapter — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Fit truthful successor work to create a computation that supports its own task and keeps the first write callable when shared support is reclaimed, while requiring an order key to preserve W. Freeze blind pairs and model width before fitting. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Carrier recovery][cr], [successor recovery][sr] and [successor-funded returns][sf] already require a useful shared feature. The interaction to isolate is a learned fallback that leaves just enough support for *conditional paired* service at equal installed utility. **Cheapest first test.** Cross shared versus separately routed C with adaptive versus fixed ordering in a smooth two-head quadratic or tiny network; ablate the C feature at matched C performance using an independent route. **Old-task retention check.** Compare rare A/C and both acquired writes at every deployment boundary and after unrelated training.

### 17. Prove that a narrow relation is enough for two different witnesses — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Build a relation whose answer partitions prove a shared continuation up to a public fork, then discharge two independently frozen witness contracts without a second read. Bill proof transfer and count the common prefix only once. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Group obstructions][go] certifies cells for a reply; the bet is a relation normalized by continuation behavior that transfers across blind witness choices without reconstructing optimizer state. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze two different witness reads before enumerating orders; compare a separate bit per witness, one higher-bandwidth query, exact joint synthesis and a third witness for which the same bit fails. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/C/B₁ through the common prefix and each branch, and B₂ after each served paired write.

### 18. Roll a finite escrow through recurrent defaults — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Carry only bounded live entitlements and scope-valid proof fragments over a series of blind pairs. Require each fallback to retire all abandoned rights while preserving earlier acquired tasks and admitting another real pair without expanding support. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Fixed-budget handoffs][fh] and [successor-funded returns][sf] already test bounded renewal; recurring asymmetric dropout tests whether an unresolved optimizer-conditioned obligation accumulates even when physical allocation stays flat. **Cheapest first test.** On three pair/timeout cycles, alternate the surviving side and interpose normal successor training; compare rolling exact planning and a growing checkpoint cache at equal accounted storage. **Old-task retention check.** Test each previously installed B and A/C after every later fallback and at a final blind paired request.

### 19. Separate exact order keys from tolerance artifacts — improvement
**Mechanism.** Report endpoint discrepancy, the successor's per-item margins, actual W success and later slot influence across a tolerance sweep. Keep the same learning-rate and slot-update clocks in comparisons. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Batch-order interventions][bo] derives the frozen heavy-ball lock; this audit asks whether purported lease service comes from hidden weight movement or time, not the advertised order-induced slot relation. **Cheapest first test.** Contrast constant-rate frozen gradients, one predeclared variable schedule and a tiny smooth curved host. Compare reset, gradient accumulation and matched-clock warmup without the order key. **Old-task retention check.** Test A/C and installed B on near-margin holdouts before W and after a slot-sensitive suffix at every reported tolerance.

### 20. Diagnose “information without admission” — improvement
**Mechanism.** Reject a relation if identifying the actual history would still leave that history blocked at the current time/capacity, or if a compact already-known certificate makes the same pre-query choice safe. Compare against a fallback that performs useful work, not refusal alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Decision-relevant queries][dq] and [group obstructions][go] already reject irrelevant splits; this is a negative control distinguishing epistemic cores from physical lease vetoes. **Cheapest first test.** Give one state an empty useful reply set because the shared support is pinned, alongside a pure ambiguity obstruction with the same public weights. Compare full knowledge and reply synthesis at equal resource caps. **Old-task retention check.** Verify A/C and the live B on the physically blocked fallback and after admitting the feasible ambiguity case.

### 21. Try to compile the lease away — improvement
**Mechanism.** Give a static cached policy, an atomic release, and independent reservations the same action menu and offline proof budget; charge memory for their complete tables and for every invalidation. Compare each useful pairing and half-use event, not just average accuracy. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Safe order families][so] and [order handoffs][oh] already compose and cache proofs. This is a kill test: if an ordinary reservation controller and cached dispatch match the proposed object at full cost, keep no primitive claim. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-price, two-dropout finite host; train/cache on one schedule distribution and score unseen combinations at the same online resource caps. **Old-task retention check.** Retest A/C and each active B through every admitted prefix in every arm, including eviction and expiry paths.

### 22. Red-team the late-arrival boundary — improvement
**Mechanism.** Check duplicate, delayed and out-of-order public events against a monotone record of which entitlement is live and which fallback has published. Treat the event that releases a reservation and the event that admits its partner as competing transitions, never both on the same unit. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Idempotent transaction protocols and [joint branch moves][jb] already address staleness and aborts; this audit detects apparent learner capacity gains caused by counting both legal-looking publication paths. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust three interleavings of arrival, timeout and subsequent reuse in both first-writer directions; compare atomic and lease controllers at identical deadlines. **Old-task retention check.** Test A/C and every legitimately installed B after each interleaving, including the suffix after late-arrival rejection.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does order-only recovery let a genuinely useful first write survive partner default without sacrificing timely paired work?** Start with 01/02/05 on the same smooth host. Cross held-order recovery with escrow/fallback, replay every dropout boundary, and compare the best stacked and exact small-host controls. Demand more useful pairs or cheaper continuation at matched single-arrival service, resources and retention; neither an atomic-only guarantee nor a permanently reserved second slot answers this.
2. **Does one lawful optimizer relation remove an attainable blocking coalition in time?** Use 03/07/20. Derive the reply sets from reachable optimizer states and real orders, not a planted lookup table. Compare with a raw read, exact core-guided search, and a synthesized common reply. A decoy split leaving a core, a late answer, or a physically impossible history must not authorize service.
3. **Can escrow measurably free capacity without extinguishing either live behavior or future choice?** Use 06/09/14 to test a third real consumer and held-out second-writer demands. Require every compatible branch to respect peak scratch, retain prior tasks and honor all advertised rights; a ledger refund or one favorable trace does not count.
4. **Can the proof and fallback be reused over repeated blind leases at bounded cost?** Use 15/18/21 across changing partners and repeated defaults. Compare cache, serial and checkpoint/replay at equal end-to-end costs. Stop if supported history coverage shrinks, proof or model state grows, or the gain vanishes outside a deliberately sharp gate.
