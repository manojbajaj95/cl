# Root: Test-time continual learners (learning during inference itself)

Root node (depth 0) · 34 candidate primitives · All tagged **Ambitious but doable (P)** · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** Standard continual learning treats *inference* (a frozen forward pass) and *learning* (an offline or post-episode weight update) as separate phases. Modern test-time training (TTT), fast weights, and context-to-LoRA hypernetworks blur that boundary only within a single prompt or episode—typically resetting fast state between tasks, applying fixed-schedule updates, or treating *thinking* (chain-of-thought, search, latent looping) and *updating* (modifying weights) as disjoint pipelines. A genuine **test-time continual learner** learns *during inference itself* across an unbounded stream of tasks. Doing so requires new computational primitives across five axes: (A) fast-weight memories that persist, compose, and revise across tasks without resetting; (B) direct in-context-to-weights transfer and bidirectional compilation inside the forward/decoding loop; (C) unified FLOP-matched allocation between thinking and updating; (D) mid-task introspective control where the model itself decides whether, where, how reversibly, and how many steps to update; and (E) serving-safe cross-inference lifecycles that graduate fast weights to slow weights without offline sleep or write-skew collapse.

**Scope and distance from existing roots.** Informed by the [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md), the [flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md), and neighboring roots—especially [test-time weights in deployment](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795570145-root-test-time-weights/tree/root-test-time-weights.md) (PR #311), [LoRA-writing learners](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795577637-root-lora-learner/tree/root-lora-learner.md), [sleep and dreaming](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795574658-root-sleep-and-dreaming/tree/root-sleep-and-dreaming.md), and [learner–world co-evolution](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795582291-root-learner-world-coevolution/tree/root-learner-world-coevolution.md) (PR #363). Whereas PR #311 focuses on *deployment governance* (authorship/provenance masking, delayed external verdicts / falsifiable writes, multi-user scope ladders, and curated-free retention meters), this root targets the **inference-time learning machinery itself**: fast-weight persistence and interference control, live ICL-to-weight compilation, thinking-vs-updating compute exchange, and mid-task plasticity decisions. It explicitly excludes ledger-closed lines: generator-identity / self-quizzing replay effects (PR #8), multi-cycle double-dosing oscillation claims (PR #30), in-context lessons as a global temperature knob / A3 margin confounds (PR #29, PR #31), prompt-addressable checkpoints packed into one shared LoRA (L1 falsified), generic weight-ISA naming (W-ISA `2611.13232` / WeightCode `2611.25476`), and first-order linear-algebra tautologies. Ambitious ideas are kept as **ambitious but doable** primitives with minimal falsifiable tests rather than discarded.

## A · Fast-weight & TTT primitives that survive across tasks

### TT01 · Spectral-band fast weights (frequency-multiplexed inference memory) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Partition each fast-weight matrix $W_{\text{fast}} = W^{(0)} + W^{(1)} + W^{(2)}$ into orthogonal decay-rate bands (sub-prompt working state $\lambda_0 \approx 0.5$, episode scratchpad $\lambda_1 \approx 0.95$, cross-task persistent band $\lambda_2 \approx 0.999$) equipped with an online *inter-band promotion operator*: singular directions that recur with consistent sign across $\ge k$ independent queries in $W^{(0)}/W^{(1)}$ are rotated into $W^{(2)}$ during inference, while one-off prompt noise decays harmlessly in $W^{(0)}$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Titans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), [Nested Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695), [Fast Weight Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27763), and [Toaster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23648) use fixed decay gates or cascaded linear memories; none promotes *specific recurring singular directions* dynamically across decay bands during live inference.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4-layer linear-attention / DeltaNet transformer on a 200-query synthetic stream mixing 5 recurring latent rules with 50 one-off distractor rules, multi-seed. Compare single-band DeltaNet, fixed multi-timescale EMA fast weights, episode-reset TTT, and spectral-band promotion at matched state bytes and FLOPs. Kill if fixed multi-timescale EMA matches both recurring-rule convergence and distractor rejection.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ (seen in queries 1–40, absent in queries 41–200) *with live $W_{\text{fast}}$ loaded* after 50 distractor queries.

### TT02 · Reversibly addressable fast-weight frames (stack-allocated inference plasticity) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Organize inference-time fast weights as a lexical call stack of *weight frames*: entering a sub-problem, tool-debugging detour, or speculative hypothesis pushes a local frame $\Delta W_{\text{frame}}$; exiting either *pops* it (exact zero-residue restoration of parent weights) or *returns* only a low-rank summary delta $\Delta W_{\text{ret}}$ into the parent frame—preventing dead-end inference updates from contaminating persistent continual state.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [RW-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28053) isolates request-level TTT state for batched serving, and [Modular TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07110) composes static modules; a *recursive push/pop/return frame algebra* for mid-task speculative fast-weight exploration is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B rank-8 fast LoRA on a multi-step synthetic coding/API stream where 40% of sub-tasks are dead-end detours or misleading error traces before finding the real fix. Compare flat persistent TTT, episode-reset TTT, and stack-framed push/pop/return TTT at matched FLOPs. Kill if flat TTT with gradient clipping or simple checkpoint-restore-on-failure ties on post-detour accuracy and state cleanliness.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure zero-drift exact restoration on old tasks $A/B$ immediately after popping 20 dead-end frames, plus $A/B$ accuracy after committing 10 valid return deltas.

### TT03 · Interference-certified delta-rule updates (null-space-projected online TTT) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Standard DeltaNet/TTT-E2E updates $\Delta W = \eta (v - Wk)k^\top$ overwrite earlier task associations whenever new keys $k$ overlap old keys. Maintain a compact low-rank sketching projector $P_{\text{old}}$ of high-usage old-task key subspaces and embed projection directly inside the *per-token inner TTT update* $\Delta W = \eta (v - Wk)(P_{\perp} k)^\top$, steering live inference writes around protected old-task subspaces in $O(d \cdot r)$ per token.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Error Correction in Trained TTT Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16268) analyzes association revision within a single sequence, while AlphaEdit and OGD project offline fine-tuning gradients; embedding a *continual old-task null-space projector inside the per-token fast-weight delta rule* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Synthetic key-value associative recall stream with 8 sequential task blocks whose key distributions have controlled cosine overlap $\rho \in \{0.0, 0.3, 0.6\}$. Compare standard DeltaNet, L2-decayed DeltaNet, and null-space-projected DeltaNet at matched state size and FLOPs. Kill if tuned decay alone matches old-block recall at $\rho = 0.6$ without sacrificing new-block accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure recall accuracy on blocks $A/B$ (blocks 1–2) after processing blocks 3–8 continuously with no fast-weight reset.

### TT04 · Cross-layer shared fast-weight bus with typed read/write ports — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Replace isolated per-layer TTT memories with one shared cross-layer fast-weight bus partitioned by *typed functional ports*: early layers write lexical/entity bindings, middle layers write relational/procedural rules, and late layers read/write calibration shifts—enforced by port-level orthogonality so writing a new entity at an early port cannot overwrite a middle-layer procedure.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Universal Test-Time Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05484) shares a single fast-weight memory across layers for long-context efficiency; adding *typed functional read/write ports with cross-layer interference isolation* for continual multi-task retention is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 6-layer small transformer on a continual stream alternating entity-renaming tasks and compositional-rule tasks. Compare private per-layer TTT, untyped Universal TTT, and typed-port shared-bus TTT at equal fast-weight parameter count. Kill if untyped Universal TTT ties on cross-type interference (retaining rules after entity renames and vice versa).
- **Old-task retention:** Score old compositional rules $A$ after 50 entity-rename inference steps, and old entity bindings $B$ after 50 rule-shift inference steps.

### TT05 · Forward-pass predictive-coding fast weights (local-error inference learning without BPTT) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Eliminate inference-time backpropagation through fast weights by giving each block a local predictive-coding fast-weight update: block $\ell$ predicts block $\ell+1$'s representation and updates its fast weights on the local prediction residual *during the forward sweep itself*, with a dead-zone threshold so already-mastered old tasks generate zero residual and zero weight change.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [DeltaNet-PC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15185) removes BPTT during *outer training* of DeltaNet, and [GradLev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34174) predicts costates for token-parallel TTT; using *local forward-pass predictive-coding residuals with dead-zone quiescence* as a continual inference-time learner is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4-layer small transformer on a continual sequence-shift benchmark (3 synthetic grammars arriving sequentially). Compare backprop TTT-E2E, DeltaNet, and forward-pass predictive-coding fast weights at matched wall-clock inference latency and parameter budget. Kill if standard DeltaNet ties on both latency-normalized adaptation speed and old-grammar quiescence.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that revisiting old grammars $A/B$ induces near-zero residual norm and preserves $A/B$ perplexity after adapting to grammar $C$.

### TT06 · Contrastive unlearning impulses inside fast weights (surgical inference-time erasure) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a live stream contradicts a previously internalized rule or fact, additive fast-weight updates merely superimpose the new association over the old one, leaving interference ghosts under paraphrased queries. Pair every contradiction-triggered write with a *two-phase erase-then-write primitive*: first inject a bounded rank-1 negative-projection impulse along the stale key-value residual to zero the old retrieval score, then write the replacement association into the freed capacity.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Error Correction in Trained TTT Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16268) shows standard TTT updates struggle to cleanly revise existing associations; an explicit *paired erase-impulse + slot-reuse operator* inside fast weights is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Synthetic associative-rule stream with 40 rules where 10 rules are revised mid-stream and queried with both exact and paraphrased keys. Compare standard DeltaNet/TTT-E2E, stronger forgetting decay, and erase-then-write fast weights at equal step budget. Kill if stronger uniform decay matches revised-rule paraphrase accuracy without hurting the 30 unrevised rules.
- **Old-task retention:** Score all 30 unrevised rules ($A/B$) under exact and paraphrased queries after 10 surgical revisions, verifying zero collateral erasure.

### TT07 · Cached micro-adapter composition (Microfitting with interference-free merge algebra) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Rather than running TTT from scratch on every recurring domain or collapsing all updates into one adapter, maintain a bounded library of cached TTT *basis deltas* $\{\Delta W_1, \dots, \Delta W_K\}$. During inference on a new prompt, solve a tiny $O(K^2)$ mixing-weight optimization over active basis deltas to assemble a bespoke inference weight state—allocating a fresh basis delta only when the unexplained residual exceeds a novelty floor.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Microfitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20258) caches per-context TTT updates for continual specialization; adding an *online inference-time basis-composition & residual-allocation algebra* that prevents linear library blowup and cross-domain overwriting is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B on a 30-domain continual stream where domains are combinations of 6 underlying latent shifts. Compare per-prompt TTT, single-adapter continual TTT, naive cache-all Microfitting, and basis-composed Microfitting at matched memory bytes and inference FLOPs. Kill if single-adapter TTT or naive cache-and-retrieve ties on unseen compositional domains at equal byte budget.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate zero-regression recall on the earliest domains $A/B$ after 25 subsequent domains, verifying that basis capping/composition does not corrupt old basis elements.

## B · In-context-to-weights transfer & live compilation

### TT08 · Closed-form ICL-to-MLP rank-1 crystallization (zero-gradient context internalization) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Exploit the analytical equivalence between self-attention over a context prefix and an implicit rank-1 contextual shift of MLP weights ([2507.16003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16003)): during inference, directly compute the closed-form attention-induced MLP weight shift $\Delta W_{\text{ICL}}$ produced by a solved few-shot prefix and *crystallize* it into a persistent low-rank delta in a single forward pass—requiring zero backpropagation, zero external hypernetwork, and zero offline distillation.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Learning Without Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16003) and [Simple Generalisation of Implicit ICL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11255) derive the implicit rank-1 shift theoretically; [Doc-to-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902) and [Internalizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11715) train separate hypernetworks. Turning the *analytical implicit-ICL shift itself into a direct, training-free continual weight-crystallization operator* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B on a sequence of 10 synthetic in-context classification/formatting tasks. After 1 forward pass on each task's 8-shot prefix, crystallize $\Delta W_{\text{ICL}}$ and drop the prefix. Compare against 1-step and 5-step TTT/LoRA fine-tuning and zero-shot baseline at matched FLOPs. Kill if 1-step LoRA SGD beats closed-form crystallization on both accuracy after prefix removal and speed.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure untouched old task $A/B$ accuracy and base WikiText perplexity after sequentially crystallizing 10 prefixes.

### TT09 · Streaming KV-to-weight eviction compiler (bounded-context continual absorption) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a fixed-size KV cache fills during long continual inference, instead of evicting old KV entries (forgetting) or summarizing them into lossy tokens, run a lightweight *eviction compiler* on each about-to-be-evicted KV block: fit a tiny localized weight delta whose Jacobian reproduces the evicted block's attention output on a compact set of active query directions, freeing KV slots while preserving functional effect indefinitely.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Cache2LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24107) and [Cortex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20399) convert whole cached documents offline; *incremental, eviction-triggered KV-to-weight compilation* operating continuously at the KV-cache boundary during streaming inference is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B with a capped 512-token KV cache on a 4,096-token continual stream containing 15 embedded task rules and facts. Compare sliding-window eviction, attention-sink retention, periodic text summarization, and eviction-triggered KV-to-weight compilation at equal KV bytes and total FLOPs. Kill if attention-sink + text summarization matches late-stream recall of early rules.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both early-stream rules ($A/B$, evicted 3,000 tokens ago) and a held-out general QA suite with the compiled eviction deltas active.

### TT10 · Context-to-weight handoff with double-dosing cancellation (subtract-as-you-absorb) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Transferring an active in-context rule into weights while the prompt remains in the KV cache causes the rule to fire twice—distorting calibration (PR #30 found keeping context raises error +3.85 pp while abrupt deletion raises it +13.25 pp). Pair every live context-to-weight transfer with a *conservation-of-effect handoff*: as the weight delta's fractional absorption $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ ramps up, apply a matched $(1-\alpha)$ attention-logit attenuation mask to the source KV tokens so the combined logit shift stays invariant throughout the handoff.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** No-Context Anchoring ([2606.11627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627)) and [Localized Context Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21427) distill offline and then drop context abruptly; PR #30 falsified growing multi-cycle oscillation but confirmed steady-state double-dosing / abrupt-drop calibration gaps. A *continuous $(1-\alpha)$ KV-attenuation + $\alpha$ weight-absorption handoff operator* during live inference is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on the PR #30 multi-lesson calibration benchmark, comparing abrupt context deletion, keep-in-context, NCA, and continuous conservation-of-effect handoff over $\alpha \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$. Kill if NCA or linear logit interpolation ties on peak calibration error across the transition.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero calibration degradation on untouched non-transferred rules $A/B$ (both in-context and in-weights) during and after the handoff.

### TT11 · Structural vs. episodic context factoring before weight transfer — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Live prompts mix ephemeral instance data (session IDs, one-off numbers) with reusable structural rules (schemas, reasoning procedures). Before compiling a context into weights during inference, run a *query-invariance filter*: probe the context's induced hidden-state shift across 2–4 cheap synthetic query perturbations; compile only the *query-invariant subspace* (structural rules) into persistent weights, while leaving the *query-specific residual* (episodic facts) in transient KV memory.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Encoding Context into LLM Weights: What Makes It Context?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17686) and [Where Should LLM Agents Adapt at Test Time?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19700) study the empirical split; PR #311 card 12 targets retrieval residuals. Using *query-perturbation invariance of induced hidden-state shifts* as an inline compile-vs-keep filter during inference is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a continual stream where each context mixes 1 reusable transformation rule and 5 one-off ephemeral facts. Compare compile-all TTT, retrieval-only, random-rank compilation, and invariance-filtered compilation at equal adapter norm and FLOPs. Kill if compile-all TTT + weight decay matches structural generalization without memorizing ephemeral noise.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure old structural rule retention $A/B$ and verify that ephemeral facts from task 1 do not hallucinate into task 10 outputs.

### TT12 · Hypernetwork delta-composer with compositional orthogonality constraints — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Context-to-LoRA hypernetworks emit a LoRA for one document in isolation, causing severe destructive interference when 10–50 contexts are internalized sequentially. Condition the hypernetwork on a compact *occupied-subspace sketch* $S_{t-1}$ of all previously emitted deltas so that each newly generated $\Delta W_t = H(c_t, S_{t-1})$ is emitted directly in the orthogonal complement of prior internalized contexts during a single forward pass.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Doc-to-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902), [Internalizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11715), and [Distributions over Weight Updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01934) generate per-document adapters independently without sequential collision awareness. A *stateful, subspace-conditioned hypernetwork compiler* for multi-context continual inference is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer (or GPT-2 small) with a lightweight hypernetwork trained to internalize 20 sequential synthetic rule documents. Compare independent hypernetwork + addition, hypernetwork + post-hoc TIES/orthogonal projection, and subspace-conditioned hypernetwork generation at equal rank and latency. Kill if post-hoc Gram-Schmidt projection of independent hypernetwork outputs matches accuracy and retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate accuracy on documents 1–3 ($A/B$) after sequentially internalizing documents 4–20 without re-reading documents 1–3.

### TT13 · Reasoning-trace crystallization (compiling multi-step CoT into single-pass weights mid-stream) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a model solves a hard recurring sub-problem via a long chain-of-thought (e.g., 300+ tokens), immediately apply a fast inference-time *trace-stripping crystallization step*: distill the verified $(\text{prompt} \to \text{final answer})$ mapping into a low-rank delta, converting slow System-2 deliberation into fast System-1 weights *within the same continual stream* so subsequent instances of the same pattern solve in 1× tokens.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Consolidating Reasoning with TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02104) merges multi-agent reasoning traces for one problem, and [Sleep-time Compute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171) pre-computes offline text summaries; *online intra-session System-2-to-System-1 weight crystallization* gated on recurring problem signatures is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a continual stream of 5 recurring symbolic/arithmetic rule families (20 queries per family), measuring total tokens + update FLOPs to reach target accuracy. Compare always-CoT, prompt-cached few-shot CoT, and online trace-crystallization TTT. Kill if prompt-cached few-shot examples achieve lower total FLOPs at equal accuracy on queries 5–20.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that crystallizing families 3–5 does not degrade single-pass accuracy on earlier crystallized families $A/B$ (families 1–2) or general reasoning probes.

### TT14 · Reversible weight-to-context decompilation (unpacking a weight delta back into editable tokens) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** In-context knowledge is inspectable and easily overridden by new instructions, whereas weight updates are opaque and sticky ([2506.20989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20989)). Train every inference-time fast-weight write alongside a lightweight *decompiler head* that maps a conflicting weight delta $\Delta W_k$ back into a short editable token sequence (or soft-prompt rule summary): whenever a live query triggers high conflict with $\Delta W_k$, the model *decompiles* $\Delta W_k$ into context, edits/resolves it via CoT, and re-crystallizes the corrected delta.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Can Gradient Descent Simulate Prompting?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20989) compares prompting vs. GD expressivity; N1 (PR #41) does offline code→weights→code denoising. An *inference-time bidirectional weight↔context codec* triggered specifically on live rule conflicts is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer on a continual rule-override stream where 30% of previously internalized rules receive partial exceptions mid-stream ("same as rule X, except when Y"). Compare direct overwrite TTT, keeping all rules in text, and decompile-edit-recrystallize at equal context budget. Kill if direct overwrite TTT with 3 gradient steps matches exception accuracy and preserves the non-excepted cases.
- **Old-task retention:** Check that decompiling and editing rule $C$ leaves untouched internalized rules $A/B$ bit-exact or within noise on held-out probes.

## C · Compute-time tradeoffs: thinking vs. updating at inference

### TT15 · Marginal-value-of-compute router: thinking FLOPs vs. updating FLOPs — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Given a fixed per-query or per-session inference FLOP budget $B$, every FLOP can buy either *thinking* (extra CoT tokens, beam branches, or looped depth) or *updating* (fast-weight gradient steps). Maintain an online *marginal-value-of-compute estimator* comparing expected gain-per-FLOP of thinking ($\partial U / \partial F_{\text{think}}$) against amortized gain-per-FLOP of updating ($H \cdot \partial U / \partial F_{\text{upd}}$ over estimated horizon $H$), dynamically routing each query's budget between the two channels.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Test-Time Scaling Regimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04001) and [Budget Internalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26172) optimize thinking compute alone; [Agentic-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12002) learns an offline TTT policy. A *unified online FLOP-exchange router* that explicitly prices thinking against persistent weight updates in one budget is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer / Qwen2.5-0.5B on a mixed stream containing both one-off reasoning puzzles (favoring thinking) and recurring 30-query domain shifts (favoring updating), under a strict matched total FLOP cap. Compare thinking-only, updating-only, fixed 50/50 split, and marginal-value-of-compute routing. Kill if a fixed 50/50 split or entropy-threshold heuristic ties on total stream accuracy at equal FLOPs.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old domain tasks $A/B$ throughout the mixed stream to confirm that routing budget to updates on recurring shifts does not erode old domains.

### TT16 · Amortized-horizon plasticity pricing (converting future-query frequency into update budget) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** A weight update costs upfront compute $C_{\text{upd}}$ plus retention risk $C_{\text{ret}}$, which loses to in-context thinking on a single query but pays off if the skill recurs $N$ times and saves $\Delta C_{\text{think}}$ per query. Maintain an online *recurrence-hazard estimator* $\hat{\lambda}(z)$ over query embeddings and authorize a weight update if and only if $\hat{\lambda}(z) \cdot \Delta C_{\text{think}} > C_{\text{upd}} + C_{\text{ret}}$, sizing update rank/steps in proportion to expected amortization.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Sleep-time Compute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171) assumes uniform offline amortization; [Test-Time Memory Evolution vs. TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32046) compares fixed budgets on static benchmarks. Making *online recurrence-hazard amortization* the economic gate for inference-time weight writes is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Continual stream with Zipfian task recurrence (20% of task types generate 80% of queries; 80% appear only 1–2 times), strict FLOP + parameter-drift budget. Compare update-everything TTT, update-never (ICL/CoT only), random update gating, and hazard-priced plasticity. Kill if simple count-threshold ("update after seeing a cluster twice") matches Pareto efficiency across accuracy, FLOPs, and forgetting.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that refusing updates on rare 1-shot queries strictly improves retention of old tasks $A/B$ compared to update-everything TTT at equal total accuracy.

### TT17 · Think-to-verify, then update (asymmetric compute split between generation and weight write) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Updating weights on cheap, unverified single-pass outputs causes self-generated collapse ([2610.05076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076)), while running heavy search/verification on every query is unaffordable. Introduce an *asymmetric verify-then-crystallize primitive*: allocate heavy thinking compute (verifier-checked search / self-consistency / backtracking) *only* on high-surprise queries to mint a certified target, then immediately spend a small update budget to write that certified target into persistent weights so subsequent queries in the same regime run at 1× cheap pass.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [TTRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084) updates on unverified majority votes; [Consolidating Reasoning with TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02104) merges traces within a single problem. Coupling *selective heavy deliberation* as an *amortized label factory for persistent continual weight updates* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a continual coding/math stream with an executable unit-test verifier and 5 sequential domain shifts (30 queries each), under a fixed total token+gradient FLOP cap. Compare uniform-compute CoT, TTRL majority-vote TTT, and asymmetric think-to-verify-then-update. Kill if uniform-compute CoT or TTRL matches end-of-stream accuracy at equal total FLOPs.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure zero-regression accuracy on old domains $A/B$ (using 1× cheap pass, no heavy thinking) after adapting through shifts 3–5.

### TT18 · Update-assisted search (using micro-TTT steps to prune tree search / beam search) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Rather than treating tree search (thinking) and TTT (updating) as mutually exclusive, interleave them *within a single hard query*: whenever a beam or tree branch hits a verified dead end (failed test, constraint violation), apply a temporary fast-weight micro-update that suppresses that failure mode across all remaining sibling branches—reducing total search width needed—then pop the temporary delta (or promote only verified principles) upon solving the query.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Provable Beam Search Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38672) and [ThinkRetrieve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10928) keep weights frozen during tree search; *intra-search fast-weight steering* where sibling branches immediately inherit micro-weight updates from failed peers is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small code/planning model on a continual suite of 50 multi-step constraint-satisfaction or code-synthesis tasks with a fixed node-expansion + update FLOP budget. Compare frozen beam search, beam search with text failure-history injection, and update-assisted beam search. Kill if injecting failed-branch summaries into the prompt beats or ties micro-TTT steering at equal FLOPs.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that popping temporary search deltas (and retaining only promoted principles) causes zero degradation on untouched old tasks $A/B$.

### TT19 · Depth-vs-plasticity exchange in looped transformers (latent recurrence vs. fast-weight writes) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** A looped transformer can spend extra inference FLOPs either on $L$ additional recurrent passes through frozen weights (latent thinking) or on $K$ fast-weight updates to the looped block (modifying the transition operator itself). Formalize the *depth-vs-plasticity switch*: monitor latent fixed-point convergence $\|h^{(\ell)} - h^{(\ell-1)}\|$ during looping; when latent updates stall (residual plateau) while output entropy remains high, switch remaining FLOPs from extra loops $L$ to a fast-weight step $K$ on the looped block.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adaptive Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748) adapt loop count $L$ with frozen weights; [ParaTTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38255) updates deep TTT memories at fixed depth. A *joint loop-depth vs. fast-weight-update controller* triggered by latent fixed-point saturation is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Tiny looped transformer (2 unique layers, up to 12 loops + rank-4 fast weight) on a continual stream mixing multi-hop reasoning tasks (needing depth) and rule-shift tasks (needing weight change) at matched per-token FLOPs. Compare adaptive looping only, fixed-loop TTT, and saturation-switched depth-vs-plasticity. Kill if fixed-loop TTT ties on both multi-hop depth tasks and rule-shift tasks at equal FLOPs.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old multi-hop and old rule tasks $A/B$ after 50 rule-shift updates, verifying that fast-weight steps on the shared looped block do not destabilize latent fixed points for $A/B$.

### TT20 · Speculative weight updates (parallel shadow-updating during streaming decoding) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Synchronous mid-task TTT stalls token streaming. Run an *asynchronous speculative weight-update worker* on the incoming prompt/context prefix in parallel while the base model streams initial tokens; at each clause or tool-call boundary, check whether the speculative delta improves likelihood on the latest exogenous tokens *and* passes a 4-probe old-task retention check—if yes, hot-swap it into the active forward pass; if no, discard it at zero latency penalty.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Speculative decoding accelerates frozen-weight token generation; [RW-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28053) schedules synchronous per-request TTT. *Asynchronous speculative weight-updating with live perplexity + retention hot-swap* is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU/single-GPU simulation with GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B on a streaming multi-turn tool/QA benchmark measuring both wall-clock time-to-first-token (TTFT) and end-to-end accuracy. Compare synchronous TTT, frozen streaming, and speculative hot-swap TTT at matched peak compute. Kill if synchronous 1-step TTT has negligible latency overhead or matches accuracy-per-millisecond.
- **Old-task retention:** Track old-task $A/B$ accuracy across accepted hot-swaps and verify that the 4-probe inline gate rejects at least 80% of planted damaging speculative deltas.

### TT21 · Overthinking-to-updating phase transition (escaping deliberation loops via targeted plasticity) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Reasoning models enter unproductive "overthinking" loops ([2610.13570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13570)) when their frozen weights contradict a new domain convention in the prompt, causing endless vacillation between prior and context. Detect *circular deliberation* mid-decoding (repeated n-gram/state cycles or oscillating answer probabilities across CoT steps) and trigger a *deliberation-break update*: pause CoT, take 1–3 targeted fast-weight steps on the local domain specification, and resume decoding from the updated weights.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Overthinking Termination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13570) simply truncates generation early; [Agentic-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12002) chooses TTT before decoding begins. Using *mid-CoT circularity detection* to trigger a phase transition from thinking to weight updating is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small reasoning-capable model (Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B) on a continual stream of counter-prior domain rules (e.g., redefined operators or inverted tool conventions) where frozen CoT vacillates. Compare frozen CoT, early-termination CoT, always-TTT, and circularity-triggered deliberation-break TTT at matched token+update FLOPs. Kill if early-termination CoT or pre-task 1-step TTT matches accuracy at equal FLOPs.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure accuracy on standard (non-inverted) old operators $A/B$ after 10 deliberation-break updates, verifying that localized updates do not corrupt base conventions.

## D · Models that decide mid-task whether, when, and how to update

### TT22 · Mid-decoding plasticity tokens (first-class `<UPDATE>` / `<FREEZE>` / `<REVERT>` control actions) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Expose weight plasticity directly inside the model's decoding action space via three control tokens: `<UPDATE>` commits a fast-weight step over the preceding marked span, `<FREEZE>` suspends fast-weight updates during noisy or speculative stretches, and `<REVERT>` rolls back the most recent mid-task update if downstream reasoning or tool output contradicts it—trained via RL on continual multi-task streams with an explicit old-task retention penalty.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [SEAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943) generates offline self-edit specifications between tasks, and [Agentic-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12002) trains a pre-task TTT policy; SM6 (PR #43) flagged write-token overlap with weight-space programming. Making *fine-grained mid-decoding `<UPDATE>/<FREEZE>/<REVERT>` bracketing* an inline continual-learning policy with retention rewards is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Tiny transformer (or Qwen2.5-0.5B with RL/bandit wrapper) on a continual stream mixing clean rule spans, misleading distractor spans, and mid-episode corrections. Compare always-update TTT, frozen ICL, and learned `<UPDATE>/<FREEZE>/<REVERT>` control at equal update budgets. Kill if a simple loss-threshold heuristic matches both distractor rejection and final accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate old tasks $A/B$ after 100 episodes with the learned plasticity policy active, confirming the policy emits `<FREEZE>`/`<REVERT>` on retention-damaging spans.

### TT23 · Gradient-norm / Fisher-conflict pre-flight gate (aborting damaging updates before they land) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Before applying any candidate mid-task gradient $g_t$, compute an $O(r)$ *pre-flight conflict score* comparing expected current-task gain $\|g_t\|^2$ against estimated old-task damage $g_t^\top \hat{F}_{\text{old}} g_t$ using a low-rank sketch of old-task Fisher directions. Mid-task, the model updates weights only if the *plasticity-to-damage ratio* $\|g_t\|^2 / (g_t^\top \hat{F}_{\text{old}} g_t + \epsilon) > \tau$; otherwise it aborts the write and falls back to in-context reasoning for that step.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Select-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36794) gates updates by query relevance; [Unsupervised Layer-Wise Dynamic TTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09719) gates by prediction entropy. Gating *mid-task update admission on the real-time plasticity-to-old-task-damage ratio* with seamless ICL fallback is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Linear/MLP and GPT-2 small continual streams where 50% of incoming updates align with old-task Fisher directions (high conflict) and 50% lie in safe orthogonal directions. Compare unconditional TTT, EWC-regularized TTT, entropy-gated TTT, and pre-flight conflict gating + ICL fallback at equal compute. Kill if online EWC regularization alone matches both new-task accuracy (including conflicted tasks solved via ICL) and old-task retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ loss across high-conflict and low-conflict segments, verifying that pre-flight abortion keeps $A/B$ strictly inside tolerance.

### TT24 · Mid-task epistemic vs. aleatoric surprise gating (update on learnable structure, not noise) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Raw token surprisal fires hardest on aleatoric noise (random hashes, typos, stochastic tool IDs), causing surprise-gated TTT to waste fast-weight capacity memorizing noise. Attach a lightweight *epistemic-disagreement probe* (2 fast-weight heads or low-cost MC-dropout variance) mid-pass and trigger weight updates *only* when epistemic disagreement is high (learnable rule shift), staying frozen when entropy is purely aleatoric.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Titans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663) gates fast-weight writes on raw gradient surprise; [WISE-ATTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37687) uses uncertainty to query human labels. *Online epistemic-vs-aleatoric decomposition* as an autonomous mid-task write gate for continual fast weights is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Synthetic continual stream mixing structured rule shifts (low irreducible entropy, high epistemic error) with high-entropy random-noise blocks (UUIDs/random tokens). Compare Titans-style raw-surprise gating, constant-LR TTT, and epistemic-disagreement gating at equal parameter count and FLOPs. Kill if raw-surprise gating with gradient clipping matches rule-learning speed without corrupting memory on noise blocks.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old tasks $A/B$ after passing 20 high-entropy aleatoric noise blocks through the live inference stream.

### TT25 · Reversibility-graded update commitment (tentative → latched → consolidated mid-task) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Replace the binary "update vs. don't update" choice with a *three-tier mid-task commitment ladder* inside a single learner: Tier 0 (pure KV/ICL, zero weight touch), Tier 1 (volatile fast-weight delta that auto-expires at episode end), and Tier 2 (persistent continual delta). Mid-task, the model escalates a hypothesis from Tier 0 → 1 → 2 only as it survives successive internal consistency checks (e.g., matching predictions on follow-up turns or tool returns).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** PR #311 card 13 promotes updates across *deployment scopes* (request → user → global) via multi-user corroboration counts; here, *intra-task reversibility-graded commitment* lets a single inference stream graduate knowledge across persistence tiers without waiting for multiple users.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a multi-turn agent stream where 50% of early-turn hints are revised or contradicted by turn 3, and 50% persist across future episodes. Compare immediate persistent TTT, episode-reset-only TTT, and 3-tier staged commitment at equal FLOPs. Kill if episode-reset TTT + end-of-episode distillation matches both mid-episode adaptation and cross-episode retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy after 40 episodes containing 20 contradicted early-turn hints, verifying that contradicted Tier-1 deltas never leak into Tier-2 persistent weights.

### TT26 · Layer-selective mid-task plasticity routing (updating only the abstraction level that failed) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Different inference-time shifts live at different depths: lexical renames belong in early layers, relational/rule shifts in middle layers, and output formatting in late layers. Run a lightweight *per-layer residual attribution check* (comparing gradient-aligned residual sensitivity across blocks) mid-task and unlock plasticity in *only* the top 1–2 implicated layers while freezing all others, minimizing collateral interference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Unsupervised Layer-Wise Dynamic TTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09719) adapts per-layer learning rates across batches by entropy; MX1 (PR #36) studied offline consolidation layer placement. *Per-step residual-attribution routing of mid-task plasticity* to the exact failing abstraction level is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 6-layer transformer / GPT-2 small on a continual stream alternating lexical-rename shifts, mid-level rule shifts, and output-format shifts. Compare all-layer TTT, fixed-last-2-layer TTT, entropy-weighted dynamic TTA, and residual-attribution layer routing at equal active-parameter budget. Kill if fixed-last-2-layer TTT or entropy-weighted TTA matches both adaptation speed and cross-level retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure retention of old lexical, rule, and format tasks $A/B$ after adapting to each shift type, checking that lexical updates do not perturb middle-layer rules and vice versa.

### TT27 · Self-calibrated update stopping rule (halting TTT iterations before overfitting or forgetting) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Fixed step counts ($K=1$ or $K=5$ TTT steps per chunk) systematically under-adapt hard shifts and over-adapt easy ones. Equip the inference loop with an *online dual-boundary stopping operator*: iterate inner TTT steps $k = 0, 1, \dots, K_{\max}$ and halt as soon as either (a) leave-one-token-out current-context gain drops below $\delta_{\text{gain}}$, OR (b) KL divergence on a tiny cached 4-token *anchor micro-batch* crosses $\delta_{\text{KL}}$—allocating 0 steps to familiar inputs and up to $K_{\max}$ steps to novel ones.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Beyond Update Counts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36109) demonstrates that raw update counts poorly predict test-time adaptation quality; standard TTT uses fixed step schedules. A *dual-boundary (gain-floor + anchor-KL-ceiling) adaptive stopping rule* inside live inference is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B on a heterogeneous stream mixing familiar in-distribution queries (needing 0 steps) and abrupt shift chunks (needing 5–10 steps). Compare fixed $K \in \{1, 3, 8\}$ TTT and dual-boundary stopping at matched average FLOPs per query. Kill if fixed $K=1$ or single-boundary loss-threshold stopping matches both shift accuracy and old-task KL.
- **Old-task retention:** Score held-out old tasks $A/B$ across 200 mixed queries, verifying that anchor-KL stopping bounds cumulative drift strictly below fixed-$K$ TTT.

### TT28 · Counterfactual no-update shadow pass (live mid-task A/B against the frozen self) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** On a small scheduled fraction of mid-task decision points (e.g., every 16th token or after each tool return), evaluate both the updated weights $W + \Delta W$ and the frozen pre-update weights $W_0$ in the same batched forward pass. If $W + \Delta W$ fails to beat $W_0$ on exogenous observation likelihood—or exhibits large logit divergence on routine non-task syntax tokens—automatically damp or roll back $\Delta W$ before completing the response.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** PR #311 card 24 regenerates past context offline with a frozen generator to estimate self-authored drift; here, a *same-pass batched frozen-vs-updated counterfactual veto* runs inline during decoding to catch destructive mid-task updates in milliseconds.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a noisy continual stream where 30% of incoming updates are corrupted or adversarial shifts. Compare unvetoed TTT, perplexity-threshold TTT, and same-pass frozen-shadow veto at matched compute (accounting for the shadow pass's ~6% FLOP overhead). Kill if simple perplexity-spike rollback matches bad-update detection AUC and stream accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy after 100 noisy queries, verifying that inline shadow vetoes block corrupted updates before they accumulate.

## E · Cross-inference lifecycle, serving-safe continual state & retention contracts

### TT29 · Inference-time consolidation without offline sleep (streaming fast-to-slow distillation) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Fast-weight state saturates over long continual streams ([2608.27763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27763)), yet offline sleep pauses ([2504.13171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)) interrupt live serving. Introduce a *read-triggered trickle-consolidation operator*: whenever a live query actively reads a fast-weight association (proving ongoing utility), transfer a tiny fraction $\gamma \ll 1$ of its functional output into a slow continual adapter via one forward-aligned distillation step while decaying unread fast weights—keeping fast capacity bounded indefinitely with zero offline downtime.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Learning, Fast and Slow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12484) alternates fast inference adaptation and offline slow RL phases; Titans/Nested Learning decay fast weights without read-triggered slow consolidation. *Read-triggered trickle consolidation amortized into live inference reads* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer on a 1,000-query continual stream with 30 recurring tasks and 100 transient one-off tasks, zero offline sleep pauses allowed. Compare fast-weights-only, periodic offline consolidation (costed against serving throughput), and read-triggered trickle consolidation at matched total FLOPs and state bytes. Kill if periodic offline consolidation at equal amortized FLOPs ties on both steady-state accuracy and old-task retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure accuracy on early recurring tasks $A/B$ (introduced at queries 1–100) at query 1,000, verifying that read-triggered consolidation transfers $A/B$ safely into slow weights before fast weights decay.

### TT30 · Batched multi-tenant continual fast-weight isolation with shared-skill extraction — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Production models serve $M$ concurrent streams in one batch ([2605.28053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28053)). Factor each stream's inference-time weight state as $\Delta W_{\text{shared}} + \Delta W_{u_i}$ and run an inline *in-batch low-rank common-component extractor* across active streams: update directions that appear consistently across $\ge k$ distinct streams in the batch graduate into $\Delta W_{\text{shared}}$ (gated by an old-task probe), while stream-specific idiosyncrasies remain isolated in $\Delta W_{u_i}$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [RW-TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28053) isolates per-request TTT states with zero sharing; PR #311 card 04 checks cross-session sign replication asynchronously. *In-batch low-rank common-component extraction* unifying batched multi-tenant serving with live continual shared-skill discovery is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 16 concurrent simulated user streams on GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B sharing 4 global domain shifts while each holding unique private preferences (plus 2 noisy/poisoned streams). Compare isolated RW-TTT, naive batch-averaged TTT, and factored shared+private common-component extraction at equal batch FLOPs. Kill if isolated RW-TTT + asynchronous FedAvg ties on time-to-shared-adaptation and private isolation.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero-regression on shared old tasks $A/B$ and zero leakage of user $i$'s private $\Delta W_{u_i}$ into user $j$'s predictions.

### TT31 · Evolution-strategy / zeroth-order inference updates for non-differentiable tool-coupled tasks — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Many live inference signals (compiler exit codes, unit-test pass/fail, discrete API statuses, symbolic verifiers) are non-differentiable, blocking gradient-based TTT. Use a *single-batch antithetic rank-1 ES step* ($\pm \epsilon u v^\top$ evaluated across 2–4 parallel speculative branches in one forward batch) to update fast weights directly from discrete mid-task verification outcomes without backpropagation, including an old-task anchor check inside the same batch.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Continual TTL with Evolution Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24137) runs ES across tasks; forward-only (PR #50) multiplexes perturbations. *Single-batch antithetic rank-1 ES coupled to discrete mid-task tool verification* (with inline retention gating) is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a discrete black-box tool-API stream where the only feedback is binary pass/fail from a scripted executor (no differentiable token target). Compare frozen best-of-4 sampling, REINFORCE/TTRL over 4 samples, and single-batch antithetic rank-1 ES at matched forward passes. Kill if REINFORCE/TTRL over 4 samples beats or ties antithetic ES on adaptation speed and variance.
- **Old-task retention:** Score 50 untouched old tool APIs ($A/B$) after 100 discrete ES updates on drifting APIs.

### TT32 · Anchor-invariant fast-weight parameterization (Cayley / orthogonal-frame updates) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Additive fast-weight updates $W + \Delta W$ inflate activation norms and logit scales over long continual streams, silently breaking downstream thresholds and old-task calibration (cf. E2 logit price index, PR #45). Parameterize inference-time continual updates as *norm-preserving orthogonal rotations* $W' = R(\Delta) W$ via fast rank-2 Cayley transforms restricted to the orthogonal complement of a small old-task anchor basis—guaranteeing exact norm preservation, stable logit temperature, and closed-form invertibility during live inference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Orthogonal Fine-Tuning (OFT) is an offline fine-tuning method; Titans, DeltaNet, and TTT-E2E use unconstrained additive updates. *Rank-2 Cayley norm-preserving fast-weight updates restricted to an old-task-orthogonal subspace* during live inference is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer / GPT-2 small on a 10-task continual inference stream (200 updates without reset), tracking both task accuracy and logit-norm / calibration drift. Compare additive LoRA/TTT, LayerNorm-clamped additive TTT, and rank-2 Cayley orthogonal fast weights at matched parameter count and FLOPs. Kill if LayerNorm-clamped additive TTT matches both calibration stability and old-task accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure untouched old task $A/B$ accuracy and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) after 200 sequential inference updates.

### TT33 · State-checkpointed inference-time continual benchmarks (measuring retention *during* and *across* streams) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Existing TTT evaluations ([2512.23675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675), [2610.36109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36109)) either measure single-sequence perplexity or reset fast weights between episodes, hiding cross-task forgetting. Define a *persistent-state evaluation primitive* (`ControlMap-CL`) that logs a 4-tuple at every inference step $t$: (1) live adaptation gain on current task $T_t$, (2) zero-reset retention on untouched old tasks $A/B$ *with live fast weights still loaded*, (3) wall-clock FLOPs per solved query (unifying thinking and updating costs), and (4) post-reset residual drift.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Beyond Update Counts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36109) builds a control map for single-regime test-time updates; standard CL benchmarks ignore inference-time FLOP/latency exchange. A *persistent-fast-weight 4-tuple continual evaluation protocol* is the missing measurement primitive for this entire root.
- **Cheapest first test:** Run 4 existing baselines (frozen ICL, episode-reset TTT, persistent DeltaNet, LoRA-TTT) through a 10-task CPU continual stream and verify that `ControlMap-CL` separates methods that tie under standard episode-reset evaluation (showing >15 pp hidden forgetting gap when resets are removed). Kill if episode-reset evaluation preserves the exact same method ranking as persistent-state evaluation.
- **Old-task retention:** Directly defines the zero-reset old-task retention metric for all other cards in this root.

### TT34 · Compositional fast-weight sandboxing (evaluating new skill compatibility with old fast weights before merging) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Even when two inference-time fast-weight deltas $\Delta W_1$ and $\Delta W_2$ each preserve untouched old tasks $A/B$ individually, activating them together can cause higher-order destructive interference (*write-skew*, cf. SD3 in PR #58). Before committing a new fast-weight delta $\Delta W_{\text{new}}$ alongside active persistent deltas $\{\Delta W_j\}$, run a *2-pass compositional commutator check* measuring $\|f(x; W + \Delta W_j + \Delta W_{\text{new}}) - (f(x; W + \Delta W_j) + f(x; W + \Delta W_{\text{new}}) - f(x; W))\|$ on cached anchor probes, triggering orthogonal re-projection only when non-commutation exceeds a threshold.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** SD3 (PR #58) identified write-skew between text lessons; [Modular TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07110) composes TTT modules without runtime non-commutation checks. A *fast-weight commutator sandbox* that catches and resolves multi-delta interference before inference-time commit is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** GPT-2 small / small MLP with 10 sequential inference-time fast-weight skills, including 3 planted pairs that individually pass $A/B$ retention checks but interact destructively when combined. Compare pairwise cosine-conflict checking, unconditional merge, and 2-pass commutator sandboxing at equal probe FLOPs. Kill if pairwise parameter cosine similarity detects the planted write-skew pairs with equal AUC.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy and joint skill-$(1+2)$ accuracy after all 10 sequential fast-weight commits.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can inference-time fast weights persist across a multi-task stream without resetting—and strictly beat episode-reset TTT and frozen ICL on both adaptation speed and untouched old-task retention? (`TT01`, `TT03`, `TT06`, `TT32`, `TT33`)**
   Almost all current TTT papers reset fast state between tasks or measure single-document perplexity, masking continual forgetting. Build the minimal persistent-state benchmark (`TT33`) on a 10-task synthetic stream with controlled key overlap and mid-stream rule revisions, and test whether null-space-projected delta rules (`TT03`), spectral-band promotion (`TT01`), erase-then-write impulses (`TT06`), and Cayley orthogonal updates (`TT32`) beat standard persistent DeltaNet, episode-reset TTT, and frozen ICL at matched FLOPs and state bytes. Drop the line if tuned uniform decay in standard DeltaNet matches both zero-reset $A/B$ retention and new-task revision speed.

2. **Can in-context learning be crystallized directly into persistent weights *during* inference—with zero offline distillation and zero double-dosing calibration loss? (`TT08`, `TT09`, `TT10`, `TT11`, `TT14`)**
   In-context learning is fast and inspectable but hits quadratic context limits; offline distillation is slow and causes calibration jumps when context is kept or dropped. Test on GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B whether closed-form ICL-to-MLP rank-1 crystallization (`TT08`), eviction-triggered KV compilation (`TT09`), continuous $(1-\alpha)$ KV-attenuation handoff (`TT10`), and query-invariance filtering (`TT11`) beat 1–5 step LoRA SGD, No-Context Anchoring, and attention-sink + text summarization at matched FLOPs and KV bytes—evaluated on both prefix-removed accuracy and untouched old-task retention.

3. **What is the true FLOP-matched exchange rate between *thinking* (CoT / search / latent looping) and *updating* (TTT / fast-weight writes) over a continual task stream? (`TT15`, `TT16`, `TT17`, `TT19`, `TT21`)**
   Inference-time scaling and test-time training compete for the exact same latency and FLOP budget, yet are almost always studied separately. On a Zipfian continual stream mixing one-off reasoning puzzles with recurring domain shifts under a strict total FLOP cap, test whether marginal-value-of-compute routing (`TT15`), recurrence-hazard plasticity pricing (`TT16`), asymmetric think-to-verify-then-update (`TT17`), and circularity-triggered deliberation breaks (`TT21`) strictly dominate thinking-only scaling, updating-only TTT, and fixed-split heuristics while preserving 1×-pass accuracy on old tasks $A/B$.

4. **Can a model reliably decide *mid-task* whether, where, how reversibly, and how many steps to update its own weights—beating fixed-schedule TTT and frozen ICL at matched compute? (`TT22`, `TT23`, `TT24`, `TT26`, `TT27`, `TT28`)**
   Fixed-schedule TTT wastes capacity on aleatoric noise, overfits easy chunks, and damages high-conflict old-task directions. On a noisy heterogeneous stream mixing learnable rule shifts, aleatoric noise blocks, high-Fisher-conflict updates, and corrupted hints, test whether epistemic-vs-aleatoric gating (`TT24`), Fisher-conflict pre-flight abortion with ICL fallback (`TT23`), dual-boundary gain/KL stopping (`TT27`), and same-pass frozen-shadow vetoes (`TT28`) beat fixed-$K$ TTT, Titans raw-surprise gating, and online EWC on the adaptation–retention Pareto front.

5. **How should inference-born fast weights graduate into slow weights and compose across concurrent streams without offline sleep pauses or write-skew collapse? (`TT02`, `TT07`, `TT29`, `TT30`, `TT34`)**
   Long-horizon inference learners eventually saturate fast-weight capacity and accumulate higher-order cross-skill collisions. Test on a 1,000-query multi-tenant stream (with zero offline sleep downtime and planted write-skew skill pairs) whether read-triggered trickle consolidation (`TT29`), in-batch common-component extraction (`TT30`), stack-allocated frames (`TT02`), and 2-pass commutator sandboxing (`TT34`) strictly beat periodic offline consolidation, isolated RW-TTT, and pairwise cosine conflict checks on steady-state throughput, shared-skill transfer, and untouched old-task retention.
