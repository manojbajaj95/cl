# Gap handoffs: move the surviving direction, then fix what scales it

Child of [Complementary batches](complementary-batches.md) ([PR #248][parent]). The [score-3 review][triage] points to #02, a required C batch that moves a persistent slot gap into a direction another task's batches already damp, and #06, a P batch that repairs Adam's first moment followed by a Q batch that repairs the second moment without undoing P. Ideas and exact arithmetic only; no learner run or GPU work.

**Bet.** A frozen, useful successor can accept a gap it could not erase on entry; an adaptive successor can also accept a nonzero first-moment residue if it can remove the gain difference that would amplify it. **Verdict.** Untested. The first claim must beat ordinary batch ordering or independently trained contraction, not just the wrong axis. The second must beat a joint objective on the executed adaptive update, not just a first-moment baseline or a reset.

## What would count

- **Freeze the service first.** A and B are established skills; two legal writeback orders produce the paired incoming states. P and Q are ordinary, required routes for incoming C work. Freeze truthful records, delivery times, permitted orders, progress floors for both C subskills, prefix A/B readers and the first sensitive adaptive update plus declared tail. Hold out paired histories and C data. Charge training, stored state, extra gradients, routing, speculative checks, and fallback; match examples, clocks, optimizer settings, capacity and useful progress. No idle waiting, synthetic targets, or slot edits in the candidate. Resets, direct slot rotation and crossed slots are diagnostic arms, not lawful handoffs.
- **Show that P changes what Q can damp.** Use paired states `(θ,m,v,t)` that came from actual writeback orders, not a free slot injection. Measure Q on the states P actually reaches, and compare with a matched P that leaves a similar raw gap but lands it in the wrong directions. Include weight drift and history-dependent forcing: Q's pressure on a slot difference need not be a fixed linear map. Freeze Q before learning P for the core claim. If P only shrinks the gap, or a matched generic reshuffle ties, there is no directional handoff result.
- **Follow the same gradient through both moments.** At a shared clock, `Δm' = β₁Δm + (1−β₁)Δg` and `Δv' = β₂Δv + (1−β₂)(g₊+g₋)⊙Δg`. Recompute Q at each post-P weight. Measure both raw gaps and the native adaptive update (with bias correction, ε, and clipping or other live branches where applicable). For diagnosis, writing the step before its learning-rate factor as `u = Dm̂` gives the symmetric identity `Δu = mean(D)Δm̂ + ΔD mean(m̂)` for the diagonal inverse denominator D. Log both terms: a tiny update gap can hide two large terms that cancel. A large shared gain can also amplify a residual even at equal v. Test slot-matched counterfactuals at the same clock, then score the real states and the next live gradient separately.
- **Retention floor in every test.** In addition to the card-specific check, score held-out A/B and rare old inputs on both trajectories at scheduled reads, both C subskills, the first sensitive adaptive step, and the declared tail. Count refusals and fallback cost on the full workload. As in the [ledger][ledger], a same-budget tie is a negative result; primitive candidates name reusable operations or objects, and improvements below are audits. Prior-art comparisons are local, not clearance.

## Two exact fixtures (not evidence of learning)

**H1 · Transfer without immediate contraction.** On a two-coordinate residual, let P send `(x,y)` to `(−y,x)` and Q send it to `(x,y/4)`. Starting at `(1,0)`, neither P nor Q alone decreases the squared gap of 1. P then Q gives `(0,1/4)` (squared gap `1/16`); Q then P still gives squared gap 1, as does replacing P by the identity before Q. For `(0,1)`, P then Q also gives squared gap 1. So the handoff depends on order and the incoming residual family. These are ideal gap maps, not gradients from a truthful C workload.

**H2 · Equal numerator is not equal update.** In a scalar [Adam][adam] moment recurrence with both betas `1/2`, take incoming pairs `m=(1,−1)`, `v=(7/4,15/4)` at the same clock. Prescribe P gradients `(−3/2,3/2)`: the result is `m=(−1/4,1/4)`, `v=(2,3)`. Prescribe Q gradients `(5/4,3/4)`: the result is `m=(1/2,1/2)`, `v=(57/32,57/32)`, so the adaptive increments agree at a shared clock. A Q alternative with the same gradient difference but gradients `(3/4,1/4)` also eliminates the numerator gap, yet leaves `m=(1/4,1/4)`, `v=(41/32,49/32)`; the positive equal numerators now yield unequal increments. Negating just the first history's Q gradient preserves the v repair but widens the m gap from magnitude `1/2` to `5/4`. These are prescribed gradient traces, not evidence the batches are reachable or productive; earlier weight drift is not erased by equal increments. Both fixtures use exact rational arithmetic.

## Idea cards

### 01. Pass the gap into a frozen successor's damping direction — primitive candidate (#248 #02)
**Mechanism.** Train P against where the live Q map contracts; expose a handoff that can leave the immediate slot norm unchanged. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Kaczmarz][kaczmarz] cycles row projections; this restates #248 #02's learned transfer into an already-useful Q. **Cheapest first test.** Use two-axis regression with a frozen, anisotropic Q; hold out histories near H1's input axis and swap in a same-norm wrong landing. **Same-budget control.** Ordinary P/Q with tuned decay and best lawful orientation from fixed preconditioning. **Old-task retention.** Check an A reader aligned with the landing axis before Q arrives.

### 02. Learn a safe preimage, not a preferred axis — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Map paired states into one of Q's disjoint damping regions while keeping both trajectories in the *same* region, without a per-history dispatch choice. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Hybrid control][hybrid] plans to reachable sets; #248 #05 allows switching C features, whereas this hands off a bounded pair into a fixed successor region with a shared gradient law. **Cheapest first test.** Use a two-kink ReLU Q with two damping cells and a mixed-cell pair whose midpoint would pass a point check. **Same-budget control.** One linear directional handoff plus exact paired checks. **Old-task retention.** Test old reads straddling each cell boundary.

### 03. Steer with curvature when entry gradients tie — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Shape P's cross-coordinate curvature so two histories develop the gap Q damps without changing P's common-start gradient; the difference appears as the reachable weights separate. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GraB][grab] balances gradient order and [trajectory matching][mtt] fits whole traces; the proposed reusable knob is a within-batch curvature change with an entry-gradient match and Q frozen. **Cheapest first test.** Compare two positive-semidefinite regression Hessians at matched entry gradient, one diagonal and one coupled; test reversed incoming weight displacement too. **Same-budget control.** Greedy gradient balancing plus terminal paired check on the same legal records. **Old-task retention.** Read B's coupled coordinate before Q can hide the effect.

### 04. Hand off a weight gap that Q converts into slot damping — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let P place a bounded discrepancy in weights, within the existing prefix contract, so Q's ordinary curvature generates forcing opposite to the residual momentum. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Echo-absorbing work][echo] #05 bounds weight drift; here drift is the carrier and must reverse Q's differential forcing when its sign is flipped. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate reachable scalar quadratic pairs; compare equal-slot-gap starts with opposite weight offsets and recompute Q in both. **Same-budget control.** Direct terminal state fitting with no carrier constraint, plus a local curvature-aware schedule. **Old-task retention.** Keep an immediate weight-sensitive A probe live; reject offsets it hears.

### 05. Land in a direction that stays damped across Q's required batches — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Teach P to land the surviving gap in a corridor followed by the full ordinary Q sequence, even when its instantaneous damping axis turns; do not refit Q. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Hybrid control][hybrid] supplies region-wise reachability; #248 #02 lands for one successor, not a moving sequence of useful gradient maps. **Cheapest first test.** Hold out a lawful two-batch Q sequence with crossed Hessian axes; include a landing that contracts on the first batch and rebounds on the second. **Same-budget control.** One-step landing plus exact two-step verification, and a single P trained on total gap alone. **Old-task retention.** Measure B after each Q batch, not just the terminal return.

### 06. Let several fixed successors accept one landing, without a route tag — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Fit one P whose reachable image is in the common damping preimage of the possible already-scheduled Q continuations, so P need not know which one arrives. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 #08 jointly trains interchangeable partner families; this freezes heterogeneous Q routes and asks whether their preimages admit one shared upstream transition. **Cheapest first test.** Try three anisotropic Q routes with a shared damped direction, then replace one to make the intersection empty under the service tolerance. **Same-budget control.** Per-successor P with charged dispatch/state and a universal P trained on terminal residual alone. **Old-task retention.** Run the old probe most sensitive to the withheld Q route.

### 07. Use mandatory P examples to make reordering unnecessary — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Repartition only the examples already owed in P so its live transition lands safely for a fixed Q arrival, with no data omitted or moved across the public boundary. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GraB][grab] balances gradient discrepancy and [OptiSelect][optiselect] scores optimizer-shaped single-step utility; this uses the downstream two-history damping preimage as the batch partition criterion. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust equal-size partitions of a tiny truthful regression set; include equal entry-gradient partitions with different post-P Q behavior. **Same-budget control.** Best of GraB and adaptive one-step ranking, with the same number of partition trials and identical Q. **Old-task retention.** Use A probes on a feature represented by just one rare P example.

### 08. Choose the landing by how far it is from failing, not how much it shrinks — improvement
**Mechanism.** Rank feasible P landings by distance to leaving Q's tested damping region under bounded input drift, not by best central contraction. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Hybrid control][hybrid] already treats margin against region boundaries; this is a robustness check on #248 #02 rather than a new optimizer. **Cheapest first test.** Fit two successful P routes, one near a ReLU cell edge; perturb using held-out legal histories at matched gap norm. **Same-budget control.** Worst-case terminal gap training with the same perturbations. **Old-task retention.** Report the first B miss under boundary perturbation as well as the nominal tie.

### 09. Make a useful branch change close a handoff rather than reopen it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Arrange for Q's actual clipping branch to be active in both histories and to cap their redirected effects so they close the gap within tolerance, without forcing identical pre-clip norms or deleting norm reads. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Echo-absorbing work][echo] #10 aims for a common live norm; here the candidate only claims closure under a declared native consumer that need not see the norm. **Cheapest first test.** Use a clipped two-output Q where equal branches can still emit different directions; include wrong-direction and independent norm-reader negatives. **Same-budget control.** Norm-matched ordinary Q plus a standalone P handoff and exact native checks. **Old-task retention.** Keep the old norm reader in the negative case and require refusal.

### 10. Ask whether a truthful first route can land at all — improvement
**Mechanism.** Separate failure to learn a landing from absence of an admissible landing: enumerate P's legal image and intersect it with Q's actually safe preimage. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Hybrid control][hybrid] uses backward reachability, and #248 #18 audits joint feasibility; this is the directional counterpart, not a new primitive. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust a finite batch menu with one unique landing and a second menu with all legal images outside Q's safe preimage. **Same-budget control.** Exact brute-force two-route replay over the same menu. **Old-task retention.** Report when a landing exists only by violating the original A/B prefix floor.

### 11. Reduce the first moment, then remove denominator amplification — primitive candidate (#248 #06)
**Mechanism.** Train P to reduce Δm; train Q on P's actual output to reduce the gain discrepancy from Δv without letting its gradient undo P. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Continual Adam][cadam] carries task-weighted second moments; #248 #06 assigns complementary effects to two mandatory batches while keeping native Adam. **Cheapest first test.** Search a tiny Adam regression for H2-like traces; measure Q's normalized update after a real P and plant a Q that fixes v but reopens m. **Same-budget control.** Joint normalized-update objective on both batches, first-moment-only shaping, and ordinary P/Q plus tuned lawful decay; v-reset and plain momentum as diagnostics. **Old-task retention.** Probe A/B coordinates with small denominators immediately after Q and at the next ordinary update.

### 12. Give Q a signed-gradient sum knob at fixed difference — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Repartition truthful Q records to change the paired gradient sum while preserving the difference needed for m; change second-moment forcing without editing either slot. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [OptiSelect][optiselect] ranks optimizer-shaped data utility; H2 suggests a more specific batch-design operation, a change in `g₊+g₋` under a bound on `Δg`. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate equal-size Q partitions after frozen P; look for a difference-matched pair where only one fixes v, including a case with no independent sum knob. **Same-budget control.** Joint adaptive-update scoring over the same partitions and number of trials. **Old-task retention.** Check A examples that share the coordinate whose common update changes.

### 13. Reserve the first-moment room Q must spend — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train P to leave a signed m residue that offsets the forcing from the only lawful v-correcting Q, instead of minimizing the magnitude of Δm as far as possible at P. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 #06 asks Q not to reopen the gap; [trajectory matching][mtt] can learn endpoint cancellation; this exposes a reusable signed precondition for a fixed Q, which must transfer across histories. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze a Q with differential forcing bounded away from zero; compare P targeting zero against P targeting its estimated compensating interval on unseen histories. **Same-budget control.** Direct end-to-end matching of native updates without an explicit reserve. **Old-task retention.** Keep an old read between P and Q able to veto too large a reserve.

### 14. Hand only the dangerous denominator coordinates to Q — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After P, identify coordinates where matched-clock, crossed-v diagnostics show that gain mismatch is materially contributing to a real reader error. Fit Q to remove those discrepancies while preserving useful adaptation elsewhere. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Recovery basins][basins] #07 admits v effects that are reader-null; this tests a division of labor in which P makes the remaining *non-null* denominator obligations sparse enough for a narrow Q. **Cheapest first test.** Use an anisotropic regression with a small-v coordinate and a high-variance distractor; include diffuse reader damage so a sparse handoff should refuse. **Same-budget control.** Full two-route adaptive-step loss and top-k raw Δv shaping at matched capacity. **Old-task retention.** Include a rare old query using the low-variance coordinate and one using the omitted distractor.

### 15. Repair the gain for the next real numerator, not today's zero — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Keep Q responsible for denominator mismatch even when P temporarily zeros the visible update; bind that obligation to the declared next useful gradient family. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 #07 holds out a forcing suffix, and [Slot echo][slot] #03 follows the evolving preconditioner; this trains Q to close a specifically latent denominator leak across successor-gradient signs. **Cheapest first test.** Start Q from a masked equal-update case; try two legal continuations with opposite nonzero common forcing and one unannounced gradient as a scope negative. **Same-budget control.** Direct native two-step trajectory loss at the same set of continuations. **Old-task retention.** Verify the old read due immediately after the latent mismatch first becomes visible.

### 16. Renew a repair after a real per-group clock split — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** If task routing legitimately skips inactive groups, make Q's required work close the *bias-corrected* gain discrepancy under the actual per-group ages; do not synchronize or rewrite clocks. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Slot echo][slot] #04 includes clocks in the statistic and [Echo-absorbing work][echo] #08 certifies skipped steps; here the recovery is carried by Q and must work over a held-out family of legal skip patterns. **Cheapest first test.** Simulate two groups with one legitimate skip; compare raw-slot-matched starts that diverge after bias correction. **Same-budget control.** Clock-aware joint shaping and exact replay across the same skip patterns. **Old-task retention.** Read the older inactive feature after its first actual reactivation.

### 17. Separate repair from a lucky cancellation of update terms — improvement
**Mechanism.** Use crossed-v and crossed-m diagnostic replays at a common clock, then run the real tail without interventions; distinguish Q repairing the gain from gain error merely canceling numerator error. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [A Step Towards Forgetting][forget] compares optimizer-history interventions while matching displacement; this adapts the diagnostic to attribute a proposed mandatory two-step repair. **Cheapest first test.** Construct two pairs with the same immediate update discrepancy, only one with both decomposition terms small, then apply a shared nonzero-gradient suffix. **Same-budget control.** Exact two-order replay with matched displacement and the same number of suffixes, without attribution. **Old-task retention.** Check whether the real suffix restores the expected A/B read, not the artificial crossed-slot run.

### 18. Ask for the Q forcing that Adam cannot realize — improvement
**Mechanism.** At fixed post-P states, solve the m and v constraints together because the same Δg both moves m and multiplies the gradient sum in v. Refuse an incompatible target rather than treating the slots as independently writable. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** The identity is [Adam][adam]; #248 #18 uses attainable-transition feasibility for slot and clip norms. This audit isolates incompatibility of two Adam moment targets even before old-task constraints are imposed. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust truthful scalar Q choices, including a state requiring Δg = 0 to retain equal m but nonzero Δg to repair unequal v in one step. **Same-budget control.** Exact search minimizing native update discrepancy over the same batches and deadlines. **Old-task retention.** Exercise the old read that triggers lawful fallback on the infeasible pair.

### 19. Redirect toward a Q coordinate with spare denominator authority — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** Choose P's landing where Q both damps the residual and has enough legal signed-and-squared gradient forcing to repair gain, even if another axis contracts faster in an m-only model. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 #19 prepares a slot-and-clip intersection; #248 #02 plus #06 separate direction and moment repair. The joint claim here requires the landing itself to change Q's attainable gain repair. **Cheapest first test.** Cross directional shaping and Q moment shaping on/off with two Q axes of equal m damping but different feasible gradient sums. **Same-budget control.** Best stack of independently fitted #248 #02 and #06, plus direct adaptive-update shaping at equal cost. **Old-task retention.** Challenge the prefix on the chosen axis and test both old readouts after Q.

### 20. Pass a repair to another required batch without forgiving its residue — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** If a lawful Q arrival is partial, compose the actual residual and remaining m/v obligations into a later already-required Q batch's admissible input set; keep the original deadline. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Redundant finishes][finishes] #14 changes fade partners while carrying tail discrepancy; this candidate needs transferable *coupled forcing* under the same optimizer, not just a matching tail score. **Cheapest first test.** Interrupt a three-batch truthful Q sequence after one batch and compare an eligible replacement against a same-utility replacement that reopens the numerator. **Same-budget control.** Independently trained continuation policies plus exact lookahead, with no extra recovery step. **Old-task retention.** Read A/B during interruption and at the unchanged deadline in both cases.

### 21. Preserve useful common gain while erasing differential gain — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Teach Q to shed the v difference but still track shared changes in gradient scale that C genuinely needs; evaluate the gain on both trajectories rather than pinning it to a common constant. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 #16 separates common and differential signals for a live norm; [Continual Adam][cadam] carries pooled second moments across tasks. Here a required Q step must retain common-mode adaptation without pooled or overwritten slots. **Cheapest first test.** Perturb common gradient scale across held-out Adam regressions while matching incoming Δv; compare response curves with an equal-gain-but-flat solution. **Same-budget control.** Fixed or pooled gain as a diagnostic; best ordinary Adam and joint update loss within service. **Old-task retention.** Include an old rare input where changing the shared step scale, even identically, can violate its floor.

### 22. Measure the order in which gain repair destroys the directional handoff — improvement (joint audit)
**Mechanism.** On an allowed Q-before-P path, test whether Q erases the anisotropy P was relying on, even if final raw m and v distances look favorable. Do not add the permutation if dispatch forbids it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #248 #03 already checks lawful orders; this is a targeted falsifier for recombining the two score-3 mechanisms, not a new scheduler. **Cheapest first test.** Use a toy where post-Q equalization changes P's effective motion and compare to plain momentum, keeping the required prefix reads live. **Same-budget control.** Exact paired replay of every lawful order and the best independently trained stack. **Old-task retention.** Keep the earliest A/B read in each legal order and score any fallback as part of the result.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can truthful P work steer a reachable gap into a frozen Q's damping region at equal P gap size?** Start with 01, 03 and 07. Compare wrong-axis and generic ordering controls at matched C progress; inspect actual post-P weights and Q forcing. H1 only demonstrates the distinction. If gain vanishes under norm matching or live Q recomputation, stop calling it a handoff.
2. **Does dividing Adam repair across P and Q beat a joint native-update objective?** Start with 11–13 and 17. Cross P and Q shaping on/off and compare their best stack to direct trajectory shaping. Test the sign-flipped Q and a numerator-equal but denominator-unequal Q from H2; report actual post-Q and next-step reader errors. Stop if the effect is just cancellation on one trace, or disappears against the joint control.
3. **Can a directional landing make an otherwise impossible denominator repair feasible?** Use 18 and 19 to construct matched lawful P landings with different feasible Q forcing. Check the best independent stack and the reversed order wherever legal. Both smaller raw gaps and safe readers are useful observations, but neither alone establishes this interaction.
4. **What survives withheld histories, successor batches and real deadline reads?** Try 05, 06, 15 and 20 without changing the service. Include missing successor overlap, a latent leak and an interrupted handoff. Compare accepted useful pairs plus fallback cost and old retention against direct paired replay and serialization; if the tail or prefix kills the effect, report the narrower scope.
