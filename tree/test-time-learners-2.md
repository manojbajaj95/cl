# Test-time continual learners (depth 1): Conservation-of-effect handoff & conflict-triggered decompile–edit–reabsorb

Depth 1 child of [PR #373](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/373) (`tree/root-test-time-learners.md`, deepening `TT10` and `TT14`, and folding in cross-pool round 106 `TFX3` and `TFX5`) · 30 candidate primitives (`T2-01`–`T2-30`) · All tagged **Ambitious but doable (P)** · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** PR #373 proposed two complementary inference-time primitives that survived review at tier 3: **(a) `TT10`**, handing a live rule from context to weights without double-firing by scaling in-context influence down by the exact effect already absorbed; and **(b) `TT14`**, decompiling a conflicting weight change back into editable text when a rule receives an exception, editing it in context, and re-absorbing it. Both fail in their naive forms for deep mechanistic reasons:
1. **Why naive scalar $(1-\alpha)$ KV attenuation (`TT10`) fails:** Softmax renormalization leaks vacated attention mass onto *other* context tokens (shifting entropy and mimicking PR #29/#31's filler/temperature confound); weight absorption is *non-uniform* across inputs and layers (95% absorbed on canonical prompts, 15% on edge cases, so a single scalar $\alpha$ simultaneously double-fires canonical cases and under-fires edge cases); concurrent multi-rule handoffs couple through shared softmax denominators; and intermediate $\alpha = 0.5$ trajectories exhibit nonlinear calibration humps even when endpoints match.
2. **Why naive weight-to-context decompilation (`TT14`) fails:** Natural-language decompilation is lossy—wiping and re-absorbing an edited summary silently destroys unverbalized sub-behaviors; shared adapters superpose multiple rules, so a conflict fires on an entangled activation; verbalizers can hallucinate unfaithful explanations; and placing decompiled text back into context while its weight delta remains active causes *reverse double-firing* that biases the edit itself.

The unifying bet of this node is that **(a) and (b) are dual halves of one reversible, conservation-of-effect transport channel between KV memory and weight memory**, extended with round-106 cross-pool findings: **selective rule-only handoff (`TFX3`)** keeping one-off facts in context across context-only/handoff/weights-only states, and a **pairwise fast-weight conflict record (`TFX5`)** catching cached delta pairs that fail together though each passes alone. The 30 primitives below dissect exact conservation-of-effect handoff (Section A), surgical residual-preserving decompilation (Section B), guarded exception editing and round-trip stability (Section C), and unified bidirectional KV↔weight transport including `TFX3`/`TFX5` (Section D).

## A · Exact conservation-of-effect handoff: beyond scalar $(1-\alpha)$ KV attenuation

### T2-01 · Value-vector residual subtraction with softmax-invariant dummy sink — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Scaling down attention *logits* of rule tokens inside softmax leaks the vacated probability mass onto all other tokens in the prompt, distorting unrelated context and shifting output entropy. Keep attention weights strictly frozen and instead scale only the *value vectors* $v_i \mapsto (1-\alpha) v_i$ of the absorbed rule span (equivalently routing $\alpha$ of its attention mass into a zero-value sink token), guaranteeing exact invariance of every other token's attention weight throughout the handoff.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Attention-sink mechanisms preserve long-context stability; [On-Policy Attention Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33200) distills attention maps offline. Using *softmax-invariant value scaling / zero-value sink routing* to decouple rule handoff from softmax mass redistribution is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a 10-rule multi-lesson prompt (each prompt containing 1 target rule + 3 active bystander rules + filler), sweeping $\alpha \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$. Compare logit-bias attenuation, value-vector scaling, abrupt deletion, keep-in-context, NCA, logit interpolation, filler, and entropy-matched controls on peak calibration error and bystander-rule distortion. Kill if logit-bias attenuation or NCA ties on peak calibration error and bystander stability.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched bystander rules in context and untouched already-weighted old rules $A/B$ at every $\alpha$ checkpoint.

### T2-02 · Query-conditioned absorption meter $\alpha(x)$ (per-input conservation instead of global scalar) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** A partial weight update $\Delta W$ rarely absorbs a rule uniformly: at step $t$ it may already produce 90% of the rule's shift on canonical inputs $x_{\text{canon}}$ but only 15% on paraphrased or compositional inputs $x_{\text{edge}}$. Compute a lightweight per-query *absorption projection* $\alpha(x) = \text{clip}(\langle \Delta h_{\text{wt}}(x), \Delta h_{\text{KV}}(x) \rangle / \|\Delta h_{\text{KV}}(x)\|^2, 0, 1)$ online and attenuate the rule's KV value contribution by $1 - \alpha(x)$ *per query*—fully suppressing KV where weights already fire while letting edge cases fall back seamlessly to KV.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Localized Context Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21427) and [NCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627) assume uniform internalization across queries; [Parametric Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38356) uses a global schedule. *Per-query functional projection $\alpha(x)$* governing live KV attenuation is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B trained 2–5 TTT steps on canonical examples of 10 rules, then evaluated on both canonical and paraphrased/compositional queries while the rule remains in context. Compare global scalar $\alpha$, per-query $\alpha(x)$, keep-in-context, abrupt deletion, NCA, logit interpolation, and entropy-matched controls. Kill if the best global scalar $\alpha^*$ matches per-query $\alpha(x)$ on worst-slice (canonical vs. paraphrase) peak calibration error.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that per-query projection stays near 0 on untouched old rules $A/B$ (no spurious suppression or double-firing).

### T2-03 · Layer-matched conservation (canceling attention writes at the exact layer the weight delta lands) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Because residual streams pass through nonlinear LayerNorms and MLPs, canceling a Layer-4 KV write with a Layer-18 LoRA write at the output logits alone still leaves intermediate layers double-fired or miscalibrated. Enforce *layer-wise conservation*: at each transformer block $\ell$, match that block's KV value attenuation $1 - \alpha_\ell$ to the cumulative functional effect already supplied by weight deltas in layers $\le \ell$, keeping the intermediate residual stream distribution constant across the handoff.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [On-Policy Attention Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33200) aligns attention offline; PR #36 MX1 studied offline layer placement for overridability. *Layer-aligned residual-stream conservation* during live context-to-weight transfer is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 12-layer / 24-layer small LLM (GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B) where LoRA consolidation is placed in layers 8–16 vs. 18–24. Compare uniform all-layer $\alpha$, output-logit interpolation, NCA, and layer-matched $\alpha_\ell$ on peak calibration error and internal residual-norm drift. Kill if output-logit interpolation or uniform all-layer $\alpha$ matches peak calibration error across both layer placements.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ consolidated in different layer bands to verify zero cross-layer interference during handoff.

### T2-04 · Semantic-vs-filler decomposition before handoff (defeating the A3 temperature/filler confound) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** PR #29 and PR #31 demonstrated that merely having text in context alters output entropy and margin even when the text is irrelevant filler. Before attenuating a rule's KV values during handoff, project each rule token's value vector into a *rule-semantic subspace* (estimated from action-swap or paired-contrast probes) and a *generic length/entropy component*; scale down *only* the semantic component by $(1-\alpha)$ while leaving the neutral entropy baseline intact until final eviction.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** PR #29/#31 (ledger A3) and [2610.27714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27714) identified filler/entropy confounds in in-context lessons; no existing context-distillation method ([NCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627), [Cortex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20399)) separates semantic rule attenuation from context-length entropy shifts.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-1.5B on the PR #29/#31 rotated-rule benchmark (passing the mode-collapse and $\ge 5$ pp lesson-lift prechecks), comparing raw KV attenuation, semantic-subspace-only attenuation, length-matched filler, entropy-matched temperature, NCA, and abrupt deletion. Kill if raw KV attenuation + temperature rescaling matches semantic-subspace attenuation on calibration and correct-flip rate.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that old rules $A/B$ keep identical margin and calibration distributions when the target rule's semantic component is handed off.

### T2-05 · Jacobian-matched handoff invariant (zero-derivative calibration stability across $\alpha \in [0, 1]$) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Even when a weight delta matches the KV rule at $\alpha = 1$ and the KV rule is calibrated at $\alpha = 0$, intermediate states $\theta(\alpha) = (W + \alpha \Delta W, (1-\alpha)\text{KV})$ can spike in calibration error due to cross-terms between attention and MLP nonlinearities. Add a cheap *midpoint-flatness regularizer* $\mathcal{L}_{\text{flat}} = \text{KL}(p(\cdot; \theta(0.5)) \,\|\, p(\cdot; \theta(0)))$ during the fast-weight update so the handoff path is explicitly trained to have flat logits and zero calibration hump for all $\alpha \in [0, 1]$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [When Context Returns (NCA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627) trains endpoints ($\alpha = 0$ and $\alpha = 1$ with/without context); enforcing *interior-path flatness* ($\alpha = 0.5$) as a first-class handoff invariant is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 10 rules, evaluating peak calibration error across a fine grid $\alpha \in \{0, 0.1, \dots, 1.0\}$. Compare endpoint-only NCA, localized context distillation, logit interpolation, keep-in-context, abrupt deletion, and midpoint-flatness-regularized handoff at equal gradient steps. Kill if NCA already achieves flat calibration across all interior $\alpha$ values within seed noise.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ at $\alpha = 0.5$ and $\alpha = 1.0$, confirming the flatness regularizer does not over-constrain or degrade old rules.

### T2-06 · Multi-rule decoupled handoff matrix (preventing cross-rule interference during concurrent absorption) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** In real continual streams, 3–10 rules sit in context simultaneously at different stages of absorption $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m)$. When their weight updates overlap in representation space, independently scaling rule $i$'s KV by $(1-\alpha_i)$ miscalibrates rule $j$. Maintain an online $m \times m$ *cross-effect Gram matrix* $G_{ij} = \langle \Delta h_{\text{wt}, i}, \Delta h_{\text{KV}, j} \rangle / \|\Delta h_{\text{KV}, j}\|^2$ and set the KV value scales to $\mathbf{c} = \text{clip}(\mathbf{1} - G\boldsymbol{\alpha}, 0, 1)$ so cross-rule spillover from $\Delta W_i$ is cancelled in rule $j$'s KV too.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Cortex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20399) and [NCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627) consolidate single or batch contexts without tracking pairwise cross-rule handoff coupling. A *coupled $m \times m$ conservation matrix* for concurrent live handoffs is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B with 6 partially overlapping rules in one prompt handed off concurrently at staggered rates. Compare independent $(1-\alpha_i)$ scaling, sequential one-rule-at-a-time handoff, NCA, keep-in-context, and Gram-decoupled scaling $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{1} - G\boldsymbol{\alpha}$. Kill if independent $(1-\alpha_i)$ scaling ties on peak calibration error across all 6 overlapping rules.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure calibration and accuracy on 4 already-consolidated old rules $A/B$ while the 6 concurrent rules hand off.

### T2-07 · Active handoff completion certificate (knowing when a KV span is safe to evict) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Abruptly deleting context before weights fully generalize causes large calibration drops (PR #30: +13.25 pp error), whereas never deleting fills the context window. Pair each handing-off rule with an online *eviction certificate*: track the worst-case output gap $\max_{x \in \mathcal{P}_R} \text{KL}(p(x; W + \Delta W, \text{no KV}) \,\|\, p(x; W, \text{KV}))$ over live queries plus 3 synthetic boundary paraphrases, authorizing KV eviction *only* once the worst-case gap falls below $\epsilon_{\text{evict}}$ (and freezing eviction otherwise).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Anchored Context Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31430) and [Privileged Context as Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07842) measure average distillation loss; a *worst-case boundary-probe eviction certificate* that directly gates live KV deletion is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 15 rules of varying difficulty (easy lexical rules vs. hard compositional rules) under a fixed KV budget. Compare fixed-step eviction, average-loss eviction, keep-in-context, NCA, and boundary-certified eviction on post-eviction calibration error and KV tokens saved. Kill if average-loss eviction matches worst-rule post-eviction accuracy at equal KV savings.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ alongside newly evicted rules to ensure delayed eviction of hard rules does not crowd out or degrade $A/B$.

### T2-08 · Mid-handoff abort and live rule-edit reconciliation — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** If a rule is invalidated or revised by the user *while* mid-handoff ($\alpha = 0.6$), continuing absorption commits a stale rule while merely editing the prompt leaves a 60%-strength weight ghost. Provide an atomic *handoff-abort transaction*: immediately subtract the tagged partial delta $\alpha \Delta W$ from fast weights and restore KV scale $1 - \alpha \to 1$ in a single step, returning the rule cleanly to pure context before applying the revision.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard TTT and [NCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627) assume static rules during distillation; [Contextual Knowledge Conflicts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03148) studies static prompt conflicts. An *atomic mid-handoff abort-and-restore transaction* is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a 12-rule stream where 4 rules are retracted or inverted mid-handoff ($\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.6\}$). Compare finish-then-overwrite TTT, prompt-edit-only without weight rollback, keep-in-context, and atomic mid-handoff abort on post-revision accuracy and ghost-rule firing rate. Kill if 2-step overwrite TTT removes the 60% ghost equally cleanly without hurting other rules.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero residual drift on untouched old rules $A/B$ and on the 8 un-retracted concurrent rules after 4 mid-handoff aborts.

## B · Surgical, residual-preserving weight-to-context decompilation on conflict

### T2-09 · Conflict-scoped activation diffing (decompiling only the conflicting slice, not the whole weight delta) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Reading an entire LoRA adapter into text (`2610.07334`, `2609.35261`) is slow, noisy, and mixes unrelated rules. When a new exception query $x_Y$ conflicts with persistent weights $W + \Delta W$, compute the *query-localized activation diff* $\delta h(x_Y) = h(x_Y; W + \Delta W) - h(x_Y; W_0)$ across implicated layers and pass *only* $\delta h(x_Y)$ to a lightweight verbalizer head—decompiling strictly the specific rule premise firing on $x_Y$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Imprint Reader](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35261), [Weight Oracles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07334), [Interpreting Weight Differences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05092), and [Natural Language Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30934) verbalize global adapter diffs or single activations offline. *Conflict-query-scoped activation-diff decompilation* triggered inline during inference is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B with 15 rules absorbed into one shared rank-8 LoRA; introduce exceptions on 5 rules. Compare global adapter readout, conflict-scoped activation-diff readout, direct TTT overwrite, all-text at equal context, offline round trip, and ROME/MEMIT on rule-identification accuracy and post-edit exception accuracy. Kill if global adapter readout or direct TTT overwrite ties on precision of isolating the conflicting rule.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that editing the conflict-scoped decompilation preserves all 10 untouched old rules ($A/B$) inside the same shared LoRA.

### T2-10 · Residual-preserving decompilation (split-and-keep unverbalized edge cases in weights) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Any short natural-language decompilation $s$ of a weight delta $\Delta W$ is inevitably lossy—dropping implicit calibration, formatting quirks, or sub-cases not stated in $s$. Before muting $\Delta W$ for editing, project $\Delta W = \Delta W_{\text{verb}}(s) + \Delta W_{\text{resid}}$, where $\Delta W_{\text{verb}}(s)$ is the component functionally reproduced by $s$ and $\Delta W_{\text{resid}}$ is the orthogonal behavioral residual. Only $\Delta W_{\text{verb}}(s)$ is replaced by the edited text $s'$; $\Delta W_{\text{resid}}$ stays untouched in weights so unverbalized sub-behaviors survive the edit.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Offline round-trip consolidation (N1, PR #41) and [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09563) reconstruct text from weights/activations without preserving the orthogonal unverbalized weight residual. *Orthogonal verbalized/residual weight splitting* during live decompilation is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B where each absorbed weight delta encodes both a main verbalizable rule AND a subtle unverbalized tie-breaker/style bias; apply an exception to the main rule. Compare full wipe-and-reabsorb, offline round trip, direct TTT overwrite, ROME/MEMIT, and residual-preserving decompilation on both exception accuracy and survival of the unverbalized tie-breaker. Kill if full wipe-and-reabsorb or direct TTT overwrite preserves the unverbalized tie-breaker equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ plus the unverbalized sub-behavior slice of the edited rule after re-absorption.

### T2-11 · Faithfulness-verified decompilation via counterfactual text-swap test — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Introspection adapters ([2604.16812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16812)) and weight readers ([2609.35261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35261)) frequently hallucinate plausible-sounding rules that do not match what the weights actually compute. Before allowing the model to edit a decompiled text $s$, run a 1-pass *counterfactual fidelity check* on 4–6 local probes around the conflict: compare $p(\cdot \mid x, s; W_0)$ against $p(\cdot \mid x; W + \Delta W)$. Accept $s$ only if KL $< \tau$; otherwise feed the highest-discrepancy probe back to refine $s$ before editing.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Introspection Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16812) and [Introspecting Alignment Shifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04347) evaluate faithfulness offline with human/LLM judges; an *inline counterfactual text-swap fidelity gate* closing the loop before any edit is applied is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B with 10 absorbed rules including 3 "deceptive/ambiguous" rules where naive verbalization misses a key condition. Compare unverified decompile-edit-reabsorb, fidelity-gated iterative decompile-edit-reabsorb, direct TTT overwrite, and ROME/MEMIT at matched forward passes. Kill if unverified 1-shot verbalization ties on post-edit accuracy across the 3 ambiguous rules.
- **Old-task retention:** Check that rejecting unfaithful decompilations prevents collateral damage to untouched old rules $A/B$ and non-excepted slices of the target rule.

### T2-12 · Superposed-delta disentanglement before decompilation (unmixing colliding rules in shared weights) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When $N$ rules are consolidated into one shared fast-weight/LoRA matrix, a conflict query $x_Y$ can co-activate two superposed rules $R_1$ and $R_2$. Decompose the conflict activation diff $\delta h(x_Y)$ using a lightweight *sparse parameter-feature dictionary* (grounded in activation space, cf. [2609.37731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37731)) into separable rank-1 components before verbalization—decompiling and editing *only* the conflicting component $R_1$ while leaving co-activated $R_2$ untouched.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Weights Read and Write Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37731) and [Adapters as Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07775) decompose weights offline for interpretability; using *online sparse-feature unmixing inside the decompile-edit loop* to prevent cross-rule contamination during exception edits is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer / Qwen2.5-0.5B with 8 pairs of co-activating rules merged into one rank-8 LoRA; add an exception to $R_1$ in each pair while $R_2$ must remain unchanged. Compare raw decompile-edit-reabsorb, sparse-unmixed decompile-edit-reabsorb, direct TTT overwrite, all-text at equal context, and AlphaEdit/ROME. Kill if raw decompilation or AlphaEdit matches $R_2$ preservation at equal $R_1$-exception accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both co-activated partner rules $R_2$ and unrelated old rules $A/B$ after all 8 exception edits.

### T2-13 · Boundary-minimal decompilation (extracting the decision boundary rather than the full rule) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a complex multi-clause rule receives a narrow exception ("except when currency is JPY"), decompiling the entire rule prose wastes context and risks mutating unaffected clauses. Instead, decompile *only the minimal conflict predicate*—synthesizing a 1-line guarded patch (`IF currency == JPY THEN new_action ELSE defer_to_weight`)—so the edit surface is strictly proportional to the exception's complexity, not the base rule's complexity.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09563) and N1 (PR #41) recover full instruction sets/programs; *boundary-minimal predicate decompilation* that leaves the base rule opaque and extracts only the exception seam is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B with 10 complex multi-condition rules absorbed into weights; add narrow 1-condition exceptions to 5 of them. Compare full-rule decompile-edit-reabsorb, boundary-minimal decompile-edit-reabsorb, direct TTT overwrite, all-text at equal context, and ROME/MEMIT at matched token and gradient budgets. Kill if full-rule decompilation ties on both exception accuracy and unaffected-clause retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Score all unaffected clauses of the 5 edited rules plus all 5 untouched old rules $A/B$.

### T2-14 · Conflict-witness synthesis (generating the minimal input pair that exposes the weight-vs-exception clash) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Users often state exceptions abstractly ("skip step 2 for internal repos") without concrete demonstrations, leaving direct TTT or ROME/MEMIT under-determined. Upon decompiling the conflicting weight premise, have the model synthesize a tiny *contrastive witness pair* $(x_{\text{keep}}, x_{\text{except}})$—differing only on the exception attribute—verify that current weights fire the old rule on both, and use the edited text *plus* the minimal pair to anchor re-absorption.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [FutureBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23601) evaluates knowledge-context conflict with external probes; PR #57 studied discriminating experiments for causal identification. *Inline contrastive witness-pair synthesis* driven by a decompiled weight premise to make abstract exception edits well-posed is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B on 10 abstractly stated exceptions (zero few-shot examples given) to previously absorbed rules. Compare direct TTT on the abstract exception text, ROME/MEMIT, all-text at equal context, decompile-edit without witnesses, and decompile-edit with synthesized witness pairs. Kill if direct TTT or decompile-edit without witnesses matches generalization to unseen concrete exception instances.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that the $x_{\text{keep}}$ half of the witness pair prevents regression on non-excepted instances of the rule and on untouched old rules $A/B$.

### T2-15 · Zero-overhead shadow-provenance index (fast pointer from conflicting activation to source delta) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a continual learner holds 50+ absorbed micro-deltas (or a multi-slot fast-weight bank), running weight-space reading across all slots on every conflict is prohibitively slow. At absorption time, write a tiny *activation-keyed locality-sensitive hash* mapping each rule's trigger-key subspace to its delta slot ID and compact structural tag; when a live conflict fires, the index pinpoints the culprit delta(s) in $O(1)$ before invoking decompilation.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Decoupled Lifelong Model Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25861) uses external key-value retrieval for factual edits; [Weight Oracles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07334) scans weights without an inline provenance index. An *$O(1)$ activation-keyed shadow-provenance index* coupling fast-weight storage to conflict-triggered decompilation is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** GPT-2 small / Qwen2.5-0.5B with 50 absorbed rule deltas; inject 15 exceptions and measure culprit-delta top-1 localization accuracy and latency vs. brute-force ablation scan, cosine-embedding retrieval over raw text logs, and global LoRA readout. Kill if cosine retrieval over text summaries at equal bytes matches localization accuracy on paraphrased conflict queries.
- **Old-task retention:** Confirm that accurate $O(1)$ culprit localization prevents mis-targeted edits from corrupting the 35 untouched old rules $A/B$.

## C · Editing, exception compilation & round-trip stability

### T2-16 · Guarded exception patches (compiling exceptions as gated overrides rather than base-rule overwrites) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Modifying a base rule's weights in place to fit an exception ("X except Y") bleeds across the decision boundary and degrades X on non-Y inputs. After decompiling and editing the rule in text, compile the exception as a *multiplicatively gated rank-1 override* $g_Y(h) \cdot \Delta W_{\text{except}}$ where the lightweight gate $g_Y(h)$ is trained to fire ($g_Y \approx 1$) on $Y$-matching activations and stay strictly quiescent ($g_Y \approx 0$) on non-$Y$ activations—leaving the base rule's weights bit-exact outside $Y$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [AlphaEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355), [BetaEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09285), and [FisherEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25914) project additive overwrites into old-fact null spaces; compiling an exception specifically as a *condition-gated multiplicative override* derived from decompiled text is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B with 10 absorbed rules receiving 1–2 exceptions each. Compare direct TTT overwrite, AlphaEdit/ROME/MEMIT, additive un-gated re-absorption, all-text at equal context, and gated override patches at matched parameter budget. Kill if AlphaEdit or un-gated re-absorption ties on the (exception accuracy, non-excepted base-rule retention) Pareto front.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure zero-regression retention on both non-excepted inputs of the target rule and all untouched old rules $A/B$ (verifying $g_Y(h) < 0.02$ on $A/B$).

### T2-17 · Differential re-absorption (writing only the edit diff $\Delta_{\text{edit}}$, never re-compiling unchanged clauses) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Re-distilling the entire edited rule text $s'$ from scratch introduces fresh approximation error into clauses that were not edited. Compute the *behavioral diff* between the pre-edit decompiled text $s$ and the post-edit text $s'$: $\Delta \ell(x) = \text{logits}(x \mid s'; W_0) - \text{logits}(x \mid s; W_0)$, and train the re-absorption update *solely* to match $\Delta \ell(x)$ on top of the existing weights $W + \Delta W$, anchoring regions where $\Delta \ell(x) \approx 0$ to exact zero change.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Localized Context Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21427) distills whole interaction contexts; [ManiEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33534) performs sequential unstructured edits directly from new facts. *Pre-edit-vs-post-edit differential re-absorption* using the decompiled text pair $(s, s')$ is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 10 multi-clause rules where each exception modifies 1 of 4 clauses. Compare full re-absorption of $s'$, offline round trip (N1), direct TTT overwrite, ROME/MEMIT, and differential re-absorption at equal gradient steps. Kill if full re-absorption of $s'$ with KL anchoring to $W + \Delta W$ ties on unaffected-clause retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both the 3 untouched clauses per edited rule and all untouched old rules $A/B$.

### T2-18 · Idempotent round-trip codec regularization (zero semantic drift across $k$ decompile–reabsorb cycles) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** In long-lived deployments, a single policy rule may undergo 5–10 successive exception edits; naive `weights → text → edit → weights` cycles accumulate paraphrase and quantization drift even on unchanged parts. Regularize the bidirectional codec with a *no-op fixed-point constraint*: when $s' = s$ (identity edit), re-absorbing the decompiled text must reproduce $W + \Delta W$ within $\epsilon$ in logit space across $k = 5$ consecutive round trips.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** N1 (PR #41) and [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09563) run single-pass round trips; ledger O1 (`IDER 2603.00624`) covers idempotent single-direction updates, not *bidirectional weight↔text round-trip fixed points* under repeated exception edits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small transformer / Qwen2.5-0.5B run through 5 consecutive decompile → (no-op or 1-clause exception) → re-absorb cycles on 8 rules. Compare unconstrained codec, offline round trip, direct sequential TTT overwrites, and fixed-point-regularized codec on drift slope per cycle. Kill if unconstrained decompile-reabsorb shows $< 1$ pp cumulative drift over 5 cycles.
- **Old-task retention:** Track untouched old rules $A/B$ across all 5 cycles to ensure fixed-point regularization does not sacrifice plasticity or erode $A/B$.

### T2-19 · Exception-hierarchy compression (collapsing stacked exceptions into a clean refactored rule) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** After 4+ sequential exceptions accumulate on one rule ("X, except Y1, except Y2 unless Z..."), stacked gated patches (`T2-16`) bloat rank and create higher-order interactions. Once exception stack depth on a rule hits $d_{\max}$, trigger an inline *decompile-and-refactor pass*: decompile the base rule plus all active exception patches into one unified text block, simplify it via CoT into a minimal canonical rule (eliminating redundant/contradictory clauses), wipe the stacked patches, and re-absorb one clean rank-$r$ delta.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [HiEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11214) uses hierarchical RL for lifelong editing without symbolic rule refactoring; N1 (PR #41) denoises single programs offline. *Depth-triggered decompile-refactor-reabsorb compression* of stacked weight exceptions is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 5 rules each subjected to 6 sequential overlapping/partially-canceling exceptions (e.g., exception 4 subsumes exception 1). Compare unbounded stacked patches, sequential AlphaEdit/ROME/MEMIT, keeping all 6 exceptions in text, and depth-triggered refactor compression at matched rank/bytes. Kill if unbounded stacked patches or AlphaEdit matches accuracy and parameter count after exception 6.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ and verify functional equivalence on all 6 exception cases immediately before and after the refactor pass.

### T2-20 · Negative-scope verification before re-absorption (proving the exception does not over-broaden) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Compiling a natural-language exception into weights frequently over-generalizes—suppressing the base rule on *near-miss* inputs outside the exception's true scope. Before committing the re-absorbed delta for edited text $s'$, generate 4 *minimal near-miss probes* (inputs satisfying all conditions of the base rule and failing the exception predicate by one attribute) and enforce a hard margin constraint during re-absorption that the base rule still fires on every near-miss probe.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** RippleEdits and [FutureBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23601) evaluate edit locality offline; *inline near-miss boundary synthesis and margin enforcement* inside the live decompile-edit-reabsorb commit gate is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 12 rule-exception pairs paired with adversarial near-miss test cases. Compare unverified re-absorption, direct TTT overwrite, ROME/MEMIT, offline round trip, and near-miss-verified re-absorption at equal compute. Kill if unverified re-absorption or ROME/MEMIT matches near-miss specificity within seed noise.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both near-miss probes of the edited rule and untouched old rules $A/B$.

### T2-21 · Reversible edit journal in minimal delta-diff coordinates (undoing a bad exception without re-decompiling) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** If a live exception is later retracted ("actually, revert that exception"), running another decompile-edit-reabsorb cycle introduces avoidable round-trip noise. Keep each unconfirmed exception as a compact, indexed *differential patch* alongside the immutable base delta until verified across $m$ turns, enabling exact bit-level rollback of the exception without touching the base rule or re-invoking the verbalizer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard model editing ([AlphaEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355)) folds edits irreversibly into base weights; PR #42 L1 failed by packing full checkpoints into one LoRA under prompt tags. Storing *unmerged differential exception patches* outside the base delta for exact rollback before promotion is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 10 rules where 5 receive temporary exceptions that are retracted 10 queries later. Compare re-decompile-to-revert, direct reverse-TTT overwrite, full checkpoint save at equal bytes, and differential-patch rollback on exact post-retraction recovery. Kill if reverse-TTT overwrite or re-decompile-to-revert matches exact pre-exception accuracy on both the rule and $A/B$.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify exact 0.00 pp drift on both the retracted rule and untouched old rules $A/B$ after retraction.

## D · Coupling handoff and decompilation: unified bidirectional KV↔weight transport

### T2-22 · Symmetric reverse-handoff during decompilation (muting weights as decompiled text enters context) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** The exact dual of `TT10`: when a conflicting weight delta $\Delta W$ is decompiled into editable text $s$ inside the live prompt, leaving $\Delta W$ active while $s$ is in context causes *reverse double-firing*—biasing the model's own chain-of-thought edit toward the stale weight prior! Couple decompilation with a *symmetric weight-muting step*: ramp $\Delta W$'s active scale $(1-\beta)$ down to $0$ (preserving only $\Delta W_{\text{resid}}$ from `T2-10`) as $s$ enters KV, so the model edits $s$ cleanly without fighting its own stale weights.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [When Context Returns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627) studies context returning *after* distillation, not *reverse double-firing during live weight-to-text editing*. Symmetric weight-muting coupled to decompilation insertion is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B on 10 strong weight-absorbed rules receiving counter-prior exceptions; measure how often the model's edit CoT successfully adopts the exception when $\Delta W$ stays active vs. when $\Delta W_{\text{verb}}$ is muted during the edit window (plus all-text, direct TTT overwrite, and ROME/MEMIT controls). Kill if keeping $\Delta W$ active during the edit window yields equal edit-CoT accuracy and calibration.
- **Old-task retention:** Confirm that muting only the conflicting rule's $\Delta W_{\text{verb}}$ leaves untouched old rules $A/B$ completely active during the edit window.

### T2-23 · Conservation-of-effect round-trip handoff (live seamless transition: weights → KV edit → weights) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Compose forward handoff (`T2-01`/`T2-02`) and reverse handoff (`T2-22`) into one continuous *live transport transaction* during an uninterrupted inference stream: smoothly ramp a conflicting rule out of weights into KV ($\alpha: 1 \to 0$), execute the exception edit in KV while serving incoming queries, and smoothly ramp the edited rule back into weights ($\alpha: 0 \to 1$)—keeping peak calibration error flat across every intermediate query throughout the entire round trip.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Offline distillation ([Cortex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20399), [NCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627)) and offline editing ([AlphaEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355)) assume stop-the-world updates; no prior method executes a *calibration-conserving live `weights → KV → weights` transaction* without service interruption.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B serving a continuous 100-query stream where 4 absorbed rules undergo mid-stream exception edits while queries continue arriving. Compare stop-the-world abrupt wipe + re-absorb, keep-in-context + NCA, direct TTT overwrite, logit interpolation, and continuous conservation-of-effect round-trip transport on peak calibration error across the entire 100-query trajectory. Kill if abrupt wipe + 3-step TTT overwrite matches peak calibration error during the edit window.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ on interleaved queries throughout all three phases ($\alpha: 1 \to 0$, edit, $\alpha: 0 \to 1$).

### T2-24 · Conflict-aware handoff pause (detecting an incoming exception mid-absorption and halting) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** If a rule is mid-handoff ($\alpha \in (0, 1)$) when a live query or user correction introduces an exception, finishing absorption wastes compute and hardens a wrong rule. Track the *directional sign agreement* between incoming live-query gradients and the active handoff delta $\Delta W$; a negative alignment spike immediately pauses handoff at $\alpha$, reconciles the exception in KV (using `T2-08` abort if needed), and resumes handoff only on the reconciled rule.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Privileged Context as Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07842) analyzes static privileged-context drift; *gradient-sign-triggered mid-handoff pause and live reconciliation* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a 12-rule stream where 4 rules receive exceptions partway through handoff ($\alpha \approx 0.5$). Compare finish-handoff-then-overwrite, always-keep-in-context, abrupt deletion, NCA, and conflict-aware handoff pause at equal FLOPs. Kill if finish-then-overwrite matches post-exception accuracy and peak calibration error.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify zero degradation on untouched old rules $A/B$ and on the 8 non-conflicting concurrent rules.

### T2-25 · Hybrid persistence split (keeping volatile exceptions in KV while stable base rules live in weights) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Immediately re-absorbing every transient exception ("for this week only, skip check Y") churns weights and risks forgetting. Introduce a *bidirectional volatility router*: stable base rules stay compiled in weights ($\alpha = 1$), while newly added exceptions live in KV ($\alpha = 0$) paired with exact local double-firing cancellation (`T2-01`) on the overridden slice, graduating into a guarded weight patch (`T2-16`) only once their observed lifetime/recurrence crosses a stability threshold.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Where Should LLM Agents Adapt at Test Time?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19700) compares static context vs. weights; keeping *base rules in weights and volatile exceptions in KV with exact conservation-of-effect cancellation* until stability is proven is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on a 200-query stream with 10 stable base rules, 5 permanent exceptions, and 10 short-lived (5-query) temporary exceptions. Compare always-reabsorb exceptions, all-text at equal context, keep-in-context without double-firing cancellation, direct TTT overwrite, and volatility-routed hybrid persistence. Kill if all-text at equal context or always-reabsorb ties on accuracy, calibration, and weight-churn-induced forgetting.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure untouched old rules $A/B$ after all 10 temporary exceptions expire, confirming zero permanent weight drift from transient exceptions.

### T2-26 · Cross-rule calibration coupling meter (detecting when handing off or editing rule $A$ destabilizes rule $B$) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Both forward handoff (`TT10`) and exception re-absorption (`TT14`) can silently shift logit margins on *untouched* rules $B$ that share attention heads or MLP features. Run a tiny 4-probe *cross-rule coupling meter* at $\alpha = 0.5$ and immediately post-reabsorption, measuring off-target calibration drift $\Delta \text{ECE}_B$ on cached probes of untouched rules $B$; if $\Delta \text{ECE}_B > \delta$, automatically project the update orthogonal to $B$'s probe Jacobian before completing handoff or commit.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [FisherEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25914) uses global Fisher matrices offline; an *inline mid-handoff ($\alpha = 0.5$) cross-rule calibration coupling meter* that triggers targeted orthogonalization before commit is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B with 12 rules where 4 pairs share strong lexical/feature overlap. Compare unmonitored handoff/re-absorption, global L2 regularization, NCA, FisherEdit, and cross-rule coupling-gated handoff/re-absorption. Kill if global L2 regularization or NCA keeps off-target $\Delta \text{ECE}_B$ equally low without losing on-target accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Directly targets and bounds off-target calibration drift on untouched old rules $A/B$.

### T2-27 · Steering-vector mediated handoff (using the model's native context-vs-memory direction to gate transfer) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Mechanistic analysis ([2609.00753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00753)) reveals low-dimensional internal activation directions that govern whether an LLM defers to context or to parametric memory. Rather than externally editing KV masks, drive the forward handoff by *gradually steering along the model's native context-vs-memory direction* scoped to the target rule's tokens as $\alpha$ moves $0 \to 1$, and flip the steering sign during conflict decompilation so the model natively defers to the editable text.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [How Do LMs Choose Between Context and Memory?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00753) extracts and tests static steering directions on fixed factual conflicts; repurposing that *native arbitration direction as a continuous bidirectional handoff dial* for continual context↔weight transfer is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B on both forward handoff ($\alpha \in [0, 1]$) and exception-edit CoT windows. Compare native steering-dial handoff against value-vector scaling (`T2-01`), logit interpolation, NCA, keep-in-context, abrupt deletion, filler, and entropy-matched controls. Kill if external value-vector scaling (`T2-01`) strictly dominates steering on both peak calibration error and ease of scoping to one rule without affecting bystander rules.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that rule-scoped steering along the context-vs-memory axis does not flip unrelated bystander rules in context or untouched old rules $A/B$ in weights.

### T2-28 · End-to-end calibration-and-exception benchmark (`HandoffEdit-CL`: unified assay for both primitives) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Establish a single CPU-runnable, multi-seed evaluation primitive that tests *both* (a) and (b) under realistic continual pressure: a 20-rule stream where each rule enters in context, hands off to weights over $T$ queries (scored on peak calibration error across $\alpha \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$ against abrupt deletion, keep-in-context, NCA, localized context distillation, logit interpolation, filler, and entropy-matched controls), followed by 3 sequential waves of partial exceptions on 40% of absorbed rules (scored on exception accuracy, near-miss specificity, unverbalized sub-behavior survival, and untouched old-rule retention against direct TTT overwrite, all-text at equal context, offline round trip, and ROME/MEMIT).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [When Context Returns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11627) tests single-cycle context return and [FutureBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23601) tests static factual conflicts; no benchmark jointly scores *interior-path handoff calibration* and *multi-wave exception decompile–edit–reabsorb* with mandatory filler/entropy and old-rule retention controls.
- **Cheapest first test:** Implement the 20-rule synthetic router/transformation stream on Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B (CPU/single-GPU, 3 seeds) and verify the baseline gap: confirm that keep-in-context, abrupt deletion, NCA, direct TTT overwrite, and ROME/MEMIT each fail at least one axis by $\ge 5$ pp beyond seed CI, establishing real headroom before piloting any primitive. Kill (redesign benchmark) if NCA + direct TTT overwrite already sit near ceiling on both axes.
- **Old-task retention:** Built into every wave as the mandatory zero-reset $A/B$ retention floor (both accuracy and ECE).

### T2-29 · Selective rule-vs-fact handoff (`TFX3`: three-state calibration parity across context-only, handoff, and weights-only) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Deepens cross-pool round 106's `TFX3` inside our conservation-of-effect handoff (`T2-01`–`T2-04`): partition each live prompt into its *reusable rule tokens* (handed off into weights with matched $(1-\alpha)$ value-vector attenuation) and its *one-off episodic fact tokens* (held at scale $1.0$ in KV and never written to weights). Enforce calibration parity explicitly across all three operational states—**context-only** ($\alpha=0$), **mid-handoff** ($\alpha=0.5$), and **weights-only-rule + KV-facts** ($\alpha=1$)—so ephemeral facts neither leak into weights nor suffer softmax distortion while the co-located rule transfers.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `TFX3` (cross-pool round 106), [Encoding Context into LLM Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17686), and [Where Should LLM Agents Adapt at Test Time?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19700). Unifying *selective rule-only absorption* with *softmax-invariant three-state calibration parity* (context-only vs. handoff vs. weights-only-rule) is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B on 12 prompts each mixing 1 reusable rule and 5 one-off session facts; score peak calibration error and accuracy across context-only, mid-handoff ($\alpha=0.5$), and weights-only-rule + KV-facts states against compile-all handoff, abrupt deletion, keep-in-context, NCA, localized context distillation, logit interpolation, filler, and entropy-matched controls. Kill if compile-all handoff + weight decay matches three-state calibration parity and zero one-off fact leakage.
- **Old-task retention:** Score untouched old rules $A/B$ and verify that one-off facts from session 1 never leak into session 10 after the rule hands off.

### T2-30 · Pairwise fast-weight conflict ledger (`TFX5`: recording cached delta pairs that fail together to trigger joint decompile–refactor) — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Deepens cross-pool round 106's `TFX5` inside our conflict-triggered decompilation loop (`T2-09`, `T2-12`, `T2-19`): maintain a compact *pairwise incompatibility record* of cached fast-weight delta pairs $(\Delta W_i, \Delta W_j)$ that each pass individually yet fail when co-activated (write-skew / non-commutation, cf. SD3 & `TT34`). When a live query activates a recorded incompatible pair, block blind additive merging and instead trigger *paired decompilation*—unpacking both conflicting slices into editable text, resolving their interaction boundary via CoT, and re-absorbing one compatible joint patch (or gated override `T2-16`).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** `TFX5` (cross-pool round 106), Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, and Text-to-LoRA / [Doc-to-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902) / [Modular TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07110) composition assume individually valid deltas compose cleanly. Coupling a *minimal failed-pair incompatibility ledger* directly to *paired conflict decompile–edit–reabsorb* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B with 12 cached rule deltas containing 4 planted pairs that each pass solo but collide when composed. Compare Task Arithmetic addition, TIES-Merging, Text-to-LoRA composition, direct TTT overwrite, and conflict-ledger-triggered paired decompile–reabsorb at matched memory bytes and compute. Kill if TIES-Merging or Text-to-LoRA composition resolves the 4 incompatible pairs equally well without a conflict record or decompilation.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure solo accuracy on all 12 rules, joint accuracy on the 4 reconciled pairs, and untouched old-rule retention $A/B$.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does softmax-invariant, query-conditioned, rule-selective conservation (`T2-01`, `T2-02`, `T2-04`, `T2-05`, `T2-29` / `TFX3`) strictly beat NCA, logit interpolation, and entropy-matched controls on peak calibration error across context-only, handoff, and weights-only states?**
   Scalar KV logit attenuation leaks softmax mass onto bystander tokens and conflates semantic rule absorption with PR #29/#31's context-length entropy effect. Test on Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B (`T2-28` Phase 1) whether value-vector scaling with a zero-value sink (`T2-01`) + per-query absorption projection $\alpha(x)$ (`T2-02`) + midpoint flatness (`T2-05`) beats abrupt deletion, keep-in-context, NCA, localized context distillation, logit interpolation, filler, and entropy-matched temperature on worst-slice peak calibration error and bystander/old-rule retention. Drop the handoff line if NCA or simple output-logit interpolation matches peak calibration error across both canonical and paraphrased queries.

2. **Can conflict-scoped, residual-preserving decompile–edit–reabsorb (`T2-09`, `T2-10`, `T2-11`, `T2-16`) beat direct TTT overwrite, all-text, offline round trip, and ROME/MEMIT on partial rule exceptions?**
   Whole-adapter readout is noisy and lossy; direct gradient overwrites and ROME/MEMIT bleed across non-excepted regions or struggle with abstract exceptions. Test on `T2-28` Phase 2 (including unverbalized sub-behaviors and near-miss boundary probes) whether conflict-scoped activation-diff readout (`T2-09`) + orthogonal residual preservation (`T2-10`) + counterfactual text-swap fidelity gating (`T2-11`) + multiplicatively gated exception patches (`T2-16`) strictly dominate direct TTT overwrite, all-text at equal context, offline round trip, and AlphaEdit/ROME/MEMIT on (exception accuracy, near-miss specificity, unverbalized-residual survival, and untouched $A/B$ retention).

3. **Does reverse double-firing during the live decompilation edit window actually corrupt edits—and does symmetric bidirectional KV↔weight transport (`T2-22`, `T2-23`, `T2-27`) cure it?**
   Putting decompiled rule text back into context while the conflicting weight delta stays active may bias the model's own editing CoT toward the stale weight prior. Run a decisive ablation on strong weight-absorbed rules receiving counter-prior exceptions: compare leaving $\Delta W$ active vs. symmetric $\Delta W_{\text{verb}}$-muting (`T2-22`) vs. native context-vs-memory steering (`T2-27`), measuring edit-CoT success rate and live mid-edit calibration error on interleaved queries (`T2-23`).

4. **Do multi-wave exception edits and co-activated delta pairs suffer round-trip drift or pairwise write-skew—and can differential re-absorption, depth-triggered refactoring, and a pairwise conflict ledger (`T2-17`, `T2-18`, `T2-19`, `T2-25`, `T2-30` / `TFX5`) bound both?**
   Single-exception, solo-delta tests hide cumulative drift and pairwise composition collisions. Run 5 sequential overlapping exceptions per rule (mixing permanent and short-lived exceptions) alongside 4 planted individually-valid-but-jointly-failing cached delta pairs (`TFX5`), testing whether differential re-absorption (`T2-17`), fixed-point codec regularization (`T2-18`), depth-triggered refactor passes (`T2-19`), volatility-routed KV/weight splitting (`T2-25`), and conflict-ledger paired decompilation (`T2-30`) beat direct sequential TTT overwrites, AlphaEdit, Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, and Text-to-LoRA composition while keeping old rules $A/B$ intact.
