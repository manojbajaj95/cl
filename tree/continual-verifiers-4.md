# Continual verifiers 4: Transfer the correction, or show why it will not fit

Depth 3 · 20 idea cards · **Ambitious but doable** · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Parent:** [#413](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/413). The [review in #6](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/c45f3572eb8701c5dfdc9ccdf48c281897430b17/reviews/flood-triage.md#L575) kept two ideas at 3, neither at 4: **CV3-05**, transplant a repair between updaters as two observed lesson transitions, using target-blind calibration and returning an infeasibility witness; and **CV3-10**, rebind a tactic intervention to a successor implementation, carrying the progress sign only when the signed contrast and no-toggle bound support it. The same review correctly demotes same-learner clean-response matching: it is too close to Update-Matched Unlearning (UMU). This node tests whether a reusable export and a useful failure witness add anything beyond the corresponding best refit.

## Literature check

Read UMU sections 3–4 and the learning-trial ablations, the [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) method overview, and targeted titles/abstracts returned by alphaXiv and arXiv. **P** means candidate primitive, contingent on the specified comparison. **I** means borrowed operation, improvement or assay. Neither a smaller package nor a checked bound is a primitive by itself.

| Already available | Boundary for this node |
|---|---|
| [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) records a temporary fine-tuning update from a copy of the defended learner and applies **that same weight update** to the defended model and the frozen base-unlearned reference. It matches updated retain distributions while suppressing recovery on the forget set, and uses ordinary learning trials for checkpoint selection. It does not just fine-tune two models on the same batch, and its reference is not a fresh clean retrain. [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) and [MUDMAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12484) also train against future adaptation. | Include faithful UMU **per recipient**, plus variants of UMU/TAR with the same clean-reference access as the proposed method. Distinguish replaying a recorded weight update from matching the functional effects of a lesson across incompatible updaters. If per-recipient methods match transfer at the same cost, there is no new repair operation. |
| [Model stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) learns a connector between representations. [Trajectory matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932), [gradient matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05929) and [cross-architecture dataset distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13007) already export future training behavior in compressed form. [Optimizer-state alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17590) explicitly matches counterfactual update directions. | Include stitching on lesson-response coordinates and cross-updater trajectory/response distillation with the **same** source observations and calibration. A two-transition representation that only reduces dimensions is a variant of stitching or distillation. A failure certificate must identify an actionable obstacle not provided by these methods at equal cost. |
| [Causal abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20161) and [compositional causal abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16612) already align interventions and compose maps. [Causal transportability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15645) and [interventional agreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) already distinguish supported transport from empirical disagreement. [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) already uses change footprints, shared cancellation and selective rechecks. | Compare learned alignment **on the same signed observables**, stacked with #408; full solver equivalence is not required of the baseline. Compare robust min–max on the same edit grammar, fixed panels, SF/GPI and fresh [VinePPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679). If alignment plus #408 ties, retain it and stop the rebinding line. |

**First-test convention.** Use harmless enumerable packing and arithmetic tasks. For repair, fix a clean v2 learner *per recipient* and give all methods the same donor observations, recipient calibration, and checked lesson budget. Charge donor fitting, recipient fitting, fresh references, storage and reuse; report both one-off cost and amortization across recipients. “Target-blind” means adaptation sees designated clean calibration lessons but no target-premise judgments beyond the exported responses. Hold out a premise, updater, and finite lesson ordering. For rebinding, freeze the old validity statements; score old proof validity separately from adaptive progress, and count unsafe carries and abstentions against a fresh independently evaluated reference. Include substitutions, shifts of tactic use without a menu change, and cases where no successor intervention is equivalent. Keep untouched old tasks A/B, including one shared-feature skill, and measure new clean learning as well as immediate outputs. All tests below are proposals.

## A · Transplant across updaters without smuggling in the teacher

### CV4-01 · Send a separating lesson instead of a failed fit — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Export a target vector for two finite lesson transitions. The recipient declares a bounded repair family and computes its reachable response region (start with an affine response and a compact convex repair budget). Return a local repair if the vector is reachable; otherwise return a separating linear combination of the two transitions and the cheapest candidate extension that could cross it. For a nonlinear fit, issue that certificate only if a sound outer region excludes the target. Validate the extension independently before expanding scope.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Reachability and separation are known ([reachable-set convex hulls](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17674)); [stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) with a constrained fit can return the same dual. Candidate only if the separating *lesson* and its extension recur across recipient classes and reduce new checks over stitching with the same solver or per-recipient [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230).
- **Cheapest first test:** Transfer a correction from SGD to a tiny optimizer with one permitted correction direction; the target changes a correlated old lesson that requires a second direction. Compare stitched fit plus duals, UMU, and reuse of the separating lesson across held-out recipients, including a feasible case. A failed nonlinear search is not evidence of infeasibility.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and the old correlated skill before and after adding the proposed direction.

### CV4-02 · Separate what the recipient cannot express from what it cannot identify — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Distinguish two failure outputs. An exact reachable set excludes the target under the declared repair budget; or the admissible clean targets include both achievable and unachievable responses. In the second case return the clean lesson that separates these possibilities, not “impossible.”
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) and constrained stitching already distinguish insufficient evidence from representational limits. An assay for the export interface, not an alternative to UMU or [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761).
- **Cheapest first test:** Make two synthetic clean references coincide on calibration lessons but diverge on an unobserved sequence, and add a second case with an exactly unattainable response. Compare confidence from UMU, response distillation and residual-based rejection to the exact toy oracle.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old valid decisions and allowed learning while leaving the ambiguous transfer undecided.

### CV4-03 · A donor-free held-out repair — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Freeze the donor export before choosing the recipient or new premise. Compare recipient fitting using two lesson transitions, the same transitions with shuffled associations, and the same bytes of examples or response trajectories. The donor is not available for follow-up requests.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Cross-architecture dataset distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13007) already targets transfer to unseen hosts. This is the decisive comparison for CV3-05, not a primitive; per-recipient UMU remains a reference arm with its fitting and reference cost charged.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hold out a two-factor optimizer/architecture cross not seen during donor fitting; measure new checked lessons to reach an independently defined error on finite sequences. Include an equal-byte batch-only file as a floor.
- **Old-task retention:** A/B and a shared-feature old exception must match the clean recipient after a held-out clean update.

### CV4-04 · Fit the recipient with no access to the target premise — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Infer from unrelated calibration lessons which response contrasts are invariant to changing the implementation of an updater, and export only those repair components. Make a component that cannot be identified from target-blind lessons an explicit obligation the recipient must fulfill locally; keep it out of the transferred write.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) plus [trajectory matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932) on the same calibration will often express this. The candidate is a decomposition of one repair into **transferable write plus missing experiment** that can be reused on other premises; no contribution if selective stitching estimates it just as cheaply.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use three optimizer/architecture pairs whose clean updates agree along one action contrast but have different order effects along another. Hide target checks until evaluation, then buy one test for the missing component. Compare ordinary and support-restricted distillation, UMU with the same available data, and the export.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain A/B and demonstrate that the locally identified component does not overwrite the already transferred one.

### CV4-05 · Ask for the one recipient change that would make a repair transferable — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Use failed imports across several recipients to derive a common separating response direction; provision only the minimal local extension that makes a held-out recipient capable of the requested learning transitions. Do not add a full adapter by default, and require the new direction to support more than the failed pair.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** This is likely constrained stitching with adaptive width; [convex distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06567) covers compression by optimization and [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) can refit any provisioned recipient. Candidate only if the failed exports deliver a reusable, smaller **capacity edit**, beating a stitched adapter with rank grown using the same residual and checks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Calibrate on two tiny recipient variants that miss the same feasible transition combination, then add one channel to a third. Compare exact rank search, residual-guided rank growth and reused direction at equal rank and labels.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve calibration tasks and old overlapping rules after the extension and another ordinary lesson.

### CV4-06 · Distinguish a repair transfer from sharing the lesson sequence — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Replace the donor's measured transitions with ones generated by a naive donor seeing the identical clean lessons. If a recipient learns equally well, credit the lesson data rather than a transplanted repair.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dataset distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10959) and [trajectory matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932) are the named alternatives. This is a teaching-versus-transfer control, including a TAR/UMU recipient given the same donor inputs.
- **Cheapest first test:** Match bytes for transition contrasts, trajectories, and the original lessons in a tiny arithmetic learner. Blind the recipients to which donor generated the export and compare the held-out sequence error.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old skill outputs and future learning rates, not just the corrected target trajectory.

### CV4-07 · Transfer with two anchors and one held-out noncommuting loop — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Use the two promised transitions to fit the local realization, then test the difference between doing their lessons in opposite orders. Match the complete sequence endpoints, not merely the average displacement; hold out update sizes and do not fit to this test.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Trajectory distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932), [TAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761), and [CV2-06](continual-verifiers-2.md#cv2-06--the-smallest-repair-commutator--ambitious-but-doable-p) already target finite learning behavior or order dependence. This is a falsifier for two-transition sufficiency, not a separate mechanism.
- **Cheapest first test:** Design SGD and momentum recipients with two matched individual transitions but opposite sequence effects. Compare calibration on endpoints versus sequences under identical compute, including counterfactual clean retraining.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B after each ordering and after a subsequent unrelated update.

## B · Rebind a signed step comparison without aligning the whole solver

### CV4-08 · Find a correspondence only where it can change the sign — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** For checked branch returns let d be the no-toggle difference and g the change of that difference under the toggle. Certify d and g separately in the successor: if intervals around them leave d + g on one side of zero, do not align the remaining states. Otherwise return the exact branch and component whose additional check could decide it. An old reversing witness alone gives neither bound.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Causal abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20161) on the same signed outputs plus [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) can express interval revalidation; [robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099) handles uncertainty. Candidate only if **reusable, partial correspondence** across held-out refactors beats that stack using identical signed observables.
- **Cheapest first test:** Swap a one-step arithmetic tactic for a two-step version; modify unrelated side outcomes equally on both branches, then add a shift affecting d but not g. Evaluate fresh branching, restricted causal alignment plus #408, and the partial-transport object at matched coverage and oracle calls.
- **Old-task retention:** Keep old valid/invalid steps and signs on unaffected branch pairs; do not carry a sign when a no-toggle shift spans the margin.

### CV4-09 · Learn the signed footprint of a replacement sequence from its intermediates — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Split a successor tactic into composed actions. Compare each intermediate's **paired** contribution under the same start states; record the first intermediate that makes the old signed contrast untransportable, or carry the contrast if residual terms are bounded. Reuse that localization at other pairs that share the intermediate but not the full tactic.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Compositional abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16612) already composes maps, and #408 already cancels shared effects. Novel only if the signed intermediate is a transferred experiment unit that reduces checks beyond compositional alignment plus #408, not because subtraction is cheap.
- **Cheapest first test:** Build two versions of a three-state rewrite with indistinguishable terminal success rates but different intermediate eligibility on one branch. Compare direct composite intervention, signed alignment and reusing intermediate checks on held-out pairs.
- **Old-task retention:** Validate old steps that pass through the same intermediate for a different reason; preserve their correct progress ordering.

### CV4-10 · Return an intervention-language obstruction — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** If no legal successor toggle matches the old reversal, split the failure into what the successor can reproduce and one observable contrast that every legal local intervention misses. Hand the unresolved contrast back to the verifier as the smallest branch family requiring independent reevaluation. Use enumeration for the declared small grammar; do not call exhaustion of a beam a proof.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional agreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) separates models with admissible experiments, and [compositional abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16612) specifies intervention maps. Candidate only if the obstruction is reusable for *renewal* across versions and buys more than alignment plus #408 with an explicit counterexample.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use a solver that merges two formerly independently toggleable tactics. Enumerate its legal toggles and measure whether carrying the obstruction reduces fresh evaluations after another interface refactor. Compare exact robust search and subset-specific revalidation.
- **Old-task retention:** Carry old signs for branches unaffected by the merged tactics and preserve their proof validity.

### CV4-11 · Do not carry a sign from matching the intervention alone — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Track the no-toggle baseline and the toggle effect under separate independently judged responses. Include adversarially chosen but harmless toy cases where each agrees but the other drifts. Treat proof validity as a separate quantity.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Causal abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20161) and #408 already support conditional reuse from these observables. This is the minimal assay for CV3-10, not a transport algorithm. UMU/TAR are not comparators for this fixed-solver test; they change the learner rather than validate a sign.
- **Cheapest first test:** Synthesize a matched toggle effect with a flipped baseline, and a matched baseline with opposite toggle effect. Compare sign carry from either single observable, the paired bounds, and fresh branching.
- **Old-task retention:** Check that cases with both observables still in scope are not refreshed or dropped unnecessarily.

### CV4-12 · Reuse one verified toggle across a new set of branch pairs — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Export a replacement tactic with a pairwise applicability predicate in terms of independently checkable preconditions, and a counterexample for the nearest losing pair. An unseen pair that satisfies the predicate can use the intervention experiment; its individual preference is never inherited from a different pair.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Causal transportability via abstractions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15645) is close for transferring effects over contexts; #408 is close for shared regimes. Candidate only if a scoped experiment has lower cost on *unseen branch pairs* than abstraction plus influence-based experiment reuse on the same contexts.
- **Cheapest first test:** Choose one shortcut and three otherwise distinct arithmetic prefixes. Hold out a fourth that shares only the applicability precondition, and a false friend where the precondition fails. Compare fresh search, fixed panel and causal retesting at matched false-carry rates.
- **Old-task retention:** Maintain old rankings and label coverage in both the admitted and excluded scope.

### CV4-13 · Carry a positive result about experiment equivalence, not policy safety — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Report whether the rebinding establishes an equivalent experiment, a known progress sign, both, or neither. Test a successor with exactly the old conditional toggle effects but a different voluntary tactic policy, and one with the same policy outputs but a different toggle effect.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional agreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) and [PAV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146) distinguish interventions and continuation policies. An audit that prevents equating alignment with a transported verdict; not a new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate the four synthetic cases and compare fixed panel, learned alignment plus #408, and a rubric that conflates the two claims. Score error and abstention under complete truth.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve unaffected old decision scores and distinguish them from unchanged theorem validity.

### CV4-14 · Test whether direct search makes the old witness redundant — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Let the strongest baseline choose a fresh successor intervention in the same grammar with the same oracle budget. Compare cold search, warm start from the old witness, and cheap rebranching; do not suppress access to signed effects in the baseline.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [VinePPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679), [robust OPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00099), and causal alignment plus #408 are sufficient controls. A speedup from seeding search is an improvement unless it supports a new transferable object.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hold out a two-step replacement, then a three-step one with a novel intermediate. Plot calls-to-decision versus false carries at matched coverage; record search misses separately from a true absent witness.
- **Old-task retention:** Require that cheaper search does not skip rare old exceptions or damage their original validity evidence.

## C · Find out whether the two transfers need one coupled operation

### CV4-15 · A joint obstruction when two individually feasible transfers conflict — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Require the transplanted repair and rebound tactic intervention to share a declared learner coordinate, such as one conditional gate that controls whether a tactic is eligible. Look for a joint target outside the attainable region even when each marginal target can be fit. Use the incompatible pair of a lesson and a forced branch to identify the smallest interface split that makes both transfers realizable; in the finite case list the rejected settings if there is no linear separator.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [CAGrad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14048) handles conflicting objectives, and constrained stitching plus [compositional abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16612) can test shared feasibility. The candidate is using a failed joint transport to learn a reusable interface split; the mathematical separator and multi-objective fit are known. Compare per-recipient UMU plus causal alignment and joint constrained distillation, all with the same shared coordinate and expansion budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** In a packing game, make one shared gate sufficient for either the clean lesson response or the correct signed tactic effect, but not both. Hold out a second task with the same conflict pattern. Compare exact joint search, residual-guided expansion and a transfer from the first conflict; include a control with no real conflict.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve old policy adaptation, old checked validity, and both sign and lesson responses on unaffected branches after the split.

### CV4-16 · Recover the correction when only one endpoint of the lesson moved — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** On a recipient upgrade, replay the two anchor lessons separately. If only one matches the donor export, preserve that independently checked component and return an executable forced-branch test of the missing component. Fit from the extra observation and a zero-change constraint on the surviving component, then verify the order interaction on an unseen test.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Trajectory matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932) can mask unaffected targets, and [#408](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/408) already selects what to recheck. Candidate only if the exported missing-component experiment recurs and reduces checked lessons beyond masked distillation plus selective revalidation and UMU.
- **Cheapest first test:** Change momentum only for updates involving a packing shortcut. Compare full replay, partial distillation with the manually chosen test, and reuse of the test generated by a previous failed export. Hold out a recipient in which changing the second anchor also changes the first.
- **Old-task retention:** Retain unaffected lesson responses and old checked step signs after repairing the second anchor.

### CV4-17 · Retire a recipient-specific workaround without deleting the abstract repair — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** An import can temporarily realize an abstract lesson effect with a workaround that couples two local actions. Record a test showing when the recipient can realize that effect directly. On another solver update replace the workaround only if an independent comparison establishes both the same clean lesson response and the same signed intervention effect. Preserve the donor export and charge all interim state.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Model stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) replaces connectors and [causal transportability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15645) adapts maps between environments. New only if one carried test can simplify *both* repairs across later upgrades beyond repeated stitching, distillation and causal alignment; otherwise this is adapter retirement.
- **Cheapest first test:** Give v1 one local implementation for an arithmetic step, v2 a two-action workaround and v3 a direct primitive. Compare retaining the workaround, fresh paired refits and test-driven retirement with equal independent validation.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve normal operation on old prerequisites under v1 and old unrelated usages after retiring the workaround on v3.

### CV4-18 · Count the honest break-even point — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Price clean recipient references, all prior donation/fitting, stored response and intervention artifacts, rechecks, and serving overhead. Compare reuse against refits as the number of premise/recipient pairs grows; include an end-to-end wall-clock accounting separately from checked labels.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Cross-architecture distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13007) already amortizes fit across recipients, and [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230) is a local post-training method with no runtime component. This is a cost assay; positive amortization is not evidence that the object is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare one, four and eight toy recipients, with an intentionally bad donor and an oracle donor reported separately. Give the strongest generic cross-recipient baseline the same opportunity to amortize.
- **Old-task retention:** At every fleet size report the worst recipient's A/B and overlapping skill, not just the mean.

### CV4-19 · Put response distillation and rebinding in the same control arm — Ambitious but doable (I)
- **Mechanism:** Train a recipient by ordinary trajectory/response distillation with UMU-style update matching, and give its verifier a signed causal alignment plus #408. Allow both to share the same calibration transcripts and optimization budget as the proposed joint object.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** This intentionally stacks [UMU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21230), [trajectory matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932) and [causal abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20161). It is the null required to test any claimed joint effect, not a new merger.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare repair alone, rebinding alone, the shared-data stack and one joint mechanism on a held-out premise/updater/tactic change; the stack gets all reusable evidence available to the joint arm.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B, one previously corrected old skill, and old proof validity and adaptive rankings separately.

### CV4-20 · Carry disagreement when a handoff needs a new clean lesson — Ambitious but doable (P)
- **Mechanism:** Two recipient realizations can satisfy the donor responses and signed witness but predict opposite effects of a specific future lesson. Export the executable lesson and forced branch on which they disagree as an unresolved part of the repair. Reuse this artifact to ask a later recipient one targeted question rather than silently choosing a realization. Test whether a negative answer can be reused to eliminate the same repair/witness equivalence class on a different premise.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interventional separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32247) distinguishes models, and trajectory-distillation ensembles could provide the competing realizations. Candidate only if reusing the coupled disagreement beats fresh active selection over the same models and experiments; otherwise it is active diagnosis.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct two local recipients identical on both response anchors and the original toggle, but different after a clean ordered lesson. Compare response-ensemble uncertainty, fixed witness refresh, active intervention selection and transferred disagreement on later held-out premises at equal calls.
- **Old-task retention:** Preserve independently confirmed old progress and adaptation, and ensure the new unresolved label does not neutralize unrelated lessons.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does exporting two transitions beat refitting the recipient? (CV4-01–CV4-07)** Freeze the donor, new recipient, premise, and reserved lesson orders before adaptation. Match clean-reference access, total update compute, bytes and independent checked lessons for per-recipient UMU, TAR/MUDMAN with the same clean target, response-coordinate stitching, cross-updater distillation, MAML-style transfer and clean retrain. Require a lower cost to match finite clean learning with A/B intact. **If UMU or TAR ties, the repair is update-aware unlearning; if stitching or distillation ties, the export is a representation of familiar transfer, not a new primitive.** Do not preserve the claim by switching metrics.
2. **Does a signed intervention witness rebind better than learned causal alignment on those same observables? (CV4-08–CV4-14)** Replace a tactic with a two-step equivalent, perturb use without changing availability, and include an untranslatable control. Give learned causal alignment plus #408 the same signed targets, grammar and no-toggle probes; include robust min–max, SF/GPI and fresh VinePPO. Require fewer calls or unsafe carries at matched coverage and validity retention. **If that stack ties, treat rebinding as causal alignment and ordinary selective rechecks.**
3. **Does an infeasibility witness change what the next recipient can learn? (CV4-01, CV4-02, CV4-05, CV4-10, CV4-15)** In a finite repair class, separately test infeasibility, missing evidence and a merely failed optimization. Compare against an exact constrained stitcher/distiller with the same separation certificates and the cheapest feasible capacity expansion. If the exported witness cannot save a check or make an unseen transfer realizable, it is a useful diagnostic, not a primitive.
4. **Is a coupled repair worth more than the best stack sharing the same evidence? (CV4-15–CV4-20)** Plant a genuine shared-coordinate conflict and a control in which the transfers are independent. Compare the joint operation against UMU/response distillation plus signed causal alignment and #408 with all donor and calibration costs included. Require improvement in *both* clean learning responses and held-out sign transport, or a cheaper valid interface split. If no interaction survives the shared-data stack, keep the two existing methods separate and close the joint line.
