# Momentum fossils: does training history survive in optimizer state when outputs match?

**Parents:** cross-pool round 24 FI3, *catch a fossil that first returns in the velocity register* ([PR #36](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/36), `ideas/flood/cross-pool-24.md`), triaged as a survivor in [`reviews/flood-triage.md` row 217](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md); [optimizer-state memory (PR #109)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/109); [function-matched twins (PR #123)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/123); [fossil beliefs (PR #132)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/132); [information-dumping map (PR #133)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/133). **Status:** ideas only. No experiments were run. Every proposed first test is CPU-scale.

**Question:** When two learners produce the same outputs, can their optimizer state (momentum, Adam first and second moments, step counter) still carry old beliefs or past training history? If so, how long does that last, what does it do to old tasks after the next write, and can continual learning use it or control it?

## Root cause: an output match checks the weights, but the learner's state also includes the optimizer

The full training state is `z = (w, m, v, t)` plus any schedule position. Outputs, and even the first gradient, depend on `w` alone. The next update depends on all of `z`. So "identical outputs" never certifies identical futures once training continues. This part is true by construction, not a finding. Three facts make it interesting for continual learning rather than trivial:

1. **Momentum is a pending displacement.** For heavy-ball `m⁺ = βm + g`, `w⁺ = w − ηm⁺` with fixed `η, β`, momentum alone still contributes `ηβm/(1−β)` of future motion (PR #109's invariant, valid only in gradient-null coordinates). At a task switch or right after a belief correction, that motion was computed on the old world. [2610.03940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03940) measures the harmful side in LMs: accumulated Adam history, mostly *older* gradients of the new task, drains probability mass from old answers while the current gradient protects them. A momentum reset that matches the update norm lowers final old-task loss.
2. **The second moment lives about 100 times longer than the first.** At default Adam settings `(β₁, β₂) = (0.9, 0.999)`, the memory lengths `1/(1−β)` are about 10 and 1,000 steps. `v` is an uncentered running average of squared minibatch gradients, roughly a diagonal empirical Fisher of *recent* data. After a switch it keeps shrinking steps on coordinates the old task used heavily. That works as an unrequested, slowly expiring importance map, and it can protect the old task or slow the new one ([2608.15824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15824) bounds the progress cost of second-moment memory). Prior work focuses on `m`. The long-lived `v` fossil is the less-explored carrier.
3. **Swapping slots gives an exact match for free.** If two heirs have the same `w` and different slots, launch outputs, first gradients and the tangent kernel are identical by construction. That passes PR #123's forward and first-gradient gates without any distillation artifact. Only the *one-update* read differs. For SGD with momentum, one update with a known `g` reveals `m` exactly. A claim of history "hidden" from all reads must therefore beat what the one-update read plus the linearized response predicts (the input–state–output operator of [2608.19762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19762)).

So the node's real question is not *whether* slots carry history; [2608.20442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20442) already shows a first-moment transplant changes nothing at the cut and later expresses a trait whose sign depends on the future route. It is: **(a)** whether slots carry *old-world beliefs and old-task protection* long enough, and nonlinearly enough, to change retention beyond the one-update read; and **(b)** whether a learner can deliberately write, pin, launder or erase that content cheaper than the obvious baselines (storing the delta, EWC, replay, reset).

## Prior-art screen and ledger constraints

The [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md) killed Noether/balancedness memory: finite-step SGD and momentum erase it, a non-conserved channel survives about as well at low LR, and a function-preserving rebalance erases it. PR #109 adds that optimizer-*conserved* quantities fail as write targets (finite steps and buffer resets break conservation, and exactly conserved quantities are gauge directions). This node therefore uses **non-conserved, decaying** slot content and claims nothing permanent. PR #121 requires a nonlinear host and a reported relaxation time. PR #114 requires reads of raw outputs and first gradients before claiming a hidden channel. Exact reversible momentum SGD is Maclaurin et al. [1502.03492](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03492), not a candidate here.

## Shared contract for every card

- **Hosts.** (i) A fixed-feature linear readout, where slot effects should be pure linear response (negative control). (ii) A width-64 two-layer ReLU MLP trained with Adam and with SGD plus momentum. (iii) The corrected-world toy proposed in PR #130 and PR #132, which has world facts, a procedure and a later belief correction. No task IDs at inference.
- **Twins by swap.** Build heirs with identical `w` and crossed slot blocks `m`, `v`, `t`, giving a 2×2×2 factorial over sources. Exact output and first-gradient match then holds by construction. Distilled or matched twins (PR #123) are a second arm, not the main one.
- **Reads to beat.** Before calling slot content hidden, report: raw outputs (equal by construction), first gradients (equal), the one-update endpoint, and the linearized input–state–output prediction over K steps. The claim is the residual beyond these.
- **Mandatory arms.** Full reset; norm-matched first-update reset (2610.03940); reset `m` only; reset `v` only; keep slots but reset the clock `t`; stateless SGD at a tuned effective LR; and an **equal-byte stored-delta** arm, which stores what the slot would have written and applies it directly.
- **Retention.** For every card, report held-out accuracy on each old task and on untouched old tasks, the worst checkpoint, and fossil rate (old-world answers given after a correction), at matched new-task progress. Report the slot-effect half-life as a function of `β₁` and `β₂`.

## Idea cards

Novelty claims are hypotheses against the screen above. Each card names its nearest prior art.

### A. Establish what survives and for how long

1. **The velocity-register fossil assay (FI3 proper).** *Mechanism:* Correct the same stale belief in two heirs in opposite orders (fact first, then procedure; or the reverse) until outputs match. Cross their `(m, v, t)` onto shared weights and run identical next minibatches. Ask whether old-world answers follow the slots after the next unrelated write. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.20442 transplants a first moment carrying a trait from one source. This card uses a *correction-order* history, factorial `m`/`v`/`t` crossing, and fossil *relapse* as the outcome. *Cheapest first test:* the corrected-world toy in a ReLU MLP with Adam, 8 seeds, counterbalanced order, K ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64}. *Old-task retention:* procedure accuracy and untouched-task accuracy at each K, plus fossil rate per slot source.

2. **The second-moment fossil.** *Mechanism:* At a task switch, `v` still encodes which coordinates the old task drove hard. Over about 1/(1−β₂) steps it shrinks new writes there, which may protect the old task. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2610.03940 and 2608.20442 study `m`; 2608.15824 studies the progress cost of `v`. Nobody measures the retention *benefit* of an inherited `v`. *Cheapest first test:* A→B on a ReLU MLP. At the switch, swap in `v` from an A-trained or a B-trained twin, with `m=0` and the same `w`. Sweep β₂ ∈ {0.99, 0.999, 0.9999}. *Old-task retention:* A accuracy after B at matched B loss. Kill if a tuned global LR schedule reproduces it.

3. **The readability ladder.** *Mechanism:* Find where slot history first becomes visible: outputs, then first gradient, then one update, then the K-step linearized response, then the true K-step response. The hidden part is the gap between the last two. *Novelty vs nearest:* the 2608.19762 input–state–output operator predicts loss response. This card turns it into a gate for every "hidden belief" claim, scored on old-task *decisions*. *Cheapest first test:* linear host (gap should be 0) against the ReLU host. Report the gap as a function of K and step size. *Old-task retention:* the gap in old-task accuracy change, not only in loss.

4. **A per-block half-life map.** *Mechanism:* Measure how long a slot swap keeps moving old-task outputs, per layer and per slot, against β₁, β₂, LR and weight decay. *Novelty vs nearest:* relaxation time is required by PR #121, but nobody has reported it for slot-carried old content. *Cheapest first test:* a geometric sequence of K after the swap, 8 seeds, fitting an exponential plus an offset. A nonzero offset is the interesting result. *Old-task retention:* the last K at which the swap changes worst-task accuracy beyond the same-source swap floor.

5. **The step counter is state too.** *Mechanism:* Adam's bias correction divides by `1−β^t`. Resetting `m` and `v` but keeping `t` (or the reverse) changes early step sizes, and an LR-schedule position carries history the same way. *Novelty vs nearest:* reset studies (2306.17833) rarely separate the clock. *Cheapest first test:* the 2×2 grid of slots × clock reset at a switch, with a tuned warmup as the rival. *Old-task retention:* A loss over the first 1/(1−β₂) steps of B. Kill if one warmup schedule explains every cell.

### B. Use slot content for retention

6. **Pinned second moment as a free importance map.** *Mechanism:* At a task boundary, freeze or slow the decay of `v` on coordinates where it is large (per-coordinate β₂ → 1) so the old task's step-size shield does not expire. Release it when B's own `v` exceeds it. *Novelty vs nearest:* EWC or Fisher-from-Adam regularizers add a penalty. This changes no loss, costs no extra estimate, and has a declared expiry. *Cheapest first test:* five-task permuted-feature MLP stream; compare reset `v`, ordinary `v`, pinned `v`, and EWC with a diagonal Fisher of equal bytes, each tuned. *Old-task retention:* mean and worst old accuracy at matched final B accuracy. Kill if EWC ties at equal tuning.

7. **Age-resolved history audit at the switch.** *Mechanism:* 2610.03940 finds that old new-task gradients drive leakage. Keep `m` but project out the components that reduce old-answer mass on a small anchor set, rather than resetting all of it. *Novelty vs nearest:* that paper uses global norm-matched resets. This card resets only the harmful directions. *Cheapest first test:* ReLU MLP A→B with 32 A anchors. Compare full reset, norm-matched reset, selective projection, and A-GEM with the same anchors. *Old-task retention:* A accuracy and answer mass, plus untouched-task accuracy. Kill if A-GEM ties.

8. **Per-source slot bank for fast return.** *Mechanism:* Keep one `(m, v)` per training data source, a tag available at train time only. When a source stops, its slots freeze as a fossil. When the source returns, restoring them gives an immediately correct preconditioner and direction. *Novelty vs nearest:* DualOptim+ keeps separate forget/retain states for unlearning, and AdEMAMix mixes timescales. This card treats the bank as a relearning accelerator. *Cheapest first test:* an A→B→A stream; compare relearning steps with the bank, with a fresh state, and with an equal-byte buffer of A examples. *Old-task retention:* steps to recover A, and B damage during the return.

9. **Slow momentum as implicit rehearsal.** *Mechanism:* A very slow EMA (β ≈ 0.9999) still points along A's gradient for thousands of steps into B. That acts as decaying replay of the old *gradient* with no old data. *Novelty vs nearest:* AdEMAMix (2611.29588) studies slow EMAs for pretraining speed. A-GEM and OGD store gradients explicitly, and are the equal-byte rivals. *Cheapest first test:* the same stream with and without a slow EMA term, at matched B speed. *Old-task retention:* A accuracy against A-GEM using a gradient budget the same size as one slow buffer. Likely an improvement, not a primitive. Kill if A-GEM ties.

10. **Second-moment armor before an announced shock.** *Mechanism:* Before a known risky update (a fine-tune or an RL phase), add a synthetic inflation to `v` on old-critical coordinates. It decays on its own at rate β₂, so protection is temporary and costs no parameters. *Novelty vs nearest:* escrow in PR #113 stores self-labels, and trust regions add penalties. This card writes protection into the preconditioner. *Cheapest first test:* announced A→B shock in a ReLU MLP; compare armor, EWC and PR #113 escrow at equal tuning. *Old-task retention:* A accuracy during and after the decay window, at matched B progress.

### C. Control, launder or erase fossils

11. **Slot laundering after a belief correction.** *Mechanism:* After a stale fact is corrected, pre-correction momentum and variance can still push toward the old world. Decay `m` faster on coordinates where it disagrees in sign with the correction gradient, and let the rest carry on. *Novelty vs nearest:* PR #132 handles fossils in weights; FI3 only detects them. This card controls them in slot space. *Cheapest first test:* the corrected-world toy with a correction and then unrelated training; compare laundering, full reset, extra correction replay, and output gating. *Old-task retention:* fossil rate after the next 64 steps, and procedure accuracy.

12. **Unlearning that also cleans the optimizer.** *Mechanism:* A checkpoint can pass a weight-only unlearning audit while its slots still hold the forgotten source's gradients, so ordinary continued training brings the source back. *Novelty vs nearest:* DualOptim+ and state-alignment unlearning (2605.17590) align states at the end. This card audits *resurgence under continued training*. *Cheapest first test:* forget one class in an MLP by gradient ascent, then continue on retain data with kept, reset and laundered slots; measure the forget-class accuracy curve. *Old-task retention:* retain-class accuracy, which must not fall.

13. **Covariant slots under function-preserving edits.** *Mechanism:* Merging, permutation alignment, rebalancing or widening changes the coordinates, so slots must be transformed to match (for example by permuting with the weights). Otherwise they inject pseudo-history. *Novelty vs nearest:* Fisher merging (2111.09832) averages importance once. PR #109 notes the gauge issue but offers no transform. *Cheapest first test:* permute-align two MLPs, merge, then continue training with merged-transformed `v`, averaged-untransformed `v`, or reset `v`. *Old-task retention:* both parents' tasks after K steps of continued training.

14. **Quantized optimizer state as a forgetting knob.** *Mechanism:* 8-bit or compressed Adam states (used in practice) erase small slot components. That may delete fossils, or may delete protective `v`. *Novelty vs nearest:* guarded compression (2611.00589) targets optimization error, not retention. *Cheapest first test:* quantize `m` and `v` to {32, 8, 4, 2} bits at the task switch only, or throughout. *Old-task retention:* A accuracy and fossil rate against bit width, at matched B progress.

15. **A fossil check for inherited checkpoints.** *Mechanism:* A resumed checkpoint often ships with its optimizer state. Before continuing, run a matched fork with the shipped slots and one with reset slots on the first K batches, and flag old-world answers that reappear only with the shipped slots. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2610.22650 measures the cost of switching optimizers. PR #130 handles inheritance without slots. *Cheapest first test:* a PR #130 ancestor with stale facts; release both forks and score fossil rate. *Old-task retention:* skill accuracy in both forks, and how often the check flags a fork with zero real fossils (false positives).

### D. Read and forecast

16. **Who owns the denominator.** *Mechanism:* Split `v` into per-task, age-resolved contributions (the second-moment version of 2610.03940's history-by-age analysis) to measure how much of today's step-size geometry an old task set. *Novelty vs nearest:* only the `m` decomposition exists. *Cheapest first test:* in a three-task stream, log per-task squared-gradient contributions and predict each old task's retention from its current share of `v`. *Old-task retention:* rank correlation between share and later retention, against a weight-distance predictor and the input–state–output operator.

17. **Slot-only forgetting forecast.** *Mechanism:* Predict the next K-step old-task loss from `(m, v)` projected onto a stored low-rank old-gradient sketch. The sketch is the declared bridge from slots to old data. *Novelty vs nearest:* GPM stores the same basis to project gradients; this card uses it to *forecast*, and to choose when to reset. *Cheapest first test:* forecast in a ReLU stream against GPM-overlap and input–state–output predictors. *Old-task retention:* missed floor crossings at equal alarm rate.

18. **The order commutator in slot space.** *Mechanism:* Two corrections applied as AB and BA can end with matched weights but different slots. Define the slot residue (the difference in `m, v` at the cut) and test whether its projection on the old-gradient sketch predicts which belief relapses. *Novelty vs nearest:* commutator memory (2609.34348) and the shuffle-order paper (2606.29554) look at weights or loss noise. *Cheapest first test:* enumerate 16 correction pairs in the corrected-world toy. *Old-task retention:* relapse prediction accuracy on held-out pairs, against the weight-commutator predictor.

### E. Ambitious: deliberate writes into optimizer state

19. **Delayed-release edits.** *Mechanism:* Write a correction into `m` rather than `w`. It changes nothing at the cut and is released over the next steps, *added to* whatever gradients arrive. A fixed stored delta cannot adapt to the intervening data; a released slot write is mixed into live updates and preconditioned by the current `v`. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.20442 shows this happens by accident for traits; nobody has engineered it as an edit primitive. *Cheapest first test:* install a fact edit as `m`-only versus as an immediate `w` delta, versus a stored delta applied after a shock. Put a damaging shock in between. *Old-task retention:* edit success after the shock and untouched-task accuracy. Kill if the stored-delta arm ties.

20. **A sleeping repair for a known future shock.** *Mechanism:* Pre-load `m` with the negative of the predicted old-task damage direction of an announced update, so that the repair is released while the damage happens. *Novelty vs nearest:* PR #113 escrow replays after the shock. This card co-schedules the repair with the shock inside the optimizer. *Cheapest first test:* A then an announced B fine-tune in a ReLU MLP. Estimate the damage direction on a short B probe and compare with escrow and with post-hoc replay at equal bytes and compute. *Old-task retention:* A accuracy at the worst checkpoint, not just at the end.

21. **A meta-learned optimizer whose state is explicitly CL memory.** *Mechanism:* Train a small learned optimizer, with per-parameter state beyond `m` and `v`, on synthetic task streams to minimize old-task loss at matched new-task progress. Then read what its state encodes. *Novelty vs nearest:* learned optimizers (VeLO-style) target speed. This card targets retention and inspects the learned state as a memory. *Cheapest first test:* meta-train on 2-task linear-to-ReLU streams on CPU and test on held-out 5-task streams. *Old-task retention:* mean and worst old accuracy against Adam, Adam with a reset, and EWC. Moonshot: the risk is that it learns EWC.

22. **Slot budget accounting: when is the optimizer cheaper memory than replay?** *Mechanism:* Adam already stores 2× the parameter bytes in slots. Treat that as a memory budget and ask which use of those bytes (pinned `v`, a per-source bank, slow momentum, or the same bytes spent on replay examples or a low-rank adapter) best preserves old tasks. *Novelty vs nearest:* none of the above compares slot bytes with replay bytes. *Cheapest first test:* take the winners of cards 6, 8 and 9 at equal bytes against reservoir replay in the five-task stream. *Old-task retention:* accuracy-per-byte frontier. Kill the slot route if replay dominates everywhere.

## Deeper questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Does an inherited second moment act as a slowly expiring old-task shield, and can pinning it beat EWC at matched plasticity?** (2, 6, 10, 16.) This is the least-covered carrier: prior work studies `m`. Start with card 2's swap at three β₂ values. Kill the line if a tuned global LR schedule or equal-byte EWC matches pinned `v` on worst old accuracy at equal B progress.
2. **Do slots carry correction-order fossils that make a corrected belief relapse after the next unrelated write, beyond the one-update and linearized reads?** (1, 3, 11, 15, 18.) This is FI3 itself. 2608.20442 makes a positive likely, so the bar is the readability-ladder residual and relapse of *beliefs*. If the linearized operator explains the effect, report a diagnostic, not a hidden channel.
3. **Can a deliberate `m`-only write beat storing and applying the same delta?** (19, 20.) The primitive would be an edit that adapts to intervening data because it is released through live updates. The equal-byte stored-delta arm is the whole test.
4. **What must forgetting, unlearning and merging do to optimizer state to stay done?** (12, 13, 14.) Measure resurgence under continued training with kept, reset, laundered and transformed slots. A clean negative ("always reset or transform slots") is still a useful rule for every other node that resumes training.
