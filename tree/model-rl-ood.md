# Model → OOD tasks → RL and on-policy self-distillation

**Working diagnosis:** The bottleneck is not simply that RL has too few labels. Its rollouts and rewards may never **distinguish the decision rule that transfers from the shortcut that wins now**. Self-distillation observes more about the same visited prefixes, but its teacher's likelihood change is not the causal effect of taking an action. It can sharpen the same shortcut faster. The proposed unit of learning is a **verified fork**: two concrete alternatives at the same decision, assessed on variations that break the shortcut. Use that unit to decide both what to explore and what should be written into weights.

## Where the failure comes from

Separate three failures; they require different remedies.

| Failure | What goes wrong | Intervention that separates it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exposure:** a useful action or prefix is effectively absent | For independent binary-reward rollouts with pass rate `p`, GRPO sees mixed rewards with probability `1 − p^G − (1 − p)^G` in a group of size `G`. A group with identical returns has no relative reward signal. When a particular strategy has probability `u`, even `N` rollouts see it at least once with probability at most `Nu`. Distillation along visited prefixes can change their continuations, but does not directly supervise arbitrary unvisited prefixes. | Force one legal strategy branch, then give the base model its normal suffix budget. If it now succeeds, distinguish access to the branch from competence after it. |
| **Identification:** source reward favors or cannot distinguish a shortcut | Two worlds can have the same complete source action–reward table and opposite target rankings. Neither more source rollouts nor denser labels derived only from observations identical across the worlds can identify which world applies. RL can generalize when the source distribution actually varies what would otherwise be a shortcut. | Cross the nuisance cue with the real rule while holding prompt templates and action availability fixed. Compare exposure-only rescue with exposure plus verifier-backed cue swaps. |
| **Credit:** the dense label answers the wrong question | A teacher scores what is plausible given a solution, critique or thinking mode. The verifier cares whether **changing the chosen action** changes expected success under the continuation policy. A source-valid action effect can still flip under shift. | At the same prefix, randomize semantic actions, complete each with the same policy, and compare signed teacher deltas to measured, within-prefix continuation effects. Repeat after the shift. |

There is a precise case where likelihood *does* mean something useful. If the teacher were exactly the original policy conditioned on its own verifier success, then Bayes gives `q(a given s and success) / π(a given s) = Qπ(s,a) / Vπ(s)` wherever the state value is positive and the action has support. Log likelihood ratios would rank actions by value in that setting. Conditioning a copy of the model on a reference solution or on feedback written after a particular rollout does not implement this posterior. Even the exact source posterior would not provide a target-world effect. The three bottlenecks are separable: perfect credit on the wrong source objective can still make OOD performance worse.

### Evidence and limits

| Evidence | What it supports—and what it does not |
| --- | --- |
| [Chu et al., *SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161) and [Cheng et al., *How RL Reshapes LLM Reasoning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04158) | Outcome RL transfers to unseen rules and visual domains in GeneralPoints/V-IRL. Cheng et al. find both gains and forgetting; Qwen3 math RL raises BoxNet Pass@1 from 3.83% to 9.88% and Pass@1024 from 34% to 51%, but shrinks high-budget AIME24 coverage despite a Pass@1 gain. Their selection-versus-execution model offers a mechanism, not proof that every transfer failure has the same cause. [Yue et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837) document the narrowing pattern across several families. Finite pass@k is coverage at a decoding budget, not a proof about literal support or what was learned from scratch. |
| [Zhao et al., OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734) and [Adhinarta et al., *Does Self-Distillation Improve Reasoning?*, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613) | The former reports higher math token efficiency. In the latter's reproduction, a Qwen3-8B thinking-on teacher on thinking-on evaluation averages 65.0 with the reference and 65.3 without it. Thinking-mode transfer is a real benefit but not evidence of extracting new credit from the solution. This is a particular implementation and not evidence that all feedback is useless. |
| [Hübotter et al., SDPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20802) and [Adhinarta et al., §§4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613) | SDPO reports reaching GRPO's final LiveCodeBench v6 score in 4× fewer generations, and separately reports 3× fewer attempts for single-question test-time discovery. The reproduction separates new cases for trained-on problems from entirely new problems: SDPO's coding gains carry to the former, not the latter, where GRPO generalizes better. It also reports OOD degradation in four of five science/tool domains with SDPO, while GRPO preserves performance. For Qwen3-8B, the training gradient's cosine with the held-out coding reward gradient is +0.121 for GRPO and −0.167 for SDPO (Table 4, pooled across four seeds). This alignment diagnostic does not establish what causes the final score. Reusing the same problem can be useful for test-time solving; it is not a training sample-efficiency result on unseen problems. |
| [Nguyen et al., *Privileged Likelihood Is Not Automatically Value*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09263) | In one AIME setup, the implemented two-sided teacher score separates correct from incorrect trajectories at AUC 0.505 (95% CI 0.483–0.524); five integration variants reach 24.2–33.9% Avg@4 vs. 64.2% for outcome-only GRPO. In a SciKnowEval Biology setting, the equivalent trajectory-level diagnostic reaches 0.813–0.924 AUC and SDPO improves held-out Avg@8. This supports checking alignment; trajectory AUC alone does not identify the credit for any individual action. |
| [Shenfeld et al., SDFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897), [Wang et al., *Denser ≠ Better*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01763), and [Wang et al., post-RL consolidation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05609) | Demonstration-conditioned self-distillation can improve skill acquisition and retention over SFT; feedback-conditioned SDPO can instead forget more than GRPO. The last paper studies **external-teacher OPD**, not OPSD: its students recover 96.04–102.73% of teacher ID scores but are 2.45–16.25% below the base on extrapolative OOD scores; addressing both teacher capability and coverage removes forgetting in their tested setting. An on-policy student prefix distribution by itself is not a transfer guarantee. |

### Exact CPU sanity check

No model fitting: two actions (shortcut and robust), one shared policy knob `p`, the probability of choosing shortcut. Robust succeeds with probability 0.75 in both worlds; shortcut succeeds with 0.95 in training and 0.05 after the shift. Increasing `p` from 0.50 to 0.881 raises source expected success from 85.00% to 92.62% and **lowers target success from 40.00% to 13.33%**. In a second target world, shortcut success could stay 0.95; the entire source table is unchanged. Here the RL gradient is exact and both actions are exposed: the sign reversal requires neither sampling noise nor verifier error. This establishes a counterexample, not the prevalence of this mechanism in LLMs.

## A hybrid worth testing, and what already exists

[RLSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03128) uses privileged likelihood to scale token updates while outcome advantages supply their sign; its reported comparison at 200 vs. 400 steps is not a result at equal total compute on structural shifts. [SDPG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04036) combines group-relative reward with full-vocabulary distillation as an auxiliary loss. [Sample routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02288), [influence calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14945) and [targeted agentic guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05266) already pursue selective combinations. “GRPO + OPSD” is not a new result.

**Verified-fork learning:** At a decision on a student rollout, use a hint-conditioned teacher to nominate one or more diverse alternate actions, not to grade them. Execute paired suffixes under the same frozen continuation policy; where possible, run the pair on a verifier-preserving nuisance swap too. Reuse the resulting estimates to learn a cheap action-value proxy. Require positive carried-forward value on the source variation meant to break the shortcut before treating the effect as transferable. Keep exploration and gradient estimation distinct: forced branches fit the proxy, or enter policy gradients only with valid sampling corrections; do not silently treat teacher-forced branches as on-policy. A teacher can improve coverage and predictive variance but cannot define the objective.

**One exact update option.** Let `z = ∇ log π(a given s)`, and let `c(s,a)` be a detached proxy fixed conditional on the state, fit from independent data or cross-fitted without the scored action/return. For on-policy suffix return `R`,

```text
g = (R − c(s,a)) z + E[a' ~ π(·|s)] [ c(s,a') ∇ log π(a'|s) ].
```

Its conditional expectation is `E[Rz given s]`; the added term cancels the expected subtracted term. Use exact sums in a small action grammar or an unbiased independent estimate; naive top-k renormalization, same-rollout hindsight, or mismatched continuation can invalidate the result. This is a known action-dependent control-variate construction (cf. [Q-Prop](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02247)), **not** a new estimator or a guarantee of lower variance or target transfer. The proposed novelty is how to acquire, validate and reuse causal branches, and use them to move beyond the source training support. Begin with enumerable choices, not a vocabulary-wide expectation for arbitrary spans.

For an exact variance sanity check, take three actions with probabilities `(0.6, 0.3, 0.1)`, Bernoulli reward means `(0.05, 0.75, 0.10)` and scalar score `z = v(a) − Eπ[v]` for logit tilt `v = (1, −1, 0)`. Enumerate each of the six (action, reward) pairs with mass `π_a Q_a^R (1 − Q_a)^(1 − R)`. All corrected means are −0.2745. Variance is 0.12705 for the optimal constant baseline, 0.10984 for the oracle action-mean proxy and 0.17261 for the wrong proxy `(0.95, 0.05, 0.10)`. Without the add-back, the oracle proxy gives zero mean and the wrong proxy gives −0.651. This verifies the cancellation and shows why a variance gate against a strong baseline matters; the oracle is not a result achievable by the teacher.

**Small first discriminating test.** In a generated symbolic DSL, cross rule, surface cue, and whether a useful branch is reachable on-policy. Train on selected combinations; seal new compositions and later rule reversals. Compare verifier-only GRPO, OPSD, RLSD, suffix-only RL with randomized forks (no teacher), and the proposed teacher-nominated forks with and without the corrected proxy. Include an exact action-value oracle and teacher controls with same-mode no hint, shuffled hint, and length/entropy-matched feedback. Measure action-effect sign agreement, verified recoveries from all-fail groups, ID and OOD pass@1/large-k at matched decode, plus old-regime retention and reacquisition on an A→B→A→C stream. Log gradient variance conditional on prefix and how often shortcuts are selected. Report runs-to-target against **both** verifier calls and total compute (including fork suffixes and teacher passes), on paired independent streams. Predeclare a meaningful target such as halving verifier calls at the same OOD score and retention, with no increase in total compute; if only the call curve improves, report the tradeoff rather than claiming a faster learner. Exact CPU evidence above does not establish that the hybrid clears this bar.

**Forward-only path.** Repeated A/B tests of weight edits on one question can estimate a local effect, not identify a transferable rule; adaptive reuse will also select noise. Backprop provides efficient local sensitivities when the loss is differentiable; paired forward scoring sees actual verifier outcomes and works on hard edits, but can scale poorly with effective search dimension. [Evolution Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864), [MeZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333), [structured zeroth-order tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16451), and [agent-authored weight-space edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382) already establish the broad ideas. Start with paired seeds across diverse forked questions, held-out untouched prompts, and a small candidate span. Test a larger model as a **proposer of edit hypotheses and predicted effects**: its benefit must beat identical edit syntax and budgets with random, activation-ranked and gradient-ranked proposals. Compare forward-only also against backprop through the **same** editable span. Do not scale to millions of passes before measuring the cost of locating one portable, scope-limited write.

## Raw ideas for the novelty reviewer

Unranked and unfiltered. “New object” is a candidate distinction, not a priority or prior-art verdict. The [ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/3a1db515868e814da7f71ad9bccd7322f8291188/reviews/idea-ledger.md) records failed pilots for weighted rare-prefix splitting against prompt upsampling, effect-class distillation against execution-reward replay, and static functional syndromes against simple sketches/replay. The [weight-space flood](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/39) already has a learned ISA, linker, ABI and transplant rejection; the [skill migration pilot](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/1) already tested saved counterfactual witnesses for recompilation. Here the distinction is branch intervention and composition, not another replay buffer, opcode proposal or checkpoint migration contract.

### Learn from experiments rather than traces

1. **Executable fork tapes.** Store a state-reset recipe, two action interventions, continuation policy/version and a sparse matrix of verifier deltas under nuisance changes. **New object:** a rerunnable local experiment with measured transport conditions, not a ranked pair of realized trajectories. **First test:** keep five tapes in a CPU puzzle DSL, change the decoder policy and compare held-out sign prediction with the same bytes of preference pairs and raw trajectories.
2. **Teacher as experimental designer.** Ask it to pick the branch pair that best separates two explicit causal models of why reward was earned; make it predict how each outcome changes the next query. **New operation:** a teacher proposes information, not an answer or a gradient proxy. **First test:** locate one faulty step in a generated six-step chain; count verifier calls against entropy, random bisection and direct teacher diagnosis.
3. **Verifier twins for identifiability.** Compile a prompt/verifier twin that preserves the intended solution program but inverts one accidental cue. Optimize for effects that survive the twin rather than average correctness on random paraphrases. **New object:** a generator for the distinguishing experiment between two concrete policies. **First test:** on a DSL with cue-colored operators, compare the same rollout budget spent on twins, domain randomization and diverse ordinary examples; seal a second nuisance generator.
4. **Fork-posterior compiler.** Convert sparse verified suffixes into a posterior over **classes of alternatives**; let a teacher amortize that posterior across isomorphic states and use the result only in a corrected estimator. **New object:** a reusable compiler from adjudicated branch tables, not token-likelihood reward shaping. **First test:** tabular grammar with exact values; compare bias and variance against an equally trained critic, state baseline and teacher log-ratio before measuring learning.
5. **Action transport maps.** From two environments, infer which branching actions should correspond under a rule-preserving transformation, including when there is no equivalent action. **New object:** an empirically testable map of interventions with a domain of definition, not alignment of output strings. **First test:** learn from two renamed DSLs and predict which action still works after a composition with an unseen type coercion; compare grammar-aware canonicalization.
6. **Equal-value gauge.** Within a verified near-tie, rescore teacher effects under permutations of wording, explanation length and reference choice. Route only sensitivities that survive these interventions into future experiments. **New object:** a measured nuisance subspace for teacher credit, rather than a scalar confidence gate. **First test:** inject a length-correlated but reward-neutral teacher score in a five-action simulator; compare with direct residualization on length and entropy.
7. **Policy-gradient circuit breakers.** Store pairs of alternative decisions whose return ordering flips only when a specific assumption fails. When a tiny set of probes detects the flip, stop using the old credit for that route and collect new branch returns. **New object:** falsifiable expiration conditions for credit, not ages or novelty scores for examples. **First test:** rotate one rule at a time in a three-rule stream; compare verifier calls to uniform critic refresh and a changepoint detector fed the same probes.
8. **Support escrow.** Reserve reusable *entry actions* for promising branches that never complete on-policy; retain their costed entry recipe but commit nothing to weights until a later observed regime verifies value. **New operation:** opening a named capability option and exercising it later with counterfactual evidence, not weighted replay of an already observed prefix. **First test:** two-hop router where the useful suffix becomes feasible at wave two; compare equally funded prompt upsampling, forced-prefix training and delayed escrow.
9. **Reversal-based credit depth.** Deliberately reverse one transition rule between matched subrollouts and estimate how far upstream action preferences change. **New object:** a measured dependency horizon that specifies which old credit must be invalidated after a local rule change. **First test:** two transition changes with the same aggregate failure rate but different depths; compare relearning cost with eligibility traces and fresh value training.
10. **Branch flow conservation test.** For an enumerable fork tree, estimates of expected return at a parent must agree with policy-weighted means of children. Use residual violations to locate where privileged predictions are inconsistent across depths; query the verifier at the smallest offending subtree. **New operation:** make teacher errors queryable through cross-depth closure, not a self-consistency vote over completed answers. **First test:** insert a teacher bias at one depth in a four-level tree; compare queries-to-localize against standard TD-error replay and independent uncertainty.

### Write, combine and inspect forward-only edits

11. **Weight-effect tomography.** Apply coded, paired combinations of candidate edits and measure the **vector** of signed decision effects on verified forks. Recover a sparse response map, then design the next perturbation to disambiguate it. **New object:** a locally reusable map from a write to its causal behavioral footprint, not a scalar finite-difference update. **First test:** estimate the maps of 32 candidate directions on a tiny learned router; compare held-out effect prediction and write selection with independent A/B, structured ES and gradient/Jacobian projections at matched compute. Include dense and nonadditive controls.
12. **Large model as diagnostic surgeon.** Show the model paired fork outcomes plus a shortlist of student activation interventions. Have it nominate an edit location, sign, predicted effect vector and one falsifying probe; run only the resulting hypotheses. **New operation:** score a proposer by causal localization and prediction under blind changes, rather than by best-of-many edited accuracy. **First test:** hide a single fault in one of eight blocks of a small router; compare top-k localization and verifier calls to activation ranking, oracle and the same proposer without fork evidence.
13. **Patch commutator calculus.** Treat adaptation writes as operations that may depend on current weights. Measure the difference between applying `A ∘ B` and `B ∘ A` on matched forks; learn a minimal correction only for the offending interaction. **New object:** a measurable noncommutativity signature predicting failures from unseen write orders. **First test:** three sequential low-rank edit constructors on a CPU network; predict failure on held-out triple orders from pair estimates against arithmetic merging, replay and simple pair overlap. Include constant additive writes, which must commute.
14. **Factorized write-and-query.** Split writes into a durable operation and a swappable dispatcher; probe the effects of swapping each component independently before choosing which to update. **New object:** counterfactual decomposition of a trained patch into transferable execution and context-specific routing, not another task-ID adapter. **First test:** train two rules under correlated routing cues; flip only the cue and compare the two swaps against oracle and learned routing of ordinary LoRAs.
15. **Mutant verifiers as write specifications.** Mutate one verifier predicate at a time; encode the desired write by an effect pattern that changes only the forks whose outcomes should be sensitive to the altered predicate. **New object:** an executable specification of edit scope by a family of counterfactual tests, instead of one output or one patch score. **First test:** in a toy symbolic evaluator, change composition but not base operations; compare collateral damage at equal coverage to a fixed unit-test pack, negative prompts and direct backprop.
16. **Latency-budgeted edit tournament.** Allocate cheap batched prefix evaluations across many weight candidates, then spend long suffixes only where a candidate's fork effect remains ambiguous; predeclare independent confirmation and audit early eliminations. **New operation:** multi-fidelity search over *where in the trajectory an edit's effect becomes identifiable*, not just early stopping by mean score. **First test:** budget 1,000 forward calls across known early-effect and delayed-effect edits; compare full-rollout successive halving and paired random search at equal retention.
17. **Behavioural write barriers.** Before a batch write, choose a small set of fork pairs whose signed effects span the suspected collateral changes. Accept, split or undo the batch based on an independent check of those contrasts. **New object:** a write barrier that localizes the offending interaction with dynamic causal probes, rather than static fact checks or accepting an update from average pass rate. **First test:** arrange two harmless individual updates whose joint application flips an old routing judgment; compare localization and false reject rate with fixed retention probes and full replay.
18. **Learn the experimenter across checkpoints.** Train a proposer from past write experiments (intervention, fork effect, cost), not successful deltas, to choose the next experiment on a new model. Account for proposer training in the amortization curve. **New object:** transferable *experimental policy* for finding an edit without transferring the edited coordinates or requiring a learned weight ISA. **First test:** hide checkpoint families and task compositions in a small router suite; compare cold and warm starts against the same archive used for nearest-neighbor edits, an activation heuristic and a random proposer.

## Ranked deeper branches

1. **Verified-fork algebra:** Can a small set of verifier-backed decision interventions predict the sign of effects on unseen compositions, and identify when it cannot? Seek a composable, falsifiable training object distinct from a demonstration or preference. Start with paired DSL rule/cue worlds in ideas 1–5; success first requires transfer beyond a strong grammar-aware replay baseline.
2. **Forward-only edit tomography:** Are behavior effects of weight edits compressible enough to recover reusable write operations from paired A/B rollouts, and can a larger model materially reduce the number of probes? Start with ideas 11–13, equal-span backprop/ES controls, and held-out write compositions; a better best-of-many score on a single prompt is not enough.
3. **Identifiability curriculum:** Can a learner generate the smallest experiment that separates a transferable rule from the most plausible shortcut before it writes to weights? Start with observationally equivalent training worlds and compare a learned experimenter with equally funded active-learning/randomization baselines (ideas 2, 3 and 18).
4. **Verifier-corrected distillation:** When does teacher-nominated exploration plus an independently fitted branch proxy beat strong reward-only branching in verifier calls *and* compute, without OOD credit bias? Start with exactly enumerated effects and move to natural-model rollouts only after both sign and variance checks (ideas 4, 6 and 10). The estimator alone is old; the research bet is a usable causal-data interface.
