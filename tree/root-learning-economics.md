# Root: Continual Learning as an Economy

Root node (depth 0) · 32 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** The interesting economy is not inside the optimizer. It is the market for what the learner can get taught next, by whom, under what rights, and with what obligations to people who already depend on it. A buyer's training today changes its demand tomorrow. A supplier can anticipate that and withhold a general lesson, invest in a proprietary sequel, or leave the market. A replay selector cannot optimize over examples that nobody has an incentive or permission to produce. The primitives below change the game that produces the stream rather than the score assigned to a fixed stream.

In a modular host, a language module and a mapping module could each have an order book for the examples they can use, post a joint order for paired labels, and accept different permissions on the same corpus. The annotator decides whether to collect that paired lot at all; a separate customer may hold a prior claim on the same supplier's output. Clearing the order changes the training inputs available to both modules even if their optimizers, architecture, and inference stay untouched.

## What this root is not

The [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md) requires a primitive (new object or operation), not an improvement by relabeling, and a retention check in every first test. The [flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md) already reviewed the [economics flood (#45)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/45) and [internal skill markets (#124)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/124). In particular, #124's internal capacity auctions reduce to an equally informed scheduler unless an actual constraint binds. This root does not resell rank, interference, plasticity, replay slots, or internal service reservations. It also does not propose optimizer-state carriers, loss meters, observability gates, weightless replacements, test-time writes, or fleets exchanging skills. Here “agents” are sellers, customers, or trustees with private objectives; the deployed learner can be a single model. Modules negotiate only where their choices affect real counterparties or enforceable permissions.

**Literature check.** The comparisons below use publisher abstracts, arXiv abstracts and, where relevant, paper text for the cited mechanism. The comparison is to the closest mechanism found, not a claim that no one has proposed the composition. Procurement already exists ([Agarwal et al., 1805.08125](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08125)); so do online label markets ([QueryMarket, 2606.17805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17805)), menus for buyers with private training data ([Selling Training Data, 2611.29971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29971)), and CL replay based on data valuation ([ASER, 2009.00093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00093)). Buying labels, pricing a dataset, or choosing high-value replay is not a primitive here.

**Shared first-test protocol.** Start with exact enumeration of the smallest game that could separate the mechanisms, followed in the same toy by an ordinary CPU tabular learner or tiny classifier updated on the acquired stream. Where licenses matter, use exact refitting from admitted history, charge retained data and refit work to all arms, and do not claim general unlearning or legal enforcement. Fix the stream of opportunities and private types across arms, not realized deliveries: sellers may best-respond to the rules. Commit offers before costs or shocks are revealed when specified. Compare the named control and a central buyer with the same information, rights, transfer tools and commitment power; also report an omniscient optimum as a bound, not a competitor. Match available funds, label opportunities, compute and storage, including side payments and unused reservations; compare new learning at matched old performance as well as old retention at matched new utility. Report seller participation and realized costs, not just the buyer's score. If a simpler mechanism or the same-powers central buyer implements the same allocation and incentives at equal cost, do not claim a new learning algorithm.

**Old-task check for all cards.** Pretrain on at least two ordinary tasks, one rare, then introduce a new task. Use a fixed held-out mix of the old tasks through normal prediction, without IDs or privileged routing, before the change, throughout the transition, and after learning and settlement. Report per-task retention and worst drop; predeclare a floor relative to the no-new-learning arm. Extra cohort-specific checks below supplement this mix. Test labels stay outside the market. No GPU is needed for any proposed test.

## A · Get a different stream, not a better score on the same stream

### LE01 · Buy the lesson that ends the supplier's monopoly
- **Mechanism:** Offer the incumbent a one-time buyout if a general lesson makes subsequent training possible from interchangeable sellers. The incumbent can choose that curriculum or keep selling narrow installments. The new transaction buys the disappearance of future dependency, not an example or a forecast.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Iterative Machine Teaching (1705.10470)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10470) chooses an efficient teaching sequence for a passive student; [Patent Buyouts](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/113/4/1137/1916997) buys out exclusion rights to disseminate inventions. Neither comparison by itself pays a teacher for rendering its *future training input* substitutable. It may be only a buyout with another success condition.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a two-round classification game: one seller can teach a latent rule at private cost or sell two cheaper exceptions. Name a range of continuation rents where a buyout induces the rule but a same-spend per-label offer does not; use a static prepaid curriculum quote as the stronger control.
- **Old-task retention:** Include old exceptions that the new rule would otherwise overwrite; score them after switching to the cheaper supplier, not just when the buyout triggers.

### LE02 · Pay for a curriculum a rival can continue
- **Mechanism:** Contract for two independent supply stages. The first teacher can charge a milestone fee only if a second teacher, chosen from a committed pool after delivery, can extend the curriculum without buying a seller-specific intermediate label convention. This is an open completion clause, not a module handoff.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Sequential Machine Teaching (1810.06175)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06175) optimizes an example sequence to reach a target; [Installed Base and Compatibility](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/installed-base-compatibility) analyzes switching and installed-base incentives. Test whether contracting on extendibility changes the first curriculum under private convention costs, beyond just prescribing an open format.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two teachers pick public or proprietary token conventions for a finite grammar. Randomly draw the stage-two supplier; compare a format mandate and a fixed completion fee. Reject if open completion does not improve entrant completion at equal price.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old grammar productions, including ones specific to the initial dialect, after the entrant supplies the extension.

### LE03 · Auction the right to be second
- **Mechanism:** Before a first provider's data type is revealed, other sellers quote prices for contingent follow-up teaching jobs. Each winner commits and can invest up front to deliver only if its specific branch occurs. The object purchased is a branch of a supply policy.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Contracts for Acquiring Information (2103.03911)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03911) studies incentives to produce costly information; [QueryMarket (2606.17805)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17805) buys arriving labels online. The proposed contrast is paying a different supplier to prepare an input that is a complement only on a branch, not paying to inspect an already available example.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two possible first feature views, two experts with private setup costs, and an XOR target. Compare immediate quote, auction after disclosure, and unconditional pre-order. Remove setup lead time as a negative case; the contract should then lose its advantage.
- **Old-task retention:** Recheck single-view old predictions when the second view arrives and on old cases where the new XOR relation is absent.

### LE04 · Buy the rejected examples as a separate lot
- **Mechanism:** A producer doing costly screening cannot package accepted cases alone if it takes a paid offer from a distinct buyer for the rejected stratum. A second, optional agreement buys that stratum before selection, giving the producer a reason not to discard informative rejects.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Optimum Statistical Estimation with Strategic Data Sources (1408.2539)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2539) pays for costly data quality; [Learning In Reverse Causal Strategic Environments (2404.13240)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13240) studies samples that respond strategically to a learner. The candidate object is a secondary market for the by-product of selection; not another score for where to query.
- **Cheapest first test:** A distributor chooses a threshold that creates two labeled strata and pays per retained stratum. Compare buying both in a single lot and a random-label quota with equal prices. Look for a case where heterogeneous downstream buyers keep the rejected stratum available when the quota does not.
- **Old-task retention:** Test old near-threshold cases and the normal old mix after teaching from the new stratum; reject a gain due to shifting errors onto old accepted cases.

### LE05 · Separate mobilization from delivery
- **Mechanism:** A buyer prepays a transferable supplier setup reservation, then separately chooses whether to commission a lesson. If it exits, another buyer can take the reservation without receiving any of the first buyer's data. Reassignment gives a small buyer a credible exit without asking small suppliers to eat full setup costs.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adverse Selection and Renegotiation in Procurement](https://doi.org/10.2307/2298088) studies time consistency of two-period procurement; [Selling Data to an Agent with Endogenous Information (2103.05788)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05788) lets a buyer acquire more information. The candidate transfer separates mobilization from already sold information and preserves the ability to produce a *different* training lot.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two buyers with different domains, one annotation shop and a cancellation shock. Compare fixed setup/no resale and cancellable per-label contracts. Count qualified sellers entering and the fraction of useful reservations actually reassigned, including a no-second-buyer case.
- **Old-task retention:** After the shock and any reassignment, evaluate both learners on their old dialects; include the buyer whose planned supplement never arrived.

### LE06 · Give a supplier a claim on the data a lesson makes possible
- **Mechanism:** Pay partly with a bounded, consented right to collect a new class of examples that becomes available only when the taught action is competent. For example, teaching one navigation routine enables the environment to reach a new region where the teacher may sample a specified number of novel tasks. This purchases effort with a real downstream production input, not a token.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Bayesian Exploration (1602.07570)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07570) uses recommendations to induce exploration by agents who produce and consume information. Here the supplier trains a persistent capability and values the *right to collect* from states that the resulting policy reaches; recommendations do not transfer that right.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two-room tabular MDP with a privately costly teaching demonstration and a second-room sampling permit. Compare a cash-equivalent stipend and nontransferable learner-only access. Sweep seller value for different room types; reject if grants never change supply net of collection costs.
- **Old-task retention:** Re-run old navigation starts and rewards after training on the newly collected examples, including the old rare goal in the first room.

## B · Trade particular rights to learn and keep

### LE07 · Return the use right, not the record
- **Mechanism:** Make a training license surrenderable at a posted buyback price. A buyer can accept a substitute delivery and surrender the licensed input; in the toy it must refit without it before receiving the buyback. The original seller sets this price ahead of competition, so it changes the entry cost for substitutes.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Price of Ignorance (2604.11511)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11511) quotes for retaining data against private deletion preferences; [SISA (1912.03817)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817) limits influence to lower removal cost. The buyback is a buyer-exercised exit term that can induce entry; neither a retention offer to data subjects nor a removal algorithm.
- **Cheapest first test:** One incumbent, a rival with private entry cost, and a small ridge classifier with an exact refit. Compare buyback with price discount of equal expected cost and nonrefundable license. Reject if entry gains vanish when refit cost is included.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate old boundary cases before surrender and after refitting on the rival's records, including a rare class missing from the replacement.

### LE08 · Sell separate inheritance, replay and teaching rights
- **Mechanism:** Quote independently for keeping a trained version in service, reusing the original example in later updates, and authorizing it as training input for a successor. A buyer can keep serving the ordinary old use while switching markets for the other two verbs. These are simulated permissions, not a claim that model derivatives are legally separable everywhere.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Purpose-based Access Policy on Provenance and Data Algebra (1912.00445)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00445) encodes allowed purposes; [Economics of AI Training Data (2510.24990)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24990) covers licensing and information asymmetry. The proposed increment is an executable, separately priced set of verbs across a CL transition. The rights themselves are not novel legal concepts.
- **Cheapest first test:** A factorized predictor with old and new label maps and two suppliers with different retention and teaching preferences. Enumerate the menus and compare a flat license with tagged but unpriced verbs. Kill if bundling achieves the same feasible trades and old/new results.
- **Old-task retention:** Exercise serve-only terms during a new training round; test old outputs together with the new task. Do not count keeping an unused old checkpoint as retention.

### LE09 · Make a portability grant run to the buyer's successor
- **Mechanism:** Buy a right whose beneficiary is the next buyer selected under a precommitted rule, not the commissioning buyer. The incumbent buyer cannot veto a lawful successor's use, and ordinary future purchases are still negotiated separately.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Data Sharing Markets (2107.08630)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08630) studies how data can be exchanged for prediction tasks; [Selling Training Data (2611.29971)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29971) offers supplements to the current buyer. The test is whether third-party beneficiary rights change investment in *portable data collection* relative to an equivalent rebate promised to the current buyer.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two generations of buyers, one supplier choosing narrow or transferable labeling, random successor type. Compare a grant to the successor, a prepaid license controlled by the first buyer, and open release; reject if all feasible purchase paths coincide.
- **Old-task retention:** Run old tasks at the successor after its first independent update, including classes the outgoing buyer found profitable but the successor does not.

### LE10 · Replace a restrictive source by independently buying the same teaching input
- **Mechanism:** A recipe can be regenerated under new terms if parties reacquire its required inputs independently and refit. A market clears the least-cost *lawful supply chain* for reproducing the lesson; copying or relabeling the same artifact does not change the terms.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Actionable Data Governance Rules (1911.08439)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08439) propagates governance rules through dataflows; [Certified Data Removal (1911.03030)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03030) formalizes removal relative to retraining. Neither of these tasks prices procurement of a new, permission-compatible chain of complements. The candidate is source substitution under independent sale, not provenance tracking or a deletion proof.
- **Cheapest first test:** A two-input synthetic classifier with one withdrawn license, a legal expensive one-for-one replacement and two cheap alternative inputs that only work together. Compare source-by-source replacement with constrained whole-chain shopping; include exact search as a no-advantage control.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old joint-input cases and single-input defaults on the actually refit learner, including a region where the cheaper lawful chain lacks support.

### LE11 · Let complementary suppliers issue a one-time joint sublicense
- **Mechanism:** A downstream teacher can resell an input derived from separately licensed complements only if the contributing owners clear a joint sublicense. The owners quote shares of the resale, with a cap fixed before their investments; none can sell away another's consent.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Informational Substitutes (1703.08636)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08636) formalizes signal complementarity; [On Supermodular Contracts (2308.07473)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07473) studies complementary effort. The candidate is a joint right to a *future sale* that gives producers reason to supply complements before a new learner enters, not splitting rewards over an already formed training coalition.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two sellers each supply a bit of a parity curriculum and a buyer enters with known probability. Compare capped joint shares, uncoordinated veto prices and a prepaid bundle. Reject if anticipated resale does not increase production at the same total cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old parity and single-bit tasks before and after the learner buys and trains on the resold curriculum, including cases outside the deal.

### LE12 · Sell permission to draw an example separately from permission to learn it
- **Mechanism:** A field operator charges a fixed draw fee; a subject, after seeing the draw context but not the buyer's model, can separately permit or decline training at a preposted price. A refusal leaves the learner with no training payload. The draw fee funds collection even from populations with mixed participation.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Buying Private Data without Verification (1404.6003)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.6003) incentivizes participation and truthful reports for a survey; [QueryMarket (2606.17805)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17805) makes sequential label-purchase decisions. The innovation to test is separating two sellers' rights and cashflows without buying rejected material or assuming the subject's preferences were known at draw time.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two populations with different private consent costs and one sampler with a fixed mobilization cost. Compare one-stage posted price and opt-in sampling; measure number and diversity of permitted teaching records, not just number drawn.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate both populations' old tasks regardless of who opted in, especially if the separate draw fee changes the training mix.

## C · Prices for a learner that changes its own demand

### LE13 · Price paths, not just datasets
- **Mechanism:** Cap the bulk quote by the price of the cheapest *permitted sequence* of incremental purchases that achieves the same declared learning state and rights. In a finite teaching graph, compute this cap from the buyer's starting history, not just set containment. A path confers no rights missing on any edge.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Query-Based Data Pricing](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2213556.2213582) prices queries by what views determine; [Cooperative Mechanics of Experience (2404.03084)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03084) values ordered experiences. The candidate pricing object is equivalence under the learner's actual training and usage history, not derivability of a query or credit for an ordering. It collapses to ordinary query pricing if order does not matter.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate histories of a finite-state classifier where lesson order matters and one route is licensable only for a narrow use. Compare set-based prices and shortest-path pricing that ignores permissions. Look for a lawful two-purchase arbitrage missed by the first, and a spurious arbitrage flagged by the second.
- **Old-task retention:** Require that supposedly equivalent paths also agree on old tasks, including a case lost by only one training order. Score in the normal learner, not by comparing prices alone.

### LE14 · Guarantee that teaching fast will not cut the teacher's pay
- **Mechanism:** Commit a sequence of future posted prices which cannot be reduced using the buyer's privately learned progress during the first contract. An independent buying window can still use exogenous shifts. The commitment removes a seller's reason to slow early teaching to protect its next quote.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Learning Prices for Repeated Auctions with Strategic Buyers (1311.6838)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6838) accounts for buyers gaming future posted prices; [Adverse Selection and Renegotiation in Procurement](https://doi.org/10.2307/2298088) addresses the procurement ratchet. The CL case to isolate is strategic distortion of *which lesson* is supplied before the learner's own progress changes its willingness to pay, not strategic bids alone.
- **Cheapest first test:** A seller chooses a productive or cosmetic exercise; an adaptive buyer resets its quote after round one. Compare a binding price path with delayed update of the same pricing rule and a one-shot completion contract. Identify a cost region where the binding path changes lesson choice.
- **Old-task retention:** At matched new progress, compare old tasks after round two, including an old task that the cosmetic exercise rehearses more than the productive one.

### LE15 · Let a buyer bid for who else may train
- **Mechanism:** Sell two lawful bundles for the same new data: nonexclusive learning and exclusivity with a specified counterparty set and sunset. Existing users' resupply rights are carved out of both. The difference in prices exposes a buyer's willingness to pay for exclusion separately from instructional value.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Selling Information in Competitive Environments (2202.08780)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08780) already models information sales with downstream competition; the candidate is a carve-out for supplying previously learned tasks while competitors contend for the next teaching input. No novelty claim for selling exclusivity.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two competing buyers and one seller holding a new corpus with an old-dialect component. Compare no exclusivity and time-limited full exclusivity at equal subsidy. Check if the contract preserves new data production without eliminating old resupply.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both buyers on the old dialect through the exclusivity period and after further training; compare against old tasks with no dedicated carve-out.

### LE16 · Charge the buyer for withdrawing the last outside supplier
- **Mechanism:** If an exclusive buyer takes a contract that makes an independent teacher exit a previously open training market, require an upfront payment tied to a binding counteroffer that would keep supply open. The revenue funds the real outside offer, not an internal loss or replay quota.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Equilibrium of Data Markets with Externality (2302.08012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08012) models buyers affecting one another's payoffs via purchases; [Too Much Data](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26296) models privacy spillovers from correlated disclosures. The candidate externality is a removed *production option*, even when the records purchased are harmless and nonrival.
- **Cheapest first test:** One teacher with fixed operating costs and two buyers; a big buyer can lock the teacher up or sponsor a separate supply shift. Compare an ad valorem exclusivity tax, an exclusivity ban and a real keep-open counteroffer. Include an entrant that exaggerates its costs.
- **Old-task retention:** Force the small buyer to train on a new domain during the period of threatened exit, then score its still-needed old specialty and the large buyer's original tasks.

### LE17 · Finance a teaching lot that is worthless half-built
- **Mechanism:** Require conditional subscriptions to a public teaching lot whose complements must be collected together. Refund everyone if the full set fails to clear, and pay a small failure bonus funded by the organizer. In the positive case, subscribers receive learning rights to the whole lot.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dominant Assurance Contracts](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1004957109535) already address provision of a threshold public good; [Informational Substitutes (1703.08636)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08636) covers informational complements. The candidate CL object is a threshold over *distinct teaching inputs* and their future use rights, not a dollar threshold for a fixed good. It may still be an assurance contract on a larger outcome space.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three subscribers, two independently costly complementary subcorpora, and private buyer values. Compare a scalar crowdfunding threshold and a fully funded advance purchase. Count completed, useful lots and losses from bonus farming.
- **Old-task retention:** Train only upon delivery; test old tasks after both successful and failed campaigns, including a task whose routine data purchases were displaced by the subscription.

### LE18 · Post the subsidy needed to make a dormant market clear
- **Mechanism:** Quote one price to a new buyer and another to a rare-domain supplier, who can independently accept or decline without revealing private types. The trade goes through only if a future beneficiary prepays the difference when the seller's quote is higher. The third party buys continued access to the supplier, not the data sold to the first buyer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Myerson–Satterthwaite's bilateral trade result](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1983/efficient-mechanisms-for-bilateral-trading/) identifies the efficiency limits of incentive-compatible, individually rational bilateral trade without outside subsidies in its setting. This card asks whether a third party's *future benefit from preserved supply* can fund the gap, not whether CL evades the theorem.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate independent buyer values and seller costs in a two-round renewal game with one future customer. Compare posted spread, a flat subsidy and the same rule funded by the future buyer. Reject if fees exhaust future surplus or the extra purchases merely crowd out ordinary instruction.
- **Old-task retention:** Include the future customer's old specialty and current buyers' ordinary old tasks after new purchases and subsidy payments.

## D · Change the structure of the supply market

### LE19 · Insure against losing a production process, not against a loss spike
- **Mechanism:** Pay a second supplier to keep an independently sourced process operational and occasionally commission small real training lots so it stays commercially viable. Activation is a supply interruption, not model behavior. Both suppliers must disclose shared upstream dependencies in the toy.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Tomlin's disruption-risk model](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1060.0515) compares mitigation and contingency sourcing. The CL question is whether repeatedly buying independently generated lessons also avoids narrowing what later suppliers can teach, beyond ordinary dual sourcing. Unlike #124's insurance, no repair capacity or model-loss claim is sold.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three data streams, two vendors share one source, a third is more expensive but independent. Compare spot procurement, diversification by vendor and exact diversification by declared upstream source. Hold total delivery count fixed and vary the correlation of supply failures.
- **Old-task retention:** After the primary outage and a subsequent task addition, evaluate old domains relying on the failed source and an unrelated old domain whose purchases competed for funding.

### LE20 · Buy compatibility once and make it open
- **Mechanism:** When old and new suppliers use incompatible label conventions, buy a one-off, open translation specification from either supplier or a third party, with all future buyers permitted to use it. Require the new supplier to price its new data without the translation bundled.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Farrell–Saloner's compatibility work](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/installed-base-compatibility) treats lock-in and migration; [Query-Based Data Pricing](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2213556.2213582) treats differently priced information views. The proposed traded input is a crossing for *existing and subsequent datasets*, not a runtime proxy, fixed interface standard (#45 E43), or the open curriculum completion contract in LE02.
- **Cheapest first test:** Simulate a proprietary-to-open label migration for two successive buyers and a tiny classifier. Compare paying for repeated database conversions, buying the spec once, and a single vendor's bundled migration. Include an allegedly compatible spec that cannot translate old ambiguous labels.
- **Old-task retention:** Train on translated actual examples; score old ambiguous and unambiguous labels after moving to the entrant and again after buying from a third seller.

### LE21 · Grant exclusivity that terminates when supply is refused
- **Mechanism:** An entrant obtains brief exclusivity in return for investing in a new lesson-production line. If it refuses a request under preannounced terms, the request automatically becomes open to competing suppliers, with no need to renegotiate the rest of the deal.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Aghion–Bolton, Contracts as a Barrier to Entry](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/2018/contracts%20a%20barrier%20too%20entry.pdf) analyzes contracts that deter entry. The candidate is a narrowly extinguishable exclusivity tied to a feasible teaching request, rather than freeing the buyer only by paying damages or waiting for term expiry.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two data vendors, private sunk costs, a risky arrival of a rare label request. Compare nonexclusivity, blanket exclusivity and release-on-refusal. Allow sham refusals or token deliveries; reject if the release clause cannot sustain genuine investment and entry.
- **Old-task retention:** Make the rare request serve an ordinary old class during new-domain expansion. Measure its accuracy through the refusal and competitive purchase.

### LE22 · License a supplier cooperative to price a coverage frontier
- **Mechanism:** Independent small suppliers pool rights to quote coverage menus for several customer cohorts, but each can withdraw specific licenses before a buyer accepts. Buyers choose bundles with coverage across cohorts rather than a dataset sized by total volume. Suppliers bargain over payments before data collection.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Data Cooperatives (2504.10058)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10058) frames member-owned stewardship; [Collaborative ML Markets (1911.09052)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09052) studies selection and replication-robust payments. The candidate object is a revocable joint offer for *future production with minimum distinct coverage*. Neither member ownership nor paying for diversity is the novelty claim.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three sellers, two dialects, one silo with cheap duplicates. Compare independent prices, a cooperative flat bundle and coverage menus. Price buyer concentration and internal holdout; reject if coverage contracts merely give the cooperative market power.
- **Old-task retention:** Include ordinary old cases from the smallest cooperative member and from a seller outside the cooperative, none of them omitted when a menu fails to clear.

### LE23 · Unbundle indispensable old data from popular new data
- **Mechanism:** Make a dominant seller quote separately for necessary old-domain replenishment and for popular new-domain data. Competitors may fulfill the old component under shared lawfully usable specifications; the buyer must be free to take the quote without buying anything new.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Bakos–Brynjolfsson, Bundling Information Goods](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.45.12.1613) studies profit and efficiency of information bundling. Here tying can change CL because the buyer periodically needs a small essential old input and the bundle channels future new-task demand to one seller. The contract to test is individual contestability of the essential teaching flow, not prioritizing its loss.
- **Cheapest first test:** A seller with an old-dialect bottleneck chooses between integrated and separate quotes; allow a small rival to sell one component. Compare mandatory unbundling with a price cap and voluntary mixed bundling. Reject if competitors cannot actually enter or if separate quotes simply increase prices.
- **Old-task retention:** Keep the old-dialect test fixed through three new-task arrivals and compare retention of a second old dialect not in the essential contract.

### LE24 · Buy a curriculum into a reusable commons only when authorized release is cheaper
- **Mechanism:** Use a one-time buyout for permission to train present and future buyers on a fixed lesson package. Pay the seller for the grant itself. Estimate whether other buyers would genuinely purchase licenses, using committed outside license orders rather than scoring the dataset, and cancel the buyout if the revealed order book is too thin.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Patent Buyouts](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/113/4/1137/1916997) already sets a buyout price to place innovations in the public domain. This is an explicit transplant: the candidate CL issue is whether an evergreen *permission to keep teaching* prevents a rare training domain from going dark after commercial demand falls, not whether public buyouts are new.
- **Cheapest first test:** A fixed data lot, stochastic entry of buyers and two maintenance-cost regimes. Compare repeated subscriptions, unconditional open purchase and purchase keyed to binding orders. Separate the case in which a fixed lot remains sufficient from one in which its labels age out.
- **Old-task retention:** Let the demand shock pass, add a new task, and test ordinary old cases for cohorts that could and could not access the bought-out lot.

## E · Decide who can consent, exit, or commit future buyers

### LE25 · Clear cycles of consent without trading consent for cash
- **Mechanism:** Each institution can grant a nontransferable, purpose-limited license directly to one other institution if it receives an acceptable license from a different institution. Clear a cycle of simultaneous grants or clear none; the institutions retain their source data ownership and nobody buys unrestricted consent.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Kidney Exchange](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10002) clears exchange cycles without money; [Data Sharing Markets (2107.08630)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08630) directly studies data exchange. The putative change is a contract that makes a teaching cycle clear only for a mutually acceptable tuple of downstream purposes and existing obligations. It is not a new matching algorithm if ordinary labeled edges suffice.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three institutions with asymmetric data needs, prohibitions on payment and purpose-dependent acceptable grants. Compare bilateral exchange, unrestricted cycle clearing and constrained cycles; try a case where a previously valid cycle is invalid after one purpose changes.
- **Old-task retention:** Each learner must still handle its old domain after accepting the new license and later accepting ordinary data outside the cycle.

### LE26 · Give a breakaway coalition its liabilities, not just its best data
- **Mechanism:** Permit a group of buyers and sellers to exit a pool with their lawfully portable data only if they can fund the continuously supplied old cohorts their contracts took on. Compute blocking coalitions over both training gains and inherited deliveries. No unused buyer data or obligations are silently moved.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Fairness in Federated Learning via Core-Stability (2211.02091)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02091) prevents subgroups from preferring another predictor trained on their own data. Here a different coalition may become viable only after paying for future deliveries to nonmembers. The object is a portable procurement obligation rather than better allocation of current utility.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate all partitions of four actors with overlapping current demand and one scarce historical supplier, with and without transferable obligations. Compare ordinary core selection and a no-exit pool. Report an empty feasible core as a result, not an excuse to drop commitments.
- **Old-task retention:** Score departing and remaining buyers after the split, including ordinary old tasks of customers who had no vote in it.

### LE27 · Sell an apprenticeship paid for by the next cohort
- **Mechanism:** A cohort needing a new skill underwrites a new supplier's apprenticeship on the condition that part of its first paid delivery serves the preceding cohort's still-needed specialty. The older buyers' only contribution is access to an authorized teaching environment; the apprentice must produce fresh material.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Samuelson's consumption-loan model](https://doi.org/10.1086/258100) shows how transfers between overlapping generations can be sustained; [Optimum Statistical Estimation with Strategic Data Sources (1408.2539)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2539) buys annotator effort. Here the transfer produces *future seller competence* in old and new domains, rather than circulating a monetary endowment.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three overlapping cohorts and a supplier who can acquire a two-dialect annotation skill only while the local expert is present. Compare a flat subsidy and equal-spend direct contracting with the aging expert. Find where the next cohort funds the apprentice willingly and not just because it bears no continuation risk.
- **Old-task retention:** Retire the original expert, then introduce a fresh label map and test whether both earlier dialects still work with the apprentice's actual deliveries.

### LE28 · Let an absent cohort bargain through a real purchase commitment
- **Mechanism:** A trustee for dormant customers can counteroffer for a recurring supply contract before the source is sold or exits. The trustee must spend from ex ante commitments the customers can redeem for later service, not votes or a hypothetical utility score. Unused amounts revert to the customers.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Matching with Contracts](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2F0002828054825466) treats choice over contracts rather than partners alone; [Flexible Design for Funding Public Goods (1809.06421)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06421) funds shared goods from present contributions. Unlike quadratic fleet funding (#45 E20), this commits a purchase option on behalf of off-market buyers against a real seller offer, with no requirement to forecast model loss.
- **Cheapest first test:** A rare customer group goes absent for two rounds while a supplier can sell exclusivity. Compare delegate purchase, earmarking funds with no purchasing authority, and ordinary individual advance ordering. Remove credible customer repayment as a negative control.
- **Old-task retention:** Bring back the absent customers after a unrelated task is learned; score their original tasks along with an old group not represented by a trustee.

### LE29 · Limit common ownership of apparently independent teaching bids
- **Mechanism:** Before awarding complementary contracts, let the buyer require that owners controlling the bids not be able to profit from the same nonperformance clause. Trade a *control split*: two economically independent deliverables, not two nominal vendor IDs.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Towards Replication-Robust Analytics Markets (2310.06000)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06000) protects reward allocation against replicated data. [Learning to Incentivize (1604.04928)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04928) notes collusion can sustain uninformative agreement. This candidate targets shared ownership and strategic delivery even when the two datasets are not copies and the physical production processes are independent (unlike LE19).
- **Cheapest first test:** Three bidders, two under common ownership. Let payment for complementary lots include one capped liability per owner, so a joint owner can profit by defaulting twice once the cap is reached. Compare deduplication, separate ownership and simply imposing a per-lot liability. Make independent firms able to collude as a counterexample.
- **Old-task retention:** After one supplier can default, add the new class and score the two historical domains separately and jointly.

### LE30 · Charge a coalition for the inference it buys about a nonseller
- **Mechanism:** Consent to buy a single view of a new domain does not authorize buying a combination whose joint use reveals another party's protected category. A designated trustee for that nonseller can license only specifically allowable joint uses or refuse entirely; a payment cannot override a nonwaivable prohibition.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [The Economics of Social Data (2004.03107)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03107) models inference externalities from correlated disclosures; [Too Much Data](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26296) studies efficiency improvements through mediated sharing. The question is whether a joint-use permission can condition on what has already been learned, unlike approving purchases from the same seller independently. This is not a new definition of privacy.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two individually uninformative binary views that jointly reveal a protected latent bit, plus a legal replacement training source. Compare single-sale approval and outright prohibition of both views. Use an exact public inference model for the toy; vary acquisition order and include a case beyond the trustee's declared scope.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate old permitted tasks on each population while the new task is learned only from allowed inputs. Audit the protected inference separately; do not count suppressing a lawful old output as privacy success.

### LE31 · Release customers from a contract when their data supplier fails, without assigning the whole estate
- **Mechanism:** On supplier insolvency, customers choose which prepaid, not-yet-delivered teaching orders to carry into a successor's partial book. A buyer's consent to assign one order does not convey prior records or new rights on unrelated orders. Competing sellers bid on only the portion they can fulfill.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Matching with Contracts](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2F0002828054825466) already lets parties choose combinations of terms. #124's bankruptcy idea concerns moving model service obligations. The narrower new candidate is consensual severability of externally procured teaching orders with different data rights, while the actual model remains in place.
- **Cheapest first test:** Fail one seller after taking three orders with heterogeneous licensing clauses; enumerate split assignments, a wholesale auction and fresh purchasing from scratch. Include loss of scale when splitting makes fulfillment more expensive.
- **Old-task retention:** Score buyers with assigned and unassigned orders after they learn a new task; include old data under rights that never moved with the estate.

### LE32 · Let an institution buy learning rights with a matched delivery instead of money
- **Mechanism:** A buyer with no cash but a callable, authorized labeling service can pay a supplier by making delivery to a third party. Clear only if the third party pays that supplier and is actually willing to accept the buyer's delivery. This triangular settlement creates fresh training data as payment without authorizing resale of old sources.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [A Marketplace for Data (1805.08125)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08125) matches streams to purchases, while [Kidney Exchange](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10002) enables in-kind exchange. The candidate operation clears a priced trade against an independently demanded future labeling service, not a consent swap such as LE25. It adds nothing if the buyer can obtain an equivalent cash advance.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three parties, one buyer faces a binding liquidity limit, and the third party chooses whether to accept its future data delivery. Compare bilateral barter, cash plus fair credit, and triangular settlement including delivery/default costs. Vary third-party demand so some trades should not clear.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate both actual learners on their original tasks after settlement, including the buyer's old domain whose labeling activity was diverted to earn the purchase.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **When will a rational teacher sell a lesson that destroys its own future rents?** (LE01–03, LE14) Begin with two dates and two curricula, private costs and a real switching decision. Find the smallest game where per-label purchase, advance curriculum purchase and committed buyout implement different *teaching choices*, holding spending and old-task floors fixed. Then ask which difference survives unknown repeat demand and an entrant who may never show. If the buyer can prebuy the same curriculum on the same terms, retire the buyout primitive.
2. **What counts as the same purchasable learning outcome when histories change both competence and rights?** (LE08–13) In a finite training graph, seek the weakest equivalence relation that preserves ordinary old behavior, the available next teaching moves and permission to take them. Exhibit one true order-dependent arbitrage and one false “equivalence” that violates a retained right. If ordinary database determinacy plus access-control labels reproduces it at equal cost, there is no separate pricing primitive.
3. **What is the price of keeping a source of learning alive without keeping its incumbent in power?** (LE16–24) Separate continuing production, competitive entry and ownership of older data. Find a demand shock where capped rescue, competitive backup and permanent public release diverge. Compare the buyer's eventual old/new frontier, intertemporal supplier entry and total transfers; reject schemes that buy retention only by concentrating the next market.
4. **Who can credibly represent the people who do not enter the next auction?** (LE25–32) Construct an instance with a dormant customer, a strategic exiting supplier and a real outside option. Compare a trustee with prepaid purchasing power, consent and assignment clauses, and an unconditional duty funded separately from the market. Look for the constraint that changes feasible trades without turning a rare old task into a permanent veto.
