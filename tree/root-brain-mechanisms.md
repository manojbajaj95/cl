# Root: Brain & Biological Mechanisms as Continual Learning Primitives for LLMs

Root node (Depth 0) · Ideas only, no GPU · Informed by [`reviews/idea-ledger.md`](../reviews/idea-ledger.md) and [`reviews/flood-triage.md`](../reviews/flood-triage.md)

**Core thesis.** Biological nervous systems learn continually across decades without task IDs, without storing raw sensory logs, and without wiping prior skills — not by packing hidden state into an optimizer's moments, but by separating *when*, *where*, *in what phase*, *under what global signal*, and *with what structural lifecycle* a weight change is permitted to occur. Current LLM continual learning treats every update as a uniform gradient step on a static, homogeneous parameter tensor. This root translates concrete mechanisms from neuroscience and biology — **sleep replay**, **neuromodulation**, **synaptic tagging and metaplasticity**, **neurogenesis and structural plasticity**, **dendritic/cortical microcircuits**, and **immune/ecological adaptation** — into **36 primitive-level continual learning objects and operations native to Transformers and LLMs**.

## Scope, Ledger Constraints, and Anti-Drift Boundary

1. **Stay far from optimizer-state lines.** The reviewer found that the optimizer-state branches (*park crossings*, *inward service*, *repair-reveal*, *route-merged folds*, Adam `m`/`v` slot packing, and second-moment basins) yield narrow variants of the same linear-response geometry. **Zero** cards below use optimizer-slot smuggling, momentum parking, or fold-state tricks. Every primitive operates on Transformer activations, attention routing, KV caches, MLP/SAE feature subspaces, residual streams, or structural connectivity.
2. **Primitive bar, not incremental regularizers.** Following the ledger's primitive-or-improvement gate, every card introduces a **new object or operation** (a decaying tag buffer, a disinhibitory mask, a phase-gated write window, an excitability register, a thymic delta filter, an allosteric binding site) rather than tuning EWC, PackNet, L2P/DualPrompt, or LoRA rank.
3. **Ledger lessons enforced in every card:**
   - *No generator-identity claims* (PR #8 falsified self-QA vs. teacher-QA): any offline synthesis/sleep primitive must control against teacher-generated or equal-format replay.
   - *No uncontrolled margin-order claims* (reverse grokking / cooldown kills): retention tests match pre-edit margin or compare at equal baseline accuracy.
   - *No first-order tautologies* (M1 kill): every card makes a behavioral or compositional claim that linear algebra alone does not settle.
   - *No oracle-center domain gates* (PR #127 developmental-growth kill): every allocation/routing primitive is strictly **task-ID-free** and **oracle-center-free**, compared against online-learned clustering or equal-byte replay.
   - *No duplicates of PR #46 (`ideas/flood/neuro-bio.md`, NB01–NB44)*: where a biological inspiration overlaps (e.g., synaptic tagging, quorum sensing, immune tolerance), the card explicitly cites the earlier toy/agent flood entry and defines a distinct **LLM-native weight/representation primitive**.
   - *Mandatory old-task retention check* (ledger rule): every cheapest first test includes an explicit check that the LLM retains its normal old tasks and base perplexity after learning the new thing.

## Idea Cards (36 Primitives Across 6 Biological Families)

### Family A · Sleep Replay & Offline Consolidation Primitives

#### BM01 · Sharp-Wave Ripple (SWR) Bridge-Stitching Replay
- **Mechanism:** During an offline sleep pass, scan recent episodic traces for latent entity/concept bridges shared across *non-overlapping* episodes (Episode 1: `A → B`; Episode 2: `B → C`), synthesize time-compressed multi-hop shortcut chains (`A → C` via `B`) that were never observed jointly awake, and consolidate those stitched transitions into MLP weights via KL-matched distillation. The new primitive is a **compositional stitch operator** over episodic memory, not verbatim replay.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Sleep-replay LLM papers ([2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979), [2605.26099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099), [2606.08447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08447)) replay or summarize individual past episodes; Synthetic Continued Pretraining ([2409.07431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)) paraphrases single documents. Neither uses biological SWR *trajectory stitching* to synthesize cross-episode shortcuts as the unit of consolidation. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-token verbatim replay of Episodes 1 & 2, plus teacher-written single-episode QA paraphrases (controlling for PR #8's format/coverage effect).
- **Cheapest first test:** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B on a synthetic 2-hop entity-relation stream (50 disjoint `(A→B)` and `(B→C)` fact pairs arriving in separate turns, never co-occurring in context). Measure held-out `A → C` compositional accuracy after equal-token offline consolidation. *Kill:* stitched consolidation fails to beat equal-token verbatim replay + single-fact paraphrase by ≥ 10 pp on held-out 2-hop queries.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 200 fixed pre-stream factual QA probes + WikiText-2 perplexity before and after consolidation; fail if old-fact accuracy drops > 1.5 pp or perplexity rises > 2%.

#### BM02 · Synaptic Homeostasis (SHY) Selective Delta Downscaling
- **Mechanism:** After a wake period of continual updates `ΔW = Σ_i u_i v_iᵀ` (or sparse feature-delta atoms in MLP/SAE space), run a data-free sleep contraction that multiplicatively shrinks every delta component proportionally to `(1 - consistency_i)`, where `consistency_i` is the fraction of distinct wake contexts that co-activated direction `i` with the same sign. Spurious one-off wake noise is downscaled to zero while recurring high-SNR circuits survive intact, restoring parameter headroom without replaying old data.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from uniform L2 weight decay / AdamW (which shrinks recurring and spurious directions equally) and from magnitude pruning (which uses raw norm rather than cross-context sign consistency). [2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979) and [2611.02216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02216) discuss sleep homeostasis conceptually or via replay; this defines a **data-free multiplicative spectral-floor operator** on wake deltas. *Strongest equal-budget control:* tuned uniform L2 shrinkage toward `W_0` (L2-SP) and magnitude-thresholded delta pruning at matched sparsity/norm.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM or 4-layer Transformer on a 10-segment stream mixing 20% recurring core rules with 80% one-off noisy/contradictory facts. Compare cumulative recurring-rule retention and new-segment plasticity across 10 wake/sleep cycles. *Kill:* SHY downscaling ties L2-SP or magnitude pruning within seed CI on recurring-rule retention or final-segment learning speed.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate pre-stream invariant capabilities (arithmetic + syntax + 100 base QA facts) after cycle 10; require ≤ 1 pp drop vs. base checkpoint and strictly better retention than L2-SP at matched new-task accuracy.

#### BM03 · NREM / REM Two-Phase Alternation (Stabilization vs. Disinhibited Recombination)
- **Mechanism:** Split offline consolidation into two alternating operators with opposite inference regimes: **NREM** runs low-temperature (`T → 0.2`) anchor-preserving distillation on verified episodic trajectories to stabilize exact recall; **REM** injects structured attention-head dropout + elevated temperature (`T ≈ 1.3`) across *pairs of distant stored episodes* to generate candidate cross-domain abstractions, admitting a REM trace to weight updates *only* if a lightweight compression/consistency check confirms it predicts both parent episodes with fewer bits than storing them separately.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Existing LLM sleep/consolidation methods ([2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979), [2611.02746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02746)) use a single static replay regime. REM-inspired recombination in vision/RL adds isotropic noise; here REM is a **compression-verified schema-discovery operator** explicitly alternated with NREM stabilization. *Strongest equal-budget control:* NREM-only replay at matched total FLOPs/tokens, and teacher-generated cross-episode summaries at matched token count.
- **Cheapest first test:** 1.5B LLM on a stream of 6 isomorphic symbolic rule domains (same underlying algebra, different surface vocabularies). Test zero-shot transfer to a 7th unseen surface domain after 3 cycles. *Kill:* NREM+REM fails to beat NREM-only on held-out isomorphic transfer by ≥ 8 pp, or hurts exact recall of seen domains vs. NREM-only.
- **Old-task retention check:** Exact accuracy on seen domains 1–6 (must stay within 1 pp of NREM-only) plus base instruction-following/perplexity probe within 1.5 pp / 2%.

#### BM04 · Slow-Oscillation / Spindle Phase-Gated Write Window
- **Mechanism:** During offline replay, compute a per-layer, per-token "UP-state susceptibility" scalar `u(l, t) = 1 - ||P_inv h_{l,t}||² / ||h_{l,t}||²`, where `P_inv` is a compact low-rank projector onto the residual-stream subspace spanned by a fixed invariant anchor panel. Weight updates are gated by a sharp binary/sigmoid phase window: gradients fire *only* at `(l, t)` pairs in an UP-state (`u(l, t) > τ`), while DOWN-state tokens pass forward with zero write contribution.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Orthogonal gradient projection (OGD / GPM / AlphaEdit [2410.02355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355)) projects the *gradient* after averaging over all tokens and layers; here the primitive gates *which token positions and layers are allowed to enter the write phase at all*, mimicking cortical UP/DOWN oscillation nesting. *Strongest equal-budget control:* OGD / null-space gradient projection over all tokens at matched FLOPs and anchor-panel size.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM continually fine-tuned on 5 sequential domain slices (code, math, legal, medical, dialogue) with a 64-prompt invariant panel. Compare retention–adaptation Pareto front against full-sequence OGD and token-loss-filtered fine-tuning. *Kill:* UP-state token/layer gating ties OGD or random token dropping at matched update norm.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure accuracy on the 64-prompt invariant panel *and* an unprojected 200-prompt held-out general-knowledge set; require ≤ 1 pp degradation on both.

#### BM05 · Reverse-Replay Branching-Point Credit Consolidation
- **Mechanism:** Immediately after a multi-step LLM rollout receives a terminal verifier signal (pass/fail), run a reverse-order KV-chunked replay from the final answer back to the prompt. At each step `t` moving backward, measure the counterfactual shift in terminal outcome probability when resampling step `t`; consolidate weight updates *only* at the earliest 1–2 "branching tokens" where the trajectory diverged toward success or failure, skipping the rest of the transcript.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Standard SFT/RL/MOPD ([2611.02746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02746)) updates uniformly across all tokens in a trajectory, causing severe style/format drift on old tasks; PRM/step-level RL trains an external value model. Biological hippocampal reverse replay propagates terminal reward backward to pinpoint causal choice points *without* a trained critic. *Strongest equal-budget control:* full-trajectory SFT on the same verified rollouts, and final-token-only / random-2-token SFT at matched forward+backward FLOPs.
- **Cheapest first test:** 1.5B LLM on a multi-step arithmetic/tool-routing benchmark (4-step trajectories, binary terminal verifier) streamed across 4 shifting tool grammars. Charge the reverse-replay counterfactual forwards in total FLOPs. *Kill:* reverse-branching consolidation fails to beat full-trajectory SFT on retention at matched new-task accuracy, or loses to random-token masking.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate old tool grammars (tasks 1–3 after task 4) and general chat/QA perplexity; require ≥ 5 pp better old-grammar retention than full-trajectory SFT at matched task-4 accuracy.

#### BM06 · Awake Micro-Sleep in Live KV-Cache Silent Subspaces
- **Mechanism:** Between user turns (idle latency), extract the active KV-cache keys `K_live` of the current conversation and compute a 1–2 step rank-1 MLP write of the most salient new turn fact constrained to `ker(K_liveᵀ)` (or low-attention-score key directions). This converts volatile context facts into semi-persistent fast weights *before* context eviction while guaranteeing zero perturbation to the active conversation's current attention readouts.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Replay in Silent DoF (2609.31630)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31630) projects replay into the null space of *past task gradients*; TTT ([2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)) and Titans ([2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663)) update fast weights on every token without protecting active KV readouts. Anchoring the silent subspace to the *live KV-cache* makes awake micro-sleep a non-disruptive turn-boundary primitive. *Strongest equal-budget control:* unconstrained 1-step TTT/LoRA update between turns, and sliding-window KV summary retention at equal bytes.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a 30-turn multi-entity conversation where early entities are evicted from the context window at turn 15 and queried at turns 20–30, while active-window entities are queried at every turn. *Kill:* KV-silent micro-sleep fails to beat unconstrained inter-turn LoRA on active-window fidelity or fails to beat eviction baseline on evicted-fact recall.
- **Old-task retention check:** After 30 turns of micro-sleep, clear the KV cache and evaluate 100 base-model QA prompts + perplexity; require ≤ 1 pp accuracy loss and ≤ 1.5% perplexity rise.

### Family B · Neuromodulation & Global-Signal Plasticity Primitives

#### BM07 · Cholinergic (ACh) Encode-vs-Recall Attention/MLP Gain Switch
- **Mechanism:** Expose a scalar neuromodulatory control `α_ACh ∈ [0, 1]` inside each Transformer block that scales the residual contribution of **parametric MLP recall** by `(1 - γ α_ACh)` while boosting **in-context attention** by `(1 + γ α_ACh)`. During *encoding* of a contradicting or novel fact (`α_ACh = 1`), old parametric associations are transiently suppressed so the forward pass forms an uncorrupted target representation before writing; at *inference* (`α_ACh = 0`), normal parametric recall resumes.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Hasselmo's cholinergic model is classic in neuroscience but absent from Transformer CL. Existing conflict-handling methods (CAD, KAFT, SLB [2604.23750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23750)) reweight output logits or fine-tune with counterfactual prompts; none dynamically decouple attention-reading from MLP-writing inside the residual stream during the *write pass itself* to prevent prior interference during encoding. *Strongest equal-budget control:* standard fine-tuning/LoRA on the same contradicting facts with matched LR, plus logit-level contrastive decoding at training time.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B/1.5B LLM updating 50 high-confidence base facts (high prior margin, where standard SFT struggles or over-writes neighbors) vs. 50 low-margin facts. Compare new-fact absorption and collateral neighbor damage with `α_ACh` active during training vs. `α_ACh = 0`. *Kill:* `α_ACh` gating fails to improve the absorption-vs-collateral-damage Pareto curve over tuned-LR SFT on high-margin overrides.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 100 unrelated neighbor facts (same entity class, untouched) + general MMLU/QA slice at `α_ACh = 0` post-update; require ≥ 4 pp less neighbor damage than matched-absorption SFT.

#### BM08 · LC-NE Unexpected-Uncertainty Broadcast via Cross-Layer Logit-Lens Disagreement
- **Mechanism:** Distinguish *expected* uncertainty (high final-layer token entropy, e.g., open-ended phrasing) from *unexpected* uncertainty (sharp KL divergence between early/middle logit-lens predictions and late-layer predictions, signaling a structural regime shift where internal priors clash with incoming evidence). Broadcast a phasic scalar norepinephrine signal `δ_NE` proportional to cross-layer disagreement that transiently opens layerwise learning rates *only* in the layers bracketing the disagreement spike, while suppressing writes during high-entropy/low-disagreement noise.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Surprise-gated memory (Titans [2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), NeuMoSync [2608.04358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04358)) gates on final-layer loss/gradient norm, which conflates aleatoric noise (expected uncertainty) with regime shifts (unexpected uncertainty). Separating them via *internal layer-disagreement* is an LLM-native implementation of Yu & Dayan's ACh/NE theory. *Strongest equal-budget control:* final-loss/gradient-norm surprise gating (Titans-style) and uniform-LR online updates at matched total update norm.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream alternating between (a) clean regime shifts (rule inversions), (b) high-entropy noisy/irrelevant distractor text, and (c) stationary segments. Measure adaptation speed on (a) and stability during (b). *Kill:* `δ_NE` fails to separate (a) from (b) better than final-token loss (AUROC gap < 0.10) or ties loss-gated updates on retention under noise.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure old-regime accuracy before and after a 500-token high-entropy distractor burst; require ≤ 1 pp drop (vs. substantial drift under loss-norm gating).

#### BM09 · Dopaminergic Three-Factor Local Hebbian Write in SwiGLU Blocks
- **Mechanism:** Equip selected MLP layers with an online local eligibility trace `E_l = Σ_t γ^{T-t} (post_t ⊗ pre_t)` accumulated during autoregressive generation with zero backprop graph. When a terminal scalar reward/verifier signal `r` arrives, compute reward prediction error `δ_DA = r - b(x)` (against a tiny linear value baseline `b`) and apply a single local rank-k update `ΔW_down,l = η · δ_DA · E_l`, bypassing backpropagation through the sequence entirely.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** MeZO ([2305.17333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333)) uses zeroth-order two-forward finite differences; three-factor Hebbian rules are studied in SNNs and small RL MLPs, rarely as a continual write primitive inside pretrained SwiGLU LLM blocks. The primitive is a **zero-backprop, O(1)-memory-in-sequence-length online write rule** compatible with live inference. *Strongest equal-budget control:* MeZO-LoRA at matched forward passes and 1-step backprop LoRA at matched wall-clock/memory budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a bandit-like constrained action/tool-routing stream (G1-style, 200 episodes) with binary terminal feedback and 2 regime shifts. Compare adaptation speed and memory footprint against MeZO-LoRA and 1-step REINFORCE-LoRA. *Kill:* three-factor SwiGLU writes fail to beat chance lift (+10 pp over frozen) or lose to equal-forward MeZO-LoRA on both speed and retention.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score a frozen 100-item general QA + base-regime accuracy panel after 200 online updates; require ≤ 2 pp drop on unrelated QA.

#### BM10 · Serotonergic (5-HT) Confirmation-Horizon Modulator
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a single learned/controllable global "patience" parameter `τ_5HT` that sets the minimum number of *independent* episodes/contexts in which a candidate knowledge delta must exhibit positive cosine agreement before graduating from a volatile fast-weight scratchpad into persistent weights. High `τ_5HT` enforces conservative, multi-evidence consolidation (protecting core facts from transient noise); low `τ_5HT` enables rapid one-shot adaptation when an environment signals high volatility.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Replay/consolidation gates (e.g. ENGRAM [2611.09472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09472), Selective Parametric Consolidation [2606.26806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26806)) use fixed thresholds or single-episode verifiers. Exposing **confirmation horizon as a continuous, globally modulatable control primitive** separates *evidence accumulation duration* from learning rate. *Strongest equal-budget control:* fixed learning-rate scaling (`η / τ`), exponential moving average of gradients, and fixed-threshold single-episode gating.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a mixed stream containing both *persistent* rule shifts (lasting 50 episodes) and *transient* 1–3 episode anomalies/typos, with a volatile vs. stable environment cue. Compare cumulative prequential loss and false-commit rate across `τ_5HT` vs. LR-scaled SFT. *Kill:* `τ_5HT` ties matched-LR SFT or EMA gradient averaging on false-commit rate at equal persistent-shift adaptation speed.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate retention of 50 stable base rules after 100 transient anomaly episodes; require ≤ 1 pp degradation under high `τ_5HT`.

#### BM11 · VIP→SST Disinhibitory Sparse-Feature Write Gate
- **Mechanism:** Invert the default state of LLM plasticity: attach a default-ON inhibitory mask (`g_SST = 0`, blocking all weight updates) to every neuron/SAE-feature in target MLP layers. A tiny context-conditioned "VIP" module fires sparsely on discrepancy signals, *inhibiting the inhibitor* (`g_SST → 1`) for only the top-k (1–3%) neurons whose features are causally active on the current error. Stability is the physical default; plasticity requires active disinhibition.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Functional Subnetwork Localization ([2610.21537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21537)) and Sparse Memory Finetuning ([2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103)) select active/high-gradient parameters *after* computing full gradients or task masks; biological cortical plasticity is gated *prior to write* by a two-stage disinhibitory microcircuit (VIP⊣SST⊣Pyramidal). Making the **disinhibitory gate** a reusable learned primitive decouples "what is plastic" from raw gradient magnitude. *Strongest equal-budget control:* top-k gradient-magnitude masking (SMF-style) and random-k masking at the same 1–3% active parameter budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on 5 sequential factual/rule updates with overlapping surface vocabulary. Compare new-fact accuracy and interference on overlapping old facts against top-k gradient masking at matched k%. *Kill:* disinhibitory gating fails to beat top-k gradient masking by ≥ 5 pp on overlapping old-fact retention at matched new-fact accuracy.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate both overlapping-vocabulary old facts (hard retention) and 100 unrelated base QA prompts + perplexity (must stay within 1 pp / 1.5%).

#### BM12 · Neuromodulatory Gain-Field Basis (Weight-Invariant Functional Sub-Circuits)
- **Mechanism:** Attach a tiny bank of `M` rank-1 neuromodulatory gain vectors (`m_k ∈ R^{d_ff}` scaling MLP channels + head-temperature scalars) across layers. A lightweight context router emits a sparse convex combination `c(x)` of these gain vectors, re-routing the *same* underlying weight matrices into distinct functional sub-circuits. Continual learning first attempts to solve a new regime by optimizing only a new gain-field coordinate `c_new` (zero weight overwrite); only when gain-field capacity saturates does it write into weights gated by `c_new`.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** IA³ / (IA)³ tunes one static multiplicative vector per task; LoRA banks store additive weight matrices; [2610.41265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41265) studies input-dependent gain on neuromorphic hardware. Here the primitive is a **hierarchical gain-first / weight-second continual write protocol** over a shared neuromodulatory basis without task IDs. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-parameter LoRA bank with cosine-similarity routing, and single shared IA³ vector with replay.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM across 6 sequential behavioral regimes (e.g., formatting, personas, rule sets) with unmarked recurrence. Measure forgetting on regime 1 after regime 6 and total persistent bytes. *Kill:* gain-field basis loses to equal-byte routed LoRA on forgetting or fails to solve ≥ 50% of regime shifts via gain coordinates alone.
- **Old-task retention check:** Base-model zero-shot benchmarks with neutral modulator (`c = 0`) are **bitwise preserved** by construction; test retention of regimes 1–5 when revisited without task IDs (require ≥ 85% retention).

### Family C · Synaptic Tagging, Capture & Metaplasticity Primitives

#### BM13 · Decaying Rank-1 MLP Synaptic Tags + Delayed PRP Capture
- **Mechanism:** During live multi-turn LLM interaction, whenever the model processes a novel factual assertion or unverified reasoning step, write a tiny decaying rank-1 **synaptic tag** `(k_tag, Δv_tag, ttl)` onto the top-activated MLP key-value rows *without altering `W`*. If a strong verification event arrives turns later (tests pass, user confirms, or a high-reward outcome occurs) — even after the original turn has left the context window — broadcast a global "PRP capture" signal that commits `W ← W + λ · Δv_tag k_tagᵀ` *only* at still-alive tagged rows, then clears expired tags.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** PR #46's NB01 sketched a generic XOR eligibility-escrow toy; Frey & Morris STC has been modeled in SNNs ([2405.16922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16922)) but not as an **LLM MLP key-value tag-and-capture primitive** for delayed post-eviction verification. Distinct from replay buffers: tags are O(rank-1) weight-space addresses, not stored text transcripts. *Strongest equal-budget control:* same-byte episodic text buffer replayed upon verification, and immediate unverified online LoRA updates.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a 20-turn delayed-verification stream: turns 1–10 contain 5 true facts and 5 false/tentative facts (evicted from context by turn 12); turns 13–20 reveal delayed verification signals. Compare true-fact retention and false-fact rejection against equal-byte text-buffer replay and immediate SFT. *Kill:* STC tags fail to beat immediate SFT on false-fact rejection or trail equal-byte text replay by > 3 pp on true-fact recall.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 100 base-model factual QA prompts + perplexity after 50 delayed-verification episodes; require ≤ 1 pp accuracy loss and ≤ 1.5% perplexity rise.

#### BM14 · Behavioral Tagging (Cross-Episode Novelty Rescue of One-Shot Facts)
- **Mechanism:** Extend BM13 so that a single high-surprise, verified "anchor" episode broadcasts PRPs that capture not only its own tagged MLP rows, but also *weakly tagged* rows from neighboring turns in the same session window whose key vectors lie within a bounded cosine cone of the anchor's latent schema. Weak, single-exposure facts that would never justify a standalone gradient step are consolidated *for free* when paired with a verified anchor event.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Biological behavioral tagging (Ballarini et al. / Moncada & Viola) rescues weak memories via temporally proximal novelty; no LLM CL method uses **cross-episode PRP spillover** to solve single-exposure consolidation without increasing false-positive writes. *Strongest equal-budget control:* lowering the verification threshold globally (admitting weak episodes directly), and uniform replay of the session window.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream where rare one-shot schema-consistent facts co-occur in sessions with verified anchor tasks, interspersed with off-schema distractor facts. Measure recall of the rare one-shot facts vs. false absorption of distractors. *Kill:* behavioral-tagging spillover fails to beat global threshold lowering on the true-vs-distractor precision/recall curve.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify that unrelated pre-stream facts and base perplexity stay within 1 pp / 1.5% across 20 spillover consolidation cycles.

#### BM15 · Per-Feature BCM Sliding Plasticity Threshold (Anti-Hijacking LTD)
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a slow exponential moving average of post-activation magnitude `μ_i(t)` for each MLP neuron (or SAE latent) `i`, setting a sliding BCM modification threshold `θ_i = c · μ_i(t)²`. When an online update touches feature `i`, scale its update by `(a_i - θ_i)`: moderate activations on a hyper-active, promiscuous feature flip sign into **targeted depression (LTD)**, automatically pruning over-generalized/hijacking features without requiring negative labels.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** BCM theory is classical in computational neuroscience but almost never applied to continually trained LLM MLP/SAE features to counter *feature hijacking* (where a newly learned LoRA/MLP direction fires indiscriminately on unrelated prompts, as seen in PR #89/#103 dilution). Distinct from weight decay or gradient clipping because the **sign of plasticity reverses** for over-active features. *Strongest equal-budget control:* L1 activation sparsity penalty, AdamW weight decay, and gradient norm clipping at matched compute.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM sequentially fine-tuned on 6 narrow domain rules (known to cause over-triggering on general prompts). Measure off-domain false-trigger rate and on-domain accuracy vs. L1 sparsity and weight decay. *Kill:* BCM sliding threshold fails to cut off-domain false triggers by ≥ 25% relative to L1 sparsity / weight decay at matched on-domain accuracy.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 200 general-domain prompts (including near-miss triggers) + base perplexity after domain 6; require ≥ 5 pp higher general-domain accuracy than standard SFT.

#### BM16 · Bidirectional Multi-Timescale Cascade Inside Transformer FFN Blocks
- **Mechanism:** Replace a single continual adapter `ΔW` on MLP down-projections with a `K=4` Benna–Fusi-inspired bidirectional chain of coupled low-rank variables `(ΔW_0 ↔ ΔW_1 ↔ ΔW_2 ↔ ΔW_3)` with exponentially spaced coupling/decay rates `τ_k = 4^k`. Online updates write only to `ΔW_0`; passive diffusive coupling transfers persistent signals down the chain (`0 → 1 → 2 → 3`) while restoring forces from slower variables pull `ΔW_0` back after transient perturbations — yielding power-law retention with O(K·r·d) state and zero replay.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Ledger #3 (rational power-law decay) was a scalar decay rate; Benna–Fusi cascades ([2405.16922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16922)) and [2608.14634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14634) operate on per-synapse scalar weights (prohibitively expensive at 4× full LLM size). Implementing the **diffusive cascade in low-rank FFN subspaces** makes it an LLM-scale continual memory primitive. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-parameter single-timescale LoRA (rank `4r`), and an independent mixture of 4 LRs (no bidirectional coupling).
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a 16-task continual stream with recurring tasks at delays 1, 4, and 16. Compare retention AUC and reacquisition speed against rank-`4r` LoRA and uncoupled LR-mixture LoRA at matched parameter count. *Kill:* coupled cascade fails to beat uncoupled LR-mixture LoRA on retention AUC across delays.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate Task-1 accuracy after Task-16 and base-model perplexity; require strictly higher Task-1 retention than rank-`4r` LoRA with base perplexity within 2%.

#### BM17 · Dendritic-Clustered Write Admission (Co-Activation Neighborhood Gate)
- **Mechanism:** Precompute a sparse co-activation graph over MLP neurons (or attention heads) on a base calibration corpus, grouping co-firing neurons into local "dendritic clusters." During continual learning, a candidate parameter update on neuron `i` is admitted *only* if at least `m` of its cluster neighbors are simultaneously tagged/activated on the current episode; isolated single-neuron updates are rejected as noise.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Mirrors biological heterosynaptic clustering on dendritic branches (Govindarajan et al.). Existing LLM sparsity methods (SMF [2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103), [2610.21537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21537)) select top-k neurons independently without requiring *structural cluster coherence* for write admission. *Strongest equal-budget control:* independent top-k neuron selection at matched update sparsity, and random-cluster masking.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream mixing coherent multi-fact domain updates (which activate clustered circuits) with 30% label-corrupted/noisy examples. Compare clean-fact learning and robustness to label noise against independent top-k SMF. *Kill:* cluster-gated admission fails to beat independent top-k SMF on noisy-stream retention or clean-fact accuracy.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate 100 untouched base facts + perplexity after the noisy stream; require ≤ 1.5 pp drop and strictly lower corruption damage than independent top-k SMF.

#### BM18 · Retrieval-Unlocked Reconsolidation Window (Targeted Fact Revision)
- **Mechanism:** Keep consolidated MLP factual weights **hard-locked** (zero gradient) by default. When a query retrieves an existing fact (detected by MLP key-row activation exceeding a retrieval threshold `ρ`) *and* receives a contradiction/correction signal, unlock *only* those retrieved key-value rows into a transient **labile window** of `K` steps (allowing overwriting or erasure), after which they automatically re-lock. Unretrieved memories cannot be touched.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Biological reconsolidation (Nader et al.) makes *only retrieved* memories labile. Existing model editing (ROME/MEMIT/AlphaEdit [2410.02355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355)) solves a global closed-form least-squares edit over whole layer matrices; continual fine-tuning leaves all rows unlocked. Turning **retrieval itself into the write-unlock primitive** scopes edits strictly to the recalled engram. *Strongest equal-budget control:* AlphaEdit / MEMIT on the same fact revisions, and unconstrained LoRA on the same layer.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B/1.5B LLM on a stream of 100 sequential factual revisions (updating outdated facts while leaving 200 related facts untouched). Compare revision efficacy and untouched-fact retention against MEMIT/AlphaEdit and LoRA. *Kill:* retrieval-unlocked reconsolidation fails to beat LoRA on untouched-fact retention by ≥ 5 pp or fails to match MEMIT on revision efficacy without needing covariance matrix inversion.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score the 200 untouched related facts + 100 unrelated base QA prompts + perplexity after all 100 revisions; require ≤ 1.5 pp untouched drop and ≤ 2% perplexity rise.

### Family D · Neurogenesis, Engram Allocation & Structural Plasticity Primitives

#### BM19 · CREB Excitability-Driven Temporal Engram Co-Allocation
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a decaying scalar intrinsic-excitability bias `e_i(t)` on a pool of writable MLP neurons (initialized to small random baseline excitability). Whenever a new episode recruits the top-k most excitable neurons (via `act_i + e_i(t)`), those recruited neurons receive a temporary excitability boost that decays with half-life `H` turns. Two episodes arriving within `H` automatically share overlapping MLP engrams (enabling associative linking without labels), while episodes separated by `≫ H` recruit orthogonal newly-excitable neurons (preventing interference) — with **zero task IDs and zero oracle domain centers**.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Directly models Silva/Josselyn CREB excitability-driven memory allocation. Unlike PR #127's developmental-growth probe (killed for using oracle task-mean RBF centers) and unlike PR #46 NB25 (explicit temporal calendar cohorts), allocation emerges purely from a **1-scalar-per-neuron decaying excitability bias** inside standard MLP routing. *Strongest equal-budget control:* online k-means/unlabeled input-clustered routing at equal bytes, and random-mask / replay baselines.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream of 6 interleaved contextual fact clusters where related facts arrive in temporal bursts (within window `H`) and unrelated clusters arrive later. Test both (a) cross-fact linking within a burst and (b) non-interference between separated clusters. *Kill:* excitability allocation fails to beat online k-means routing on interference or fails to show stronger within-burst associative transfer than random neuron masks.
- **Old-task retention check:** After cluster 6, evaluate cluster 1 recall + 100 base QA facts; require ≥ 8 pp better cluster-1 retention than standard LoRA and ≤ 1.5 pp base QA drop.

#### BM20 · Fixed-Budget Rotating Newborn-Neuron MLP Slab (Adult Hippocampal Neurogenesis)
- **Mechanism:** Attach a fixed-size auxiliary MLP slab (`N` neurons, zero-initialized output projections at birth so base behavior is untouched) whose neurons cycle through a 3-stage biological lifecycle: (1) **Immature** (high LR, broad input tuning, lateral k-WTA competition to rapidly separate new patterns), (2) **Mature** (frozen input keys, low LR, sparse stable readout), and (3) **Recycled** (periodically distilled into the main MLP via sleep replay, then reset to zero-output immature neurons). Total parameter count never grows.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** NORACL ([2604.27031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27031)) and Growing/Elastic Nets ([2608.01475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01475)) expand/prune vision MLPs; Progressive Nets grow unboundedly. The primitive here is a **conserved-capacity rotating neurogenesis lifecycle** (immature → mature → distill-and-recycle) grafted onto a frozen/slow LLM backbone. *Strongest equal-budget control:* static equal-width LoRA/adapter + replay at the same total parameter count and distillation compute.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a 10-task sequential domain stream where total extra parameters are capped at 1% of base weights. Compare final-task plasticity (plasticity loss check) and cumulative retention across all 10 tasks against static equal-size LoRA + replay. *Kill:* rotating neurogenesis fails to preserve higher task-10 plasticity or higher task-1..9 retention than static equal-size LoRA + replay.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score tasks 1–5 after task 10 plus base perplexity; require ≤ 2% perplexity drift and positive retention gain over static equal-size LoRA.

#### BM21 · Layer-Staggered Re-Openable Critical Periods (E/I Balance Gate)
- **Mechanism:** Assign each Transformer layer `l` a developmental plasticity state `p_l ∈ [0, 1]` governed by an E/I maturity index (early lexical/syntactic layers start closed `p_l ≈ 0`; mid-late factual/reasoning layers start open `p_l ≈ 1`). Expose a formal **critical-period re-opening primitive**: if late-layer adaptation plateaus while residual error on a new domain stays above threshold (e.g., a new tokenization/syntax/programming language), transiently re-open layer `l-1`'s plasticity window for `S` steps, then re-close it once residual error drops.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Layer freezing / gradual unfreezing (ULMFiT) is a fixed one-shot schedule; [2610.21537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21537) localizes subnetworks statically per task. Modeling **reversible, error-triggered critical-period re-opening** protects foundational early layers during routine factual updates while still permitting deep representational shifts when late layers genuinely saturate. *Strongest equal-budget control:* uniform all-layer LoRA, fixed late-layer-only LoRA, and heuristic loss-threshold unfreezing.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a two-regime stream: (a) 4 factual/domain updates (solvable in late layers) followed by (b) a ciphered/shifted syntax domain (requiring early-layer adaptation). Compare both regimes against all-layer LoRA and late-only LoRA. *Kill:* re-openable critical periods fail to beat all-layer LoRA on (a) retention or fail to beat late-only LoRA on (b) adaptation.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure base syntax/perplexity and factual QA after both (a) and (b); require strictly lower base degradation during (a) than all-layer LoRA and ≤ 2 pp drop after (b).

#### BM22 · Continuous Dendritic Spine Turnover (Filopodia Sampling & Stabilization)
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a fixed sparse budget of `B` tentative rank-1 "filopodia" connections across attention/MLP interfaces. Every `W` episodes, sample new candidate filopodia aligned with recent high-residual activation directions (zero-initialized output gain), test their marginal prequential loss reduction over `W` episodes, **stabilize** (promote to persistent sparse weights) the top fraction that improve loss *without* hurting invariant probes, and **prune + resample** the rest.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Dynamic sparse training (SET, RigL) rewires weights during i.i.d. pretraining based on weight/gradient magnitude; here **filopodia turnover** is a continual-learning structural-search primitive gated by *prequential utility + invariant safety* at constant parameter count. *Strongest equal-budget control:* RigL-style gradient-magnitude regrowth, and fixed sparse LoRA at equal active parameter count.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on an 8-shift non-stationary stream with 0.5% sparse writable parameters. Compare cumulative prequential loss and plasticity across shifts vs. fixed sparse LoRA and RigL regrowth. *Kill:* spine turnover fails to beat fixed sparse LoRA or RigL on cumulative prequential loss by ≥ 5% across shifts 4–8.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify invariant probe accuracy stays within 1 pp of base at every turnover checkpoint (hard-gated by stabilization rule) and held-out old-shift recall beats RigL.

#### BM23 · Astrocytic Conserved Plasticity-Energy Reservoir
- **Mechanism:** Equip each Transformer block (or attention head / MLP slab) with a scalar metabolic reservoir `E_l(t) ∈ [0, E_max]` that regenerates slowly at rate `ρ` per step and is consumed proportionally to `||ΔW_l||_F` whenever the block updates. If a sudden burst of correlated, repetitive, or adversarial examples hits block `l`, `E_l` depletes and automatically throttles further writes in that block (routing residual error to alternate blocks or deferring updates) until the reservoir recharges.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Gradient clipping bounds single-step norm, not *cumulative burst energy* over a window; EWC penalizes movement from a static anchor `W_0`. An **astrocytic depletable/regenerating energy budget** provides task-boundary-free protection against catastrophic burst overwrite while naturally permitting slow, sustained multi-epoch learning. *Strongest equal-budget control:* global gradient clipping, per-step LR warmup/decay, and EWC at matched compute.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream punctuated by 3 abrupt 50-step "spam/burst" segments (repetitive narrow domain or noisy data) interleaved with steady multi-domain learning. Measure damage caused by bursts vs. steady-state learning speed. *Kill:* energy reservoir ties tuned gradient clipping or EWC on burst-forgetting at matched steady-state learning speed.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score old-domain accuracy immediately after each burst segment; require ≥ 6 pp better burst survival than gradient clipping with base perplexity within 1.5%.

#### BM24 · Adaptive Myelination (Early-Exit Residual Shortcuts for Crystallized Skills)
- **Mechanism:** When a continually learned skill or factual mapping stabilizes (low prediction variance across repeated queries over `M` episodes), compile a lightweight **myelinated shortcut** — a low-rank gated skip connection from an early layer `l_1` directly to a late layer `l_2` — that executes the crystallized skill while bypassing intermediate layers `l_1+1 … l_2-1`. Intermediate layers are then physically freed to undergo plastic updates for new skills without perturbing the myelinated path.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Early-exit Transformers (CALM, LayerSkip) optimize inference speed on a *static* pretrained model; PR #46 NB05 modeled integer propagation delays in a tiny recurrent graph. Here **myelination is a continual-learning decoupling primitive**: crystallizing a stable skill into a bypass physically insulates it from mid-layer plasticity while reducing inference latency. *Strongest equal-budget control:* frozen mid-layer LoRA adapter at equal parameters, and static early-exit without mid-layer re-use.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM learns Skill A (syntactic/factual transform), crystallizes a myelinated shortcut `L4 → L20`, then undergoes aggressive unconstrained fine-tuning of layers `L5–L19` on conflicting Skill B. Compare Skill A retention and latency vs. standard LoRA. *Kill:* myelinated Skill A loses > 3 pp during mid-layer Skill B training or fails to beat frozen-adapter routing at equal bytes.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify both Skill A (via shortcut) and untouched base tasks (non-shortcut path, gate ≈ 0) stay within 1.5 pp of pre-Skill-B accuracy.

### Family E · Dendritic, Cortical-Column & Hippocampal-Circuit Primitives

#### BM25 · Active-Dendrite Multi-Branch Context-Gated SwiGLU
- **Mechanism:** Augment each MLP neuron `i` in selected Transformer blocks with `K` lightweight "dendritic segments" (tiny low-rank context vectors `d_{i,1}, …, d_{i,K}` driven by a running pooled context vector `c`). A neuron fires and receives gradient *only* if its somatic feedforward input AND at least one dendritic segment match (`max_k σ(d_{i,k}ᵀ c)`). Multiple incompatible contextual policies share the *same* somatic MLP weights by firing on different dendritic branches, with no task IDs.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Active Dendrites (Numenta / Iyer et al. 2022) demonstrated context-gated MLPs on small vision/RL benchmarks; Thalamically Routed Cortical Columns ([2602.22479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22479)) route across whole LoRA columns. Embedding **per-neuron multi-branch dendritic gating directly into SwiGLU** operates at single-neuron granularity without duplicating whole column weights. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-parameter MoE/LoRA router (column-level routing) and HyperNetwork-conditioned MLP bias.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on 6 context-dependent rule-conflict tasks (same query string requires different outputs under different conversation/system contexts, arriving sequentially). Compare sequential forgetting and context-switch accuracy against equal-parameter routed LoRA. *Kill:* active-dendrite SwiGLU fails to beat equal-parameter routed LoRA on forgetting or fails to generalize to held-out context paraphrases.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score tasks 1–5 after task 6 plus base-model QA with neutral context; require ≥ 5 pp better retention than standard LoRA and ≤ 1.5 pp base QA drop.

#### BM26 · Residual Predictive-Coding Error Layers for Local Continual Updates
- **Mechanism:** Attach a tiny top-down predictor `P_l(h_{l+1}) → ĥ_l` to each Transformer block `l`, pre-calibrated on the base model's normal residual stream. On any new continual-learning example, measure the local layerwise prediction error `e_l = ||h_l - P_l(h_{l+1})||` relative to its calibrated baseline, and restrict weight updates strictly to the 1–2 layers where `e_l` spikes — letting local predictive-coding surprise decide *which* layer's internal world model needs revision.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Predictive coding networks (Rao & Ballard; Millidge et al.) replace backprop entirely on small vision nets; ROME ([2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)) uses expensive causal-tracing intervention sweeps to locate edit layers. Using **calibrated top-down residual prediction error** provides a single-forward-pass, label-free layer localizer for LLM continual learning. *Strongest equal-budget control:* uniform all-layer LoRA, random-2-layer LoRA, and gradient-norm layer selection at equal trainable parameters.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a mixed stream of shallow lexical shifts (early-layer error) and deep factual/reasoning shifts (mid/late-layer error). Check whether `e_l` correctly localizes the shifted layer and beats gradient-norm layer selection on retention. *Kill:* `e_l` layer localization fails to beat random-layer or gradient-norm layer selection on old-task retention at matched new-task accuracy.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate 100 base QA + syntax probes after 5 mixed shifts; require ≤ 1.5 pp drop and strictly less interference than all-layer LoRA.

#### BM27 · Hippocampal Activation-Index Pointers (Pre-Consolidation Episodic Reinstatement)
- **Mechanism:** Instead of storing raw text episodes in a prompt (which causes attention crowding/dilution, ledger PR #89/#103) or immediately editing weights, store each episode as a **compact hippocampal index pointer**: a `(key_embedding, sparse_layer_steering_code)` tuple (a few hundred bytes) that, on retrieval, reinstates the episode's mid-layer residual pattern via a lightweight additive injection. Periodically distill frequently reinstated pointers directly into MLP weights and retire the pointers.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** RAG / A-MEM stores text tokens; TF-Engram ([2607.07388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07388)) hashes token n-grams into static embedding tables; [2606.26806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26806) consolidates text into LoRA. A **reinstatement-pointer index** directly implements Teyler & DiScenna's Hippocampal Indexing Theory in LLM residual space, decoupling episodic capacity from context-window length. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-byte text RAG (top-k retrieval into prompt), and equal-byte per-episode rank-1 LoRA bank.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B/1.5B LLM on the repo's PR #89/#103 multi-rule accumulation benchmark (48+ concurrent non-conflicting rules where in-context rules lose ~56–58% uplift). Compare rule margin and multi-rule composition against equal-byte text RAG and rank-1 LoRA bank. *Kill:* activation-index pointers suffer the same dilution curve as text RAG or fail to compose 2 simultaneously reinstated pointers.
- **Old-task retention check:** With index retrieval off (or on unrelated queries), base-model outputs are untouched (0 pp drop); after sleep distillation of 20 pointers, verify base QA stays within 1.5 pp.

#### BM28 · Dentate-Gyrus Sparse Key Orthogonalizer + CA3 Attractor Readout
- **Mechanism:** Place a two-stage hippocampal front-end in front of a writable parametric key-value memory layer in an LLM: (1) a **DG expansion-sparsification** projection (`d → 8d` with frozen random/orthogonal projections + `k`-WTA sparsity) that separates *near-duplicate or conflicting keys* (e.g., temporally versioned facts) into nearly orthogonal sparse addresses; and (2) a 2-step **CA3 recurrent Hopfield attractor** over stored sparse patterns that completes noisy/paraphrased queries back to the exact clean sparse address before reading the value.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Sparse Memory Finetuning ([2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103)) and product-key memory use learned dense/product keys that collapse near-duplicates onto overlapping slots; Hopfield layers (Ramsauer et al.) do pattern completion without a DG orthogonalization front-end. Pairing **DG orthogonalization + CA3 attractor completion** targets the exact paraphrase-vs-collision tradeoff exposed in `tree/forward-trace-compile.md` (where paraphrase fitting leaked +34.7 pp onto wrong subjects). *Strongest equal-budget control:* standard dense key-value memory (PKM/SMF) and cosine-keyed external table at equal memory bytes.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream of 100 near-duplicate entity pairs (e.g., `"Alice Chen (2024)"` vs. `"Alice Chen (2026)"`) queried with held-out paraphrases. Measure cross-entity leakage and paraphrase recall vs. dense PKM and cosine retrieval. *Kill:* DG+CA3 fails to cut cross-entity leakage by ≥ 40% vs. dense PKM at matched paraphrase recall.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 100 base-model factual QA prompts + perplexity with the DG/CA3 memory attached and populated with 100 entries; require ≤ 1 pp accuracy loss.

#### BM29 · Apical-Basal Dual-Stream Credit Assignment in Transformer Blocks
- **Mechanism:** Augment selected Transformer layers with a lightweight parallel **apical feedback stream** (running alongside the standard **basal** inference residual stream) that carries structured future-turn feedback or verifier critiques backward across turns/layers. At each block, compute local weight updates from the divergence between basal and apical activations (`ΔW_l ∝ (h_apical,l - h_basal,l) h_basal,l-1ᵀ`), leaving the basal inference pass strictly unchanged whenever the apical stream is silent.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Segregated-dendrite / burstprop models (Guerguiev et al., Payeur et al.) exist for small feedforward vision networks; TextGrad ([2406.07496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496)) passes text critiques to prompts. Embedding an **apical feedback stream directly into Transformer residual geometry** creates a local, non-disruptive credit-assignment primitive for continual weight updates. *Strongest equal-budget control:* standard backprop LoRA from the same terminal verifier/critique, and prompt-only critique conditioning (Reflexion-style).
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a 4-task sequential symbolic reasoning stream with turn-level natural-language/structured feedback. Compare task-to-task retention and credit accuracy vs. standard backprop LoRA at matched update FLOPs. *Kill:* apical-basal updates fail to beat standard backprop LoRA on retention or lose > 5 pp on new-task accuracy.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate tasks 1–3 after task 4 + 100 base QA prompts with the apical stream turned off; require ≤ 1.5 pp base drop and better old-task retention than backprop LoRA.

#### BM30 · Cortical Lateral-Inhibition (k-WTA) Write Competition Across Sub-Blocks
- **Mechanism:** Partition an adapter or MLP expansion layer into `M` fine-grained sub-blocks and apply differentiable **Mexican-hat lateral inhibition** (center excitation, surround suppression) across sub-block norms during training forwards. Only the winning `k` sub-blocks stay active and receive gradients on the current batch, while inhibited neighbors receive strictly zero gradient — driving automatic, task-ID-free modular specialization without an external router network.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Standard MoE routers suffer router collapse and catastrophic router forgetting when trained sequentially without replay; [2602.22479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22479) uses explicit thalamic routing. **Lateral-inhibition write competition** replaces a learned router with self-organizing competitive exclusion directly on activation norms. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-parameter top-k MoE-LoRA with load-balancing loss, and dense LoRA + replay.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on 6 sequential, unmarked domains (no task IDs, no domain labels) with `M=16, k=2` sub-blocks. Measure sub-block specialization entropy and domain-1 retention after domain 6 vs. MoE-LoRA. *Kill:* lateral-inhibition competition collapses to ≤ 2 sub-blocks across all 6 domains or loses to equal-parameter MoE-LoRA on domain-1 retention.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score domains 1–5 after domain 6 + 100 base QA prompts; require ≥ 5 pp higher mean old-domain retention than MoE-LoRA and ≤ 1.5 pp base QA drop.

### Family F · Immune, Epigenetic & Ecological Biology Primitives

#### BM31 · Somatic Hypermutation & Clonal Selection of Framework-Locked CDR Perturbations
- **Mechanism:** Lock 99.5% of the LLM ("immunoglobulin framework") and designate a tiny sparse set of rank-1 "CDR" (complementarity-determining region) slots on 2–3 mid-layer attention/MLP interfaces. Under a new task or rare failure mode, spawn `P` localized random/directed micro-mutations of the CDR slots, score each clone in forward-only passes on both (a) the new task and (b) a compact self-tolerance panel, and **clonally expand** only the highest-affinity self-tolerant clone into the active bank.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Evolutionary model merging / LoRAHub searches over whole pre-trained adapters; MeZO averages perturbations into one gradient estimate. **Framework-locked CDR hypermutation** turns continual adaptation into a *zero-backprop, self-tolerance-filtered clonal selection primitive* that can run on inference-only hardware. *Strongest equal-budget control:* MeZO-LoRA at matched forward passes, and random-search over unconstrained LoRA at matched parameter count.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM (CPU inference-only) on 5 sequential rare-skill / edge-case adaptation slices (20 examples each). Compare affinity gain and zero-regression compliance vs. MeZO-LoRA at matched forward-pass count. *Kill:* CDR clonal selection fails to beat MeZO-LoRA on retention-constrained accuracy or fails to improve over frozen base by ≥ 10 pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Hard self-tolerance gate during selection + held-out 200-item base QA & perplexity audit post-selection; require ≤ 0.5 pp drop on base QA.

#### BM32 · Thymic Negative Selection on Atomic Weight-Delta Components
- **Mechanism:** Before committing any continual-learning update `ΔW` (produced by SFT, RL, or consolidation), decompose `ΔW` into atomic rank-1 singular components or per-neuron row deltas `ΔW = Σ_j a_j`. Pass each atom `a_j` through a fast "thymic negative selection" screen against a compact self-antigen panel of core base capabilities (syntax, safety, arithmetic, core facts): **delete** every self-reactive atom whose perturbation on the self-panel exceeds threshold `ε`, and commit only the surviving self-tolerant sum `ΔW_safe`.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** PR #46 NB34 ("tolerance nursery") narrowed an *agent interrupt's trigger detector* on synthetic states; orthogonal projection ([2610.40483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40483)) projects the *whole* update vector uniformly, which can attenuate useful new-task directions alongside harmful ones. **Atom-level thymic deletion** operates selectively on the *spectrum/neurons of `ΔW`*, discarding toxic atoms cleanly while keeping benign new-task atoms at full strength. *Strongest equal-budget control:* global KL-regularized SFT, uniform norm scaling of `ΔW`, and SVD-based orthogonal projection (OGD/AlphaEdit) at matched self-panel compute.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM fine-tuned on 5 sequential tasks; decompose each task's LoRA/MLP `ΔW` into per-neuron or rank-1 SVD atoms and sweep deletion threshold `ε`. Plot new-task accuracy vs. base-task retention against OGD projection and KL-SFT. *Kill:* thymic atom deletion fails to dominate both uniform scaling and OGD projection on the new-vs-old Pareto front.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate both the self-antigen panel AND an *unseen* held-out base capability panel (to verify non-overfitting to the thymus); require ≤ 1 pp drop on the unseen panel.

#### BM33 · Epigenetic Mitotic Bookmarking for Post-Reset / Cross-Upgrade Fast Reacquisition
- **Mechanism:** When a deployed LLM undergoes a periodic weight reset (to clear accumulated drift) or a base-model upgrade, extract a minuscule set of **epigenetic bookmarks** — sparse directional seeds / low-rank anchor perturbations (`< 0.05%` of weights, scaled so rest-state logit shift is `< 0.01`) along the principal task-adaptation directions previously discovered. At rest the model behaves identically to the fresh/upgraded base, but when a few examples of a previously mastered domain reappear, the bookmarks nucleate **3–10× faster reacquisition** than cold-starting.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Connects to ledger Top-10 #5 (*relearning as the read head*, PR #72) and model-upgrade obsolescence (Fleet Q2/Q3), but turns the phenomenon into a **designed forward-transfer primitive** inspired by mitotic bookmarking of transcription factors. Distinct from saving full task adapters (which go stale on base upgrades and consume linear storage per task). *Strongest equal-budget control:* cold-start fine-tuning on the same few examples, equal-byte stored LoRA deltas (for reset), and random-direction low-rank warm starts.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM (or 4-layer Transformer) trained on 4 domains, reset to `W_0` with bookmarks retained (`||ΔW_bm||` bounded so base KL `< 0.01`), then re-exposed to 5–20 examples per domain. Measure steps-to-criterion vs. cold-start and equal-byte LoRA. *Kill:* bookmarked reset fails to cut reacquisition steps by ≥ 2× vs. cold-start at base KL `< 0.01`, or fails to beat equal-byte stored LoRA after a base perturbation.
- **Old-task retention check:** Before re-exposure, verify zero-shot base QA and perplexity are within 0.5 pp / 0.5% of the fresh base; after re-acquiring Domain 1, verify Domains 2–4 bookmarks remain intact and base QA stays within 1.5 pp.

#### BM34 · Allosteric Conformational Modulation Sites on Frozen Weight Matrices
- **Mechanism:** Rather than adding linear deltas `W + ΔW` (which superpose and interfere across tasks), keep the core weight matrix `W = U Σ Vᵀ` frozen and install tiny **allosteric regulatory sites**: low-rank effector-triggered gates `g_k(e)` that apply localized multiplicative scaling or Givens-like 2×2 rotations to *disjoint* singular-value bands of `Σ` when an effector cue `e` (domain/context signature) binds. Multiple continual updates occupy non-overlapping allosteric sites on the same matrix with bounded cross-talk.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Singular-Vector Selection (2610.11076)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11076) selects singular vectors for static additive fine-tuning; OFT/BOFT applies global orthogonal rotations per task. An **allosteric binding-site primitive** decouples *effector recognition* (binding site) from *conformational effect* (disjoint singular-band modulation), allowing compositional multi-effector binding on one frozen matrix. *Strongest equal-budget control:* equal-parameter additive LoRA bank, and OFT with matched block size.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on 5 sequential tasks + held-out pairwise combinations of two tasks active in the same prompt. Compare single-task retention and 2-task composition against additive LoRA and OFT at equal parameters. *Kill:* allosteric sites tie additive LoRA on 2-task interference or fail to compose two simultaneous effectors without > 5 pp degradation.
- **Old-task retention check:** When no effector binds (`g = 0`), base outputs are **exact**; evaluate single-task retention of Task 1 after installing Tasks 2–5 (require ≥ 95% of initial Task-1 score).

#### BM35 · Ecological Character Displacement (Repulsive Key-Space Niche Partitioning)
- **Mechanism:** When a new continual-learning write collides with an existing stored memory (detected by high cosine similarity `> ρ` in MLP key space or attention routing between the new input and a protected anchor), instead of overwriting the old memory or freezing the new one, execute a **symmetric repulsive character-displacement update**: push the new memory's key and a small plastic copy of the colliding old memory's key apart along an orthogonal residual direction while preserving *both* outputs on their respective contexts — actively manufacturing non-overlapping niches on demand.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Orthogonal projection (GPM/OGD) only constrains the *new* update to the complement of old keys, which fails when new and old keys are nearly collinear; PR #46 NB02 traded territory via boundary probes in a toy linear bottleneck. **Character displacement** is an LLM-native *joint repulsive key-splitting operator* that resolves collinear collisions directly in MLP/attention key space. *Strongest equal-budget control:* GPM/OGD projection onto the old-key null space, and joint replay of the colliding old + new examples.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a stream of 40 intentionally collinear/overlapping factual rules (high cosine similarity in mid-layer residual space, conflicting outputs). Compare both new-rule acquisition and colliding-old-rule retention against GPM and equal-step joint replay. *Kill:* character displacement fails to beat GPM by ≥ 8 pp on colliding pairs or loses to equal-step joint replay at matched compute.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score both the 40 colliding old rules AND 100 non-colliding base QA facts + perplexity; require ≤ 1.5 pp drop on non-colliding base tasks.

#### BM36 · Quorum-Sensed Autoinducer Phase Transition (Context → Weights Crystallization)
- **Mechanism:** Every in-context fact, user preference, or learned rule remains purely in external/episodic context initially (zero weight-overwrite risk) while incrementing a decaying **autoinducer sketch** keyed by semantic cluster + distinct-episode counter. Parametric consolidation into LLM weights triggers *only* when a cluster's autoinducer concentration crosses a sharp quorum threshold `Q*` across independent episodes — turning the ad-hoc "when to consolidate" decision into a sharp, hysteretic phase-transition primitive.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** PR #46 NB36 used spatial quorum sensing across *multi-agent workers* to expand write permissions; Selective Parametric Consolidation ([2606.26806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26806)) and ENGRAM ([2611.09472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09472)) trigger consolidation by per-item verification or fixed episode counts. Using a **decaying cross-episode autoinducer quorum** filters out both one-off noise and single-session repetition spam while crystallizing truly recurring knowledge cleanly. *Strongest equal-budget control:* fixed-frequency periodic consolidation, raw occurrence-count thresholding (without distinct-episode/decay gating), and pure RAG with no consolidation.
- **Cheapest first test:** 0.5B LLM on a 200-episode stream mixing (a) 20 genuinely recurring persistent rules, (b) 40 one-off rules, and (c) 20 "burst-spam" rules repeated 10× in a single episode then never again. Measure persistent-rule latency/accuracy vs. weight bloat and forgetting. *Kill:* quorum crystallization fails to reject burst-spam better than raw-count thresholding or fails to beat periodic consolidation on retention at matched consolidated-fact recall.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate 100 base QA facts + perplexity after 200 episodes; require ≤ 1 pp drop (vs. larger drift under periodic consolidation) and preserved recall of crystallized rules with context cleared.

## 5 Ranked Deeper Questions (Child Branches to Spawn)

1. **Rank 1 · Does an LLM fundamentally need an offline "sleep" phase, or can every sleep function be realized online inside silent degrees of freedom?**
   - *Why it is deep:* Biology pays an enormous evolutionary cost (vulnerability, lost foraging time) for offline sleep. Recent LLM papers split sharply: [2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979) and [2605.26099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099) argue LLMs need offline sleep cycles, while [2609.31630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31630) claims replay in silent degrees of freedom removes the need for an offline phase. Separating *which* functions genuinely require sensory disconnection (e.g., BM01 bridge stitching, BM02 SHY downscaling, BM03 REM recombination) from those that work online (BM06 awake micro-sleep) would establish a foundational architectural law for continually learning LLMs.
   - *Discriminating test:* Match total FLOPs and wall-clock budget on a 1.5B LLM across a 2-hop compositional + noisy stream, pitting online silent-DoF updates against periodic NREM/REM sleep cycles; measure 2-hop shortcut emergence and base-task retention.

2. **Rank 2 · Is LLM continual learning bottlenecked more by *where* updates land (structural/engram allocation) or *when/under what global state* they are permitted (neuromodulatory & disinhibitory gating)?**
   - *Why it is deep:* Most LLM CL work focuses on *where* (LoRA placement, sparse masks, MoE routing). Biological cortex pairs spatial sparsity with rapid global state switches (ACh encode/recall, LC-NE unexpected uncertainty, VIP→SST disinhibition, reconsolidation windows). If global state gating beats spatial allocation at equal parameter/compute budgets — or only works when combined with it — that immediately determines which primitive family should anchor the next generation of CL architectures.
   - *Discriminating test:* 2×2 factorial pilot on 0.5B/1.5B crossing spatial allocation (BM19 CREB excitability / BM20 neurogenesis) × global gating (BM07 ACh switch / BM08 LC-NE broadcast / BM11 disinhibition) at matched writable parameter count on a stream with both high-margin overrides and distractor bursts.

3. **Rank 3 · Can biological *active forgetting* primitives (SHY downscaling, BCM anti-hijacking LTD, thymic atom deletion, spine turnover) improve old-task retention *without* storing or replaying old data?**
   - *Why it is deep:* Machine learning equates forgetting with failure and fights it with replay or freezing. Biology treats selective forgetting/downscaling as an *active homeostatic primitive* that raises signal-to-noise ratio and prevents feature hijacking. If data-free biological downscaling/deletion (BM02, BM15, BM32, BM22) closes even half the gap between plain SFT and full replay on old-task retention, it unlocks replay-free continual learning under strict privacy/storage budgets.
   - *Discriminating test:* Pit data-free SHY downscaling + BCM LTD + thymic atom deletion against equal-compute L2-SP, gradient clipping, and 5%/20% experience replay across a 10-domain LLM stream, measuring old-task accuracy and feature-hijacking rate.

4. **Rank 4 · When should a continual learner modify *how frozen weights are expressed* (gain fields, active dendrites, allosteric sites, activation-index pointers) versus *modifying the weights themselves* (neurogenesis, character displacement, reconsolidation)?**
   - *Why it is deep:* Expression-level primitives (BM12, BM25, BM27, BM34) offer near-zero base forgetting by construction, but may hit a sharp expressivity ceiling on deep compositional reasoning or multi-hop synthesis where weight-level primitives (BM01, BM18, BM20, BM35) shine. Mapping the exact crossover between expression-level routing and weight-level rewriting (at matched bytes and latency) directly complements the repo's steering-tomography and rate-drift work without touching optimizer states.
   - *Discriminating test:* Sweep task count (`N = 4 → 64`) and compositional depth (`1-hop → 3-hop`) on 0.5B/1.5B comparing gain-field/allosteric/dendritic gating against neurogenesis/character-displacement weight writes at matched persistent bytes.

5. **Rank 5 · What is the capability ceiling of local, zero-backprop biological write primitives (STC tags, three-factor Hebbian writes, apical-basal predictive error, CDR hypermutation) in pretrained LLMs?**
   - *Why it is deep:* Backpropagation through long autoregressive agent trajectories is memory-bound, non-local, and hard to run continuously during live inference. Biological local write rules (BM09, BM13, BM26, BM29, BM31) run in O(1) sequence memory and naturally support delayed verification, but it is genuinely unknown whether they can learn multi-step procedural skills in a Transformer or are fundamentally limited to single-hop factual binding and local policy steering.
   - *Discriminating test:* Head-to-head comparison of three-factor SwiGLU (BM09), STC tag-and-capture (BM13), and apical-basal credit (BM29) against equal-FLOP LoRA backprop and MeZO across three difficulty tiers: (1) single-fact binding, (2) contextual rule switching, and (3) 3-step algorithmic composition.
