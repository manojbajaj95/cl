# Verifier-backed forks → an algebra of effects or a witness that one is missing?

Parent: [`tree/rl-composable-forks.md`, PR #79](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/79/files), ranked branch 1 · Depth 4 · Idea generation only; no pilots run.

## Root cause

**The parent is missing a measurement, not a clever basis.** A fork at a decision state compares two interventions, `a` and `a′`, completed by policy `π`. Its scalar effect is `Δ(V) = E[V | do(a), π] − E[V | do(a′), π]`. Even if names, predicates, and decision states line up across tasks, single-check scores do not specify how often the checks pass *on the same continuation* under either arm. That missing dependence can change with the action, and updating `π` can change it again without changing the single-check scores. A new coordinate can reorganize information that was measured; it cannot recover information the fork discarded.

PR #79 gives an arithmetic example, not a failed pilot. The marginal success pairs are `(0.9, 0.6)` under `a` and `(0.7, 0.8)` under `a′`. The individual effects are `+0.20` and `−0.20`. Under independence the conjunction scores are `0.54` vs. `0.56` (effect `−0.02`); another compatible coupling gives `0.60` vs. `0.50` (effect `+0.10`). The Fréchet intervals for conjunction success are `[0.50, 0.60]` and `[0.50, 0.70]`, hence the effect can be anywhere in `[−0.20, +0.10]` without further constraints. Any deterministic transformation of those same marginal inputs, linear or nonlinear, returns the same answer for both worlds. The parent's reported task-vector interaction contrast of `+6.9` vs. `+0.3` points under a template change is a separate literature observation ([2608.09490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09490)), not evidence that these fork effects were measured or that a particular coordinate works.

There are two easy ways to mistake an identity for a primitive:

- At fixed `π), the full signed continuation law `Pπ(·|do(a)) − Pπ(·|do(a′))` acts linearly on verifier scores, but `V₁ ∧ V₂` is a product of scores, not their sum. Keeping the whole law is a useful oracle, not a small transferable representation. Even that signed difference alone need not supply the two arm-wise laws for nonlinear decoding or for transport to a changed continuation policy.
- If checks really are independent **within each arm**, the difference of the two **log success probabilities** for a conjunction adds across checks (when all success probabilities are positive). Neither ordinary log odds nor differences of raw probabilities do that in general. A proof of the product identity does not show where independence holds, at what cost to check it, or whether it survives learning.

The candidate primitive is a **compact effect record plus a composition rule with an explicit validity domain**. For a new task, return a numerical effect or interval, and, when the sign is unresolved, specify which observation could resolve it. Keep the realized residual `r = Δjoint − Δdecoded` (only measurable with joint truth) separate from a **pre-query** failure flag. A certified interval that spans zero means the available observations do not determine the sign under the stated assumptions, not that the actual prediction is wrong. A retrospective residual or calibrated error score alone proves neither non-identifiability nor retention.

A plausible positive case is a shared ordering of checks with small sufficient conditioning sets. In each arm, log conjunction success is the sum of log pass probabilities conditional on earlier checks passing. The full chain rule is free but entails a separate measurement for every relevant context. The research question is whether a bounded context gives reusable addends, and whether a failed or unobserved conditioning assumption can trigger a cheap, targeted repair. Decode the two arm probabilities before subtracting: adding log ratios alone does not recover the magnitude of the probability difference.

## Prior art

Screened using the PR #6 [idea ledger and flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6/files) and an **abstract-only** literature scan (Hugging Face paper API and arXiv abstracts; alphaXiv quota unavailable). The distinctions below are hypotheses, not claims of exhaustive novelty.

| Tangent-space task arithmetic [2305.12827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827); Tangent Transformers/TAFT [2307.08122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08122) | A more additive coordinate obtained by linearizing fine-tuning is already a baseline. |
| Task-feature specialization [2604.17078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17078), KFAC/dataless disentanglement [2602.17385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17385), gradient plus second-order account of task vectors [2508.16082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16082), provable task-vector effectiveness [2504.10957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10957) | A specialized subspace, whitened basis, or curvature correction is not a new effect object. Ask what becomes identifiable from different observations. |
| Early alignment as a mergeability forecast [2606.19549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19549); shared LoRA B-space interference [2604.16826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16826); common/unique subspaces (CUSD) [2611.04108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04108) | Alignment predicting a failed merge is the closest control for a residual alarm; it is not itself a certificate about opposite-sign worlds. Match the early update/gradient information and the false-alarm rate. |
| aTLAS block scaling [2407.02880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02880), Fisher merging [2111.09832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09832), layer-adaptive Fisher merging and nonlinear responses to merge coefficients [2603.21705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21705) | Reweighting directions or showing a nonlinear coefficient response is not sufficient. |

Use these as controls where applicable. |

**Shared controls.** Compare against a constant training-set predictor, raw scalar-fork addition and retrieval, linearized/tangent-space addition, Fisher-weighted merging, and the early-alignment mergeability forecast of [2606.19549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19549). For weight-writing variants, score all policies on the same fork/verifier targets and give the forecaster the same early update budget; for purely tabular or symbolic tasks, the weight-space controls need a common trainable router to be meaningful. A toy counterexample with no weights can establish a separation, not a win over those controls. Also compare the strongest per-card control below, a same-budget fresh target fork, and the parent's per-clause marginals plus Fréchet bounds. For merged mechanisms, stack the relevant controls at equal cost; the ledger rejects sparse second-order merge surrogates that merely tie direct group audits and lesson-interference predictors that merely tie function-vector cosine.

**Shared first-test protocol (proposed, not run).** Start with exact finite transition tables or a tiny CPU string DSL with constrained decisions and verifiers; use a small trainable router for the continual-learning arm. Hold out verifier families, compositions and policy versions, not just rollout seeds. Include both composable and opposite-sign observational twins, and test naturally occurring compositions separately from hand-constructed twins. Before ranking failures, check target spread against seed spread and split-half truth reliability (or enumerate exactly). Match verifier evaluations, continuation samples, storage and update budgets; report error on accepted effects, coverage at matched false-alarm rate, and cost to resolve abstentions. In each retention check, train on new tasks B using the proposed record, then evaluate normal old tasks A without updating or replaying them first: report old-task success change against the same-budget control and the frozen pre-B reference, alongside B learning. The idea-specific retention readout below is additional; frozen-score accuracy alone never counts as retention.

## Raw ideas (unfiltered)

### A. Measure what a basis change cannot recover

1. **Proof-carrying abstention.** **Mechanism:** Return two feasible arm-wise continuation laws, both consistent with the stored observations, with opposite target signs; pair the witness with a query that distinguishes those laws, without claiming it resolves every remaining law. **Why new:** A checkable counterexample and repair operation, not a stronger point predictor or merely PR #79's interval. Related to PR #73's witnesses; here the witness is executable as two continuations and evaluated by its repair cost. **First test:** Enumerate feasible laws for four checks; compare witness-guided queries with arbitrary joint queries from the same Fréchet intervals. **Retention:** After B, independently revalidate A's certificates against fresh A forks. **Kill:** If explicit witnesses resolve no more sign decisions per call than interval-width sampling, they are just a verbose bound.
2. **Disagreement separation oracle.** **Mechanism:** Select one observable predicate whose outcomes split the *entire* set of compatible laws by target sign, or prove no query in the allowed family can separate it in one shot. **Why new:** PR #79 #17 selects by effect variance; this asks for a worst-case sign decision over an experiment language. **First test:** Enumerate query trees over six Boolean predicates; compare worst-case depth and average cost against variance and direct target measurement. **Retention:** Check earlier A decision trees still resolve their signs after B or explicitly lose validity. **Kill:** Stop if fresh target forks or ordinary variance ranking match cost at the same coverage and error.
3. **Privacy-preserving coupling vault.** **Mechanism:** Keep arm-specific aggregates of allowed co-occurrence queries, with the query chosen to preserve sign decisions without releasing trajectories or prohibited joint cells. **Why new:** A capability-limited effect record with a joint privacy/identification frontier, not the ledger's coded merge interference or an unrestricted low-rank sketch. **First test:** For a four-check table with one protected variable, compare sign coverage against equal-bit noisy count release and a direct permitted audit under the same declared disclosure bound. **Retention:** Check A coverage under the remaining disclosure budget after learning B. **Kill:** If direct noisy counts dominate the privacy/utility frontier, discard the vault.
4. **Exchangeable effect tiles.** **Mechanism:** Store each arm's histogram of the number of passing checks in an exchangeable block, plus a certificate of the symmetry scope. Decode multiple thresholds from one record. **Why new:** The reusable unit serves an entire verifier family, not a per-clause basis or graph of pairwise interactions; equal two-way marginals can leave different count tails. **First test:** Predict held-out threshold and conjunction signs under shuffled check names; compare pair sketches, simple count histograms without a scope check, and full joint audits. **Retention:** Measure old rare-threshold success and certificate failures after B breaks a symmetry. **Kill:** If the scope check buys nothing when exchangeability breaks, keep the ordinary histogram, not a primitive.
5. **Quantifier witnesses.** **Mechanism:** Store the arm-wise distribution of the *first failing index* for a family of ordered universal verifiers, and track censored observations separately from passes. **Why new:** Gives one record for a family of prefix quantifiers and distinguishes unobserved later checks from success; clause averages cannot recover the stopping distribution. **First test:** Generate matched marginals with different failure clustering; predict withheld prefix lengths and compare survival curves, pairwise coupling and direct prefix checks. **Retention:** Recheck A's long-prefix completion and stopping tail after B. **Kill:** If standard survival summaries already transport across reordering or there is no reuse beyond literal prefixes, this is a survival-analysis application.
6. **Contextual parity probes.** **Mechanism:** Use an exactly queryable higher-order contrast only when two candidate worlds match *all* lower-order measurements; index the contrast by the verifier context where it is needed. **Why new:** Stores the missing interaction as a targeted diagnostic rather than fitting the ledger-killed coded pairwise merge surrogate. **First test:** Use even/odd three-bit parity twins with matching single and pair marginals; then test held-out contexts with direct group audits and sparse second-order regression at equal queries, including the cost of constructing probes. **Retention:** Test A compositions that require the stored contrast and ordinary non-parity A tasks after B. **Kill:** If direct group queries are as cheap over multiple downstream uses, the parity witness is a counterexample, not a reusable record.
7. **Conditional log-pass coordinates.** **Mechanism:** Keep two tables of local conditional log pass probabilities keyed by the smallest shared conditioning sets that predictions actually need. Add within each arm only when those sets are supported and validated for the composed check sequence; use a targeted conditional audit if they are not. **Why new:** The chain rule is old. The candidate object is a reusable context reduction with arm-specific scope and a repair query, not PR #79's choice between global probability transforms. **First test:** Compare held-out conjunctions in a sparse conditional network and a parity world with matched local statistics; test against fitted Bayesian networks, independence, and full-prefix counts at equal verifier cost. **Retention:** Check A conjunction effects and normal A success after B induces a hidden dependency. **Kill:** If ordinary Bayesian-network queries with the same observations and a support check tie, or context size grows with every composition, this is just the chain rule.

### B. Compose traffic at a boundary, not credit numbers

8. **Interventional continuation seam.** **Mechanism:** Split a pipeline at a boundary; record arm-dependent flows into seam states and continuation values after *forced seam assignments*, not merely after naturally reached states. **Why new:** PR #79's transfer matrices place the burden on a given abstraction; forced seams test whether a proposed state actually separates upstream action from downstream outcome. **First test:** Use two upstream histories with equal seam labels but unequal effects after the same forced assignment; compare seam splitting with observationally fitted matrices and direct forks. **Retention:** Recheck A seam sufficiency and normal A success after B. **Kill:** If causal seams need essentially the full history to match fresh forks, there is no reusable compression.
9. **Reachability-aware null effect.** **Mechanism:** Distinguish `zero on supported states`, `undefined off support` and `cancels across states`; propagate these three cases through compositions. **Why new:** A zero becomes a typed observation, not an orthogonal gradient or an unconditional sign certificate. The three cases give equal training scalars but behave differently under a changed routing distribution. **First test:** Hold out a composition that routes mass to a formerly unreachable branch and another that reweights canceling branches; compare with support-only and unconditional-interval controls. **Retention:** Re-evaluate formerly reachable low-traffic A states after B. **Kill:** If a support bitmap plus empirical conditional means ties the richer record, use that baseline.
10. **Adaptive verifier filtration.** **Mechanism:** Record what a verifier has seen before it selects the next question; compose with kernels conditioned on that information, treating unasked checks as missing. **Why new:** Targets verifiers that change what they inspect based on the fork's answer, not the fixed predicate lists or code-conditioned regressor in PR #79. **First test:** Give two adaptive inspectors the same atomic check frequencies but different selection rules; compare predictions with inverse-propensity weighting and full recorded histories. **Retention:** After B, test A under its original inspector and the withheld rebalance of its query order. **Kill:** If standard off-policy evaluation at the same logging cost ties, this is a logging convention.
11. **Resource-indexed fork convolution.** **Mechanism:** Carry each arm's joint histogram over elapsed resources and seam state; compose while decrementing a shared budget before decoding success. **Why new:** Arms with identical total success and mean cost can reverse under a shared deadline, which a scalar efficiency penalty or untyped transfer matrix misses. **First test:** Two-stage repair chains under withheld deadlines; compare success-plus-mean-cost, exact resource DP and empirical trajectory replay with matched memory. **Retention:** Check old deadline tails and unrestricted A success. **Kill:** If equal-size trajectory replay or ordinary resource DP answers the same family without a reusable seam record, drop it.
12. **Semantic splice as a negative control.** **Mechanism:** Swap a continuation fragment between fork arms only where public boundary state matches, and treat changed effects as evidence of private state leaking across the splice. **Why new:** A cut validator for proposed additive coordinates, not PR #57's rule-vs-shortcut experiment or PR #79's generic composition fuzzer. **First test:** Add hidden counters to a string transducer; compare detection per execution with natural path perturbations and forced seam assignments. **Retention:** Recheck A cut validity under learned B routing. **Kill:** If splicing mostly creates invalid histories, or the two simpler controls localize the same breaks, the splice offers no evidence.
13. **Refundable effect composition.** **Mechanism:** For a detachable subflow, store context needed to remove its contribution from an already summarized composite, together with explicit causes that make deletion impossible. **Why new:** Unlike the flood's commutative update rules, the operator removes a component of a *behavioral prediction*, not a weight update or checkpoint. **First test:** Delete one branch from a shared-state verifier pipeline; compare incremental predictions with full recompilation and marginal subtraction at equal bytes. **Retention:** Remove a B component and verify established A behavior and fork signs without restoring the old policy. **Kill:** If only independent components admit valid removal, subtraction already solves the interesting part that remains.

### C. Make version changes explicit

14. **Policy-overlap debt.** **Mechanism:** Transport the part of each arm's law covered by logged continuation support and carry unmatched mass as an interval contribution through downstream verifiers. **Why new:** A forecast that says which arm and seam lost support, not PR #79's local drift slope or an alignment confidence score. **First test:** Switch a finite router to previously unseen paths without changing early gradient alignment; compare clipped importance weighting, vanilla support bounds and direct forks. **Retention:** Evaluate A support debt and A success after each B transition. **Kill:** If ordinary off-policy bounds give the same risk/coverage and query allocation, the accounting adds nothing.
15. **Ancestor-only recalibration.** **Mechanism:** Build a dependency map from continuation transition changes to effect records; invalidate only downstream uses reachable from a changed mechanism, keeping all uncertainty tags in the graph. **Why new:** A compositional invalidation calculus specific to interventional credit, narrower than the PL flood's cache invalidation analogy; equal observed task accuracy need not imply equal fork reuse. **First test:** Alter one stochastic subroutine, including a case with grossly unchanged marginal success; compare verifier calls with fresh refit and ordinary incremental dataflow that sees the same dependency graph. **Retention:** Report stale A forks missed and unaffected A decisions unnecessarily revoked. **Kill:** If generic incremental recomputation gives the same guarantees and cost, no new primitive.
16. **Mixture-version interpolation certificate.** **Mechanism:** If the continuation selects its version once, after the intervention and independently of the arm, interpolate the *arm distributions*; if it can switch versions at each step, require switch-conditioned observations. **Why new:** The proposal is to detect and repair a change in what a policy-version label means, not to rename the exact linear identity for whole-policy mixtures. **First test:** Compare episode-level and step-level mixtures with equal average routing and opposite conjunction signs; run policy-conditioned kernels and naive interpolation controls. **Retention:** Check old A policies and held-out A switch schedules after B. **Kill:** If switch-conditioned records cannot beat ordinary policy-conditioned rollouts at equal budget, only the known mixture identity remains.
17. **Paired cross-version bridges.** **Mechanism:** Execute old and new continuations from the same arm-specific saved seam, then fit bounds on transport using paired transition outcomes and a separate accounting for seams that vanished. **Why new:** PR #79 conditions on checkpoints; this stores explicitly matched counterfactual pairs with a declared support boundary, not a low-rank task/policy split. **First test:** Compare saved-seam pairs with independent resampling, CRN without seam matching and fresh target forks after a local routing edit. **Retention:** Ask whether the same bridge budget catches A regressions at rare states. **Kill:** If common random numbers alone tie, the bridge is simply paired variance reduction.
18. **Moving sign boundaries.** **Mechanism:** Store finite witness policies on either side of an effect reversal and use interval checks to test whether the boundary persists along a path of learning updates; do not infer monotonicity from the endpoints. **Why new:** An expiring boundary witness, not a Jacobian norm or PR #79's one-step slope. **First test:** Hold out middle checkpoints with matched endpoints but different sign excursions; compare equal-budget adaptive bisection, uncertainty sampling and a second-order continuation fit. **Retention:** Track A sign reversals and recovered A success after B and return to A. **Kill:** If excursions are detectable only by auditing every checkpoint or ordinary adaptive sampling ties, the boundary is not a portable record.
19. **Translate the verifier, retest the intervention.** **Mechanism:** Under a policy upgrade that changes available actions, map both fork arms through an executable action refinement and keep a third arm that disables the refinement's added decision. **Why new:** Separates change in intervention from change in continuation; a rename or cross-model alignment can silently change the estimand. Unlike PR #51's generic action map, the third arm audits the discrepancy. **First test:** Refine a one-step primitive into two actions with a new bypass; compare to direct action-ID alignment and an unguarded executable map. **Retention:** Verify that old A actions retain their intended effect on normal A tasks under the upgrade. **Kill:** If the extra arm catches no distinct sign errors or costs as much as fresh target measurement throughout, keep the simpler mapping.

### D. Turn repair into an operation

20. **Counterexample-driven coordinate split.** **Mechanism:** When a local summary aliases histories with opposite composed signs, split the coordinate using the cheapest observation that distinguishes the histories; keep the old projection for verifiers that do not need the split. **Why new:** PR #60 allocated edit addresses after collisions; this split preserves an old effect algebra as a coarser projection while adding a measurable intervention distinction. **First test:** Withhold new verifier families that successively separate aliased states; compare ordinary partition refinement, a learned abstraction and full-log replay at matched cost. **Retention:** Verify old A predictions do not need new reads and the learned policy still solves A. **Kill:** If standard refinement yields the same sharing and all safe splits track the full state, no new representation.
21. **Proof of first-order sufficiency on a restricted query family.** **Mechanism:** Certify which specific verifier queries have constant effect across all compatible continuation laws, and attach that query family to each record rather than claiming universal linearization. **Why new:** Linear algebra with a *given* law or a known basis is a control; the proposed operation is to learn and check scope cheaply across unseen verifier syntax and policy changes. **First test:** Mix unseen syntactic rewrites of identifiable and non-identifiable queries; compare an exact constraint solver and a static rank/span test with the same source observations. **Retention:** Revalidate A's scoped certificates under B and check ordinary A success. **Kill:** If a static span-membership test or a standard constraint solver ties on soundness and cost, this is the vocabulary of PR #78, not a new primitive.
22. **Associativity repair.** **Mechanism:** When two parenthesizations of a lossy composition disagree, return the intermediate statistic that must be shared to make them agree at the decoder, or exhibit indistinguishable records with conflicting target signs. **Why new:** Exact kernel multiplication is already associative; the object here is a repair for different *lossy* compilation orders and not PR #79's overlap gluing score. **First test:** Compose three small transducers via two quantization orders; compare selective refinement with uniform precision, exact replay and a simple discrepancy alarm. **Retention:** Reuse old A summaries after adding B operators that were not available when A was summarized. **Kill:** If increasing precision uniformly or standard numerical error propagation buys the same recovery, no algebra primitive.
23. **Counterexample escrow for on-policy writes.** **Mechanism:** Allow a cheap decoded effect to drive an update on B only while storing an executable witness and its pre-update A behavior; if the witness fails after the write, recompute at the nearest sufficient seam before revising credit. **Why new:** The escrow is a repairable *effect assumption*, not PR #67's weight rollback or PR #79's random verifier correction. **First test:** In an A→B→A router stream, compare witness-triggered credit revision with random spot-checks plus replay and fresh target forks at equal total cost. **Retention:** Report worst protected A-task loss before correction as well as post-correction A success. **Kill:** If random checks plus replay match B learning and both A retention measures, targeted escrow is just a gate.
24. **Ambiguity budget for a sequence of choices.** **Mechanism:** Allocate queries by the effect of a sign error on downstream reachable decisions, carrying dependence between unresolved signs instead of assigning each fork a separate confidence tax. **Why new:** PR #80 carries ambiguity about hypotheses; this composes operational consequences of ambiguous credit and allows one observation to discharge several decisions. **First test:** A two-stage router where a single latent coupling controls three forks; compare independent uncertainty sampling, robust planning with independent intervals, and a joint-law oracle. **Retention:** Reserve the same verifier budget for A and check worst-task A success after B. **Kill:** If ordinary robust planning given the same feasible set does the allocation just as well, it is a planning application.
25. **Trade a point prediction for a reusable falsifier.** **Mechanism:** For two high-performing effect algebras that agree on current compositions, archive a verifier family where their predictions diverge under a future policy change, together with a generator that can recreate that divergence. **Why new:** PR #57's crucial experiments distinguish current strategies; this is a future-version falsifier of a *composition law* that may be cheaper to store than either rival model. **First test:** Hold out the policy change on a small transition family; compare a generated falsifier with retaining raw forks, a PR #79 composition fuzzer and randomly chosen stress tasks. **Retention:** Include ordinary A tasks and previously informative A discriminators; test that the generator still separates the candidate laws. **Kill:** If ordinary counterexample replay or a stress-test generator with no algebra input has equal future discrimination per byte, the falsifier is not a new object.

## Ranked deeper sub-questions

1. **Can a compact additive coordinate carry a cheaply repairable proof of its own composability boundary?** (#1, #2, #7, #20–#22.) Highest chance: an effect record could return a decoded estimate on one query and two opposite-sign witnesses plus a discriminating measurement on another. This is stronger than learning an additive chart or predicting which updates merge. First split: does the witness reduce verifier calls on withheld compositions at the same soundness and coverage as Fréchet bounds plus fresh audits?
2. **Can forced continuation seams give a small alphabet that survives both new verifier programs and policy writes?** (#8–#12, #15, #17.) A validated seam would be an interface for effect reuse, not a static representation. The decisive comparison is against an observational transfer matrix: does forcing the seam rule out aliasing, and can it do so at substantially less cost than retaining histories?
3. **Can one restricted-family record identify enough different downstream effects to amortize a higher-order measurement?** (#3–#6, #13.) The candidate primitive is a scoped measurement object reusable by verifiers that were never run. It loses if ordinary sufficient statistics or same-budget direct group audits dominate after charging for scope validation.
4. **Can effects survive a learner update without silently changing the intervention being predicted?** (#14, #16–#19.) A versioned record that can name a support or intervention break is more promising than another drift regressor. Kill the branch if naive off-policy reuse plus fresh forks matches how often old-task effects and behavior can be retained.
5. **Can a sound abstention prevent forgetting without turning every B update into a replay or verification pass?** (#23–#25.) Lower primitive odds: this is a decision policy unless there is a repairable obligation from branch 1 that ordinary uncertainty gating cannot express. Require B learning and A retention wins over stacked random checks plus replay at the same total budget.
