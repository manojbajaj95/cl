# Cross-curvature brakes: spend missing C progress to brake another coordinate

Child of [Weight-gap handoffs](weight-gap-handoffs.md) ([PR #272][parent]). The [score-3 review][triage] keeps #05, where P plants a gap on a coordinate that Q couples to the momentum coordinate, and #16, where the lagging history's extra P movement is itself missing C progress. Ideas and exact arithmetic only; no learner run or GPU work.

**Bet.** The same truthful C update can improve a lagging history on C and leave a weight difference that Q turns into a brake on a different coordinate. **Verdict.** Untested. An exact quadratic shows that a cross landing can reduce reader error, but a larger gap can merely move error from one old reader to another. Neither reviewer seed is new here; the candidate operation is using *the same* C progress as the directional landing, beyond what a stack of separate learning and damping steps buys.

## What would count

- **Same contract as the parent.** A and B are established skills; two permitted writeback orders produce paired states `(θ,m,v,t)`. P and Q are already-due C work over truthful records. Freeze the task menu, schedule, held-out reads, acceptable C gains, and tail before selection. No slot writes, resets, waits, future labels, hidden order tags, or synthetic targets in the candidate. Bill any extra probes, examples, retained state, failed attempts, and fallback. Slot resets, crossed slots, and edited Hessians are diagnostic only.
- **Name the differential and the reader.** With heavy-ball, `Δm' = βΔm + Δg_Q`. For common quadratic Q and a landing `d` on x, the contribution to y is `H_yx d`. For smooth nonlinear Q use the gradient difference over the two actual endpoints, not a Hessian at one endpoint extrapolated across a boundary. Individual weights and slots may both be large even when the differential is small. Score absolute held-out A/B performance on each history as well as paired reader error, from P's first write through the tail; a smaller differential is not retention if both histories fail.
- **No credit for making the leader worse.** Declare what the lagging history was missing on held-out C before P. Require it to close that deficit by improving on the same frozen C slice, with a floor on the leading history and a check after Q. Match actual C progress per history for comparisons, not just pooled training loss. For joint cards compare each mechanism alone and their best stack, including [C-work pricing][work], at the same data, work, observations and retention floors.
- **Check the obstruction before fitting.** At matched C progress and A/B retention, ask whether the truthful menu reaches more than one landing and whether reader error varies beyond measurement noise. The [ledger][ledger] closes the retention-matched plasticity-reserve forecast line and the switched-Gramian penalty; this node does not revive either. Use exact quadratic arithmetic or actual native paired rollouts, with sign-flipped and uncoupled negative controls. If a tuned diagonal P, a same-coordinate handoff or matched extra C work ties, say so. Prior-art comparisons are local, not clearance.

## One exact fixture (not evidence of learning)

**X1 · A light brake helps both reads; a hard brake can just move the damage.** Take paired difference `s₀ = (Δx, Δy, Δm_x, Δm_y) = (d, 0, 0, 1)` at the post-P cut. Let Q have Hessian `[[3,b],[b,3]]`, with `β = 1/4` and learning rate `1/6`. The old y reader sees no gap at the landing tick, but the x reader sees `d`. Q's first momentum difference on y is `1/4 + b d`. To count the whole tail exactly, form the transition matrix `T` from `Δm' = H Δθ + Δm/4`, `Δθ' = Δθ − Δm'/6`. For each row below solve `S − Tᵀ S T = diag(w,1,0,0)`; the energy including the landing tick is `s₀ᵀ S s₀`. For these b values the Hessian eigenvalues lie in [1,5] and the heavy-ball modes are stable.

| Q coupling b | Landing d | First Δm_y | y-only energy (w = 0) | Joint x+y energy (w = 1) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0 | 1/4 | 4967/1424640 | 1/210 |
| 2 | −1/32 | 3/16 | 187283/121569280 | 59/17920 |
| 2 | −1/16 | 1/8 | 44473/91176960 | 37/6720 |
| 2 | −1/8 | 0 | 8067/7598080 | 47/2240 |
| 0 | 0 | 1/4 | 5/1728 | 5/1728 |
| 0 | −1/32 | 1/4 | 5/1728 | 1829/442368 |
| −2 | 0 | 1/4 | 4967/1424640 | 1/210 |
| −2 | −1/32 | 5/16 | 2307001/364707840 | 533/53760 |

At `d = −1/32`, y energy falls by about 56% and joint energy by 31%; removing the coupling removes the y benefit, and reversing it hurts. Halving first-step momentum with the larger `−1/16` gap makes joint energy about 16% worse than no landing; zeroing it makes joint energy over four times worse. These are prescribed differences, not evidence that a legal P reaches them, that x movement is useful C, that either history retained absolute task service, or that the cross landing beats a tuned baseline.

## Idea cards

### 01. Plant the gap on x; let Q brake y — primitive candidate (#272 #05)
**Mechanism.** Use the truthful P menu to plant a gap outside the sensitive y read, then route its effect into y through Q's cross-curvature. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Nesterov lookahead][sutskever] uses an optimizer-chosen offset; #272 #05 already gives the cross-task version. The test is legal reachability and a reader benefit diagonal tuning cannot recover, not discovery of an off-diagonal Hessian. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a fixed two-feature P menu around X1, checking coupling signs and all reachable prefixes. **Same-budget control.** Best y planting, tuned diagonal P, waiting and a reset as diagnostics. **Old-task retention.** Check A on y and B on x in both histories, including the tick hidden from y.

### 02. Make the x landing the lagging history's missing C progress — primitive candidate (#272 #16 × #05)
**Mechanism.** Choose a due P batch so extra useful movement by the lagging history leaves the right differential on x for Q to brake y. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #272 #16 supplies useful progress and [#259][work] prices C work; neither alone requires the held-out improvement to land in the *off-diagonal* braking direction. **Cheapest first test.** In three-feature regression, start from a pair with a verified C deficit; enumerate P and cross beneficial versus misaligned C directions with coupled versus uncoupled Q. **Same-budget control.** Best stack of separate C catch-up and y or x handoff, equal-work plain C, and a diagnostic reset. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B on x, y and their correlated rare slice during P and Q; retest both histories on C after the tail.

### 03. Brake through a middle coordinate that never gets a planted gap — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** P lands on x; due Q₁ converts it into a z update; due Q₂ couples z to y even though neither successor directly couples x to y. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Switched reachability][hybrid] explains the product of maps; #272 #18 tests order for one landing. The claim is useful access to a brake forbidden by either direct path alone, without a z write by P. **Cheapest first test.** Three-feature heavy-ball with exact enumeration of truthful P and two sparse Hessians, including reversed Q order and a severed edge. **Same-budget control.** Best direct landings and native two-order rollout search. **Old-task retention.** Check B on the temporarily loaded z at the intermediate cut and A on y throughout.

### 04. Let two required successors return the borrowed coordinate — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Q₁ takes a useful x gap into y damping; Q₂ removes residual x debt without reopening y, so x is safe for a later use that could not start after Q₁ alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Gap handoffs][gap] damps state discrepancies and [successor-funded returns][returns] buys later service; demand a paired path with both braking and a time-bounded x release. **Cheapest first test.** Two-feature quadratic with a fixed x reuse at the second cut; enumerate sign pairs and include a Q₂ that repairs x but re-excites y. **Same-budget control.** Independent x repair plus best brake, exact schedule search, and a release check at every cut. **Old-task retention.** Check old x calls before and after the release, and y after Q₂.

### 05. A brake that switches sign at a useful feature boundary — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use due C work to move one history across a feature boundary, where the same Q record couples x to y with a different sign. Take credit only for the actual gradient difference across endpoints. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #272 #06 uses a stiff versus flat cell; this needs sign reversal of the off-diagonal response, not just a larger gain. **Cheapest first test.** Two ReLU features with opposite x–y coupling in adjacent cells; enumerate near-boundary P and test a host with both cells sharing the sign. **Same-budget control.** Best within-cell cross landing, tuned diagonal P and native rollout search. **Old-task retention.** Check held-out A/B near both sides of the boundary, not just the changed history.

### 06. Split one C deficit into ordinary installments that open the second brake — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** The first useful installment changes which feature the second P batch trains. That second movement closes the remaining C deficit while generating a cross landing unavailable from either batch at the starting weights. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Installment turns][installments] already crosses a feature boundary for a cap-safe suffix; here test whether the newly reachable suffix lands a genuinely useful cross-coordinate brake. **Cheapest first test.** Two due ReLU batches, both orders, plus a fixed-cell copy with the same starting gradients. **Same-budget control.** Best installment-then-handoff stack, static batch matching, and equal-work plain C. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B after installment one and after the second closes the C gap, then probe after Q.

### 07. One useful landing, two conflicting momentum debts — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Try to pay y and z debts with one legal C movement on x; when the ratios conflict, use a second *already useful* C direction rather than inventing a correction record. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Gradient surgery][pcgrad] resolves conflicting simultaneous task gradients; this is feasibility of a future paired response under the ordinary Q update. **Cheapest first test.** Four-feature regression, one rank-one and one rank-two cross block, with debtor vectors both inside and outside the legal response cone. **Same-budget control.** Independent per-receiver brakes, PCGrad and exact menu enumeration. **Old-task retention.** Check separate old y and z calls as well as a call using both; averages conceal an unpaid debt.

### 08. Use one physical gap for two deadlines without double-counting it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Credit the landing on a second receiver only if the first due read and update leave enough useful differential for the second deadline across all admitted orders. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Successor-funded returns][returns] checks live capacity for two calls; #272's common landing fits alternatives. Here the later brake must survive an earlier brake actually being used. **Cheapest first test.** Two Q batches that individually brake, one pair whose product exhausts the gap too early and one that leaves enough. **Same-budget control.** Independent landings, common-landing minimax, and exact joint-prefix search. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B at both deadlines including the order that spends the gap early.

### 09. Complementary useful movements cancel an old read without cancelling the brake — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Close two measured C deficits with signed movements whose reader projections cancel, but whose Q cross-responses add. Require both C slices to benefit in held-out data. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [OGD][ogd] and #272 #12 already use reader null spaces; this asks whether actual deficit-closing batches can assemble a neutral landing more cheaply than null projection plus separate C work. **Cheapest first test.** A three-feature menu with old read `x₁+x₂` and Q coupling through `x₁−x₂`, including a menu where C only permits same-sign movements. **Same-budget control.** OGD plus the best independent brake, and plain C matched per slice. **Old-task retention.** Test the nominal null read and a rare old input with unequal x weights at each prefix.

### 10. Let the old-use cost choose which real C deficit to close first — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** If two overdue C slices yield equal total gain but different off-diagonal responses, choose the slice whose movement brakes the reader with the tighter live deadline. Both slices still get their declared C service. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GraB][grab] orders gradients for training and [#259][work] prices work. The additional claim is exchanging the *timing* of equal useful work for reader safety through Q. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze two slices with opposite coupling signs and swap old-read deadlines without changing inputs. **Same-budget control.** C-loss ordering, GraB, tuned diagonal P and the separate catch-up-plus-brake stack. **Old-task retention.** Check both old reads before and after each deadline, including the reader not prioritized first.

### 11. Learn whether C paid for the brake or just for a benign mean shift — improvement (causal audit)
**Mechanism.** Factor mean position and paired gap in a diagnostic, then keep the real endpoints for the candidate. Require a gain attributable to Q's response to the differential, rather than both histories simply moving toward a safer absolute region. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Standard factorial ablation; it removes a confound in #272 #16, not a new optimizer. **Cheapest first test.** Exact quadratic with one finite-difference null case, then a tiny ReLU host where the same mean change can move both histories onto a flat cell. **Same-budget control.** Mean-only and gap-only crossed states as diagnostics; ordinary same-C P as the deployable control. **Old-task retention.** Score absolute A/B error on both paths so common damage cannot pass.

### 12. Match Q's marginal features and cut only the cross edge — improvement (falsifier)
**Mechanism.** Replace a putative x–y channel in a diagnostic with a sign-balanced, truthful Q design of matched feature norms and work; also use the algebraic diagonal ablation to isolate the edge exactly. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Covariance ablation is standard and #272 #05 already asks for an uncoupled Q; use it to separate coupling from easier training, not to claim a mechanism. **Cheapest first test.** Linear regression with matched diagonal, initial mean gradients and opposite or zero off-diagonal; measure actual Q progress instead of assuming the designs are equally useful. **Same-budget control.** Repeat no landing and tuned P under each Q. **Old-task retention.** Check both the `x+y` and `x−y` slices, which can swap difficulty despite matched marginals.

### 13. Carry only the braking response across equivalent feature bases — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** Define sender and receiver directions by declared C and old reads, and test whether their measured transfer survives a change of coordinates when the full optimizer is transformed accordingly. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #272 #15 uses output-space handoffs; linear coordinate covariance is not a contribution. The audit separates that identity from robustness to a changed optimizer. **Cheapest first test.** Rotate X1 and its readers; then rescale features with unadjusted diagonal learning rates as a genuinely different host. **Same-budget control.** Matched output-space handoff and tuned diagonal steps in the new host. **Old-task retention.** Check the same held-out old functions before and after rebasing.

### 14. Distinguish a cross brake from an Adam denominator brake — improvement (native-state audit)
**Mechanism.** Require lower reader error due to an off-diagonal gradient response, not solely a swollen second moment that slows all future movement on y. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Adam][adam] and #272 #07 already explain second-moment effects. The audit tests attribution when both mechanisms predict smaller first displacement. **Cheapest first test.** On the same two-feature menu, compare native Adam, diagnostic fixed denominators and a Q with no cross edge; include a follow-on task that needs rapid y movement. **Same-budget control.** Tuned diagonal Adam and the parent's joint-moment repair. **Old-task retention.** Check ordinary A/B through the tail and after y is recruited again.

### 15. Branch on a lawful C read only when it identifies the needed brake sign — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Take an ordinary C read after a safe common prefix; commit to a suffix only if every history compatible with that output admits its sign and progress floor. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Installment turns][installments] branches on a public observation; the added test is a read separating *required brake signs* at matched observed C loss, not merely identifying the lagging history. **Cheapest first test.** Three aliased pairs, two requiring opposite signs; replay with a quantized read that merges those two. **Same-budget control.** Fixed common suffix, equal-work diagnostic read, and a hidden-state oracle as a ceiling. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B for the whole observation class, including the pair that forces refusal.

### 16. Close a delayed C deficit only when the upcoming Q can spend it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When truthful C work can legally be ordered two ways, move scarce C catch-up to the cut where the resulting differential is useful; do not delay beyond the fixed C service floor. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #272 #10 asks about unknown arrival time and [GraB][grab] balances fixed examples. Here test whether allowable timing buys a useful directional landing without extra work or a future ticket. **Cheapest first test.** Two release windows, one early C deadline, and one Q arrival where early catch-up dissipates the gap before use. **Same-budget control.** Earliest-deadline C, best fixed schedule and order enumeration. **Old-task retention.** Test the old y service while waiting for each permitted Q and x before the C deadline.

### 17. Learn two deficits without making a cycle of incompatible pairwise brakes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For three or more legal histories, synthesize one native P policy and derive all pair gaps from the actual landings. Reject a set of desired pairwise offsets if it cannot close around the history cycle. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Pairwise-potential consistency is standard; #272's common landing covers many successors, not one lawful policy realizing gaps for multiple history pairs while serving C. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three histories and two useful P directions; include a case where every pair passes in isolation but no shared menu choice passes the triangle. **Same-budget control.** Pairwise oracles, best common P and exhaustive joint search. **Old-task retention.** Check absolute A/B/C performance on all three histories; do not omit the history common to the two losing pairs.

### 18. Preserve a nonlinear old read by moving along its level set only as far as C pays — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use successive truthful C steps to follow an old read's curved level set until Q sees enough differential to brake y. Finite-prefix safety, not just a tangent-null direction, decides admissibility. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [OGD][ogd] projects onto local first-order constraints, and #272 #12 already names A-null landings. The stronger claim is a legal finite path whose curvature matters and whose steps are independently useful C. **Cheapest first test.** Tiny two-layer regressor with an old product read `xz`; enumerate C steps and include paths with matched endpoints but unsafe middle reads. **Same-budget control.** Reprojected OGD plus an independent landing and an exact level-set oracle. **Old-task retention.** Check old product reads at each step and a held-out input outside the fitted level set.

### 19. Spend the landing only when both histories can still learn the next C slice — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Refuse a brake that quiets y by pushing both histories into a dead feature cell for a declared follow-on C slice. Choose a reachable landing preserving a useful gradient there, not merely preserving current C outputs. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** The ledger's plasticity-reserve predictor failed after retention matching; do not forecast a generic reserve. Require a named, already-scheduled acquisition test and an actual independent gain above matched controls. **Cheapest first test.** Two-feature ReLU menu, two landings matched on current C and old reads, and an already-due next slice that needs the live feature. Stop if there is no gain spread. **Same-budget control.** Actual short rollouts, tuned P and plain C; no Gramian score. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B and previously learned C before and after the next acquisition, not just when the feature first goes quiet.

### 20. Follow more than one signed path only if they reinforce on real runs — improvement (obstruction audit)
**Mechanism.** Test whether two candidate x→y paths reinforce, cancel, or change sign across legal schedules; treat pairwise signed edges as hints, not as an additive certificate. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Switched reachability][hybrid] covers composition; the ledger's killed switched-Gramian ranking must not be smuggled back in. **Cheapest first test.** Four-feature quadratic with opposite-sign paths and two Q orders; compare actual rollouts with the local sign rule. **Same-budget control.** Enumerated paired rollouts and strongest single-path handoff. **Old-task retention.** Read both intermediate features and y after the cancelling order.

### 21. Test for a no-go when every useful C move strengthens the debt — improvement (falsifier)
**Mechanism.** Enumerate the actual legal, retention-safe P menu and mark directions that advance lagging C but increase the y response under Q. Distinguish an empty joint menu from first-step reinforcement that still lowers tail reader error. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** #272 #19 proposes a wrong-sign control; a finite feasibility audit makes the no-go local instead of asserting that optimization cannot escape it. **Cheapest first test.** Mirror C's held-out target in a small regression and compare both worlds at matched incoming deficits, with no cherry-picked Q. **Same-budget control.** Equal-work extra C, best non-C planting where allowed, and exact native tail search. **Old-task retention.** Check the old-reader floors in the feasible and refusing worlds; refusal is not successful C service.

### 22. Charge the conjunction for its strongest separate stack — improvement (go/no-go audit)
**Mechanism.** Measure the frontier of held-out lagging C gains against worst-prefix old-task loss and tail reader error. A joint choice counts only if the best separate C-plus-brake stack cannot reach the same point on equal resources. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** This is the [ledger][ledger]'s merge rule applied to #272 #05 and #16, not a new search criterion. **Cheapest first test.** Small exact menu with both a deliberately easy tie and a held-out interaction case; enumerate both families and publish the full frontier, not just their selected winners. **Same-budget control.** Tune diagonal P, same-coordinate landing, cross landing, extra C steps, and the best legal stack; the exhaustive joint oracle is a ceiling. **Old-task retention.** Require all A/B slices, including the rare sender-coordinate read, to pass on both histories before a point enters the frontier.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can the same truthful P movement buy held-out C catch-up and cross-coordinate braking beyond the best separate stack?** Start with 01, 02, 12 and 22. Match gains per history and prefix retention before looking at reader energy. If replacing the coupling or mirroring the useful C direction leaves the advantage intact, the proposed interaction did not cause it. If the stack ties, keep the cross-curvature explanation but drop the joint-primitive claim.
2. **When is a blind spot at the landing tick a transfer of damage rather than a useful brake?** Start with X1, 07, 09 and 18. Report the sender, receiver and rare mixed readers separately across prefixes. A first-step sign, one favourable projection or a smaller paired gap cannot substitute for absolute retention.
3. **Can ordinary successor sequences brake and then release the borrowed direction before its next use?** Cross 03, 04, 08 and 20 on a frozen two-order family. Test lost edges and reversed orders before increasing host complexity; if direct landings or matched native planning tie, the sequence is not buying a reusable operation.
4. **What should a tag-free policy do when useful progress and brake sign disagree across indistinguishable histories?** Use 15, 17 and 21. Separate physical infeasibility, missing public evidence and inability of the search to certify; charge refusals and audit work against useful C service.
