# Post-training OOD 2: what survives when the hint is gone?

**Status:** Ideas only. All first tests are CPU proposals; no learner run or GPU use.

**Parent:** [Post-training for lifelong OOD learning](root-post-training-ood.md) ([PR #309][parent]). **Review seed:** [Flood triage, lines 426–429][triage]; line 427 is the #309 verdict, and **A6 (hint-bandwidth ratcheting)** is its sole score-3 idea. The adjacent roots are not seeds for this node. A6 finds the shortest successful hint for the current learner, then tries to transfer the skill one rung down. The reviewer requires full-solution OPSD + GRPO, R³, same-length filler, random rung, a task with no useful short hint, and five old families evaluated at zero hint bits. Follow the [idea ledger][ledger]: distinguish primitives from improvements, price stacked controls, and test ordinary old tasks.

## Where A6 breaks

1. **A successful message need not be teachable.** A one-bit action label can solve a binary fork without revealing how to resolve the next one. Conversely, a long explanation can supply no information unavailable from the public task. Literal length, hidden information, and unaided reconstruction cost are three different axes.
2. **There may be no ladder.** Two short hints can each fail yet work together; a longer hint can block a valid alternative; a learner update can reverse which hint helps. Searching rungs and reporting the lucky first pass conflates assistance with sampling noise.
3. **Withdrawal changes the states the learner reaches.** A later step that works with a hint may never be visited without it. The learner can also keep treating silence, timing, or hint format as a cue. Neither copying the successful completion nor making assisted batches rarer establishes that the learner recovered the missing decision.
4. **A lower bill now can raise the next bill.** The cheapest scaffold for one task can overwrite a route used by old tasks or leave a private dependency that a new task cannot satisfy. Count the cost of later zero-hint learning and the capability lost along the way, not just the best rung reached today.

**Literature boundary.** I searched Hugging Face Papers for adaptive/annealed hints, reverse curricula, privileged distillation, symbolic counterexamples, and intervention-based imitation; the linked paper records were checked against their abstracts on HF or arXiv. These are bounded comparisons, not exhaustive novelty claims. Plain withdrawal is already covered: [R³][r3] slides the starting point back along a demonstration; [PrefixRL][prefix] varies off-policy prefix length and demonstrates unprefixed transfer; [Unlocking the Unsolvable][unlock] explicitly withdraws partial teacher traces; [ADHint][adhint] adapts hint prefix length to difficulty; [HATCH][hatch] anneals assisted trajectory weight. [HiLL][hill] already teaches a hinter to favor hints with lower reliance and better unaided transfer. [Guide][guide] uses natural-language guidance with the learner deriving the chain itself; [PSP][psp] places privileged information in the sampler instead of the loss; [JOLT][jolt] regularizes the teacher toward what the student can use. **Do not claim a new primitive for shorter prefixes, adaptive hint weights, low reliance, or a different teacher prompt.** [OPSD][opsd] uses reference reasoning as privileged context, whereas [SDPO][sdpo] distills feedback-conditioned predictions. Their [OOD reassessment][reassess] is evidence against assuming privileged content drives transfer, not evidence that every short hint harms it. Also out of scope: ledger-killed EMA/refresh thresholds, sequential teacher depletion, generator-identity replay, and rare-event prefix splitting.

## Common CPU test contract

- **Worlds.** Small finite grammar/proof, observation-tool, or planning environments with enumerable solution classes and exact verifiers; use a tabular policy or tiny shared-feature learner as appropriate. Plant a positive (material assistance but reconstructible from public input) and a negative (no affordable hint helps or the answer depends on genuinely hidden state). These tests can falsify mechanisms, not establish LM-scale transfer.
- **Meter.** Freeze the codebook and public decoder per comparison. Charge transmitted hint bits including choice, timing, termination and any task-dependent metadata, plus verifier calls (including failed trials), rollouts, update steps, public tool work and carried bytes. Price preparation/search and reusable dictionaries separately and report their amortization. Compare at the same resource caps or show a Pareto curve; do not convert a token count into an estimate of actionable information.
- **Controls and estimand.** Match starting checkpoint, environment, training task arrivals, fresh rollout seeds and total budgets. Compare parent A6 and the reviewer's controls, plus the nearest comparator named on each card; when combining mechanisms use the strongest same-information stack, not each component alone. Test the rung on samples independent of those used to choose it; measure fixed-budget zero-hint Pass@1 and calls to reach a preset zero-hint target on unseen variants and later tasks, including failures to reach it. Check high-budget Pass@k separately where policy diversity matters. For a stochastic pilot, pair independent streams and report intervals (at least 10 seeds for an anomaly follow-up); don't read a threshold from a single lucky pass.
- **Retention (every card).** Keep five prelearned families out of hint selection and testing of new tasks. Evaluate their ordinary tasks before the new stream, after each consolidation boundary, and after later transfer, with no hints, retrieval aids added by the method, or extra tools. Report per-family loss, including rare previously valid routes, against pre-update and matched GRPO/strongest control. Parent screen: aim for at least +5 pp on held-out unaided new tasks versus full-solution OPSD; reject if old family mean drops >2 pp or falls below GRPO retention. Card-specific checks below are in addition to that common panel.

## A. Make the unit of assistance identifiable

### 01. Teach the decision, hide which world it came from — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** At two public histories with different correct decisions, ship the *same* hint. It names a procedure that must recover the choice from public evidence rather than supplying a recommendation correlated with the answer. Admit a hint only when it works on both histories. **Novelty / control.** [HiLL][hill] prices dependence for correct trajectories; this is a shared-message constraint across answer-changing worlds. Compare HiLL-style reliance + answer-scrubbed hints under the same bits. **Cheapest first test.** In a two-register DSL, hold the hint fixed while swapping which register contains the needed predicate; compare with a one-bit action label and with a procedure whose predicate is not publicly visible. **Old-task retention.** Re-score old register tasks where either register really matters; report the worse branch, not just the aggregate.

### 02. Close the silence channel — improvement
**Mechanism.** Make the timing and absence of help independent of the hidden lesson label: use mandatory fixed response slots and condition the teacher's candidate set only on logged public events. Compare the same public policy after replacing all slots by literal emptiness. **Novelty / control.** [HATCH][hatch] changes the weight of assisted samples; this is a protocol fix for selection leakage, not a new signal. Compare a padded random-rung schedule plus HiLL-style withdrawal. **Cheapest first test.** Plant a hint scheduler whose choice to send nothing reveals parity although payloads are blank; reject the fix if a metadata-only decoder still beats chance or content-based teaching loses all advantage. **Old-task retention.** Re-score old tasks with meaningful in-world delays, which must not be erased along with teacher timing.

### 03. Keep the minimal successful antichain — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use independently retractable semantic atoms; retain subset-minimal successful sets, and record witnessed inhibitory additions. Select erasures from that frontier instead of extrapolating up or down a total order. **Novelty / control.** [ADHint][adhint] varies a trajectory-prefix ratio; the proposed object is a non-monotone set of alternatives and conflicts, not a longer ladder. Compare exhaustive same-atom search and A6 at fixed queries; claim only an amortized win against exhaustive search. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate four hints with two disjoint minimal successful pairs and one long conflicting superset; train on unseen mixtures. **Old-task retention.** Individually check two previously learned solution routes that require different pairs of underlying skills.

### 04. Cost the hint by its deployable replacement — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Attach a public reconstruction program to a hint and label its execution cost and domain of validity. Teach a replacement for the expensive step, not the shortest string; unobservable hints receive no finite reconstruction certificate. **Novelty / control.** [Conditional Information Bottleneck for reasoning][cib] compresses completions under a semantic prior; the candidate object is a verified public implementation of a privileged input, not surprisal or a length penalty. Compare that penalty plus an exact per-task verifier and the shortest public script. **Cheapest first test.** Use lookup, parity, and hidden-bit forks with equal-length hints but different public compute bills. **Old-task retention.** Time normal zero-hint execution on old cheap- and expensive-lookup tasks; flag extra compute as well as errors.

### 05. Teach with certified ambiguity — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reveal a set of viable strategies with no unique action implied at the target decision, and require the learner to discriminate members using public evidence. Certify residual ambiguity by showing two compatible public prefixes whose optimal actions differ under that message. **Novelty / control.** [Guide][guide] gives natural-language help without supplying the chain; the extra object is a checked ambiguity requirement on the advice, not a shorter sketch. Compare diverse Guide-style hints plus a matched-size random candidate set. **Cheapest first test.** Two of four legal rewrites remain after each hint, and opposite public guards select between them; compare against a singleton answer hint and a set that omits a viable option. **Old-task retention.** Score old tasks stressing each rewrite, and track whether Pass@1 hides loss of an alternative at Pass@k.

### 06. Give the learner a reproducible disproof, not a repair — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A privileged teacher chooses a witness against the learner's current hypothesis, but sends only a public query recipe. The learner must generate the disconfirming outcome itself and train the subsequent change of hypothesis; the successful solution is never shown. **Novelty / control.** [SymCE][symce] trains verified counterexample emission, and parent B6 predicts failure given an external counterexample; here the supervision is the learner's *ability to recover* a useful falsifier from public queries. Compare B6 + query imitation and same-budget random counterexamples. **Cheapest first test.** Distinguish absolute-value from sign rules by reconstructing one legal boundary query; include a hidden counterexample that cannot be reproduced. **Old-task retention.** Verify old true rules still receive acceptance, and score old design tasks that need negative as well as positive examples.

## B. Withdraw the dependency, not the prompt

### 07. Commute the erasure with the transition — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For a hinted prefix and a public reconstruction, compare “erase, reconstruct, then act” against “act with the hint, then erase” using the resulting public state and legal continuation set. Transfer only a verified action class, not the hinted trace. **Novelty / control.** [DAS][das] uses interchange interventions to test causal abstraction and parent B3 tests suffix execution; this uses a commuting check as a gate for removing a specific informational dependency before the decision. Compare B3 plus reliance masking; an identical continuation trace is not required. **Cheapest first test.** In a two-stage rewrite system, two routes agree on the current answer while one silently consumes a resource needed for a later query. **Old-task retention.** After erasure, execute old multi-step tasks that spend the resource on a different later query.

### 08. Backchain to the first unaided state that can service the lesson — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Pair a hinted decision with the earliest predecessor on *actual zero-hint rollouts* from which a public reroute can reach an equivalent decision. Train the learner to reach it as well as act there, with separate verifier credit. **Novelty / control.** [R³][r3] and [Unlocking the Unsolvable][unlock] start along a demonstration; [SPLICE][splice] repairs at a checked prefix. Here the reroute must start on unassisted occupancy and may use neither prefix. Compare both a reverse curriculum and same-budget direct training on reroute + decision as a stacked baseline. **Cheapest first test.** A five-state graph with a hinted step behind a door reachable unaided only by a different detour; remove the detour as a negative. **Old-task retention.** Check old tasks where the door is irrelevant or the detour is costly so routing does not become unconditional.

### 09. Relocate the hint across the decision cut — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Deliver the same semantic hint before or after one candidate decision; score both downstream behavior and the next unaided attempt. Only a before/after contrast at one cut is used to choose a dependency to replace, with feedback after a decision barred from acting as its input. **Novelty / control.** [AHEAD][ahead] supplies correction on selected error steps; the claim is an intervention on the availability of identical information around a cut. Compare step selection + delayed-feedback training with the same timestamps and calls. **Cheapest first test.** In a symbolic tool sequence, a lemma arriving one action late cannot repair an irreversible branch but still helps a reversible one; test whether the cut predicts which public subskill needs training. **Old-task retention.** Separately evaluate old tasks with real late feedback and irreversible early choices.

### 10. Train public sensing to replace privileged seeing — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make a granted hint an obligation to find a public action that produces decision-equivalent evidence. Once a replacement query is verified, train evidence acquisition and consumption separately and remove the privilege. **Novelty / control.** [PSP][psp] leaves privilege in the sampler; [oracle-planner imitation][oracle] imitates an expert with full state. Compare both with an active-sensing baseline given the same tool budget. The claim requires reuse of an evidence contract across new queries, not just cheaper action imitation. **Cheapest first test.** A partial-observation maze where an existing public sensor supplanting a teacher direction is useful on held-out layouts; contrast an irreducibly hidden goal. **Old-task retention.** Check old navigation families for wasted sensor calls and unchanged performance when observation is already sufficient.

### 11. Distill what *not* to infer from a hint swap — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Cross task and hint, including hints lawful on one task but invalid on its public lookalike. Penalize copying the assisted action only when the crossed hint conflicts with publicly checkable evidence. Keep the hint's action effect on the valid pair. **Novelty / control.** [Deconfounded imitation][deconfound] intervenes on initial states to mask spurious features; parent B2 wants invariance to nuisances. Here the same hint is a valid causal input in one cell and misleading in another. Compare validity-flag augmentation plus invariance loss. **Cheapest first test.** Cross hints about an operator with tasks where the public operand type either permits or forbids it. **Old-task retention.** Make sure old tasks still obey a real operator constraint; a policy that ignores all restrictions does not pass.

### 12. Redeem assistance as a public fallback — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When no single unaided action can match a privileged policy, train a public abstention or bounded recovery action that protects the downstream objective under all still-possible worlds. Measure recovery against an oracle only after enforcing the same observation cut. **Novelty / control.** [Oracle-planner imitation][oracle] targets an informed planner's choices; [PSP][psp] moves privileged information out of the loss. This variant trains the set-valued fallback certificate rather than matching a hidden-state decision. Compare robust POMDP planning/behavior cloning on the same observable states; any claim is about reusable certificates at lower amortized cost. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two indistinguishable worlds with opposite optimal guesses and a legal abort-and-retry action; also test a case with no safe fallback. **Old-task retention.** Track false abstention on old tasks with enough evidence and clean recovery on genuinely ambiguous ones.

## C. Make a hint repay its cost on later work

### 13. Split a reusable decoder from its one-off payload — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Spend early bits on a reusable rule for decoding a class of hints, then separately withdraw later task-specific payload. Test full withdrawal by having the student infer the payload from public input through its learned decoder. Price dictionary setup and obsolete entries. **Novelty / control.** [Skill-SD][skillsd] distills compact dynamic trajectory summaries; compare that plus amortized dictionary compression and [SKiC][skic] prompting. Here the object is a decoder/payload split with an explicit zero-payload test, not reusable prose. **Cheapest first test.** Three DSLs with common type rules but different local operands; the third must infer its operand without either a prompt dictionary or a hint. **Old-task retention.** Run old dialects without a dictionary and measure broken legacy payload meanings after the decoder is updated.

### 14. Recover joint solvability without ever showing the joint hint — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use only one component hint per training instance and train composition by making the learner verify the interface between one acquired and one reconstructed component. At zero bits it must reconstruct both and compose them in a held-out order. **Novelty / control.** [SKiC][skic] and [least-to-most prompting][least] demonstrate composition in context; parent D3 supervises seams. Compare an explicit seam loss + independently ratcheted hints; the claim needs a gain from asymmetric erasure, not ordinary seam labels. **Cheapest first test.** Two noncommuting finite transductions and one held-out stacking order; include incompatible component pairs. **Old-task retention.** Exercise each old transduction alone and old compositions whose order must not be swapped.

### 15. Pay for a common prerequisite only once — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Index each assisted transition by which publicly learnable prerequisite was supplied, and verify that independently rediscovering one prerequisite removes hint debt on multiple unrelated tasks. Schedule a single teaching event if the substitution works in all named tasks. **Novelty / control.** [Iterative machine teaching][imt] optimizes examples for learner convergence, and [Skill-SD][skillsd] transfers trajectory summaries; here a single counted teaching event discharges several counterfactually verified dependencies. Compare pooled teaching selection + Skill-SD at the same task budget. **Cheapest first test.** One predicate is reused in list closure and graph reachability; a correlated but unrelated task should not be credited. **Old-task retention.** Check old tasks that use the predicate with opposite downstream goals or don't need it.

### 16. Design the hint to be translated, not paraphrased — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Pair an executable semantic atom with interpreters for two publicly different representations. Use commuting execution as the admission criterion and erase the whole atom at test. Keep a failed translation if it identifies where the claim stops being portable. **Novelty / control.** [Procedure transfer across representations][repr] studies code/graph/language transfer with two-stage RL; parent D2 tests structural morphisms. Here the transfer target is a minimal *removable intervention* transported through a checked interpreter, not a policy or a paraphrase score. Compare isomorphic data augmentation plus D2 and matched A6. **Cheapest first test.** Interchange adjacency lists and a stack encoding where one representation omits edge direction. **Old-task retention.** Test old directed and undirected families in both native formats without translators at deployment.

### 17. Allocate hints by the future debt they eliminate — improvement
**Mechanism.** Pick between equal current-cost hints using a cross-fitted estimate of how much unaided adaptation they save on later, different tasks. Use only past task families to fit that estimate; charge probes on tasks that never need the hint. **Novelty / control.** [HiLL][hill] weights same-problem transfer and [IMT][imt] selects lessons to accelerate convergence; a future-family horizon changes the objective, not the underlying teaching primitive. Compare HiLL plus simple prerequisite frequency and multitask sampling. **Cheapest first test.** Stream two equal-lift hints, one useful later and one equally easy but perishable; reverse the future mix out of sample to test overplanning. **Old-task retention.** Track infrequent early families that a future-heavy scheduler might deprioritize.

### 18. Archive what makes a hint invalid — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Store a small falsifiable applicability test with each reusable hint atom; a failed public test blocks that atom even if it worked on a near neighbor. Train decision boundaries from these failures, not a generic confidence threshold. **Novelty / control.** [Skill-SD][skillsd] includes natural-language mistakes and workflows; parent D6 saves boundary task kernels. Compare those plus exact nearest-neighbor refusal. The candidate is a checked predicate on intervention validity shared across differently worded hints. **Cheapest first test.** Reuse arithmetic cancellation in integer and modular DSLs with opposite conclusions at a noninvertible element. **Old-task retention.** Keep old valid cancellations fast and verify old exception tasks still select the fallback without access to the archive.

## D. Protect the unaided learner while teaching it

### 19. Compare the loss of a hint with the loss of a skill — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For two equally effective withdrawals on a new task, choose based on estimated change in specific old zero-hint decision margins under the different training signals. Learn a small mapping from a removable hint atom to these interactions; validate on held-out old states, never on the final retention panel. **Novelty / control.** [MOPD replay][mopd] distills old behavior and parent F1 projects unsafe targets; compare both together with direct trial updates. Claim only transfer of the mapping across hint variants at lower cost, not superiority to exact rollout with identical cost and state. **Cheapest first test.** Tiny shared-feature classifier with two ways to teach the new decision, only one preserving an old opposite-sign route; swap the interference feature in a negative. **Old-task retention.** Check old cases at matched starting margins and score actual downstream behavior, not the predictor alone.

### 20. Contract a rung only after two independent reconstructions — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Admit withdrawal once two different *public evidence paths* yield the same verified decision, and train on one while testing on withheld path failures. Do not count two sampled completions from the same evidence source. **Novelty / control.** [Self-consistency][sc] votes across sampled chains; parent C5 verifies across contexts. Compare both with source-diverse sampling and A6. The candidate is a redundancy constraint on the causal evidence behind unaided competence, not agreement alone. **Cheapest first test.** Two independent facts support a rewrite; selectively remove one fact in a new instance and insert a common-mode false fact in both. **Old-task retention.** Score authentic single-source old tasks separately so a two-path admission gate does not ban old inference.

### 21. Separate “don't copy” from “don't learn” — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Earn an assisted rollout in a privileged sandbox, then replay only its public observations into matched alternative actions. Distill a preference if the ordering survives across compatible hidden states; otherwise distill a public query or no preference. **Novelty / control.** [JOLT][jolt] regularizes the teacher toward student logits and [PSP][psp] keeps PI out of the loss; parent E6 provides exact branch advantages. Compare JOLT + PSP-style sampling + public-state branch evaluation. The proposed reusable object is a preference with a certified observation domain, not another KL mask. **Cheapest first test.** A small POMDP with routine actions invariant across worlds and one world-sensitive choice. **Old-task retention.** Verify old observation-conditioned preferences still switch in the right direction when real public evidence changes.

### 22. Audit a learned procedure by releasing the wrong hint — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After reaching zero bits, intervene briefly with an in-codebook decoy that would have worked for a superficially similar task. Train on a public verification action that rejects the decoy, then measure whether truly unaided transfer persists. The decoy is never part of ordinary deployment. **Novelty / control.** [Verbal regularization][vrsd] dampens the influence of privileged information on the teacher; [deconfounded imitation][deconfound] masks confounders. Compare adversarial augmentation plus matched correct/filler hints. The candidate is a post-withdrawal executable test whose failure selects a targeted public check, not a reward for mere hint resistance. **Cheapest first test.** Alternate congruent and misleading proof hints across an operand shift; include legitimate advice to rule out universal rejection. **Old-task retention.** Score old unaided shortcut-free tasks and verify the check doesn't slow down routine old solves.

### 23. Identify which skill has been replaced by the tutor — improvement
**Mechanism.** Replay old public decisions after each new hint-assisted update, applying the same semantics-preserving renamings and factual reversals on both sides. Report newly missing information sensitivity and discriminate it from a global margin shift. **Novelty / control.** [SDFT][sdft] preserves capabilities through on-policy demonstration distillation; [causal-abstraction bucketing][buckets] localizes intervention failures. This is an assay, not teacher-depletion learning or a new retention algorithm. Compare zero-hint score + margin-matched ablation alone. **Cheapest first test.** In a shared-feature learner hide a dependence on advisory format that standard old accuracy misses but a factual flip exposes. **Old-task retention.** Require both normal old success and the correct flips on matched old cases; score a margin-matched unrelated interference negative.

### 24. Preserve an alternative before removing the preferred route's hint — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Bind withdrawal to a verified guarantee that at least one pre-existing solution route remains viable when the newly hinted route is unavailable. The contract is exercised by disabling the new route, not by comparing raw entropy. **Novelty / control.** [Feedback Distillation][lean] reports diversity retention, and parent C1 maintains solution clusters. Compare both plus matched support-floor replay; the claim is preserving a specific counterfactually exercisable fallback during withdrawal, not diverse sampling by itself. **Cheapest first test.** A graph with two routes; hints concentrate on the short path, which is later blocked. Compare a distractor route that looks diverse but shares the same blocked edge. **Old-task retention.** Measure normal old graph tasks and old edge-failure variants, at equal decoding budgets.

### 25. Retire a hint with a reversible legal substitution — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Record a pair of executable substitutions: the learner's public alternative to a hint, and a public test that restores the distinction if the alternative is no longer valid. Exercise the test without reinstalling privileged context or weights. **Novelty / control.** [PrefixRL][prefix] tests generalization after removing a prefix and [PSP][psp] resamples with instruction. Neither motivates treating withdrawal itself as a checked substitution with a public recovery operation. Compare exact per-instance validity checking + A6 and a frozen checkpoint; the claim is reuse under later task changes. **Cheapest first test.** A public shortcut legitimately replaces a hint until a small grammar revision breaks its assumption; compare worlds with and without observable revision evidence. **Old-task retention.** After recovery, re-run old grammar versions and ensure the recovery does not install a globally incompatible rule.

### 26. Spend paired hints to identify an interaction, then erase both — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Allocate a four-cell intervention (neither atom, each singly, both) at a shared public decision, learn to reproduce only the verified interaction, and refuse to generalize if the interaction disappears under an answer swap. **Novelty / control.** The [ledger][ledger] kills direct quartet distillation because its objective was just a reweighting; this card does not claim a new quartet loss. The candidate is an executable public substitute for a joint intervention, with a costed test across tasks. Compare direct quartet distillation + best two independently learned substitutes plus a public composition routine. **Cheapest first test.** Use a DSL where either fact alone is useless but both identify a feasible plan; include a task with additive effects and a hidden fact. Drop the primitive claim if the stacked control reproduces the result. **Old-task retention.** Recheck old tasks where either fact alone remains sufficient and where their conjunction is false.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **When does successful assistance admit a public replacement rather than a shortcut?** Start with #01, #04 and #10. Hold message and assisted gain fixed while crossing answer-changing worlds, then compare reconstructible procedures, expensive public queries and unobservable bits. If HiLL-style reliance plus a matched sensing policy explains the result, do not posit a new signal.
2. **What should be contracted when there is no monotone ladder?** Start with #03, #07 and #26. Enumerate a non-monotone hint frontier with conflicting additions; ask whether executable erasure predicts future zero-bit gains above A6, PrefixRL and exact per-task search amortized honestly.
3. **Can a teaching event lower the bill for several different future tasks?** Start with #13–#16. Hold total instruction and execution budget fixed and withhold later compositions entirely. Require savings on the later unaided tasks over the strongest shared-skill + per-task withdrawal stack, not merely compression of hints on seen tasks.
4. **Can withdrawal preserve useful old options where new-task accuracy is the same?** Start with #19, #21 and #24. Equalize new-task unaided accuracy and old starting margins, then disable the new route and exercise old ones. If ordinary old replay and resource-matched direct validation match the contract, keep the simpler baseline.
