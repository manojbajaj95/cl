# Practice-order interference: why the order of practice, not its mix, sets retention in nonlinear learners

Parent: [PR #119](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/119) finding 4 (reweighting learning-progress picks so the *expected* update equals the uniform mixture does not recover uniform's retention; "the mixture sets the mean of the retention vector, the selection sequence sets its excursions") and cross-pool **FCU8** in [PR #36](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/36) (`ideas/flood/cross-pool-17.md`: learn which two practice lessons can safely follow each other, from forward forks, at a fixed marginal mixture). **Node question:** hold the practice histogram fixed. When, how much and through what state does the *order* of practice decide what a nonlinear learner retains, and is there a primitive that controls retention through order alone?

**Short answer.** Order sets retention through two channels, and the probe below separates them. (1) **Excursions at a fixed mixture**, which every host has, linear included: with the same 4,000 steps split equally over 4 tasks, going from block length 1 to 500 drops time-averaged worst-task competence from +0.47 to −1.25 in a ReLU MLP and from −0.00 to −1.17 in linear regression. This is PR #119's excursion effect, and it is the part a first-order (Taylor/commutator) view already predicts once blocks are long against 1/(ηλ). (2) **History carried in the state.** In hosts that are linear in their parameters (linear regression, frozen random features), order memory is *exactly* erased by re-mixing: after 4,000 shared shuffled steps the forward-vs-reverse order gap falls from 1.10 to 0.000. In a trained-feature MLP it is not erased on deployment timescales: a blocked first phase still costs the worst task −0.29 ± 0.14 (mean −0.12 ± 0.05) against a shuffled first phase after 4,000 identical shuffled steps, and leaves a unit-to-task allocation footprint twice the minibatch-noise floor (L1 0.196 vs 0.090) while outputs are close to converged. The cost has relaxed by 12,000 steps (−0.01 ± 0.04), and at width 64 the fwd-vs-rev output gap is within the seed floor, so the main nonlinear effect here is **slow relaxation of a hidden allocation state, not permanent hysteresis**. Only at width 4 (capacity below the task count) does a residual gap survive 12,000 steps (+0.049 ± 0.045 above floor), a borderline sign of order-selected multistable allocations. So "why order, not mix": the mixture fixes the attractor; order fixes where the learner is relative to it, and nonlinear learners take far longer to return because the path reshapes which features serve which task. Retention measured at any finite horizon shorter than that relaxation time is set by order.

## Root cause: the mixture fixes the attractor, order fixes the state and the clock

1. **Linear-in-parameters learners forget their order.** SGD on quadratic losses is a random affine contraction; any two histories driven by the same subsequent stream converge geometrically (synchronization of random contractions). The order footprint decays at a rate set by the smallest practiced curvature, so it is gone once re-mixing runs longer than ~1/(ηλ_min). Probe Q1: the 1.10 gap goes to 0.000 ± 0.000 (lin) and 0.001 (rf). Order matters in these hosts only through the excursions it produces *while* it is running (Q2), which is why PR #119's IPW fix failed in its linear host: it fixes the mean, not the excursions.
2. **Nonlinear learners store order in an allocation state.** In the MLP, which units carry which task (|v_j| × mean ReLU activation on task k) is shaped by the order of practice. Re-mixing reconciles the outputs long before it reconciles the allocation: ownership distance between fwd and rev histories stays 2.2× the floor while output gaps sit near it. Retention later depends on that allocation (who can be overwritten by whom), so two learners with matched functions can still differ in what they will forget. This is a state variable that neither the practice histogram nor the current loss vector contains.
3. **The relaxation clock is long.** The blocked-history cost is still −0.29 worst-task after 2× the phase-1 budget of perfectly mixed practice and only gone by 6×. A deployed learner that re-mixes for less than its relaxation time is retaining what its *order* gave it. This is a quantity (τ_order) that CL benchmarks do not measure and that the ledger's kill of bounded-delay scheduling did not cover: that kill holds for reorderings inside windows short against 1/(ηλ), where the order effect is O(η²); blocks and self-curricula runs are not short.
4. **First-order order predictors cover only part of it.** The Lie-bracket early-warning paper ([2611.20087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20087)) predicts order gaps for slow forgetting but fails for fast forgetting and half-learned tasks, where residual gradients dominate. Both failure regimes are where the allocation state is still moving. Pairwise forks (FCU8) measure the excursion channel well; whether they measure the allocation channel is the open question (sub-question 3).

**What survives.** A primitive that controls retention through order needs (a) an excursion model (pairwise or block-length kernels, FCU8), (b) a model of the hidden allocation state and its relaxation clock, and (c) evaluation at the horizon the learner is actually deployed at, not after convergence. The cleanest new object is τ_order and the allocation footprint behind it.

## Screen and required controls

- Read [PR #6's ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6/files) first. Relevant kills: **memory-loop Lyapunov exponent predicts order dependence** (falsified: an early Lyapunov signal lost to a direct two-order divergence control, so any order predictor here must beat that direct fork), **bounded-delay stability scheduling** (in-window reordering is O(η²) and gradient accumulation removes it at equal latency; ideas below either work at block scale or state why they escape this), **learning-progress self-curricula** (ALP-GMM, self-play cover it; PR #119 sharpens the failure). The ledger's pattern "a first-order Taylor consequence presented as a mechanism" is the main risk for the excursion ideas; they are marked.
- Siblings: PR #119 ideas 11 (excursion-bounded scheduling) and 14 (learned interleave granularity) are the excursion-channel controls and are not repeated; Floquet forgetting under cyclic curricula is CD3 in [PR #74](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/74); replay timing is [PR #113](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/113).
- Nearest work (alphaXiv search and abstracts; not novelty clearance): Lie bracket of task gradient fields as an early-warning signal for order-dependent forgetting ([2611.20087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20087)); optimal task order for CL ([2502.03350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03350)); task granularity in CL ([2606.08013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08013)); structured training order ([2403.09613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09613)); block reshuffling ([2604.00260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00260)); task-order effects in class-incremental CL ([2211.15470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15470)); order-robust CL via additive parameter decomposition ([1902.09432](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09432)); critical learning periods ([1711.08856](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08856)); permutation-based SGD and flip-flop orders ([2102.09718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09718)); Git Re-Basin permutation alignment ([2209.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836)).
- Required controls for every idea: **matched practice histogram** (exact per-task step counts, same minibatch pool), shuffled order and random block permutations at the same histogram, the direct two-order fork divergence (the ledger's winning control), the Lie-bracket predictor of 2611.20087 where an order predictor is claimed, and the seed floor (same order, different minibatch stream). Report worst-task competence over the stream and at the deployment horizon, not only at convergence, and the new-task learning curve.

## Raw ideas

### A. Order as a hidden state with a clock

1. **τ_order, the order-memory clock.** **Mechanism:** fork the learner, give the forks two orders of the same practice, then feed both the same shuffled stream and fit the decay of their divergence (output and allocation separately); τ_order is the time constant. Retention at horizon T is order-set iff T ≲ τ_order. **Why it might be new:** CL reports retention after a fixed protocol; nobody reports how long order memory lasts, and the probe shows it is 0 for linear-in-parameters hosts and >2× the phase-1 budget for an MLP. **Cheapest first test:** Q1b with N2 swept on a log grid, across width, lr and depth; fit exponential decay. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task gap at each N2, against the seed floor.
2. **Latent hysteresis in function-matched twins.** **Mechanism:** two learners with matched outputs but different histories (fwd vs rev, then re-mixed until output gap ≤ seed floor) are given the same new task E; if they forget A–D differently, order is stored in a state the function does not show. **Why it might be new:** the test makes "order sets retention" a claim about future forgetting, not current loss; Q3's allocation gap predicts it. **Cheapest first test:** Q1 MLP twins after N2 = 12,000, then 1,000 steps on a fifth task. **Old-task retention check:** per-task drop of A–D during E, fwd twin minus rev twin, vs seed-floor twins.
3. **Allocation odometer.** **Mechanism:** track the unit-task ownership matrix A (or a cheap sketch from activation statistics on a few canaries per task) online; use its drift, not loss, to decide when an order footprint is dangerous. **Why it might be new:** order effects that are invisible to losses are visible here; it is a state estimator for channel 2 in the same role that practice odometry plays for unpracticed tasks in PR #119. **Cheapest first test:** in the probe, predict which task will lose most under a new block from A alone vs from current losses vs from gradient cosines. **Old-task retention check:** realized per-task drop under the block.
4. **Quasi-static hysteresis loops.** **Mechanism:** sweep the practice mixture slowly from A-heavy to B-heavy and back; plot competence vs mixture. Loop area that does *not* shrink as the sweep slows is true multistability (order selects an allocation); area that shrinks is lag. **Why it might be new:** a clean operational test separating the two channels; standard in physics, absent in CL. **Cheapest first test:** two-task MLP host at H ∈ {2, 4, 64}, three sweep speeds. **Old-task retention check:** competence of the task the sweep leaves last, at both ends of the loop.
5. **Capacity-forced order selection.** **Mechanism:** when units are fewer than tasks need, tasks compete for units and the order picks the winner (first-mover capture or last-mover overwrite); the probe's H = 4 residual (+0.049 above floor after 12,000 steps) is the hint. **Why it might be new:** turns task order into an allocation decision with a predictable winner, which a scheduler could exploit to protect the tasks it cares about. **Cheapest first test:** Q4 with H ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8} and all 24 block orders; regress final per-task competence on block position. **Old-task retention check:** per-task competence after 12,000 re-mixed steps vs the floor.
6. **Order-annealing bursts.** **Mechanism:** a short burst of shuffled practice at raised learning rate (or with noise injection) shrinks τ_order by melting the allocation, after which normal practice re-settles; pay the burst instead of a long re-mix. **Why it might be new:** a reset for order debt, distinct from replay (no old data beyond the histogram) and from plasticity resets (targets the order footprint). **Cheapest first test:** after a blocked phase 1, compare 4,000 normal shuffled steps vs 500 hot steps + 3,500 normal. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence at end and during the burst (bursts may themselves forget).
7. **Critical-period ordering.** **Mechanism:** the first blocks fix the feature basis; put first the task whose features are most reusable by the others (measured by the overlap of its subspace with theirs), so later tasks build on rather than overwrite it. **Why it might be new:** critical learning periods (1711.08856) show early deficits are irreversible; this asks which *task* should own the critical period for retention, at a fixed histogram. **Cheapest first test:** all 24 orders in the MLP host; correlate first task's subspace centrality with final worst-task competence. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task and first-task competence at the end.
8. **Order-aligned merging.** **Mechanism:** two learners trained on the same data in different orders differ by a unit permutation plus residue; align with Git Re-Basin before averaging and test whether the merged model inherits the better retention of either. **Why it might be new:** history-induced permutation misalignment as a CL object; averaging fwd/rev forks without alignment may be why order-averaged ensembles disappoint. **Cheapest first test:** fwd and rev MLPs after phase 2; naive average vs aligned average vs each parent. **Old-task retention check:** per-task competence of the merge.
9. **Optimizer-state carryover.** **Mechanism:** Adam's second moments and momentum are set by the last block, so the next task is learned under a preconditioner shaped by its predecessor; order then sets retention through the optimizer even in a linear host. **Why it might be new:** a third order channel that the parameter-space commutator ignores; testable by resetting state at block boundaries. **Cheapest first test:** the probe with Adam, fwd vs rev, with and without state resets, linear and MLP. **Old-task retention check:** order gap after re-mixing for each condition.

### B. Sequence kernels beyond pairs (FCU8 and successors)

10. **FCU8 pair kernel, in a host where order is not erased.** **Mechanism:** estimate the ordered-pair effect K(i→j) on every old task from forward forks, subtracting isolated effects; design block orders at the fixed histogram by a min-max path over K. **Why it might be new:** FCU8's claim that pairwise harm is invisible to frequency weighting; the probe says its first test must be nonlinear, because in a linear host the pair effect is pure excursion and decays to zero. **Cheapest first test:** MLP host, K = 4, forks of 200 steps per pair; compare designed order vs 20 random orders vs direct two-order fork (ledger control). **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence during and after the designed schedule.
11. **Minimal Markov order of retention.** **Mechanism:** fit the final retention vector over all permutations of 4–5 blocks with additive, pairwise and triplet terms; the order at which residual variance drops below the seed floor tells whether pair kernels (FCU8) suffice or the learner needs a state model. **Why it might be new:** a measurable "interaction order" of a learner's retention functional; it decides between whole families of schedulers. **Cheapest first test:** 120 permutations of 5 blocks in the MLP host, 4 seeds each. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence per permutation; R² of each model.
12. **Learned order dynamics for planning.** **Mechanism:** fit a small surrogate (per-task competence plus an allocation sketch from idea 3) on fork logs, then plan block orders with MPC over the fixed histogram. **Why it might be new:** the PR #119 lookahead oracle is one-step and needs all losses; this is multi-step, uses a learned state, and does not evaluate every task each step. **Cheapest first test:** probe; MPC with a linear-plus-ReLU surrogate vs FCU8 kernel vs random orders. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence over the stream at equal steps.
13. **Gating-overlap predictor.** **Mechanism:** predict harmful orders from the fraction of hidden units active on both tasks' inputs (and their sign agreement), not from gradient cosines or the Lie bracket. **Why it might be new:** in ReLU nets interference goes through shared active units; the bracket fails exactly when gating is still changing (fast forgetting, half-learned tasks). **Cheapest first test:** rank pair kernels from idea 10 by gating overlap, gradient cosine, Lie bracket and the direct fork; Spearman vs realized pair harm. **Old-task retention check:** the realized old-task drop the ranking is scored against.

### C. Controllers that act through order alone

14. **Permutation-only retention control.** **Mechanism:** the histogram is fixed by the world (data quotas, user demand) and the learner may only choose the permutation within each window; choose it online from the kernel or surrogate to maximize the worst task. **Why it might be new:** curricula choose frequency; this is the control left when frequency is not yours, and it is exactly PR #119's open gap. **Cheapest first test:** probe with per-window quotas, windows of 100–400 steps; greedy kernel permutation vs random vs shuffled. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence at every window boundary.
15. **Minimal reorder-buffer law.** **Mechanism:** data arrives blocked; a reorder buffer of size W lets the learner interleave locally. Predict the W needed to reach shuffled retention from τ_order and block length, and reconcile with the ledger kill (which holds only for W ≪ 1/(ηλ)). **Why it might be new:** a quantitative law linking a cheap systems knob to a learner property, with the kill as its small-W limit. **Cheapest first test:** sweep W ∈ {1, 10, 100, 1000} with blocks of 500 in all three hosts. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence over the stream vs W.
16. **Order-aware self-curriculum.** **Mechanism:** port PR #119's ALP selector to the MLP host and add a kernel penalty for following a task with one it harms, plus an allocation-drift penalty (idea 3); keep the IPW mixture correction. **Why it might be new:** directly tests whether the PR #119 residual (IPW −0.059 vs uniform) is order-caused and order-fixable. **Cheapest first test:** PR #119 probe policies in the MLP host: ALP, ALP+IPW, ALP+IPW+order penalty, uniform, lookahead. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence over the second half.
17. **Annealed block length.** **Mechanism:** start blocked (fast per-task fit, distinct allocations), then shrink b toward 1 to let the MLP find the joint solution (Q2: b = 1 gives mean 0.71 vs 0.05 at b = 500); test whether the start shapes a better allocation than always-interleaved. **Why it might be new:** a blocked-to-interleaved path at fixed histogram as an allocation-shaping tool, not just a desirable-difficulty heuristic. Risk: an improvement if it only matches b = 1. **Cheapest first test:** probe; b schedules 500→1 vs constant 1 vs constant 10. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence at the end and under a later new task (idea 2).
18. **Palindromic block schedules.** **Mechanism:** run blocks A B C D D C B A (Strang/flip-flop splitting), which cancels the leading commutator term; any order effect left is the nonlinear allocation channel. **Why it might be new:** flip-flop orders are known for single-task SGD convergence (2102.09718); here they are a *diagnostic* that subtracts the first-order excursion channel at block scale. Risk: ledger's "Taylor consequence" pattern if used as a method rather than a probe. **Cheapest first test:** fwd, rev and palindromic schedules at equal histogram in lin and MLP hosts. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence and the residual fwd-vs-palindrome gap after re-mixing.
19. **Commutator-penalty training.** **Mechanism:** penalize the divergence between two micro-forks that take the next two blocks in opposite orders (a finite-difference bracket), so the learner prefers parameters where task updates commute. **Why it might be new:** 2611.20087 uses the bracket to predict; this optimizes it away, at the cost of one extra fork. Must beat simply shortening blocks. **Cheapest first test:** probe MLP; penalty weight sweep vs b = 10 interleaving at equal compute. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task competence and order gap after re-mixing.

### D. Architectures and settings where order should or should not matter

20. **Commuting parameterizations.** **Mechanism:** make each task's updates act on coordinates whose updates commute (elementwise gains, additive per-task adapters, sparse routed experts) and measure whether τ_order and the order gap collapse to the linear-host value. **Why it might be new:** commutativity as an explicit design target for order-robust CL; additive decomposition (1902.09432) is one instance but was not framed or measured this way. **Cheapest first test:** MLP vs MLP with per-task elementwise output gains vs additive adapters, Q1 and Q1b. **Old-task retention check:** worst-task gap after re-mixing and with a new task.
21. **Order footprints in tiny transformers.** **Mechanism:** attention heads allocate like units; domain-blocked SFT vs shuffled SFT at the same mixture may leave a head-ownership footprint that predicts later forgetting. **Why it might be new:** carries channel 2 to the host the field cares about with a measurable state (head ownership by attribution). **Cheapest first test:** 2-layer transformer on CPU, 4 synthetic sequence tasks, Q1/Q3 protocol. **Old-task retention check:** per-task accuracy after re-mixing and after a fifth task.
22. **Order as an unlearning lever.** **Mechanism:** at fixed histogram, choose the order that maximizes forgetting of a target task (practice it early, then the tasks that capture its units), with no gradient ascent. **Why it might be new:** the dual of ideas 5 and 7; order-only unlearning is auditable because the data and counts are unchanged. **Cheapest first test:** H = 4 MLP, all 24 orders, pick the worst for task A. **Old-task retention check:** the other tasks stay above a stated floor; relearning speed of A.
23. **Order-robust evaluation.** **Mechanism:** report retention as the distribution over random permutations at a fixed histogram (worst and spread), together with τ_order. **Why it might be new:** turns order from a nuisance into a reported property; prior order-robustness work (1902.09432, 2211.15470) reports variance over task orders, not the clock or the allocation channel. Risk: a metric, not a primitive. **Cheapest first test:** the probe's three hosts, 20 permutations each. **Old-task retention check:** it is one.

## Sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Is order hysteresis ever permanent?** Does a capacity-limited nonlinear learner have multiple stable allocations that order selects (quasi-static loop area that does not shrink, H = 4 residual surviving longer re-mixing), or is every effect slow relaxation? A yes makes order a first-class CL control with irreversible consequences. Ideas 4, 5, 22.
2. **What sets τ_order, and is it longer than deployment horizons?** How does the order-memory clock scale with width, depth, learning rate and optimizer state, and can a burst (idea 6) or a parameterization (idea 20) shorten it? Ideas 1, 6, 9, 15, 20.
3. **Are pairs enough?** What is the minimal Markov order of the retention functional in a nonlinear host, and does FCU8's pair kernel capture the allocation channel or only the excursion channel? Ideas 10, 11, 12, 13.
4. **Does a hidden allocation footprint predict future forgetting?** Do function-matched twins with different histories forget differently under a new task, and does an allocation sketch predict it better than losses, gradient cosines and the Lie bracket? Ideas 2, 3, 8, 21.
5. **Can order alone close PR #119's gap?** At a fixed histogram, how much of the lookahead ceiling can a permutation-only controller recover, and is the IPW residual order-caused? Ideas 14, 16, 17.
