# Test-time weights III: make a live write leave itself open to defeat

**Status:** Ideas only; no implementation, learner runs or GPU. **Parent:** [Test-time weights II](test-time-weights-2.md) ([PR #313](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/313)). **Ledger read first:** [Idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/5e9fd48a1e67e598092b9ab9f50e730ff4214283/reviews/idea-ledger.md). **Review seeds:** [Flood triage, rows 430–433](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/5e9fd48a1e67e598092b9ab9f50e730ff4214283/reviews/flood-triage.md#L430-L433) ([PR #6](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6)). The #313 row is 431: [#22](test-time-weights-2.md#22-recognize-when-a-write-disarms-its-own-test-p) protects chances to observe counterexamples; [#01](test-time-weights-2.md#01-make-a-write-predict-how-it-would-lose-p) settles on events where the write and its frozen predecessor disagree, abstaining if none arrive. The other rows review other nodes.

## What might be new?

A write can keep every receipt path open but move traffic away from the cases that would tell it apart from its predecessor. Or both predictions can remain in disagreement while the write diverts only its prospective losses into a slow or silent route. Neither a disagreement count nor a receipt count detects both failure modes. The proposed object is a **right to keep a particular change active**, indexed by which ordinary requests could distinguish the competing forecasts, which result could defeat the write, and what authority remains if those opportunities disappear. Revoking that right is not a causal verdict that the write caused the disappearance.

## Literature check and test contract

Targeted web searches returned abstracts and method excerpts on test-time settlement, selective labels and censoring, differential testing, safe policy improvement, sequential off-policy inference, and evidence transfer. The cards link the relevant sources; the deltas are hypotheses, not exhaustive priority claims.

- **Strong prior-art floor.** [TTT Settlement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076) already scores updates on independent real text before commitment; [aTTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03441) already studies the loop where acting generates future training text. [Algorithmic Censoring](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3617694.3623247) identifies subgroups that stop producing labels and uses recourse and exploration to restore feedback. [Performative Prediction with Selective Labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08272) already uses bounds on selectively observed risk; [Actions Have Consequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26908) studies intervention-based detection. [Disagreement-based online active learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09056) already uses disagreement to decide when to acquire labels. Disagreement, robustness to missing data, random audits and reversible edits are baselines, not inventions.
- **Shared host.** Use finite tables, then at most a tiny CPU classifier on scripted document-routing or parcel-service streams. Pin the predecessor, candidate, claim, outcome, denominator, eligible contrasts, receipt semantics and expiry before exposure. A shadow may predict outcomes of the **executed** action on the same pre-action context; that does not reveal what its preferred action would have done. Use existing logged variation only where propensities, overlap and interference assumptions support the contrast; otherwise bound or abstain. No new live probes in the candidate arms. A randomized exploration control, when used, pays for its actions and labels. Evaluator-only twin worlds and reruns never feed adjudication.
- **Separate the counts.** Count upstream arrivals, registered disagreements, opportunities to observe *either* result, and actual labeled verdicts separately, including cases that vanish or time out. Pin cohorts before routing when available. Separate outcome prediction from utility, pending from absent, unsupported from refuted, and service restriction from withdrawal. Compare against the best applicable stack: #313 #01 + #22, [#311 #07](root-test-time-weights.md#07-falsifiable-writes-p) all-event confirmation, ordinary paired prequential loss, always-expire, held-out gating and Settlement, plus each card's named local control. Include true repair with unchanged opportunity, genuine removal of demand, and harmful disappearance. Match bytes, scoring passes, attempts and exposure, or report the latency–harm tradeoff instead.
- **Retention is a constraint, not a training source.** Freeze old-task fixtures before edits; evaluate before, during and after provisional exposure and any fallback or removal, with the worst old slice shown separately. Count wrong service and unserved old requests even without complaints. Compare new-task gains at the same old-task tolerance and useful service coverage; all-abstain is not a win. Use paired streams and multiple seeds if advancing past enumeration. **P** = candidate reusable primitive; **I** = improvement or audit.

## A. Keep the right kind of counterexample observable (#313 #22)

### 01. Follow the arrival, not the receipt (P)
- **Mechanism:** Give an upstream request an observation obligation keyed before the policy picks a route. Transfers inherit the obligation; a lost transfer still counts against the write that diverted it. Only an outcome-capable terminal path discharges it, not a syntactic acknowledgement.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Algorithmic Censoring](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3617694.3623247) safeguards labels for persistently unobserved groups. The candidate primitive is a per-write obligation that follows a request through route changes, where per-route coverage can look healthy.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two queues with identical receipt counts; only one loses difficult transfers. Compare a frozen cohort denominator plus ordinary missingness correction to the transferred obligation. Drop the primitive if the fixed-cohort gate matches verdicts and exposure.
- **Old-task retention check:** Include old requests that historically passed through both queues; measure correct completion, not number of acknowledgements.

### 02. Give failures and successes separate paths to the verdict (P)
- **Mechanism:** Register which terminal paths could reveal each outcome separately. A success-only integration grants positive evidence only about claims it can actually refute; it cannot replace a bidirectional receipt path with the same event rate.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Selective Labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08272) shows why a selected sample gives biased risk. Here the write declares a two-outcome observation interface; route capabilities known before outcomes, not estimated positive rates, constrain its authority.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare success-only, bidirectional and silent routes with equal visible volume. Include a real fix whose bidirectional route emits zero failures on the toy stream. Reject if a route-stratified missingness gate reproduces both the real and sham verdicts.
- **Old-task retention check:** Replay old fallback and rejection cases with complete evaluator labels, even on routes that emit no negative receipts.

### 03. Give delayed negative evidence the last word (I)
- **Mechanism:** Attach a maturity watermark to each disagreement cohort. Early successes cannot settle a write until the declared window in which corresponding negative outcomes could arrive has closed; late cases beyond that window are still missing, not successes.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Capacity-Constrained Delayed Feedback for Online Conformal Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17059) already treats delayed and lost feedback separately under bounded tracking. The narrow improvement is settlement on a maturing disagreement cohort rather than on all pending predictions.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use two delay tables with matching true errors and different arrival order. Compare event-count waiting, fixed time expiry and delayed-feedback weighting using the same memory. Reject if a fixed lag matches harmful-keep rate and time-to-benefit.
- **Old-task retention check:** Include slow legacy requests and score interim SLA misses as well as eventual correctness.

### 04. Cap a write's exposure by its unresolved cases (P)
- **Mechanism:** Reserve provisional authority when a distinguishing request arrives; release the reservation only when eligible evidence arrives or stop new exposure at the cap. Timeout does not refund it. Normal downstream service continues under the predecessor where fallback is feasible.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Safe Policy Improvement with Baseline Bootstrapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924) backs off to a baseline where data are insufficient. The proposed operation spends authority on *outstanding* verdict obligations rather than lifetime counts or arbitrary traffic fractions.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two burst schedules with equal total volume and delayed receipts, one with many simultaneous disputes. Compare a static canary fraction and a generic pending-request cap at matched cumulative exposure; reject if either produces the same harm–latency frontier.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score old priority requests during bursts and while the cap forces fallback, including availability.

### 05. Detect lost observation with unrelated outcomes (I)
- **Mechanism:** For a claim with a credible unrelated control outcome that shares the reporting channel, flag coincident loss of that outcome and losses on the claim. Suspend forecast settlement, without inferring that the edit caused a channel outage.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Negative Controls](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/) already use unrelated exposures or outcomes to detect bias. The application is a veto tied to an edit's referee, not a new identification technique.
- **Cheapest first test:** In a ticket table cross true repair with a reporter outage and an outage affecting only cases in dispute. Compare global heartbeat and route coverage gates. Drop it if the control does not add a useful detection regime; reject any inference based on a control the edit itself directly changes.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check old tickets on the shared reporting channel and old tickets on a separate channel through each outage and recovery.

### 06. Treat transfer and closure as different ways to lose a witness (P)
- **Mechanism:** Register competing terminal events separately. An independently verified resolution can end an opportunity legitimately; an unverified closure cannot. Preserve the distinction in settlement instead of crediting all requests that disappear from the active queue as solved.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Separable effects in competing-event settings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8536652/) already separates the pathways by which treatment changes competing event rates. The proposal turns verifiable terminal-event types into explicit reasons to release or retain a live observation obligation, without estimating separable causal effects from unsupported logs.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate solved, transferred and auto-closed tickets with the same observed complaint rate. Compare competing-risk summaries plus an ordinary utility gate. Reject if the event-specific obligation does not save any useful authority.
- **Old-task retention check:** Incidence of correct resolution and wrongful closure on old transfer-prone tasks, not just complaint incidence.

### 07. Keep an independent view of vanished demand (P)
- **Mechanism:** Where an existing intake process sees demand before it reaches the learner, join it to served requests without enabling the learner to change its definition. If hard requests stop reaching the learner but remain visible at intake, suspend that slice's claims; if they vanish upstream too, report the original slice as no longer evaluable.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Benchmarks for Detecting Measurement Tampering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15605) detects false agreement among measurements; the proposed independent-intake view instead protects the *denominator*, even if all downstream measurements are honest.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct paired streams with authentic lower demand versus suppressed intake handoffs, matched on served traffic. Compare #313 #22 with ordinary intake/serve ratio alarms. Drop the primitive if the alarm and the parent gate recover the same safe scopes at equal bytes.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate old low-frequency requests from intake through service, including those never assigned to the learner.

### 08. Bind observation assumptions to the route interface (P)
- **Mechanism:** Keep per-claim dependencies on route capabilities such as “can signal rejection” and “can link closure to original request.” An interface change suspends only claims depending on the capability lost, pending fresh evidence under the new interface.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Owned Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15917) scopes edits by ownership and controls withdrawal; [transportability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01603) separates changed mechanisms across populations. The intended operation is suspension by changed *measurement capability*, not by edit owner or version number.
- **Cheapest first test:** Swap a reporter and one field in a six-claim table, with an interface change that preserves capabilities as a control. Compare resetting all claims, refreshing by label yield, and dependency tracking at equal metadata cost. Kill if tracking buys no additional safe active claims.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score unchanged old routes and old routes depending on the changed field before and after the swap.

## B. Make the predecessor a meaningful rival (#313 #01)

### 09. Compare decisions at the point of disputed utility (P)
- **Mechanism:** Pin the consequence model, downstream decision threshold and costs for the claim separately from the raw forecast. Register disagreements that would cross that decision boundary, including two forecasts on the same side of a probability of one-half. Settle predictive merit and observed utility separately.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Threshold-weighted scoring](https://doi.org/10.1198/jbes.2010.08110) already emphasizes important regions of a predictive distribution. The putative primitive is a write right scoped to specific disputed decisions, not a new proper score.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use three common-action outcomes where the same probability shift is irrelevant for cheap actions and decisive for a costly one. Compare fixed-threshold disagreement plus weighted paired Brier loss. Kill if that stack gives the same deployment decisions at matched scoring.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test old low-cost and high-cost actions with their original asymmetric error costs.

### 10. Refuse forecasts that win either way (I)
- **Mechanism:** Before a write receives exposure, compute its reachable score margin under both possible outcomes on registered common-action contrast cells. If no feasible result can overturn its current standing within the horizon, close or narrow the claim instead of calling it still testable.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Differential testing](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/bill-mckeeman/DifferentailTesting.pdf) surfaces divergences without supplying a correctness oracle; [Off-Policy Confidence Sequences](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/karampatziakis21a.html) already support monitoring evidence over time. This is a reachability audit for the defeat threshold, not an inference guarantee.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hand-enumerate possible outcomes in five remaining slots for two otherwise identical pending writes. Compare expiry, minimum disagreement counts and generic sequential futility stopping. Reject if the latter fully subsumes the audit.
- **Old-task retention check:** During the audit compare rare old requests before and after a forced narrow scope, not just new-case verdicts.

### 11. Let disjoint disagreements earn disjoint permissions (P)
- **Mechanism:** Pre-register disagreement cells with different operational consequences. Allow an edit to settle only on scopes that have a representative separating cell, so abundant easy contrasts cannot certify a rare decision the predecessor would have taken differently.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Online disagreement-based active learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09056) queries where hypotheses differ; [SPIBB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924) falls back under sparse data. The new interface would bind the resulting permissions to a single weight write with independent adjudication per scope, not just train or route unsupported examples differently.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use two disagreement cells with opposite edit quality and 20:1 traffic. Compare pooled paired scores plus baseline-on-rare and an ordinary per-cell gate. Drop the primitive if per-cell gating gives the same partial permissions at equal storage and scope granularity.
- **Old-task retention check:** Track old rare-cell completion, unaffected common-cell service and old tasks crossing the scoped boundary.

### 12. Expose agreement regions to a harm veto, not a merit vote (I)
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a separate readout when the write and predecessor predict the same binary outcome. These events can veto harmful service but cannot provide positive evidence for that claim; otherwise agreement can dilute failure on the contrast set.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Settlement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076) scores independent validation before keeping an edit; [#313 #03](test-time-weights-2.md#03-give-confirmed-writes-a-utility-veto-p) already separates prediction and utility. This is only an allocation of their roles across agreement and disagreement.
- **Cheapest first test:** Give a tiny classifier identical hard predictions but worse calibration or action cost on a high-volume old slice, and improvement on the new disagreement slice. Compare paired loss and the parent utility veto. Keep only if the asymmetric rule improves the harm–latency frontier.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure unchanged-label old tasks for calibration, action cost and completion separately.

### 13. Do not pick the rival that is easiest to beat (P)
- **Mechanism:** In addition to the frozen predecessor, pre-register a cheaper plausible no-write rival, such as a frozen model with a receipt decoder update. Allow evidence against the predecessor to settle only that comparison; withhold authority for the weight change if it never separates from the cheaper rival.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Differential testing](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/bill-mckeeman/DifferentailTesting.pdf) uses multiple comparable systems. Here rival-specific verdicts give different deployment rights: beating the old predictor is not the same as earning a persistent weight update.
- **Cheapest first test:** Cross format drift with actual consequence drift in a four-cell table where both rivals fit the former. Compare three-way prequential model selection and Settlement at matched evaluations. Kill if generic model selection gives the same preference ordering and old retention.
- **Old-task retention check:** Use old-format tasks and newer tasks that still depend on the old predictor; require correct service under the decoder-only fallback.

### 14. Make specialization claims symmetric across rival win cells (P)
- **Mechanism:** Before exposure, identify observable contexts in which each side says the other would be wrong under a defined common action. Issue a narrower claim if ordinary traffic samples only candidate-favored cells; do not certify broad replacement by extrapolating a one-sided set.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Specialist prediction](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258616) already combines experts that abstain on parts of the stream. This binds the replacement right to seeing both directions of a declared disagreement, not a new specialist aggregation rule.
- **Cheapest first test:** Give two predictors opposite strengths and skew the arriving context mix, then swap it. Compare per-slice calibration and minimum overlap gates with two-sided permissions. Reject if a standard stratified gate matches protection at the same selective coverage.
- **Old-task retention check:** Freeze old predecessor-favored cases, including the ones absent during initial adjudication.

### 15. Hold the action still for one kind of verdict (P)
- **Mechanism:** Declare a predictive-only right for a write when both forecasts are scored on the action actually taken, and a separate acting right requiring evidence over both policies' actions. Evidence may settle one right without the other; no missing counterfactual is filled in by the shadow.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Anytime-valid off-policy inference](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3643693) already estimates target-policy values from supported logs, and the parent already uses a frozen-acting-policy control. The proposed operation makes prediction and acting rights independently grantable and revocable for the same delta.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate two-action worlds with identical common-action forecasts and opposite value on a rarely logged alternative. Compare world-model-only and a separate risk/OPE gate at matched samples. Kill if the independent-rights wrapper adds nothing to the stacked control.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check both legacy action choice and legacy consequence prediction under predictor-only exposure and full deployment.

### 16. Test continuity of the contrast when a rival becomes cheap to approximate (I)
- **Mechanism:** Compress the snapshot only if the approximation provably preserves counterexample membership and verdict signs on a pinned finite contract, or fall back to the exact rival at the boundary. Do not accept approximation on average loss alone.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [#313 #25](test-time-weights-2.md#25-let-evidence-cross-versions-only-if-the-rival-would-still-disagree-p) transfers obligations across versions; [metamorphic testing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3143561) uses relations to test outputs. This narrows the problem to a cheaper *fixed* comparator, not a new settlement claim.
- **Cheapest first test:** Quantize two-dimensional linear rivals with near-tie predictions; enumerate a fixed input grid. Compare an exact snapshot, uniform quantization and confidence-based fallback to exact scoring. Stop if savings require missing even one pinned counterexample or if ordinary fallback matches them.
- **Old-task retention check:** Run near-boundary legacy cases outside the compression-fit batch and report any changed verdict and service action.

## C. When a write changes which disagreements can be observed (#313 #01 × #22)

### 17. Account for missing disagreement before counting missing receipts (P)
- **Mechanism:** Evaluate both frozen forecasts at a shared pre-routing intake state where the consequence is defined, and register at least one required disagreement cell there. Track how many such cases still reach an outcome-capable path. If the contrast only exists downstream, do not impute it at intake.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Algorithmic Censoring](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3617694.3623247) tracks missing labels and [online disagreement-based active learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09056) targets contrasts. This operation keys observability to a disagreement cohort *before* an edit can divert its members rather than matching aggregate label support after routing.
- **Cheapest first test:** Make two routes whose total coverage and visible disagreements match, but one loses only predecessor-favored intake contrasts. Compare the two parent gates stacked with a fixed support gate, plus exposure-matched exploration. Drop the claim if stratifying generic support by frozen intake disagreement ties it at equal bytes and harm.
- **Old-task retention check:** Follow old ambiguous arrivals through all routes, counting correct service and hidden wrong acceptance independent of the log.

### 18. Make an observation-only switch that cannot change the forecast (P)
- **Mechanism:** Where reporting is independently configurable, permit a narrow fallback that restores the predecessor's reporting path for ordinary requests while leaving the candidate's forecast and service decision intact. Charge the reporting overhead; do not send extra requests. If reporting also changes treatment or outcomes, the switch is not diagnostic and grants no permission.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Separable effects](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8536652/) already decomposes treatments into components along different pathways. The proposed primitive is a mechanically restricted report-only fallback for a live edit's disputed slice, not discovery of separable causal effects.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use an independently controlled delivery flag, then break independence by making delivery change whether a request is serviced. Compare parent gates plus full rollback, always-on frozen reporting, and matched exploration. Drop it if always-on reporting dominates or the broken pair still settles.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure old decisions and delivery latency together, including tasks whose behavior depends on receiving a receipt.

### 19. Settle only if every admissible missing-label completion agrees (I)
- **Mechanism:** On a fixed upstream contrast cohort, compute the minimum and maximum supported paired score gap by assigning the two possible labels to each missing case in turn. Allow keep or reject only if the whole interval crosses the corresponding threshold; otherwise restrict or abstain. Do not apply this to policy utility without action support.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Performative Prediction with Selective Labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08272) already optimizes worst-case observed-data risk, and [Identification and Statistical Decision Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11318) studies decisions under partial identification. This is an edit-level bounds baseline, not a new missingness estimator.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate ten binary requests and compare complete-case scoring, global worst-case bounds, and bounds restricted to pinned contrasts. Reject if improved coverage comes only from changing the target population without saying so.
- **Old-task retention check:** Separately score the old contrast cohort and old agreement-region tasks; narrower bounds cannot excuse hidden failures outside the merit test.

### 20. Track the minimum missing losses that would reverse a write (I)
- **Mechanism:** Alongside the verdict, report the smallest admissible number of missing outcomes on a frozen contrast cohort that could overturn it. Apply a pre-registered tolerance for that sensitivity; do not treat the bound as evidence that those losses occurred.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Lee's sample-selection bounds](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-937x.2009.00536.x) and [selective-labels risk bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08272) already address uncertain outcomes under selection. This is a finite-stream robustness diagnostic for write settlement, not a new bound.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hand-sort possible paired-score swings on two equal-size partly censored cohorts, one with missing high-leverage cases. Compare card 19's full bounds and a count-only sensitivity rule. Drop it if it adds no decision-useful operating point.
- **Old-task retention check:** Include rare old high-leverage cases while varying the sensitivity tolerance; check what is lost through excess fallback as well as unsafe keeping.

### 21. Distinguish losing a witness from inheriting someone else's outage (P)
- **Mechanism:** If a pre-existing, comparable service lane stays on the predecessor, use its contemporaneous observation opportunities to separate a shared outage from a candidate-specific disappearance. When the lanes differ in unmeasured ways, use the comparison only as a warning, not attribution.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Off-Policy Confidence Sequences](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/karampatziakis21a.html) already handle supported comparisons in continuing traffic. The proposed operation distinguishes suspending a claim's verdict from debiting its deployment authority, based on evidence about the observation mechanism.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a common outage, an edit-local outage and a confounded lane switch, each with equal candidate-side silence. Compare the two #313 seed gates plus ordinary lane dashboards and matched-propensity gating. Kill it if the stack reaches the same outage/withdrawal tradeoff.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure old tasks across both lanes, including the lane that receives increased load when candidate authority is reduced.

### 22. Keep a disagreement live until its downstream effect can be seen (P)
- **Mechanism:** For a claim whose first decision changes which later disputes arise, register one bounded trajectory contrast and the terminal receipt paths under each supported continuation. Do not settle a trajectory claim from isolated one-step agreements or successes.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Off-policy evaluation for slate recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04812) already addresses support in combinatorial actions; [aTTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03441) studies how actions shape future learning data. The narrower object is a write obligation spanning the reachable path to its own delayed falsifier, not a new OPE estimator.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use a two-turn repair task where both first responses get acknowledgements but only one leaves a route to diagnose a later failure. Compare one-step disagreements plus terminal utility gating and exact supported path replay. Kill if the generic terminal gate ties at equal exposure or if no continuation contrast is supported.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate old two-step requests to completion, including cases that look correct on the first turn but fail the old second-turn goal.

### 23. Mark where a write exhausted its ability to be compared (P)
- **Mechanism:** When a candidate changes routing by degree, pre-register allowed exposure levels with nested permissions. Stop increasing authority at the first unsupported level, without erasing evidence for lower levels or laundering that evidence into full deployment.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SPIBB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924) already restricts policy deviations in uncertain states; [Performative Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673) already makes distributions deployment-dependent. The proposed reusable operation is to carry a *scope of settlement over exposure levels* for a particular edit.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use three fixed exposure levels where feedback at low exposure is representative but high exposure removes a whole route through queue congestion. Compare a matched static canary and a generic coverage-gated rollout. Reject if either attains the same accepted exposure and hidden-error frontier.
- **Old-task retention check:** At each exposure level score old cross-route tasks and congestion costs, including old work handled by the unchanged path.

### 24. Transfer a favorable verdict and its negative route together (P)
- **Mechanism:** To reuse a verdict in a new lane, require that both the forecast contrast and its failure-observation contract transfer. If familiar contexts still distinguish the predictions but the new lane cannot report rejection, let it inherit neither the acting right nor the settled score.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Transportability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01603) already distinguishes changes in causal mechanisms, while [#313 #25](test-time-weights-2.md#25-let-evidence-cross-versions-only-if-the-rival-would-still-disagree-p) transfers pending contrast across versions. Here a settled deployment right is portable only with continuing observability in the destination lane.
- **Cheapest first test:** Clone a scripted service twice: in one clone change only label formatting; in the other remove negative receipts. Compare always-transfer, restart, and the stack of forecast equivalence plus a destination support gate. Kill if the stack strictly dominates or ties scope and time-to-benefit.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check old tasks in the source and both destinations, measuring incorrect rejection and silent acceptance separately.

### 25. Require a finding that could undo a second write without touching the first (P)
- **Mechanism:** Make a later write conditional on preserving one supported case that distinguishes the second delta from the first-only policy. If it disables that case, suspend only the second write's authority on the intersecting slice; do not reopen the first verdict by default.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Owned Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15917) supports exact scoped withdrawal, and #313 already [audits interactions](test-time-weights-2.md#23-give-intersecting-writes-one-joint-chance-to-be-wrong-p) and [reserves referee power](test-time-weights-2.md#24-reserve-future-referee-power-across-competing-edits-p). The proposed distinction is a directional dependency: retain the first write's authority while the second must remain separable from it under the active composition.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate base, A, B and A+B in a tiny service table where only B-on-A diverts A's remaining failures. Compare full composite scoring, the parent's joint audit and dependency-scoped rollback. Kill it if a generic per-composite support gate with the same snapshots preserves as much behavior.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test old A-only, B-only and intersecting requests with both writes active and after narrowing or removing B.

### 26. Build twin logs that reverse the right decision (I; audit)
- **Mechanism:** Construct two worlds with identical upstream contrasts, actions, visible outcomes, timestamps and missing receipts. In one the missing cohort succeeds and in the other it fails. Require the learner to give the same unsupported verdict in both; then add one ordinary separating receipt in the smallest version where it changes the bound.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Actions Have Consequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26908) and [partial-identification decision theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11318) already establish distinguishability limits; [#313 #26](test-time-weights-2.md#26-prove-when-two-causal-stories-cannot-choose-a-write-i-audit) does the same for echo versus drift. This fixture targets selective loss *inside* a frozen predecessor contrast and is deliberately not a primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Write two six-row tables with one unlabeled high-leverage case. Reject any method that settles opposite ways from identical inputs or keeps a hidden bad write. A method that never acts after the decisive receipt is a safe but unhelpful control.
- **Old-task retention check:** Put an old task in the missing cohort and one in the unaffected cohort; score both externally, including fallback behavior in the supported slice.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can a write remain falsifiable on the same disagreements it can make disappear?** Start with 17 and 26, then test 01 and 02 separately. Require a win over the two #313 seed gates plus support stratified by frozen intake disagreements, and include real fixes at fixed opportunity, harmless demand loss, and censoring at equal visible volume.
