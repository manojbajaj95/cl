# Node: can we write only the part of an edit that survives training?

Parent: [PR #105 edit-description-lower-bound](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/105), especially sub-questions 1–2 (training invariants and specification dependencies).

**Question:** What part of an edit is invariant under later training, and can an editor write only into that part without freezing the learner or giving up old tasks?

## Root cause: receiver-dependent coincidences are not writable invariants

PR #105 counts the distinct tuples of requested post-edit behaviors along a trajectory of *unedited* receivers. At installation, `set x to 1` and `add 1 at x` can share a message if the receiver answers `0` there. A later answer of `2` makes them different requests with different outcomes. The message has not changed; what it omitted was a distinction the initial receiver made unnecessary. The union of confusion graphs over later receivers exposes that missing distinction. More importantly for this child, a distinction that remains readable in the receiver is not necessarily one an editor can change selectively. The parent counts descriptions; it does not implement an edit, run training on an edited receiver, or show that a learned edit was erased.

**What was measured.** In the six-input ℤ₄ lookup-table probe (1,197 set/add/remap requests; 200 trajectories per arm and horizon), the full intent costs `log₂(1197) ≈ 10.23` bits. The mean log-count of trajectory classes rises from 8.07 to 9.71 bits with one randomly rewritten cell per round and from 8.06 to 10.10 with two. After 32 rounds those increases are 1.64/2.16 (76%) and 2.04/2.17 (94%) of the initial **description saving**, not percentages of edits forgotten. A global shift takes back 46% in one round. With three cells shielded, the cost at 32 rounds is 8.95 bits versus the probe's exhaustive reachable-set estimate of 8.98. That controlled arm is evidence that reachable receiver states determine which collisions persist in this toy, not evidence that SGD has a useful invariant. The code counts classes exactly per sampled trajectory; the reported fractional bits are means of log-counts, not lengths of a deployable code. The two-anchor control changes the receiver used in the computation and costs 12.07 bits already at installation; it is not an equal-budget guarantee on the original request.

### A writable invariant needs a readout, a lift, and a noninterference check

Let `z` include weights **and learning state** (e.g., optimizer moments), `T_b` be one later training update on batch `b`, `E_r` a write, and `h` the behavior that the obligation tests. Declare a training family before searching for an invariant; training on directly contradictory labels cannot both overwrite and preserve the same output. One candidate is a readout `I` on reachable states with `I(T_b z) = I(z)` for every allowed `b`. It becomes a memory primitive only if an editor can realize a requested change `e_r` on that readout, `I(E_r z) = e_r(I(z))`, the obligation can be served by that change, and a lift into actual learning state leaves other behavior and new-task learnability intact. Withholding test batches, orders, and paraphrases tests generalization of all three claims. An invariant can be constant, redundant, outside the edit span, or expensive to change safely.

An exact positive control: in a linear learner `w ← w − η x(xᵀw−y)`, if every legal future `x` lies in `U`, `P_{U⊥}w` is conserved and a write in `U⊥` leaves future training predictions unchanged. An edit read on `x_e` with `P_{U⊥}x_e = 0` cannot use this channel at all. If protected old inputs span the entire complement, a nonzero write there cannot leave all their predictions unchanged either. Adding weight decay or one legal feature outside `U` can break the candidate invariant. This makes a cheap positive control and two negative controls, not a proposed rediscovery of null-space CL.

A weaker object can still be useful: the **effect of a write** on matched edited and unedited runs can persist without either receiver having a relevant invariant output. E.g., under `w ← w+b`, a fixed additive write preserves the contrast `w_edited−w_base` even as both absolute values drift; it can implement a relative offset, not a fixed target. Evaluate separately (i) decoding the description after training, (ii) the installed edit meeting its absolute or relative obligation **without reapplication**, and (iii) old behavior on tasks not targeted by the edit. An editable readout, a stable paired contrast, and a readout manufactured by restricting training are different hypotheses. Label which one is being tested.

### Prior-art screen and kill controls

- [PR #6 ledger, triage and top 10](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6): do not revive functional syndromes as a memory carrier without beating raw replay, conservation-of-weight statistics without finite-step/optimizer/rebalancing controls, or frozen slots as a new primitive. The Nerode item already studies equivalence under continuations and [#105](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/105) already applies it to training; the candidate here is a **selective intervention**, not a new quotient count. Gauge-orbit tags hide metadata; moving an observable obligation is a different bar. Test both initial margin and initial edit success before attributing later differences to training invariance.
- [#92](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/92) and [#96](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/96) separate intent, recipient-specific write, and outcome across bases. The meta-editor learns write policies from outcomes; [#97](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/97) asks how to find credit outside the writable span. Here the obligation is assumed known; the missing object is the part of a feasible write that later training will not undo. Finding outside credit does not establish durability, nor does a durable direction establish credit.
- Abstract-only arXiv screen: [OGD 1910.07104](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07104), [Adam-NSCL 2103.07113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07113) and [Null-LoRA 2512.15233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15233) cover projection/null-space baselines; [EWC 1612.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796), [PackNet 1711.05769](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05769) and [HAT 1801.01423](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423) cover importance and reserved-capacity baselines. [ROME 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262) localizes an immediate write; [tangent-space task arithmetic 2305.12827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827) is a baseline for linearized effect separation. [2511.05852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05852) directly tests knowledge-edit survival under fine-tuning, and [2608.24988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24988) separates recovery of behavior from reversal of the weight edit. Neither persistence alone nor a weight-distance proxy is a novelty claim. This screen is not a full novelty search.

For the cards below, compare to plain editing, the strongest obvious card-specific baseline, and equal-byte replay or explicit intent plus rewrite where applicable. Account for decoder, masks, optimizer state, side information, labels, and extra forward/update budget; charge protected capacity even if the parameter count stays fixed. Match install success and margin, and report new-task progress alongside old-task mean, worst group, and worst checkpoint. Score intended revisions separately from forgetting. All proposed first tests fit on CPU; none are claimed as results.

## Raw ideas (no self-filtering)

### A. Find a writable invariant rather than a stable feature

1. **Read–write rank test.** *Mechanism:* find common left unit eigendirections of permitted affine training maps, then compute the rank of their pairing with allowed edit directions after eliminating old-output changes. *Why it might be new:* prices the number of independently writable obligations, not the dimension or stability of a readout. *First test:* enumerate maps in a four-coordinate linear learner with a large invariant subspace but rank zero for one edit family. *Retention:* score held-out old examples after writing each independently controllable coordinate and after held-out training sequences.
2. **Minimal lift with a no-go witness.** *Mechanism:* solve `Cδ = d` (the requested invariant change), `J_old δ = 0` and an edit budget; on failure return a dual contradiction identifying which old obligation blocks the durable write. *Why it might be new:* distinguishes “cannot install” from “can install, but cannot make durable here” with a checkable obstruction. *First test:* compare exact LP answers with ordinary one-step edit reachability on a frozen-feature regressor. *Retention:* replay the old constraints on unseen examples, including the group cited by the obstruction.
3. **A write that becomes legible only after the learner settles.** *Mechanism:* choose a target basin of the declared training updates by crossing a boundary once; do not store the target value in a coefficient the updates repeatedly overwrite. *Why it might be new:* uses basin membership as the write readout, not a regressor for weight drift or a readout via relearning speed. *First test:* small coupled double-well objective with a learned binary behavior; compare a barrier-crossing write to a matched immediate-margin nudge. *Retention:* track old linear readout error during settling as well as in the final basin.
4. **Transferable reachability witness, not transferable coefficients.** *Mechanism:* certify the same invariant-space counterfactual with different concrete lifts at two points of one training trajectory. *Why it might be new:* treats success across checkpoints as a test of write access, not proof that an obligation or weight delta transfers. *First test:* rotate the editable feature basis between two checkpoints of a two-layer linear learner and test a lift fitted to checkpoint A versus re-solving from the witness at B. *Retention:* check both checkpoints' original old tasks and novel points on their protected decision boundaries.
5. **Optimizer-state completion.** *Mechanism:* when weights alone cannot lift a change to an invariant, solve jointly for the smallest change to weights and moments that yields the desired conserved readout. *Why it might be new:* uses optimizer state to complete a behavioral write under a fixed optimizer, not to store a new learning rule. *First test:* in a future-gradient-null coordinate with momentum `v⁺=βv`, `w⁺=w−ηv⁺`, test the invariant `w−ηβv/(1−β)` and compare aligned versus untreated momentum at matched initial effect. *Retention:* test unrelated old linear tasks along the transient, not just after moments decay.
6. **Break the invariant before trusting it.** *Mechanism:* search legal minibatch sequences for a counterexample to `I(T_b z)=I(z)` on *both* sides of an edit; refine the candidate instead of constraining those batches. *Why it might be new:* tests the training family where the write moves the learner, rather than certifying a readout only on the pre-edit path. *First test:* enumerate length-three sequences in a branching piecewise-linear learner where an edit crosses into a different update region; compare to checks on the unedited path. *Retention:* record worst old-group loss on the falsifying sequences and on a disjoint sequence set.

### B. Preserve a relation even when its operands move

7. **Moving-baseline offsets.** *Mechanism:* write a shared contrast between matched inputs whose absolute scores are both pushed by subsequent updates. *Why it might be new:* explicitly chooses a relative obligation based on cancellation, instead of treating absolute target erosion as forgetting in a task that only requires a difference. *First test:* train a toy scorer on drifting group intercepts; compare fixed-target and offset writes at matched initial pass rates. *Retention:* evaluate old absolute-calibration tasks separately from old contrast tasks; cancellation must not hide a calibration loss.
8. **Observable commutator.** *Mechanism:* choose `E` to minimize `h(T_b E z)−h(E T_b z)` on obligations while allowing unrelated outputs to change; then verify the obligation on both paths, not just their equality. *Why it might be new:* tests a write that can be moved across learner updates, rather than an order heuristic for two edit commands. *First test:* in a tiny nonlinear regression model, fit against single batches and test longer unseen interleavings; include a commuting no-op to catch vacuous wins. *Retention:* compare old tasks on both paths after every swap and at the end.
9. **Mixed-difference writes.** *Mechanism:* when allowed training changes additive main effects but a task needs a pair interaction, select an edit readable as the four-corner contrast `f11−f10−f01+f00`. *Why it might be new:* writes into a cancellation identity of the *training* nuisance family without freezing individual predictions or units. *First test:* binary factor regression with a held-out stream that violates additivity as a negative control; compare to writing the four outputs separately. *Retention:* test untouched pairs and all four old marginal predictions where absolute behavior is protected.
10. **Carry a contrast through a rotating chart.** *Mechanism:* infer a basis change from shared unlabeled observations and transport just the coordinates of an installed contrast, with no old labels or re-solving the edit. *Why it might be new:* needs measurable, composable transport of an obligation under learning, not a one-shot cross-base delta alignment. *First test:* compose rotations in a rank-two learned feature plane, then test nonlinear feature drift and a loop that returns to the same input distribution. *Retention:* evaluate prior contrast tasks and original untransported outputs after the loop.
11. **Order-only edit on a declared support.** *Mechanism:* for a ranking obligation, change an order relation and use a readout insensitive to later common monotone calibrations, while pinning any old absolute outputs that need values. *Why it might be new:* chooses the smallest serviceable type of behavior change (an ordering rather than values) before writing. *First test:* add one pairwise ordering to a small scorer under changing temperatures/biases; compare to an absolute score edit and an ordinary rank-loss edit under feature drift. *Retention:* check held-out old pair rankings and a separate absolute-score panel.
12. **Closed-cycle effect, not endpoint agreement.** *Mechanism:* test whether a transported edit returns the same obligation around training paths that restore the observed unedited state; a loop defect rejects the proposed readout. *Why it might be new:* measures path dependence of the edit even when endpoint checks of the base and old tasks initially agree. *First test:* two small update sequences with matched final unedited outputs but different moments; compare the edited-versus-unedited contrast through a common next step. *Retention:* test old tasks at loop closure and after that step, including ones that reveal the hidden-state difference.

### C. Construct write access without permanently reserving slots

13. **A write as a conserved total shared by moving parts.** *Mechanism:* couple ordinary updates so they can move content between two heads while preserving an editable sum used at inference. *Why it might be new:* makes the protected object an observable sum, not a hidden weight statistic or a frozen head; both heads may change. *First test:* two-head linear learner with exact transfer updates; compare with a frozen head, a norm channel, and unconstrained training at equal capacity. *Retention:* evaluate old sum-based and head-specific tasks, including after finite steps, momentum, and function-preserving rebalancing.
14. **Minimum change to the update law.** *Mechanism:* given one requested invariant contrast, solve for the smallest change to each training update that keeps that contrast while letting unrelated outputs drift. *Why it might be new:* only if it spends less plasticity by enforcing a sufficient relation, not a smaller sample of fixed old logits under OGD. *First test:* linear contrast benchmark with deliberately drifting calibrations, versus OGD on the same old examples and OGD with the contrast gradient as a strong null. *Retention:* check both edit obligations and prior tasks at matched new-task loss; kill the proposal if the contrast-gradient baseline ties.
15. **Refresh a basis, not its payload.** *Mechanism:* when upcoming training requires a new protected basis, perform an invertible change of readout coordinates that keeps existing edit effects while reusing all parameters. *Why it might be new:* requires exact obligation-preserving capacity turnover, not task-specific mask accumulation or reapplication of old edits. *First test:* alternating rank-one tasks in a fixed two-dimensional latent space; compare frozen allocation and equally sized replay, and plant a third constraint to force an impossibility. *Retention:* check every previous task before and after each basis refresh, including the task displaced by the third constraint.
16. **Split only the unstable residue.** *Mechanism:* compile a full request into an installed invariant effect plus a separately budgeted transient residual with an explicit unmet-obligation signal if it expires. *Why it might be new:* allocates storage and write capacity by semantic necessity under training, not by estimated half-life of a whole edit or bit count of its description. *First test:* two-coordinate regression with one editable invariant and one drifting operand; compare all-or-nothing writes and equal-byte full-intent reapplication. *Retention:* report performance on old tasks sharing either coordinate and charge errors where the expiring residue is still required.
17. **Share a compensation across independent writes.** *Mechanism:* derive one predictable adjustment that removes future drift common to several installed edits, without retaining a per-edit target to replay. *Why it might be new:* a single repair is justified by a shared training mode rather than by individual syndromes of failure. *First test:* several linear contrast writes with common gain drift plus independently perturbed gains; compare an analytic shared correction with equal-cost per-edit replay and simple scalar calibration. *Retention:* test untouched old outputs in the affected and unaffected groups, not just the repaired edits.
18. **Let a different computation carry the invariant.** *Mechanism:* if the learning path changes which circuit computes an obligation, require two carriers to agree on overlap and switch only with no loss in the observable contrast. *Why it might be new:* preservation is an obligation-level handoff between distinct computations, not reviving an old key or retaining a dormant module. *First test:* a small learner with two redundant features whose reliability alternates; compare overlap-checked switches to fixed routing and trainable gating with the same total state. *Retention:* test the old tasks at the handoff boundary and on contexts where the two features disagree.

### D. Identify what no invariant write can buy

19. **Merger gives a forgetting certificate.** *Mechanism:* find allowed updates sending states for two distinct edit targets to the same full learning state; no deterministic readout there can satisfy both. If every permitted choice of lifts admits such a merger, no editor can promise both targets under that training family. *Why it might be new:* gives a no-go for installed memory under the specified updates and budget, not a bound on a message to a decoder. *First test:* exhaust tiny finite learners with a reset update, then add one bit of protected state and compute which mergers are unavoidable. *Retention:* include protected old tasks as obligations in the same enumeration; exclude mergers that require an illegal overwrite.
20. **Protected capacity under repeated writes.** *Mechanism:* count how many distinct accumulated obligations can be assigned distinguishable invariant states online without violating old behavior, allowing different orders with the same obligations to share a state. *Why it might be new:* measures usable write capacity under training and sequential edits, rather than total edit count at collapse or an equivalence test for two edit records. *First test:* enumerate all three-edit streams in a four-cell toy with a fixed protected-state budget; compare exact capacity to independent single-edit counts. *Retention:* test the oldest edit and untouched old outputs after each prefix, for all orders.
21. **One minibatch as a counterexample to a relative edit.** *Mechanism:* find a legal batch that preserves a candidate absolute task panel but destroys an installed pairwise contrast, then ask whether excluding it removes genuinely needed learning. *Why it might be new:* prices the missing expressivity of a *specific* invariant in units of new-task progress rather than norm or horizon. *First test:* enumerate candidate batches in a tiny pair-ranking dataset and build the best retained-edit/new-task frontier with and without the witness batch. *Retention:* compare unseen old pairs to the old calibration panel that the witness matched.
22. **Noise and precision budget for writable state.** *Mechanism:* compare finite-precision distinguishability of invariant values with its real-valued controllability; state an edit capacity only at measured behavioral tolerances. *Why it might be new:* identifies when an exact conservation identity is useless for installing multiple readable obligations, distinct from protecting a weight watermark. *First test:* quantize an exact controlled linear learner at several precisions; compare direct stored labels and a frozen slot at equal bits. *Retention:* check all old tasks including near a decision boundary, and report loss during rather than only after training.
23. **A held-out intervention test for learned invariants.** *Mechanism:* discover candidate readouts from paired training forks, then require interventions on a readout to change the claimed obligation and interventions on its complement not to. *Why it might be new:* rejects correlation with trajectory identity or margin by testing the causal sufficiency of the editable part. *First test:* plant one real invariant and one observationally correlated decoy in a small learner; check recovery on forks with independently varied batch order and initial conditions. *Retention:* score unrelated old tasks under both interventions and on held-out forks.
24. **Invariant violation distinguishes drift from a legitimate revision.** *Mechanism:* permit a named edit operation, but not ordinary training, to move an observable value; when the stream appears to request a contradictory update, test whether it supplies the authority/evidence to invoke that operation. *Why it might be new:* specifies two transition laws for one behavioral variable, rather than equating every persistent target with a forever-frozen label. *First test:* interleave benign distractor training with explicit revisions to one synthetic rule, compare to constant freeze, stream replay and a task-ID gate. *Retention:* track false changes to unrevised rules and check that authorized revisions do not restore superseded answers.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Can we find and selectively lift a nontrivial edit into a learner's writable invariant?** (1–6, 23.) Strongest candidate: a read/write pair with a prospective certificate that a requested behavioral distinction is controllable, survives training, and leaves other old tasks intact. Start with the exact linear oracle, fail it deliberately with span expansion, then require a learned version to beat OGD and equal-byte replay on held-out nonlinear trajectories at matched plasticity. Reject stable but unwritable features and any certificate valid only before the edit.
2. **Can a write commute with ordinary learning on the obligations that matter, without a fixed state coordinate?** (7–12.) Candidate: a transportable behavioral effect, even when no useful absolute readout is constant. Look for stable relative effects when both baselines move, and disprove transport with unseen orderings or a closed-loop defect. A no-op, margin advantage, or failure on absolute obligations mislabeled as relative does not count.
3. **What is the minimal intervention on learning needed to make a missing write channel?** (13–18, 21, 24.) Candidate: preserve a sufficient observable relation or hand it between computations while allowing both states to learn. Require a Pareto gain over OGD on the same relation, replay, and frozen capacity; measure the cost of excluding the very batch that would break the invariant.
4. **When does training provably leave too few writable distinctions?** (19–22.) Candidate: a finite-state converse that returns a collision witness or a lower bound on the protected capacity needed for a stream of compatible edits. Require it to predict the onset of failure under held-out schedules better than counting single-edit successes. If no edit-observable channel exists under the declared family, store the missing obligation explicitly or relax the family; renaming the transient state will not fix it.
