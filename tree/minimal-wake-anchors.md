# Minimal wake anchors: the least side information that makes a wake provably land on the current skill

**Parent:** [Wrong wake fixed points](wrong-wake-fixed-points.md) ([PR #146](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/146)), developing its card #19 (minimum anchor bits), card #20 (cross-skill anchors), and ranked questions 4 and 5. **Grandparent:** [Rented constraints](rented-constraints.md) ([PR #145](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/145)). **Triage:** [flood-triage.md rows 234–235](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md#L234) (rented constraints and cross-pool round 30: ECR8 paired-world audits for wrong-but-stable wakes, ECR2 Nerode quotient over wake futures, ECR1 lease-charged edit bits). **Ledger:** screened against [reviews/idea-ledger.md](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md) (L1 prompt-checkpoint kill, composition-contract kill, margin and predictor gates, equal-byte/equal-bit controls) and [flood-top10.md](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-top10.md) (#2 Nerode quotient, #4 rate–drift law, #7 gauge-orbit tags). **Status:** Ideas only; no experiments run; no GPU.

**Node question:** PR #146 showed that a label-free wake `W` cannot tell a σ-flipped old answer from the right one (by equivariance) and that loss-descending wakes revive deeper retired versions `A_v1` over a newer `A_v2` (BCE4). What is the **least side information** — a few labels, a hash, a paired-world audit, a version stamp, or a co-resident check — that makes a wake provably land on the current skill `A_v`? And is that minimum bit bound a **new object**, or does it reduce to existing bounds (zero-error side-information coding / top-10 #4's rate–drift law, teaching dimension, active TTA, or saved deltas)?

## Root cause and whether the bound is a new object

Let `A_v` be the current version of a buried skill, `L` the allowed lease family (the set of lease-end states `θ_end` reachable under B's leased training from awake state `θ_0`), and `W` a frozen label-free wake operator. From any `θ_end ∈ L`, `W` (and its perturbed restarts) can settle on a finite or low-dimensional **reachable attractor set** `Attr(W; L) ⊆ Fix(W)` containing the true `A_v*`, its symmetry twins `σA_v*` (`σ ∈ G_A`), deeper retired basins `A_1*, ..., A_{v-1}*`, and spurious attractors (collapse, mixtures, B-absorbed states).

Define the **wake-anchor bound** `κ(A_v, L, W)` as the minimum number of bits of side information `s` (stored at burial/revision time or queried at wake time, with every index, seed, and metadata bit charged) such that a wake protocol `W_s(θ_end)` lands in the basin of `A_v*` for every `θ_end ∈ L`. Four distinct bit costs enter `κ`:

1. **Symmetry-breaking bits (`log₂ |G_A|`).** Every label symmetry `σ ∈ G_A` that commutes with `W` creates an indistinguishable twin `σA_v*`. Breaking `G_A` requires at least `log₂ |G_A|` bits (1 bit for binary polarity; up to `log₂(K!)` for `K`-class permutation, or less if B's lease or a co-resident skill already breaks a subgroup).
2. **Version-dominance bits (`log₂ |V_A(L)|`).** Distinguishing current `A_v*` from deeper retired basins `A_1*, ..., A_{v-1}*` reachable from `L` is an *ordering* problem (recency against basin depth), not just class disambiguation. A label placed where `A_v = A_{v-1}` spends bits without breaking staleness.
3. **Spurious-basin exclusion bits.** Collapse, mixtures, and B-absorbed fixed points can be pruned by label-free structure (PR #146's partition floor and B-constraint) when they have distinct geometry, or by side information when they do not.
4. **The drift-compatibility tax (the top-10 #4 loan).** An anchor written at burial time `t_0` is read at wake time `t_1` *after* B's lease. Any state-space or activation-space anchor (a weight/feature hash) is decoded using the drifted host `θ_{t_1}`, so it pays top-10 #4's zero-error loan `log₂ χ(⋃_{t ∈ L} G_t)` over the union of confusion graphs along `L`. Input-space anchors (labels on public inputs) avoid decoder drift, but pay **pointer bits** (`log₂ N` to name which input) unless the probe is deterministically keyed, and still fail staleness if they miss the changed region `{x : A_v(x) ≠ A_{v-1}(x)}`.

### Is `κ(A_v, L, W)` a new object?

An honest answer splits into the **collapse cases** (where `κ` is old theory in new clothes) and the **three irreducible properties** (where it is genuinely new):

- **When `κ` is NOT new (reductions to watch for and test against):**
  - *Passive post-wake selection* (`W` runs blind, then `s` picks among `Attr(W; L)`): `κ` reduces to Witsenhausen's zero-error side-information rate / top-10 #4's rate–drift law (`⌈log₂ χ(G_wake)⌉`) with `W(θ_end)` as the decoder, or to auditor-assisted model-identity verification ([2610.25982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25982)).
  - *Pure label-set verification* (`s` is a set of `(x, y)` pairs ruling out every wrong attractor in `Attr(W; L)`): `κ` reduces to the **teaching dimension** (Goldman & Kearns 1995) of `A_v` inside the hypothesis class `Attr(W; L)`, or budgeted active test-time adaptation ([WISE-ATTA 2609.37687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37687), [2611.09084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09084)).
  - *External pointer or coordinate reset* (`s` indexes a stored checkpoint or resets `P_A` coordinates): reduces to saved deltas, PackNet, or content-addressed versioning ([2609.15576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15576)) — true by construction.
- **When `κ` IS a new CL object (three falsifiable signatures):**
  1. **Dynamical steering vs. passive verification (`κ_steer ≠ κ_select`).** Static coding theory and teaching dimension treat the decoder/concept class as fixed. Here, an anchor injected *during* `W` (e.g. a 1-bit nudge at a pitchfork bifurcation, or a half-space tilt on a proximal step) changes which basin `θ_end` flows into, whereas a non-differentiable hash only *verifies* endpoints after `W` finishes. A measurable gap `κ_select − κ_steer` (or its converse when blind restarts + tiny hash beat weak steering) is a dynamical control property of `(W, L)` absent from static rate–drift or teaching-dimension bounds.
  2. **Dynamical decompression / amplification ratio (`ρ = H(A_recovered) / κ ≫ 1`).** Storing `b` bits of replay labels specifies at most `O(b)` bits of `A` in a naive or linear host. A wake anchor leverages the surviving unlabeled payload in `θ_end` plus `W`'s contraction so that `κ ≈ log₂ |G_A| + log₂ |V_A| ≪ H(A)`. In the PR #145 linear-host negative control (where B overwrites `P_A`), `κ = H(A)` (`ρ = 1`). Beating equal-bit replay and equal-bit saved deltas by a large `ρ` is the operational proof that `W` is decompressing a surviving payload rather than re-learning from `s`.
  3. **Coupled symmetry × recency identifiability under lease drift.** Top-10 #4 prices decoder drift (no basin-depth staleness); teaching dimension prices hypothesis separation (no lease drift or attractor dynamics); recurrent-state migration bounds ([2611.19345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19345), [2609.25757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25757)) price behavioral quotients across model swaps (no competing repair attractors). Here, the minimal anchor sits on the joint frontier of *drift invariance*, *symmetry breaking*, and *recency dominance*.

## What would count

Every card inherits the PR #142, PR #145 (row 234), and PR #146 contracts (randomized dormancy at fixed size; A passes when learned, may fail during the lease, must pass after `W_s`; frozen wake protocol on disjoint development histories; six-way wrong-answer taxonomy: correct, σ-flip, collapse, mixture, absorbed into B, stale `A_v1`; σ-twins, margin-matched `A_v1→A_v2` revision twins, A-naive hosts, and the linear-host negative control). This node adds:

- **Strict total-bit accounting.** Every bit of side information is billed: label bits, **input-pointer bits** (choosing 1 input out of `N` public items costs `⌈log₂ N⌉` bits unless the probe is deterministically keyed from a public rule), hash digest bits, version-stamp bits, audit-pair bits, and any per-skill threshold bits. Nothing is "free" unless it already exists in the live host (e.g. live C or live B).
- **Mandatory equal-bit controls.** Every card must beat, at the **exact same bit budget `b`**, (i) labelled A replay (`b` bits of `(x, y)` pairs, pointer bits charged), (ii) a quantized/low-rank saved weight delta `Δθ_A` of `b` bits, (iii) a random-placement anchor of `b` bits (proving anchor *choice* matters), (iv) plain 0-bit `W` plus PR #146's label-free detectors, and (v) GPM/TRGP and DER++ at matched B progress.
- **Ledger discipline.** Match `A_v2` margin across revision twins (no margin confound); gate every predictor/audit card on target variance exceeding seed variance; never count an external key-value lookup to stored weights as a wake anchor (true by construction); score old-task retention (untouched A, co-resident C, and B kept after wake) in every first test.

## Idea cards

Shared toy unless stated otherwise: width-64 ReLU MLP host; tasks A (binary or 4-class), C (untouched co-resident), and B (leased onto A's GPM directions as in PR #145 card 1); `W` = 4 frozen public SGD or proximal steps; 16 development and 16 held-out histories; A⁺/A⁻ σ-twins, `A_v1→A_v2` margin-matched revision twins, A-naive hosts, and a linear-host negative control. "Retention" always reports A (per twin/revision family and unchanged inputs), C throughout, and B kept after wake.

### A. Measuring and separating `κ(A_v, L, W)` as a new object

#### 1. Wake-anchor bit curve and amplification ratio `ρ(b)` (primitive; audit)

- **Mechanism:** Sweep total anchor budget `b ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64}` bits on a buried skill `A_v`, plotting post-wake accuracy and wrong-basin rate (per failure type) for anchored `W_s` versus equal-bit replay and equal-bit saved deltas. Define the empirical amplification ratio `ρ = H(A_recovered) / b*`, where `b*` is the minimum bits to cross a fixed accuracy floor (e.g. 95%) with < 1% σ-flip and stale rates. This directly tests whether `κ` exists as a sub-linear regime (`b* ≪ H(A)`) rather than collapsing to replay.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Replay-necessity lower bounds ([2006.05188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05188), [2204.10830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10830)) bound memory without a wake operator; PR #146 card 19 proposed checking anchor bits on σ-twins only. This measures the full `(b, failure-type)` curve across both symmetry and staleness and establishes `ρ` as the core metric against equal-bit replay and saved deltas.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4-class A (`log₂ 4! ≈ 4.6` symmetry bits) + 1 revision (`1` version bit), 16 held-out histories × 8 seeds on CPU MLP. Linear-host negative control must give `ρ ≈ 1` (anchored `W_s` ties equal-bit replay). Claim dies if `b*` for anchored `W_s` is within 2× of equal-bit replay or equal-bit saved deltas.
- **Old-task retention check:** A per σ-twin and revision twin (anchor inputs excluded from eval), untouched A, C throughout, B kept after wake.

#### 2. Steering-vs-verification gap `κ_select − κ_steer` (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Deliver the exact same `b`-bit anchor in two modes: **steering** (a differentiable hinge/nudge applied inside `W` at step 0 or at the bifurcation step) versus **verification** (run `K` perturbed restarts of blind `W`, then pick the fixed point that best matches the `b`-bit anchor). If `κ_steer < κ_select` at matched compute, the anchor is acting as a dynamical control signal that shapes basins, separating `κ` from static Witsenhausen coding and top-10 #4.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Top-10 #4 ([PR #105](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/105)) and Heegard–Berger / Witsenhausen coding analyze static decoding with side information; teacher-anchored selection ([2609.31155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31155)) selects among finished candidates. Comparing *in-dynamics steering* against *post-hoc selection* at equal bits and equal FLOPs is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** On the MLP with σ-twins and `T_1/T_2 = 4` revision twins, compare steering (`W_s`, 1 run) vs verification (blind `W` × `K ∈ {4, 16}` restarts + `b`-bit selector) at `b ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}` and matched total backward passes. Report both gaps: `κ_select − κ_steer` and when verification beats steering (e.g. high-curvature barriers).
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type, C, and B after the steered vs selected wake (steering must not distort B more than a blind restart).

#### 3. Anchor drift-loan crossover: input-space vs. state-space anchors (primitive; audit)

- **Mechanism:** Compare equal-bit **input-space anchors** (labels on deterministically keyed public inputs) against **state-space anchors** (signed hashes of hidden activations or `J_W` linear response) as lease length `H ∈ {0, 4, 16, 64, 256}` B-steps grows. Top-10 #4 predicts state-space anchors pay a growing drift loan `log₂ χ(⋃_{t ≤ H} G_t)` as B moves the host, while input-space anchors stay drift-free until `A` itself is revised. Measure the crossover lease length `H*`.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Top-10 #4 ([PR #105](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/105)) measures description-bit growth under drift in a lookup-table receiver; [CacheReforge 2609.30884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30884) bounds stale KV recovery under evolving adapters. Neither compares input-space vs state-space wake anchors across lease length at equal bits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Sweep `H` and `b ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}` on the MLP; measure anchor decode error and post-wake A accuracy for both anchor types versus equal-bit replay. Claim dies if state-space anchors do not degrade faster with `H` than input-space anchors at equal `b`.
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type across `H`, C throughout, B progress at lease end `H`.

#### 4. Wake-attractor teaching dimension vs. skill teaching dimension (primitive; audit)

- **Mechanism:** On a small finite-family skill class `H_A` (e.g. parity/threshold rules on `d` features), compute both the classical **teaching dimension** `TD(A_v, H_A)` (minimum labels to rule out all rival rules in `H_A`) and the **wake-attractor teaching dimension** `TD(A_v, Attr(W; L))` (minimum labels to rule out all empirically reachable wrong fixed points of `W` under `L`). Test whether `W` shrinks teaching dimension from `Ω(d)` to `O(log |G_A| + log |V_A|)`, and check whether adding a teaching label *inside* `W` creates new fixed points not in `Attr(W; L)` (which would prove `κ` is not even `TD(A_v, Attr(W; L))`).
- **Nearest art / delta:** Teaching dimension (Goldman & Kearns 1995) and PR #143's witness counting assume a fixed hypothesis class. Measuring how a weight-space repair operator compresses teaching dimension — and whether steering alters the attractor set itself — directly answers whether `κ` is reducible to teaching dimension.
- **Cheapest first test:** `d ∈ {8, 16, 32}` linear/2-layer feature rules in an MLP; enumerate `Attr(W; L)` via 64 perturbed restarts + σ-twins + revision twins; compare `TD(A_v, H_A)`, `TD(A_v, Attr(W; L))`, and empirical `b*` of steered `W_s`.
- **Old-task retention check:** A on held-out inputs (teaching set excluded), C, B after wake.

### B. Minimal-label anchors: 1-to-few bits in input space

#### 5. Single-bit bifurcation-point label (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Rather than anchoring on a random A input, find at burial time the public input `x*` whose A-output changes most along the leading unstable singular vector of `I − J_W(θ_0)` (PR #146 card 2's pitchfork mode), and store its 1-bit sign `y* = sign(f_A(x*))` (plus pointer bits, or restrict `x*` to a tiny fixed candidate pool of size `M` so pointer cost is `log₂ M`). During `W`, apply a soft sign constraint on `(x*, y*)` precisely when `σ_min(I − J_W)` dips. One bit placed on the unstable manifold breaks σ-symmetry with maximal dynamical leverage.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Active learning / uncertainty sampling picks high-entropy inputs of the current classifier; [WISE-ATTA 2609.37687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37687) queries high-uncertainty test inputs. Here the input is chosen by the **repair operator's bifurcation spectrum**, not by classifier entropy (which is zero at the awake fixed point).
- **Cheapest first test:** Pool size `M = 16` (4 pointer bits + 1 label bit = 5 bits total); compare bifurcation-selected 1-label anchor vs random 1-label anchor, highest-margin 1-label anchor, and 5-bit replay on σ-twins. Gate: target leverage spread across the 16 candidates must exceed seed noise.
- **Old-task retention check:** A per σ-twin (excluding `x*`), C, B kept after wake.

#### 6. Change-frontier label: 1 bit on `A_v1 ≠ A_v2` (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** At revision time (`A_v1 → A_v2`), the host sees both the old answer and the new one. Select 1 public input `x_Δ` from the **change frontier** `{x : A_v1(x) ≠ A_v2(x)}` with the largest `A_v1`-vs-`A_v2` logit contrast, and store its new label `A_v2(x_Δ)` (keyed from a small public pool so total cost is `1 + log₂ M` bits). During `W`, enforce agreement on `(x_Δ, A_v2(x_Δ))`. Because `A_v1*` and `A_v2*` disagree maximally at `x_Δ`, this single label blocks BCE4 stale revival even when `A_v1` has a much deeper basin (`T_1/T_2 = 16`).
- **Nearest art / delta:** Replay of revised examples (DER++/experience replay) stores whole batches of revision data; PR #146 card 13 suppresses `A_v1` at revision time without stored bits. Using a single change-frontier bit to bias a **public wake** against a deeper retired basin is new and directly tests the version-dominance half of `κ`.
- **Cheapest first test:** PR #146 card 8's grid (`T_1/T_2 ∈ {1/4, 1, 4, 16}`, matched `A_v2` margin); compare 1 change-frontier label + `W` against 1 random `A_v2` label + `W`, equal-bit revision replay without `W`, and PR #146's proximal wake.
- **Old-task retention check:** `A_v2` on changed inputs (excluding `x_Δ`) and unchanged inputs, `A_v1` absence, C, B.

#### 7. Coset-generator anchor for multi-class symmetries (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** For a `K`-class skill with symmetry group `G_A` (e.g. `S_K` permutations or cyclic shifts), naive per-class anchoring stores `K` labelled examples. Instead, store a minimal **group base** — the fewest class-identity bits (`⌈log₂(K!)⌉` bits for `S_K`, or `⌈log₂ K⌉` bits for cyclic shifts, encoded as pairwise orderings `y(x_i) < y(x_j)` on `m` public probes) that fixes only the identity element of `G_A`, leaving `W` to recover the class boundaries from the surviving partition.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Group bases (Sims 1970) are classical in computational group theory; unsupervised clustering + Hungarian matching uses labelled validation sets post-hoc. Using a minimal group-base anchor inside a weight-space wake tests whether `κ` scales as `log₂ |G_A|` rather than `O(K)` examples.
- **Cheapest first test:** `K ∈ {2, 4, 8}`-class A on CPU MLP; compare minimal group-base anchor + `W` against `K`-shot replay, random equal-bit labels + `W`, and equal-bit saved delta. Kill if minimal base fails to beat random equal-bit labels or needs `Ω(K)` full examples.
- **Old-task retention check:** Per-class A accuracy (excluding anchor probes), permutation-error rate, C, B.

#### 8. Zero-index deterministic public-probe anchor (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Naming which input `x_i` a label belongs to costs `⌈log₂ N⌉` pointer bits, which can dwarf a 1-bit label. Eliminate pointer bits by hashing `(skill_id, version_v)` to pick `m` deterministic inputs `(x_1, ..., x_m)` from the frozen public stream and storing *only* the `m`-bit binary vector `(sign(f_A(x_1)), ..., sign(f_A(x_m)))`. Total storage is literally `m` bits (e.g. 4 bits), with zero pointer overhead; if `m` exceeds `log₂ |G_A| + log₂ |V_A| + log₂(1/δ)`, a random deterministic tuple separates all wrong attractors with probability `≥ 1 − δ` by Johnson–Lindenstrauss / random-hash collision bounds.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Universal hashing and random-probe watermarking; equal-bit replay must pay pointer bits (or store raw inputs). Zero-index public-probe anchors isolate the **pure label-bit floor** of `κ` by removing addressing overhead.
- **Cheapest first test:** `m ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}` bits stored (0 pointer bits) vs equal-bit replay (which at 16 bits can store at most ~1 indexed label) and vs card 5/6 (which pay pointer bits for optimal probe placement). Tests whether *more random zero-index bits* beat *fewer optimally placed indexed bits* at equal total storage!
- **Old-task retention check:** A per twin and revision family (excluding the `m` hashed probes), C, B.

#### 9. Active wake-time label query on attractor disagreement (improvement)

- **Mechanism:** Store **0 bits** at burial time. At wake time, run PR #146 card 1's perturbed-restart census to find the candidate fixed points `Attr(W; θ_end) = {θ_1*, ..., θ_r*}`. If `r = 1` (unique basin), spend 0 bits. If `r > 1`, pick the public input `x_q` where the candidate fixed points disagree maximally and request **1 live label** `y_q` (charged as 1 query bit + interaction cost), eliminating all incompatible attractors; repeat until `r = 1`. Expected cost is `0` on safe leases and `⌈log₂ r⌉` bits only when ambiguity actually occurs.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Query-by-committee (Seung et al. 1992) and budgeted active TTA ([WISE-ATTA 2609.37687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37687), [2611.09084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09084)) query over model uncertainty on a test stream. Here the committee is the discrete set of **weight-space fixed points of `W`**, so queries are zero when the lease stayed inside a unique basin and only trigger on genuine multi-attractor wakes. Weakness stated up front: blind to pure σ-flips if *all* restarts land in `σA_v*` (then `r = 1` is wrong).
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare active attractor-disagreement querying against fixed burial-time anchors (cards 5, 8), random active queries, and WISE-ATTA-style entropy queries at equal average label bits across mixed safe/unsafe leases.
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type (explicitly reporting the missed pure-σ-flip rate when `r = 1`), C, B.

### C. Hashes and cryptographic / geometric commitments

#### 10. σ-breaking signed output sketch (differentiable perceptual hash) (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** PR #146 card 6's spectral fingerprint is σ-invariant (blind to polarity flips). At burial/revision time, draw a deterministic public random matrix `R ∈ {−1, +1}^{b × M}` (from a public seed, 0 stored bits) over `M` deterministic public inputs `X_pub`, and store the `b`-bit signed sketch `h = sign(R · f_A(X_pub)) ∈ {−1, +1}^b`. Unlike a cryptographic hash, `h` is **differentiable through a soft hinge** `∑ max(0, γ − h_j (R · f_θ(X_pub))_j)` inside `W`, breaks σ-flips (`R·(−f_A)` flips every bit of `h`!), catches collapse and stale `A_v1`, and averages over `M` public inputs so it is less fragile than a single point label.
- **Nearest art / delta:** SimHash / 1-bit compressed sensing (Boufounos & Baraniuk 2008) and random-projection sketches (killed in the ledger for *sparse fault localization* in MEMIT because damage was diffuse, PR #24). Here the sketch is NOT localizing sparse damage; it is **breaking global symmetry and selecting among discrete wake attractors** in `b` bits.
- **Cheapest first test:** `b ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}` bits on the MLP with σ-twins and revision twins; compare differentiable-sketch `W_h` vs zero-index point labels (card 8) at equal `b`, equal-bit replay, and PR #146 card 6's unsigned spectral fingerprint.
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type (excluding `X_pub`), C, B kept after wake.

#### 11. Anti-anchor: Bloom-filter / diff-hash of retired versions (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** When `A_v1` is revised to `A_v2`, store a tiny `b`-bit signed sketch (card 10) of the **retired** version `A_v1` (or a `b`-bit Bloom filter of hashes of `A_1, ..., A_{v-1}`) rather than of `A_v2`. During `W`, add a **repulsive margin** pushing `f_θ(X_pub)` away from any retired sketch. Tests a clean theoretical question: is *negative* side information ("never land on a retired version") cheaper or more robust to further refinement than positive specification ("land on `A_v2`")?
- **Nearest art / delta:** Negative-gradient unlearning and Hopfield unlearning / Daydreaming ([2604.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19258)) suppress old attractors at revision time; PR #146 card 13 modifies weights at revision time. Storing a compact **negative commitment** that stays active during future wakes is new, and directly tests negative vs positive side information at equal bits.
- **Cheapest first test:** On `V ∈ {2, 4, 8}` sequential revisions of A (`T_1/T_2 = 4`), compare `b`-bit repulsive anti-anchor + `W` against `b`-bit positive anchor (card 10) + `W`, PR #146 card 13's revision scar, and equal-bit replay. Prediction: anti-anchors alone cannot stop σ-flips of `A_v2`, so report σ-flip vs stale rates separately.
- **Old-task retention check:** `A_v2` on changed and unchanged inputs, `A_1..A_{v-1}` absence, C, B.

#### 12. Access-invariant payload-margin hash (drift-resistant state anchor) (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** State-space hashes fail because B's lease rotates and rescales the access layer (the top-10 #4 drift loan). Instead, hash a quantity invariant to access-layer rescaling and rotation: the `b`-bit sign pattern of **pairwise relative margins** `sign(‖h_p(x_i)‖ − ‖h_p(x_j)‖)` or signed cosine projections against a fixed public probe pair inside the **payload** coordinates `p` (PR #145 card 2's split). Because B only leases access coordinates `a`, this hash pays zero drift loan along valid leases while still breaking σ-flips (for signed payload coordinates) and detecting silent payload loss.
- **Nearest art / delta:** PR #145 card 2 splits access vs payload; [2605.21972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21972) studies label-free post-pruning recoverability; PR #106 studies training-invariant edit content. Hashing an access-invariant payload functional is the wake-anchor counterpart of training-invariant descriptions.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compare access-invariant payload hash vs raw activation hash (card 3) and input-space sketch (card 10) at `b ∈ {4, 16}` across lease lengths `H ∈ {4, 16, 64, 256}` under PR #145 card 2's access-only lease and under an unrestricted lease (negative control: must break when B writes payload).
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type, payload decodability, C, B.

#### 13. Commit-and-open wake verification (cryptographic hash + restart search) (improvement; audit)

- **Mechanism:** Store a `b`-bit cryptographic hash (e.g. truncated SHA-256 of the quantized argmax vector of `A_v` on deterministic `X_pub`, `b ∈ {8, 16, 32}`). This hash provides **zero gradient and zero steering** — it can only accept or reject a completed wake. Pair it with `K` perturbed restarts (or different public batch orders) of blind `W`, returning the first fixed point whose hash matches (or abstaining if none matches). Establishes a clean empirical upper bound on pure verification (`κ_select`) with exact false-accept rate `2^{-b}`.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Cryptographic commitments and checkpoint integrity ([2609.15576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15576), [2610.25982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25982)); PR #146 card 1's restart census has no acceptance oracle. Here the hash is a pure audit baseline that separates "can blind `W` *reach* `A_v*` on some restart?" from "does `W` need *steering* to reach it?".
- **Cheapest first test:** `b = 16` bits (false-accept `< 2^{-15}`), `K ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64}` restarts on σ-twins and revision twins; compare against steered 16-bit sketch (card 10) and equal-bit replay. Claim dies as a method if blind restarts never hit `A_v*` on deep stale twins (which simultaneously proves steering is necessary!).
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type on accepted vs abstained wakes, C, B.

### D. Paired-world audits and version stamps

#### 14. Paired-world minimal audit anchor (ECR8 operationalized) (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Directly develops `flood-triage.md` row 235's ECR8: construct at burial/revision time a tiny set of `m` **answer-changing paired worlds** `{(x_i, w_i^{(0)}, w_i^{(1)})}` where `A_v` must flip its answer between `w_i^{(0)}` and `w_i^{(1)}`, whereas a stale `A_{v-1}`, a B-absorbed shortcut, or a constant collapse keeps the answer fixed (or flips in the opposite direction). Store only the `m`-bit **contrast sign** `sign(f(x_i, w_i^{(1)}) − f(x_i, w_i^{(0)}))` (1 bit per pair, zero absolute labels). Use the contrast sign both to certify leases offline (ECR8) and to anchor `W` online.
- **Nearest art / delta:** PR #143 / PR #144 use crossed-evidence pairs during training; ECR8 (`flood-triage.md` row 235) proposed offline lease certification from paired worlds; [2607.01854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01854) uses a two-signal audit for abliterated checkpoints. Using a `m`-bit paired-world contrast sign as a **minimal wake anchor** — and testing whether 1 contrast bit beats 1 absolute label bit on both σ-flips and stale revival — is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** Fact-coupled skill A (as in PR #143's crossed-evidence toy) with σ-twins and `A_v1→A_v2` revision twins; compare `m ∈ {1, 2, 4}` paired-world contrast bits against `m` single-input label bits (card 6/8) and equal-bit replay, plus random paired audits of equal size (ECR8's required control).
- **Old-task retention check:** A on held-out paired worlds (both current and counterfactual premises), untouched facts, C, B.

#### 15. Epoch-keyed wake operator `W_v` (monotonic version stamp) (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Instead of a single version-blind `W`, parameterize the frozen public wake family `{W_v}` by a `⌈log₂ V⌉`-bit monotonic version stamp `v` (e.g. `v` seeds a deterministic public-batch weighting and auxiliary self-consistency projection). At revision `v`, spend a few steps making `A_v` a fixed point of `W_v` while ensuring `A_{v-1}` is *not* a fixed point of `W_v` (an epoch-specific basin shift). At wake time, supplying the `⌈log₂ V⌉`-bit integer `v` runs `W_v`, which selectively contracts to `A_v*`. Total side information: `⌈log₂ V⌉` bits (2 bits for 4 versions).
- **Nearest art / delta:** Ledger kill L1 (prompt-addressable checkpoints) packed past versions into one LoRA under prompt tags and lost to equal-byte saved deltas while hurting current task 1 by 8.12 pp. Here we do **not** try to preserve or serve past versions — we only want the **current** version `v` to be the unique attractor of `W_v`, avoiding the capacity competition that killed L1. Still, L1's equal-byte saved-delta control and current-task retention floor bind this card strictly.
- **Cheapest first test:** `V = 4` sequential revisions (2-bit stamp) on CPU MLP with `T_1/T_2 = 4`; compare epoch-keyed `W_v` against version-blind `W`, proximal `W` (PR #146 card 9), 2-bit change-frontier label (card 6), and equal-byte saved delta. Kill if `W_v` hurts current `A_v` or C by > 1 pp (the L1 failure mode) or fails to beat version-blind proximal `W`.
- **Old-task retention check:** Current `A_v` (before and after lease), absence of `A_1..A_{v-1}`, C throughout, B kept after wake.

#### 16. Half-space version stamp on proximal wakes (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** PR #146 cards 9–10 showed a proximal wake lands on `A_v2*` inside the Voronoi radius `d(A_v1*, A_v2*)/2`, and fails once B pushes `θ_end` across the bisector into `A_v1`'s cell. At revision time, record a **1-bit version orientation** relative to a public-data proxy for the revision axis `u_v = (g_pub(A_v2) − g_pub(A_v1)) / ‖·‖` (or a low-bit quantized direction `u_v` of `b` bits), and add a linear half-space tilt `−α ⟨θ − θ_end, u_v⟩` to the proximal wake. This shifts the Voronoi boundary away from `A_v2*`, extending recency-safe lease radius past `d/2` at minimal bits.
- **Nearest art / delta:** PR #146 cards 9–10 (proximal wake and Voronoi radius) use isotropic `‖θ − θ_lease-end‖²`. Tilting the proximal metric along a `b`-bit revision orientation is new and directly tests how many bits buy how much extra Voronoi radius beyond `d/2`.
- **Cheapest first test:** Sweep lease radius multipliers `{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0} · d/2` on PR #146 card 10's setup with `b ∈ {1, 4, 16}`-bit tilted proximal wake vs isotropic proximal wake (`b = 0`) and equal-bit saved delta. Claim dies if the stale-revival knee does not shift past `1.0 · d/2`.
- **Old-task retention check:** `A_v2` on changed/unchanged inputs, `A_v1` absence, C, B per radius multiplier.

#### 17. Live-premise version stamp (0-extra-bit causal anchor) (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Connects BCE4 (`flood-triage.md` row 232) and PR #147 (data-only edit cost): when `A_v2` differs from `A_v1` because an underlying fact/rule premise was updated (and that current premise is already live in the environment or prompt), the **live premise itself** is a 0-extra-bit version stamp. Run `W` on public queries paired with the live premise and constrain the fixed point to satisfy premise-following sensitivity (output moves when the live premise is perturbed on a counterfactual twin), rejecting any fixed point that serves the hardcoded retired `A_v1` answer.
- **Nearest art / delta:** [FutureBench 2611.23601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23601) evaluates knowledge–context conflict; BCE1/BCE4 (`flood-triage.md` row 232) score whether a wake restores procedure vs retired facts. Using the live premise as a **0-bit version stamp inside `W`** tests whether reading skills have `b_ver = 0` while memorized skills pay `b_ver > 0`.
- **Cheapest first test:** Crossed-evidence toy (PR #143) with high-crossing (reading) vs low-crossing (memorizing) hosts under `A_v1→A_v2` premise updates; compare 0-bit live-premise `W` vs plain `W` and 1-bit change-frontier label (card 6). Prediction: 0-bit stamp works on reading hosts and fails on memorizing hosts.
- **Old-task retention check:** Procedure accuracy at both live and novel supplied premises, absence of retired `A_v1` answers, untouched facts, C, B.

#### 18. Chained revision-delta parity for deep version histories (improvement; audit)

- **Mechanism:** When a skill undergoes `V` revisions (`A_1 → A_2 → ... → A_V`), storing a fresh label per version costs `O(V)` bits, while a version index costs `O(log V)` bits. Test the scaling law of `κ` with `V`: store a chained `b`-bit rolling digest over the changed-input signs of revisions `1..V` (or `⌈log₂ V⌉` binary-search probes over the version lattice) and measure whether `O(log V)` bits suffice to exclude *all* `V − 1` retired basins or whether competing deep basins force `Ω(V)` bits.
- **Nearest art / delta:** Merkle/hash chains in version control; continual-learning forgetting curves rarely test *multi-version staleness* (`V > 2`). This is a clean scaling audit for the version-dominance term `log₂ |V_A(L)|` in `κ`.
- **Cheapest first test:** `V ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}` revisions of A on CPU MLP with varied training lengths `T_1, ..., T_V`; compare `O(log V)`-bit chained anchor vs `O(V)`-bit per-revision anchors and equal-bit replay.
- **Old-task retention check:** Current `A_V` accuracy, per-version stale revival rate across all `v < V`, C, B.

### E. Co-resident anchors, impossibility, and total-bit audits

#### 19. Coupled-skill checksum: shared 1-bit parity across A and C (primitive)

- **Mechanism:** Extend PR #146 card 20 (using live C to anchor A): instead of coupling A and C's full readouts (which risks distorting C or reducing to SFT on C-generated labels, a ledger kill), store only a **1-bit joint parity** `p_{AC} = sign(f_A(x_pub)) · sign(f_C(x_pub))` on one deterministic public probe. When A is leased and C is live, `p_{AC}` + live C pins A's polarity (`0.5` bits per skill); when C is later leased and A is live, the *same* bit pins C. Across `M` skills, an erasure-coded parity check over skill signs breaks `M` independent polarity symmetries in `O(1)` bits per simultaneous lease (e.g. 1 global parity bit protects any single leased skill!).
- **Nearest art / delta:** Parity/XOR erasure coding is classical; ledger killed composition contracts as training (SFT equivalence). Here the parity bit is **not** a training target for A — it is a 1-bit symmetry breaker for `W` whose control is SFT on C-derived labels at equal steps, and it amortizes `κ` below 1 bit/skill across a skill library!
- **Cheapest first test:** `M = 4` binary skills `{A_1..A_4}` + 1 global XOR parity bit on a deterministic public probe; lease one skill at a time to B and wake with the parity constraint. Compare against 4 per-skill 1-bit anchors (4 bits), plain `W` (0 bits), and SFT on C-derived labels.
- **Old-task retention check:** All `M` skills (the woken skill and the `M − 1` live anchor skills — live skills must not drift when used as checksum partners), plus B after wake.

#### 20. Gauge-orbit in-weights anchor (0 external bits) (primitive; moonshot-leaning)

- **Mechanism:** Connect top-10 #7 (gauge-orbit metadata) to wake anchoring: at burial/revision time, apply a function-preserving hidden-unit permutation to A's payload layer so the unit order relative to a canonical public-activation sort encodes `b` bits (the 1-bit polarity parity + `⌈log₂ V⌉`-bit version stamp). External side information is **0 bits**; the wake reads the permutation bits before running `W_s`. Explicitly faces top-10 #7's known risk: canonical order drifts under B's lease, so this tests whether permutation-coded anchors survive an access-only lease (PR #145 card 2) better than activation hashes (card 3).
- **Nearest art / delta:** Top-10 #7 ([PR #53](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/53)) encodes provenance in neuron permutations; neuron-permutation watermarks ([2312.14182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14182), [2112.14108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14108)). Using gauge-orbit bits specifically as a **self-contained wake anchor** under access-only leases is new, and has a sharp falsifier (bit error > 10% after 1 lease kills it).
- **Cheapest first test:** Width-64 MLP, 8-bit permutation tag encoding polarity + version stamp; run access-only lease (PR #145 card 2) vs unrestricted lease; measure tag decode error and post-wake A accuracy vs 8-bit external anchor (card 8/10) and equal-bit replay.
- **Old-task retention check:** Outputs bitwise identical before lease; A per twin/revision family after wake, C, B.

#### 21. Payload-death phase boundary: where `κ` jumps to `H(A)` (audit)

- **Mechanism:** Systematically sweep lease invasiveness (increasing leased rank `r` from access-only into payload directions in the nonlinear MLP, plus the linear-host control) and measure the minimum anchor bits `b*(r)` needed for 95% post-wake A accuracy. The theory predicts two regimes: a **low-bit plateau** (`b* ≈ log₂ |G_A| + log₂ |V_A|`, `ρ ≫ 1`) while payload survives, followed by a sharp **phase transition** where `b*(r)` jumps to `Ω(H(A))` and anchored `W_s` collapses onto the equal-bit replay curve (`ρ → 1`).
- **Nearest art / delta:** PR #145 card 21 measures critical lease rank for plain `W`; [2605.21972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21972) measures sparsity thresholds for label-free post-pruning recovery. Mapping the **bit-cost phase transition** of `κ(r)` directly separates "anchor-assisted wake" from "disguised replay".
- **Cheapest first test:** Sweep leased rank `r / d ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}` × `b ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64}` on nonlinear MLP and linear host; plot `b*(r)` and `ρ(r)` with 8-seed CIs.
- **Old-task retention check:** A per failure type, payload linear-probe decodability at lease end (must drop at the same `r*` where `b*(r)` jumps!), C, B.

#### 22. Total-bit Pareto frontier: anchor + wake vs. replay, saved delta, and GPM (audit)

- **Mechanism:** Put every anchor family on one common ledger: input-space labels (cards 5, 6, 8), active queries (card 9), signed sketches (card 10), anti-anchors (card 11), payload hashes (card 12), paired-world contrasts (card 14), version stamps (cards 15–17), and coupled checksums (card 19). Charge every bit (label, pointer, seed, digest, stamp) and every wake FLOP, and plot the Pareto frontier of **(post-wake A correctness on σ-twins + revision twins, B progress, total bits)** against equal-bit replay, equal-bit saved delta, and GPM at matched C retention.
- **Nearest art / delta:** PR #146 card 23 priced label-free guarantees against saved deltas; PR #144's `(C, D)` ledger priced edit lifecycles. This is the decisive cross-family audit that determines whether *any* minimal-anchor wake beats equal-bit replay and saved deltas in the real operating region.
- **Cheapest first test:** 16 held-out histories × 8 seeds on the shared MLP benchmark across `b ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64, 256}` bits; report the winning method at each bit budget and whether a strict `ρ > 1` regime exists.
- **Old-task retention check:** Full six-way taxonomy for A (correct, σ-flip, collapse, mixture, absorbed, stale), untouched A, C throughout, and B kept after wake.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does `κ(A_v, L, W)` have a genuine sub-replay regime (`ρ = H(A_recovered) / κ ≫ 1`), or does equal-bit replay / saved delta tie it once every pointer and metadata bit is charged?** Cards 1, 8, 21 and 22. This is the make-or-break question for the entire node: if `b*` for anchored wakes is within 2× of equal-bit replay or quantized deltas once pointer bits are billed, minimal wake anchors are not a new primitive. If 1–8 zero-index bits recover a multi-kilobit skill across σ-twins and revision twins (while failing on the linear-host control), dynamical decompression is real.
2. **Is there a real steering-vs-verification gap (`κ_select ≠ κ_steer`)?** Cards 2, 5, 10 and 13. This decides whether `κ` is genuinely a *new dynamical object* or a specialization of static zero-error side-information coding (top-10 #4) and teaching dimension (card 4). Showing that 1–4 bits injected at a bifurcation or as a differentiable sketch steer `W` out of a deeper stale basin that blind restarts + a 16-bit hash never escape — or conversely that blind restarts + hash strictly beat weak steering — would separate `κ` cleanly from static bounds.
3. **When does the top-10 #4 drift loan make a few input-space bits beat a larger state-space hash?** Cards 3, 10 and 12. State-space anchors are decoded through the drifting host `θ_end`, so they should decay with lease length `H` unless restricted to an access-invariant payload functional (card 12). Pinning the crossover `H*` connects this branch directly to top-10 #4's rate–drift law and PR #145's access/payload split.
4. **What is the cheapest recency anchor that stops BCE4 stale revival without paying `O(V)` replay bits?** Cards 6, 11, 15, 16, 17 and 18. Symmetry breaking is conceptually clean (`log₂ |G_A|` bits), but stale revival pits recency against basin depth. Testing whether a 1-bit change-frontier label (card 6), a `⌈log₂ V⌉`-bit epoch-keyed wake (card 15), a tilted proximal wake (card 16), or a 0-bit live-premise stamp (card 17) stops stale revival at `T_1/T_2 = 16` without L1's current-task retention penalty is the sharpest practical test.
5. **Can paired-world contrasts (ECR8) and co-resident parity checksums push anchor cost below 1 bit per skill?** Cards 14 and 19. A single contrast bit on an answer-changing paired world targets both σ-flips and stale shortcuts simultaneously, and a global XOR parity bit across `M` co-resident skills amortizes symmetry breaking to `1/M` bits per skill — a concrete, falsifiable test of whether anchors compose across a skill library.
