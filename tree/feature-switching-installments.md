# Feature-switching installments

**Status:** Ideas only; exact toy arithmetic, no learner training or GPU work. **Parent:** [Installment turns](installment-turns.md) ([PR #262][parent]), cards 04 and 06. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 377][triage] scores both at 3: let a first truthful record change the features used by the next record, and use an ordinary public read to pick a pre-certified ending for all histories consistent with that read. **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger].

## Can useful training change both what the learner can do and what an observer can safely tell it to do?

An installment need not merely turn an existing slot. It can change the *next record's gradient map*, and the same update might make a public response distinguish histories that require different endings. Call the proposed object an **observation-indexed installment**: an opener, a declared ordinary read, and a menu of certified suffixes, with neither hidden-state inspection nor online trial writes. The interesting case is when useful training creates both a feasible actuator and enough public information to choose it. A gate flip and an informative read are not independently sufficient.

**Verdict.** The toy below separates feature-dependent execution from frozen features, and public branching from a fixed word, at the same deadline. Passive motion makes the same read informative, a reset also meets the deadline, and an exact belief-space planner recovers the public policy. This is an existence example, not evidence that one record creates both capabilities or that the method has an advantage. Look for repeatable lawful supply, an effect beyond separately optimizing the opener and read, or a cheaper certificate with the same decisions.

**Nearest prior art.** [Iterative machine teaching][teaching] already picks sequences for a changing student; [GradMatch][gradmatch] chooses examples to match gradients; [GraB][grab] orders real gradients. [ReLU verification][reluplex] already handles activation boundaries. [POMDP planning][pomdp] and [robust MPC][mpc] already solve the general decision problem with the same actions and observations; [finish menus][menus] and [menu replacements][replacement] already certify and reserve continuations. The distinction to test is whether *ordinary learning itself* changes suffix realizability and carries the decision witness, under a native optimizer, a hard momentum cap and uninterrupted old-task service. A cheaper interface must beat general verification and exact search on all-in cost, not by omitting states. The ledger's bounded-delay scheduling result mandates legal accumulation at equal latency; its failed reserve predictors mandate matching absolute incoming gain and retention. Its read-pinning result is a warning that an unchanged public value says little about the downstream effect of a write.

## Contract

- Fix verified labels, available record pools and multiplicities, the host and optimizer, legal batching and service arrivals, and the deadline before the first write. No synthetic gradients, extra optimizer slots, or unbilled replay. The executed suffix gradient is recomputed natively at the live weights. Compare reset, direct slot write (illegal ceiling), frozen features, a host with no reachable feature switch, static selection, and equal-deadline accumulation where legal.
- A hidden history is a full state, including weights, slots and clock. For heavy-ball with unit step, use `p = βm`, `m' = p + ∇ℓ_r(w)`, and `w' = w − m'`. Check `||m'|| ≤ ||p||` on every admitted record and every compatible history, not against pre-decay momentum. Check held-out old A/B/installed C at all service prefixes, on declared coasts, and on safe refusal. Match absolute incoming gain separately from the seal and charge refused or delayed service. A reset control need not obey the installment cap; it owes the same deadline, utility and old floors.
- Before opening, fix the read, its actual precision and latency, and the certified menu. For public value `z`, let `I_z` be the set of all post-opener states capable of producing `z` after any allowed noise or intervening work. If `L(s)` is the set of reserved suffixes that pass cap, service, terminal seal and useful gain from `s`, this branch may use only `∩_{s ∈ I_z} L(s)`. An empty intersection authorizes refusal, not a majority vote or a new unverified suffix. Certify the timeout's old service too. Compare the fixed word, a read before the opener, a read with no separating power, separate diagnosis plus best lawful opener, exact belief-space planning, and a hidden-state oracle (ceiling only).
- Compare budgets using the same available records, and bill menu proofs, all held reservations, read or service latency, and the executed word. Report certified coverage, legal finishes by the deadline, worst-prefix old performance, and absolute incoming gain. Report the feature-switch effect and the public-read effect separately before testing their interaction.

## Exact two-tick witness: one opener, two public endings

Let weights be `w=(a,b,c)=(0,0,0)` and momentum be `m=(−2,σh,0)` with `σ ∈ {−1,+1}` and `h ∈ {4,65/16}`. The four histories start with identical weights. Use heavy-ball with `β=1/2`, unit step and fixed squared losses `(f−label)²/2`. The legal pool contains one copy each of the following singleton records; waiting is legal, batching is not.

| Record | Native output | Verified label |
|---|---|---:|
| `P` | `(3a−c)/2` | `−1` |
| `Q₊` | `ReLU(a−b+4c−13/4)` | `3/32` |
| `Q₋` | `ReLU(a+b+4c−13/4)` | `3/32` |

Both Q gates initially sleep. P gives `w₁=(−1/2,−σh/2,1/2)`, `m₁=(1/2,σh/2,−1/2)`, reducing the squared norm from `1+h²/4` to `1/2+h²/4`. Only `Q_σ` now has an active feature: its preactivation is `h/2−7/4` and its residual is `h/2−59/32`. An ordinary read publishes **only the sign of** `b`. In either sign branch the menu must cover *both* magnitudes, not just the smaller slot.

| Hidden magnitude | Suffix from either sign branch | Terminal `a` | Inherited → outgoing squared norm |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| `4` | `Q_σ` | `−29/32` | `9/8 → 521/512` |
| `65/16` | `Q_σ` | `−15/16` | `4737/4096 → 4617/4096` |
| Either | `Q_{−σ}` or wait | `−3/4` | Equal; fails to seal |

Seal by tick two requires `a ≤ −29/32` at landing and through zero-gradient coast. P takes its own absolute residual from 1 to 0, and the chosen Q takes its residual from `5/32` or `3/16` to `3/32`. The independent incoming read `a` with target `−29/32` improves at both ticks for either magnitude. Held-out toy old reads A (`−3 ≤ a ≤ 2`), B (`|b| ≤ 17/4`) and installed C (`|a+b| ≤ 25/4` and `|c| ≤ 1`) pass at entry, after P, after each chosen Q and on their coasts, including a timeout after P. With no interleaved training in this toy, every coast point lies on the segment from `w` to `w−m`, so the affine bounds need only its two endpoints.

**Why the controls fail or tie.** Freezing the Q gates in their initial cell gives zero Q gradient and no seal. Waiting or running Q first gives the same first state: `a=1,c=0,b=−σh/2`. The sign is already informative, but both Q gates stay dormant and P now exceeds the cap. After P only the matching Q seals. Erasing the read or coarsening it to a constant puts opposite signs in the same fiber with disjoint safe ending sets. Reading before P gives zero for every history; the slot oracle adds no coverage here. Resetting at entry and then doing P and waiting reaches `a=−9/4` at tick two with old reads intact; that matches feasibility, not absolute incoming gain. The small rational pool was chosen to show the mechanism, not to demonstrate how often such records occur naturally.

## Idea cards

### A. Change which gradient the next truthful record supplies

#### 01. Open a dormant actuator — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use ordinary record P to make an existing feature supply a gradient to Q that Q could not supply on entry; reserve the resulting *record-to-gradient transition*, not a vector. **Novelty vs nearest.** Restates [#262][parent] card 04. [Teaching][teaching] already uses changing gradients; require a new feasible suffix versus the same-feature host, static [GradMatch][gradmatch], legal accumulation and reset plus best word. **Cheapest first test.** The two-tick witness, then remove its hard-coded mirror symmetry with an independently sampled labeled pool. **Old-task retention.** Hold out examples that use the opened feature and check them before it opens, after Q and through coast.

#### 02. Swap the backward reader without moving the forward read — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Route a fixed Q through two different existing features that give it the same prediction at the boundary but different Jacobians; expose the new derivative as the installment's actuator. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Read-pinning][pinning] matched forward values and failed downstream; here matched Q values are an identification control, and the direction change is the proposed reusable operation. Compare Jacobian-matching protection for old tasks and a residual-matched linear host. **Cheapest first test.** Two mirrored branches with equal nonzero Q residuals; interchange their Jacobians in a diagnostic clone and see whether cap feasibility reverses. **Old-task retention.** Include held-out examples that use both branches and a normal old write before measuring the later read.

#### 03. Borrow an old feature and repay it with ordinary work — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Allow P to route Q through a currently old-owned feature while old reads stay within a registered corridor. Require a second useful record R to return the route to its old domain, leaving Q's useful effect behind. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Finish reservations][menus] reserve capacity; this borrows an actual derivative and returns it without restore. Compare a dedicated spare feature, checkpoint restore, and jointly searched P/Q/R at equal bytes and service. **Cheapest first test.** Bank two initially identical units, disable the spare in one arm, and add a harmful R that restores old logits but not the old next-update response. **Old-task retention.** Check old reads at borrow, use, and return plus the first normal old update after return.

#### 04. Require two ordinary records to unlock one feature — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Put two shared gating conditions on Q so P and R each open one, while neither alone can activate Q. Publish the two prefix obligations without making either record a special unlock example. **Novelty vs nearest.** Gated models and [ReLU reachability][reluplex] already express conjunctions; the candidate is a useful work sequence that supplies a multi-record key under the cap. Compare a one-key gate, legal accumulation, and exact search. **Cheapest first test.** A two-gate CPU model with real three-class labels; enumerate P/R omissions and both orders. **Old-task retention.** Check the old class that uses each gate before the other gate opens, and after Q.

#### 05. Open rank, not just a new direction — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Seek a prefix that exposes jointly independent native gradients for a later record family, enough to satisfy two independent seal faces without modifying the optimizer. **Novelty vs nearest.** [GradMatch][gradmatch] and local controllability already use spans; certify a *reachable change of lawful span*, not a large Gramian or a predicted reserve. **Cheapest first test.** A two-face seal and a three-record post-opener family; compare a pool whose individual gradients rotate but stay rank one, and enumerate actual bounded words to rule out a rank-only explanation. **Old-task retention.** Include held-out reads aligned to both new axes and a mixed read at each suffix prefix.

#### 06. Land inside a cell that remains useful after a normal write — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify the switched cell as an interface for Q only if the same unmodified Q still makes useful progress after a declared normal A write, with the new incoming slot. Allow a bounded region, not a one-point activation flag. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Robust MPC][mpc] and the [parent][parent]'s service clock already cover disturbances; the proposed abstraction is a reusable, record-conditioned neighborhood that covers both feature and slot behavior. **Cheapest first test.** Two openers with equal immediate Q success, one near a gate that the A write closes; compare exact re-verification and charge proof cost. **Old-task retention.** Check A's actual gain, held-out B and installed C before and after the interleaving, and after Q's coast.

#### 07. Train an opener supply, not a privileged opener — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** During development, shape a shared host so independently sampled ordinary mini-batches install one of several useful feature routes. The deployer cannot pick synthetic labels, see future examples, or rely on a named unlock item. **Novelty vs nearest.** [MAML][maml] trains for fast useful adaptation; the test here is held-out coverage of cap-safe feature changes and transferable endings, not lower one-step loss. Compare MAML with the same cap audit, frozen features, and a separately tuned teaching policy. **Cheapest first test.** Solve a tiny gate model on one pair of stream distributions and enumerate on held-out task/record combinations; reject a win tied to one hand-picked P. **Old-task retention.** Require joint passage on held-out A/B/installed C after adaptation to each stream, including rejected openings.

#### 08. Measure the size of the switch effect at the same optimizer cost — improvement
**Mechanism.** On a cloned diagnostic host, continue the starting cell's affine map for Q while retaining the actual weights, residual calculation on that map, and inherited slot; separately match the true Q residual to isolate the Jacobian effect. **Novelty vs nearest.** This is an intervention, not a new primitive; compare [GraB][grab], legal accumulation, same-cell word search and live execution to isolate activation switching from ordinary order effects. **Cheapest first test.** Sweep away from one ReLU boundary, with a same-cell host where both ablations should agree with execution. **Old-task retention.** Use margin-matched old examples and report the first differing old read, not just terminal performance.

### B. Choose an ending from an ordinary public response

#### 09. Compile an ending menu over public fibers — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After P, use a read that would already be served, then execute a pre-certified Q-word common to every compatible state. **Novelty vs nearest.** Restates [#262][parent] card 06; [POMDP policies][pomdp] already branch on observations, and [finish menus][menus] already reserve tails. Test reuse of an ordinary response in place of a separate diagnostic at the same sound coverage. **Cheapest first test.** Replay the exact witness with the sign read, a constant read, and a fixed word. **Old-task retention.** Use different held-out A/B/installed C examples on every branch and on safe refusal.

#### 10. Learn the coarsest read that preserves a safe decision — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Compress states by whether a public response can name a *common element* of their safe suffix menus, rather than reconstructing states or clustering by slot distance. Keep incompatible groups as hyperedges: compatibility can fail on three states even when every pair shares an ending. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Value equivalence][value-equivalence] already abstracts models for planning; this targets set-valued continuation feasibility under existing public observations. Compare exact partition search, information gain, and pairwise conflict coloring. **Cheapest first test.** Give three aliases suffix sets `{X,Y},{Y,Z},{X,Z}`; any two can share a branch but all three cannot. **Old-task retention.** Realize each omitted suffix as an old-read failure on the corresponding alias; verify that no allowed grouping hides it.

#### 11. Make useful work pay for observability — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Among equally useful cap-safe openers, prefer one that creates a robust public distinction between histories whose certified suffix sets conflict, not one that reveals the most about their slots. **Novelty vs nearest.** Dual control and [belief-space planning][pomdp] already value informative actions; the proposed object is a reusable opener that earns the distinction as a by-product of required learning. Compare passive coast plus the same read, separately scheduled diagnosis, and exact adaptive search. **Cheapest first test.** Two equally good P records and two history pairs; only one P resolves the pair that needs different endings. **Old-task retention.** Match old margins before P and verify those same examples at the read time and after both endings.

#### 12. Give a rounded response a lawful refusal band — improvement
**Mechanism.** Widen each observation fiber to include all possible histories under declared rounding, noise and stale reads. Use only an ending safe for the widened union; reserve an explicit no-seal continuation for unresolved bins. **Novelty vs nearest.** Standard set-membership filtering and [robust MPC][mpc]; the claimed improvement would be smaller proofs without a coverage loss relative to exact interval replay. **Cheapest first test.** Move the witness response toward a quantization boundary; include a bin containing both signs and a conservative bin containing one sign. **Old-task retention.** Serve held-out old queries while refusing and check the conservative branch at either edge of its bin.

#### 13. Join public reads across independent clients without sharing traces — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let two ordinary, already-served outputs each convey one coarse fact; compose their certified constraints to select a suffix neither one could safely authorize. The menu sees the allowed output symbols, not optimizer traces or unbilled hidden probes. **Novelty vs nearest.** Distributed diagnosis and [POMDP observation models][pomdp] already combine evidence; test a composable ending certificate using existing service outputs at the same information and latency. **Cheapest first test.** Four histories and two one-bit reads where only the joint symbol identifies a safe ending; add a missing-read case and a correlated false-independence trap. **Old-task retention.** Score both old clients on independent examples, including the one whose ordinary read arrives late.

#### 14. Make two ambiguous reads become decisive as a sequence — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Retain the same hidden trajectory across two ordinary responses surrounding a required record, so their transition can certify an ending even when neither response alone is enough. **Novelty vs nearest.** Belief filtering already retains history; compare exact [POMDP filtering][pomdp], a last-read policy and a richer single read at the same latency. The reusable object would be a bounded response-transition signature that travels with a lawful record. **Cheapest first test.** Two histories with the same response alphabet and opposite allowed two-symbol words; add a third that shares a whole word and disallows the chosen ending. **Old-task retention.** Check normal reads immediately before and after the middle update and during a timeout after the first symbol.

#### 15. Use a useful old prediction as a check on the menu itself — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** An ordinary old-service prediction can veto a stale menu when its published value is inconsistent with all covered entry histories. It cannot admit an unseen history; an in-range but uncertified state remains a negative control. **Novelty vs nearest.** Runtime monitoring and [check-as-work][check-work] already use useful work to test claims. The distinct test is whether a service response catches stale *ending scope* earlier or more cheaply than version stamps plus exact recheck. **Cheapest first test.** Insert two host changes, one that shifts the response outside the covered range and one invisible change that invalidates Q. **Old-task retention.** Hold out reads not used in the veto, check the refuse path, and require exact safety despite the invisible change.

### C. Couple changing features and changing information

#### 16. Make the same update open the route and create the read — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose P jointly for its change to Q's native gradient and for its change to the public partition over reachable slots. The gain must require sharing that one useful update, not stacking two freely available components. **Novelty vs nearest.** Dual control already couples acting and sensing; the proposed CL operation is a truthful installment that changes both derivative and observation under the native optimizer. Compare best independent opener plus diagnosis, their best legal stack, reset, and exact public-state search. **Cheapest first test.** Cross live versus frozen features with live versus coarsened reads, then add two useful P choices: one only opens, one only splits. **Old-task retention.** Match absolute incoming gain and cap slack where possible; score worst-prefix A/B/installed C and all timed fallback branches.

#### 17. Choose an order, not extra inventory — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve exactly the same two suffix records for every branch; the public read selects their order because the first changes the feature seen by the second. Each branch uses both records once. **Novelty vs nearest.** [GraB][grab] orders gradients and [finish menus][menus] reserve different tails; the candidate is a common record multiset whose cap-safe ordering depends on publicly distinguishable feature effects. Compare legal accumulation, a fixed best order, and exact contingent ordering. **Cheapest first test.** Find two aliases with opposite required Q/R orders; permute the read to force the wrong order on purpose in a clone. **Old-task retention.** Check old examples between Q and R as well as after both, so equal terminal weights cannot conceal a service lapse.

#### 18. Certify an ending for a new opener without seeing its history — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish an ending precondition on feature responses and full-state uncertainty bounds. A new truthful opener can earn that certificate if its entire public fiber maps into the precondition, even if it exercises unrelated features first. **Novelty vs nearest.** The [parent][parent] already proposes reusable suffix preimages; add generalization across opener families without certifying them by matching an activation mask or one observed gradient. Compare full re-verification and generic [ReLU verification][reluplex]. **Cheapest first test.** Hold out one opener family; include an impostor with the same mask and read but a changed downstream Jacobian that makes Q violate the cap. **Old-task retention.** Recheck held-out A/B/installed C on every admitted new family and on the impostor's fallback.

#### 19. Carry a branch-specific next-installment right — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose Q not just to finish today but to leave an advertised, publicly readable menu feasible after the next ordinary arrival. Certify the future menu over compatible descendants of today's branch, not over a hand-picked endpoint. **Novelty vs nearest.** The [parent][parent] leaves a usable next refill; [menu replacement][replacement] and [robust MPC][mpc] already plan future commitments. Test whether an already earned feature permit is reusable without retaining branch traces or running full search each round. **Cheapest first test.** Two equal-gain endings seal today, but only one leaves a common future Q on both descendants after a declared C arrival. **Old-task retention.** Check old service across both rounds, including a missed arrival and the later coast.

#### 20. Sell the same feature permit to a different truthful record — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve an effect region and certified public guard rather than a record ID. Charge a replacement's proof and only swap in a real record whose state-dependent gradient meets the region on the whole live fiber at the original deadline. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Menu replacement][replacement] already reserves alternate finishes and [GradMatch][gradmatch] matches example effects. The difference to test is whether a guard survives loss of its original record and validates a new feature user more cheaply than step-by-step recertification. **Cheapest first test.** Withdraw Q after P; offer two lawful substitutes with the same midpoint gradient, only one of which stays within the region on both aliases. **Old-task retention.** Check held-out reads on the strongest alternate, the rejected substitute's refusal, and the original deadline coast.

#### 21. Spend the observation before an ordinary update erases its meaning — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Bind a read to a warranty over intervening ordinary work. A suffix may remain authorized after the visible response changes, but only if the earlier read's *reachable image* still lies in its certified region; otherwise require a pre-certified reread or refuse. **Novelty vs nearest.** Timed reachability and the [parent][parent]'s service clock already handle delays; the candidate is transport of an existing branch proof across legal feature changes rather than expiry by elapsed time alone. **Cheapest first test.** Insert one of two required updates between the read and Q, one preserving Q's feature regime and one flipping it without changing the reported symbol. **Old-task retention.** Check old reads through both insertions, at the attempted use of the stale choice, and on the reread path.

#### 22. Discover an interface that stays the same when the host changes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Distill the reusable part of the menu into a public rule over observation transitions and certified suffix effects; require a new host to re-prove its local realization without retraining the chooser. Do not transfer raw slots or feature IDs. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Value equivalence][value-equivalence] and abstraction already permit portable policies; the candidate is a portability test for *training actions* whose feature semantics and optimizer differ but whose legal suffix effects agree. Compare fresh exact planning, matched-data transfer, and portability by read-pinning. **Cheapest first test.** Permute two small hosts' feature identities, then change the optimizer; require a negative host where identical read traces hide a different suffix effect and transfer must refuse. **Old-task retention.** Use each host's own old-test suite and normal next update, with the same service budget as the freshly synthesized policy.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can useful records create both the actuator and the observation at lower cost than separate work?** Start with 01, 09, 11 and 16. On independently sampled CPU streams, compare the best separate opener-plus-diagnosis stack, frozen features, fixed sequence, legal accumulation and reset plus best legal word at equal deadline, incoming gain and old service. Retire the mechanism claim if a matched stack ties or if qualifying words depend on a hand-picked record.
2. **How small can a sound ending certificate be?** Use 06, 10, 12 and 18. Test held-out opener families with higher-order conflicts, quantization, and matching reads with divergent gradients. Require the decisions of exact public-information search at lower total cost, or specify the coverage conceded; one unsupported admission falsifies soundness.
3. **Can one record budget support different endings without extra inventory or downtime?** Use 13, 14, 17 and 21. Require both orders to use exactly the same records; bill observation delay, missed reads and fallback. Reject a result that only beats a fixed word because it holds more items, or buys a useful finish by delaying old service.
4. **Can the permit survive new arrivals or a changed host without becoming full replanning?** Use 19, 20 and 22. Hold out the next request or host under a declared family, and compare exact replanning, reauthorization and a fresh chooser. Count certificate construction and revocation, and require a delivered correction with retained old service, not just a favorable read or more nominal headroom.
