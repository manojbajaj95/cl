# Can forward-only credit certify a missing address and mint a new coordinate cheaply?

Parent: [PR #78, fwd-credit-address-book](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/78), deeper questions 1–2. Builds on the [PR #50 forward-only probe](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/50) and the [PR #70 forward operations node](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/70). Ideation only: no pilots, simulations, or measurements were run for this node.

## Root cause

**The parent uses the same restricted interface to decide whether the book is missing credit and to look for the missing direction. It can measure failure inside that interface, but cannot attribute that failure to anything outside it without another source of information.** A new input cluster does not show that a new write is needed; conversely, the same inputs with a changed rule can need a new write. CaLLM's [prototype expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36994) is a routing solution, not a credit test.

- **Three spans, three questions.** At a fixed checkpoint, let `S` be the linear span of available edits with routes held fixed; let `Q` be the span of edits you actually probe. For a smooth held-out loss with local gradient `g`, an antithetic pair estimates `g·q` (up to noise and finite-difference error); even exact pairs along a basis of `Q` identify only `P_Q g`. If `Q ⊆ S`, the local losses with gradients `g` and `g+v` for any `v ⟂ S` can agree on *every* probe, not just on average. Toy example: losses `1+a²` and `1+a²−b` agree for all book coefficients `a` at `b=0`; only the second admits a local escape along `b`. The statement is about this edit oracle, not all possible forward observables: a known computation graph, a teacher's counterfactual outputs, a new input intervention, or a complemented edit can break the tie if it adds information. A gradient oracle can expose missing local credit but would not be forward-only, and will also miss a purely nonlinear escape with zero slope.
- **Activation span is not edit span.** For a linear map `W` with layer inputs `h`, let `H` be the span of its unmodified inputs on the scoped examples. A write `u vᵀ` with `v ⟂ H` has exactly zero effect there, even at finite amplitude, if the inputs stay fixed. Indeed a differentiable loss's isolated `W` gradient on those examples is a sum of outer products with those same inputs. AGZO ([2601.17261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17261), for its activation-guided linear-layer perturbations) and ZO-Act ([2607.01125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01125), fixed one-shot activation basis) restrict those perturbations to activation-derived directions. Neither restriction supplies a test of what lies beyond it. But a write outside `S` can still use old activations, e.g. with a new output direction. To *mint a feature*, ask instead whether a new evaluated feature column is outside the span of the old feature columns on the same inputs, with evidence on unseen examples. That can require tapping a different input, changing upstream computation, or adding a nonlinear/structural operation. A vector orthogonal to the old sample activations is not itself that feature.
- **Residual loss is not residual credit.** Bad optimization, stale directions, weak feedback, a bad router, an overly tight retention cap, and an unreachable target can all yield a large residual. The parent's sign-shuffle can detect signal against a noise null, not prove the book cannot achieve the target; even a win by an outside edit is not decisive if a different in-book combination could match it. The useful claim is scoped: fix checkpoint, tasks and probe distribution, legal edit family, radius and retention cap; upper-bound the best feasible in-book gain and lower-bound the gain of a legal outside witness. Report **in-book witness**, **outside needed within scope**, or **undetermined**. A projected gradient norm at most certifies a local first-order statement. Scores may also need a bounded-noise or smoothness assumption for a finite-query certificate; an unrestricted nonlinear black box can hide a better edit between any finite set of probes.
- **A detection budget is not a minting budget.** In the unstructured local linear toy, `k` scalar directions give at most `k` independent constraints; exact recovery of an arbitrary direction in a `d`-dimensional complement requires `d` independent scalar measurements. An isotropic unit probe there has mean squared overlap `1/d` with a fixed unit direction, so median/noise-floor effects matter even for detection. This is not a universal parameter-count law: [effective-dimension ES analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29795) separates reward-relevant dimension from ambient parameter count. Structured probes, side information, and asking for a witness instead of an entire direction may cut the cost. Validate the *committed* edit on fresh data: [ES Paradox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04113) shows individual offspring can mostly lose while an aggregate edit improves; the [neural-thicket null](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01262) warns that selecting winners can mistake noise for experts.

### What existing methods buy

| Family | What is already available | What remains open here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maintained or sampled ZO geometry | [AdaSub-ZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15225) learns a basis from finite differences; [AIM-ZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35257) maintains a broader evolving activation-informed space; [LOZO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07698) varies low-rank perturbation subspaces; [ZO-SAH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06125) fits curvature within sampled subspaces. | Tracking descent, enlarging the probe space, or fitting a better local model isn't a scoped upper bound on the old book or a functionally useful new feature. [Basis drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10929) limits how long a checkpoint-local bound can be reused. |
| Read/write asymmetry | [READ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31600) gives a new adapter one-way access to old input subspaces; [RASP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03513) reads all atoms but routes gradients to a sparse unlocked subset. | These constrain interference or plasticity. They don't decide from forward evidence whether to grow the readable feature set in the first place. |
| Complementary writes | [Null-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15233) adapts in weight null spaces; [Adam-NSCL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07113) projects updates into approximate old-feature covariance nulls; [CoSO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11816) uses evolving gradient-SVD subspaces with historical orthogonality. | An old-task null can protect an update, but it doesn't supply a source of new-task gain. With identical fixed inputs an exact input null is also silent on the new tasks. |

## Raw ideas (unfiltered)

Mechanisms and novelty claims below are hypotheses, not literature verdicts. Nearest internal overlaps are from the [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/0e7f92122843f201676d38c6a1c84bc9d9871ac3/reviews/idea-ledger.md) and [flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/0e7f92122843f201676d38c6a1c84bc9d9871ac3/reviews/flood-triage.md) ([PR #6](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6)). Don't bring back a falsified plasticity-reserve predictor as an allocation rule, sparse syndromes as a guarantee for all facts, or L1 prompt checkpoints as a retention baseline. Parent residual scoring, gated products, circuit pairs, kinks, forward Krylov and seed books are already named in #78; the ideas below ask what additional evidence or operation would make growth possible.

**Common cheapest-test rules.** Use a tiny planted toy with in-book, outside-book, and unlearnable/confounded cases; include familiar inputs with changed credit and novel inputs with unchanged credit. Keep the learner forward-only; an exact projection, gradient, or enumeration can be a test-only label, not hidden supervision. Separate fit, mint/selection, and held-out evaluation; compare at equal total forward calls/examples (charge teacher or intervention queries), storage, and capacity where applicable. For minting compare against a tuned static book, activation-guided ZO, rank-matched random growth and input-novelty triggering; include tuned ES and evaluate the aggregate, not just its best probe. Every retention check below means returning to held-out normal old tasks after the new write (including after a later write where called out), against the frozen baseline and random and supervised/gradient updates matched for actual parameter-delta norm (Frobenius over edited tensors, including realized adapter matrices, not perturbation radius or factor norms). Use GRPO instead of supervised updates for reward-only tasks. For new slots use the same zero-output expansion for controls; for unlike architectures also report function-space displacement on a separate common calibration set and account for extra bytes/compute. Compare forgetting at matched new-task gain where attainable as well as at fixed budget; report unattainable matches. This addresses the [update-norm confound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01624), not a claim that equal norm guarantees retention.

### A. Buy a certificate without identifying every direction

1. **A signed output contrast as a dual witness.** *Mechanism:* for a frozen affine book readout, find weights over labeled examples that annihilate all book response columns but have nonzero dot product with the desired output change. For squared loss the normalized contrast lower-bounds the residual in that frame. Redeem it only with an independently tested edit that closes the contrast and improves held-out loss. *Why new:* an inspectable set of contradictory demands with a bound, not #78's significance test for leftover variance or #57's generic witness set. *Cheapest test:* a linear toy with targets made identical on fit cases but different on held-out paired inputs; compare with an exact least-squares oracle and test a planted escape. *Retention:* old regression rules sharing the same response columns.

2. **Certify a region with upper and lower gain bounds.** *Mechanism:* use directional differences with error bars and a justified bound on curvature to upper-bound gain throughout the book's trust ball; pair that with a lower bound from an executed outside edit. Abstain if they overlap. *Why new:* ZO-SAH fits curvature to choose a step; the object here is an exclusion interval, not the fitted optimum. A fitted Hessian alone does not certify anything. *Cheapest test:* a bounded quadratic with a deliberately bad in-book solver and a curved in-book optimum; compare interval verdicts to the exact constrained optimum. *Retention:* old quadratics whose basins touch the ball, not just separated tasks.

3. **An ambiguity pair as a query contract.** *Mechanism:* if the transcript admits both an in-book and an outside-credit explanation, return both explicit toy models plus the cheapest permitted intervention on which they disagree. No mint on the transcript alone. *Why new:* #73 reports non-identifiability of obligations; this proposes a credit-specific query that breaks it and must change the growth verdict. *Cheapest test:* two masked circuit teachers with identical book responses; price one input-pair edit against random complement probes. *Retention:* both teachers' shared old rule after the chosen edit; abstention remains at the frozen baseline until an edit passes.

4. **A Pareto certificate for retention-blocked credit.** *Mechanism:* upper-bound attainable new-task gain in the book under several old-task loss caps, then compare a single tested outside witness at each cap. Distinguish “new rule fits but overwrites old” from “new rule cannot fit even without protection.” *Why new:* READ and RASP enforce write restrictions; the certificate diagnoses when a restriction demands new capacity. *Cheapest test:* two coupled linear outputs where one target is in-book on its own and infeasible only with the old output pinned; compare to exact constrained regression. *Retention:* the pinned behavior plus an old variation held out of cap fitting.

5. **Disproof by a small unsatisfiable core.** *Mechanism:* encode old and new probe obligations as exact inequalities over bounded, discrete book coefficients; extract a minimal inconsistent subset and look for a new feature that makes that subset satisfiable. *Why new:* LP39 asks what can't be re-derived; this yields an explicit contradiction in the *current edit family* and a target for changing that family, not a rule for deleting lessons. *Cheapest test:* enumerate a two-bit routed book with two old obligations and a conflicting new one; add a planted discriminator and check both the core and fresh combinations. *Retention:* ordinary examples from both old obligations outside the core.

6. **A holdout oracle for exploratory searches.** *Mechanism:* separate book exhaustion and escape discovery into two adaptive searches; submit their respective scoped bound and final candidate once to untouched examples. Calibrate the false-mint rate against label-swaps/nuisance controls that also undergo selection. *Why new:* a certificate must survive *both* adaptivity loops; an ES winner or a sign-shuffled residual alone only addresses part of the neural-thicket selection null. *Cheapest test:* no-credit and planted-credit linear problems, increasing the candidate count while gating on a fresh split. *Retention:* unchanged earlier tasks, including one rare mode missing from the selection split.

### B. Mint a feature without searching every weight

7. **A preimage splice for collapsed pairs.** *Mechanism:* find examples that the writable layer maps to the same state but that need different outputs. Read one upstream scalar that separates them and connect it to the readout using an otherwise zero-output port. Verify the resulting feature column escapes the old columns. *Why new:* #78 credit-sign mitosis splits an existing address; an exact state collision needs a new input to the book, not a new route on that state. *Cheapest test:* a frozen bottleneck discards one of two bits. Compare upstream tap search to AIM-ZO and random edits at equal calls. *Retention:* old first-bit rule, including cases where the tapped bit is spuriously correlated.

8. **A symmetry channel from orbit contrasts.** *Mechanism:* apply known label-preserving or label-transforming input actions; take a signed average of their feature evaluations to isolate a missing symmetry component, using a newly available upstream/input channel if the old layer is invariant. *Why new:* unlike #78's holographic composition or the gauge-orbit metadata idea, the orbit supplies task-side information that constructs a feature, not a code over edits or a weight symmetry. *Cheapest test:* an order-symmetric model asked for an antisymmetric pair score; compare an antisymmetrized tap with random features. *Retention:* old symmetric pair scores, including unseen pairs from the same orbits.

9. **Parity-selected input observables.** *Mechanism:* query labels on chosen subcubes of a binary input, take signed sums to cancel low-degree effects, and identify a planted sparse parity before implementing it as a small input-fed module. *Why new:* related to sparse Fourier/junta learning, not a new parity algorithm; the proposal is to recover a missing *input observable* rather than PR #70's sparse operation dictionary from perturbation bits. *Cheapest test:* switch from an additive rule to one unknown low-order parity at fixed input dimension. Break the sparsity assumption with a dense nuisance as a negative case. *Retention:* old additive and previously minted parity tasks after enabling the new observable.

10. **Temporal memory as a new input axis.** *Mechanism:* when equal current states require different actions, introduce a state coordinate computed over past observations, selected by which histories resolve the paired contradiction. *Why new:* #78 new keys separate current activations; this escapes a stateless address family using observable history, rather than widening its same-step feature basis. *Cheapest test:* same current observation, reward keyed to the previous symbol; compare one-bit history candidates to width-matched stateless ZO. *Retention:* old memoryless rule on histories where the learned lag is irrelevant.

11. **A slot born from a missing relation, not high activation.** *Mechanism:* construct small relational joins over named input positions, such as equality of a query and a distant key after renaming. Let paired traces that differ only in that relation choose the join; mint only if the new column survives held-out entity permutations. *Why new:* CaLLM seeks a novel input cluster; the address here is a relational observable invariant to changing entity names. Risk: known probe-based concept learning; only credit-triggered attachment is proposed. *Cheapest test:* an unordered summary loses which key a query binds to; compare equality-join candidates to fresh dense units. *Retention:* old bag-of-items classifications on the same inputs.

12. **Change the sampling lattice.** *Mechanism:* actively observe a missing resolution, phase, or viewpoint instead of editing blind weights. A candidate coordinate is a contrast between sample grids; charge for the added observation. *Why new:* unlike #78 kink addresses, this creates information not present at the current layer's input. It assumes access to an additional sensor/view, not free exploration in a fixed model. *Cheapest test:* parity of within-cell position aliased by a coarse sensor; compare a half-cell staggered observation with spending the same budget repeating the old sensor. *Retention:* prior coarse-label task under the new sampling path and budget.

13. **A teacher gives a predicate, not a delta.** *Mechanism:* query a verifier for a single distinguishing bit on matched failure/success examples, then seek a cheap observable that predicts that bit; accept the bit predictor only if its output leads to a retained, useful edit on unseen tasks. *Why new:* unlike #50's counterfactual write compiler, privileged supervision narrows feature discovery without specifying a target model state or weights. *Cheapest test:* compare one oracle intermediate parity bit per example to terminal-only labels at matched oracle budget. *Retention:* earlier rules that share the same bottleneck and no longer have teacher access.

14. **A boundary wire for count saturation.** *Mechanism:* retain a higher-order bit from a computation that otherwise clips or folds counts. Open a new connection only when exact activation collisions differ in the bit and labels follow it. *Why new:* #78 moves activation kinks; this exports information lost by a fixed operator rather than moving where it fires. *Cheapest test:* a counter clipped at seven, asked to separate counts 8–15 from 16–23; compare a tap on the 16s bit with bias edits and width growth. *Retention:* old within-range counting and old saturated cases after deployment of the same wire.

### C. Find an escape without mistaking geometry for credit

15. **A sentinel for complement energy, not a gradient estimator.** *Mechanism:* probe random directions in `S⊥` and estimate the mean squared antithetic slope after subtracting independently estimated noise power; use a small predeclared sequential schedule to decide if further search is justified. Calibrate by effective dimension and model curvature. *Why new:* AdaSub-ZO uses slope feedback to fit directions; this buys a detection decision at lower resolution, and reports no useful mint until an edit redeems it. *Cheapest test:* planted equal-projected-slope losses with zero versus nonzero complement energy; compare calls to controlled false alarms versus estimating the full direction. Use the same capped ES write after the detection gate. *Retention:* old tasks sensitive to that complement as well as ones that are null there.

16. **Group tests on complementary edit blocks.** *Mechanism:* recursively split a bank of allowed outside-book blocks; use signed independent probes to estimate responsiveness energy of each group, not summed slopes that can cancel. Buy a fresh A/B in the best surviving block. *Why new:* LOZO samples low-rank subspaces for optimization; this tests a hierarchy for localization of sparse leftover credit and can refuse an empty/incoherent tree. *Cheapest test:* one planted responsive edge in a binary tree with decoy sign cancellation; compare matched-query flat scan and low-rank random search as active block count increases. *Retention:* earlier edges, particularly old operations in neighboring blocks.

17. **A zero-slope escape witness.** *Mechanism:* use two-sided measurements across nested radii along predeclared outside directions to find negative curvature that survives held-out forward tests; require an in-book upper bound before attributing the escape to missing coordinates. *Why new:* not #78 Krylov growth from an existing slope, nor PR #70's antithetic old-damage proxy; this is a witness for a skill whose improvement is invisible at first order. *Cheapest test:* a toy gate with flat first-order signal and one downhill off-book direction, plus a saddle with the same curvature in-book to catch false mints. *Retention:* old gate behavior on both signs and on inputs near its switching boundary.

18. **A bid for a cheaper side-information channel.** *Mechanism:* spend one question on the cheapest candidate channel (hard output bit, input toggle, logit margin, upstream tap), chosen by which expected answer would divide the surviving explanations. Stop buying when the best bound will remain inconclusive at the available budget. *Why new:* #57's general discriminating experiments and #73's keep-bit budget are neighbors; the additional object is the cost of certifying or closing a specific projected credit gap with alternative interfaces. *Cheapest test:* a hidden-mode toy where a logit readout wins on one mixture and an input toggle wins on another; compare against buying only more ES calls. *Retention:* acquisition and old return scores at the same total channel budget.

19. **An activation-collision ledger that expires on evidence.** *Mechanism:* attach to a mint the paired examples it separates and the downstream outcomes they must retain; if later checkpoints resolve the pair with the old book, rerun the bounded test and consider removing the added axis. Drift in the activations alone does not revoke it. *Why new:* addresses persisting by behavioral late binding (#78) and the drift paper are close; here the proof of *necessity* expires and must be redeemed, not the address located again. *Cheapest test:* induce an invertible basis rotation (no expiration expected) and then introduce an actual feature change (possible expiration); compare to fixed-time or cosine-triggered reassessment. *Retention:* old tasks after subsequent writes and after any proposed removal, including old tasks that never touched the original collision pair.

20. **Measure the rank of unresolved demands before minting.** *Mechanism:* after removing the attainable book responses in a frozen-output toy, form residual target columns for several skill demands. Ask what rank any added shared feature block must have to span those demands within tolerance. This gives a lower bound, not the actual block. *Why new:* PR #70 factors a task × perturbation matrix to find reusable operations; here the residual spectrum is a bound on the number of additional independent response columns under a shared linear readout. *Cheapest test:* mix multiple new tasks sharing one missing latent feature with tasks having independent missing features; compare predicted width to exact rank and validate an actual block. *Retention:* all prior tasks through a shared readout at the added width.

### D. Make an outside gain compatible with old behavior

21. **A new feature with a compensating in-book write.** *Mechanism:* measure the new feature's old-output effect, express the removable part using a small book response basis, and jointly write the novel feature and its cancellation. Reject when the cancellation also destroys new gain. *Why new:* Adam-NSCL forbids motion in old feature directions; this permits motion and uses existing writes to pay for measured damage. Related to SD3 write interactions, but cancellation is required for a single escape, not measured as an incidental conflict. *Cheapest test:* outside feature leaks linearly into two old outputs; compare to null projection, using only forward secants to fit the compensation. *Retention:* held-out old cases with varied mixtures of both outputs, retested after a second compensated write.

22. **A conditional port with an exact non-application claim.** *Mechanism:* attach a new feature via a branch whose activation predicate is identically false on a stated old-input grammar. Prove non-application for the grammar and still measure retention outside its guaranteed slice. *Why new:* READ asymmetry protects old adapter outputs; the stronger claim here is that a certified input family never executes the new write, even if the old and new features use identical directions. The closest flood overlap is P3 exact fallback; the extra condition is a checkable coverage claim for the old grammar. *Cheapest test:* finite-state old language disjoint from a new guarded sublanguage, plus an intentionally overlapping case where the proof must fail. *Retention:* old grammar examples and near-boundary old traffic not used to choose the guard.

23. **A two-sided null: exact old silence, observable new signal.** *Mechanism:* before searching the old-input null, look for a changed or additional input view with a nonzero projected component there. Prefer a candidate backed by a lower bound on new-view activation in the null and an upper bound on old-view leakage; this checks viability only, not gain. Discard the complement if both views are silent. *Why new:* Null-LoRA, Adam-NSCL and CoSO provide complementary update spaces; the added operation is a forward viability check on the *input source* supplying credit. *Cheapest test:* identical old/new fixed features versus a case where the new view exposes one new coordinate. Compare null-only search with the viability gate before fitting the readout. *Retention:* old views and transformed old views unseen in null estimation.

24. **A replacement receipt rather than a permanent append.** *Mechanism:* hold capacity fixed; show that a proposed new coordinate plus a smaller retained book reproduces the old output obligations and meets a new one that the full old book could not. Commit replacement only with both receipts. *Why new:* #78's fusion uses credit collinearity and LP39 uses re-derivability; here the same transaction certifies capacity release and a reachable-set gain, not rank or predicted reserve alone. *Cheapest test:* a linear stream with two functionally redundant old columns (compare to a deliberately rare old behavior that makes them distinct); compare LRU and fit-only column pruning. *Retention:* all old skills, with the rare return case and a further mint testing that the released slot was truly reusable.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Can a scoped reachability certificate survive a change of probe set?** (1–6, 19.) Candidate primitive: an independently checkable **book upper bound + outside witness + abstention rule**, with explicit assumptions and expiration. Start with frozen affine responses and matched old-loss caps; fail if poor in-book optimization or fresh examples regularly invalidate the verdict. Highest chance: the object is already well-defined on toys and could outlive any one search algorithm.
2. **Can an indistinguishable pair compile to an extra observable?** (7–14, 23.) Candidate primitive: a **feature-birth witness** that names the lost distinction, the cheapest source that restores it, and an attachment that preserves old behavior. The test is whether the compiler transfers across counterfactual rule families and beats random growth in total calls, not whether it solves one planted XOR.
3. **When is it cheaper to certify a complement than to learn it?** (15–18, 20.) Candidate primitive: a **query budget for escape detection and localization** in terms of signal, noise, structure, and effective dimension. Compare certification, search, and privileged side channels on the same examples; no claim of a gain for arbitrary dense credit.
4. **Can a growth receipt prove a safe capacity trade?** (4, 19, 21–24.) Candidate primitive: a **replacement receipt** with separate checks for removed obligations, new gain, and future scope. Rank lower: exact protection may apply only to narrow grammars or linear views; outside those cases this could collapse back to replay plus null projection.
