# Split-pocket park entry

**Status:** Ideas only; no training or GPU work. **Parent:** [Momentum-sealed park entry](sealed-park-entry.md) ([PR #260][parent]). **Review seeds:** [Flood triage][triage], #260 cards 03 and 07 (both score 3). **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger].

## Can one entry word buy two safe landings and let useful work finish the landing?

**Verdict: candidate primitive, not a win.** One truthful public entry word may leave lawful histories in disconnected safe pockets of the same relation, with no pocket-specific action, while the first independently useful B update spends residual inward carry before it crosses a second reader's floor. The joint bet is cheaper entry or more timely B work at the same old-task floors than the best lawful stack, not a win over a convex relaxation or a reset.

**Nearest prior art.** [Sealed entry][parent] already pulls a finite build back through the native optimizer; [relation parks][relation] already support curved relations and finite truthful chords; [momentum landing][landing] already prices inherited velocity; [sealed gates][gates] already bound the coast. Disjunctive verification (e.g. [trace partitioning][partition]) and [robust MPC][mpc] already reason about nonconvex reachable sets and timed transitions. No new reachability machinery is claimed. The ledger killed bounded-delay scheduling against accumulation at equal latency: seek differences in *due service and useful build coverage* under the same arrival grammar, not just terminal order sensitivity.

## Contract and deciding controls

- Before entry, freeze the public observation, lawful compatible histories, truthful C word pool, permitted B batches and arrival windows, native optimizer semantics, due readers, B milestones and final witness. Pair weights, slots and clock as actually reached; a disconnected scatter of trajectories is not evidence of disconnected *safe* pockets. Specify a relation with a genuine separator in the declared state domain and verify that separating region fails a named read. The old service is not allowed to pick whichever output happens to survive.
- Choose one E for the whole compatible fiber. Apply actual gradients; certify all due reads during entry, before/after arrivals and at any intervening published reader time. Track a union of pocket-conditioned envelopes jointly with clock, arrival state and completed set. Branches are for proof only; the runtime follows the same public protocol for the same arrivals on every history. Keep all legal traces, including pocket crossings if native transitions can jump a gap; a jump is lawful only if every actual read passes. No hidden tags, slot writes, synthetic steering, extra carrier, or change in the arrival menu after commitment.
- Distinguish a genuine no-op (neither slots nor clock advance) from a native idle update (run the declared optimizer, including any momentum, decay and clock behavior). Certify earliest, latest **and interior** arrivals and every legal first B identity. Count a B update only if it improves its own frozen task and meets its milestone; use an offline time-matched idle or withheld-impact counterfactual to locate the threatened old read. If B may be absent or miss its window, include that trace or deny admission under the original offer. Never rename forced precedence as order freedom.
- Compare the best connected **safe** #260 seal (not only a bad hull), #255 relation park, #260 B-independent seal, #242 momentum landing, stronger truthful SGD entry at matched C cost and gain, slots reset at the same clock, and accumulation/fixed-order at the same latency and exposure. An oracle with a separate entry per history is a ceiling, not a lawful solution. Give the strongest public stack both disjunctive verification and timed checking, independently or jointly optimized as it permits. Cross split/single and live-B/B-independent certificates. Include a connected-safe merger case and an unsafe separator case; simply replacing a convex hull by an exact union is an audit, not a primitive. Bill proof, gradient, stall, preflight and live-byte costs. Compare on-time B coverage at matched A/installed-C floors and incoming-C utility; a tie with the jointly verified stack retires the joint claim.

## Exact paper check: two lanes, one braking batch

Use native heavy-ball updates `m′=m/2+g, w′=w−m′` coordinatewise with unit step, no decay and atomic reads. Each named loss is a sum of half-squares on its named coordinates only. Let the state coordinates be lane `x`, nuisance `q`, and live B carrier `b,u`. The frozen old-A checks are `3/4≤x²≤5/4` and `q≤0`; installed C checks `q≥−3/5`. The public observation at entry is `x²=1, q=0` and equal ticks; the lawful prefix alternatives are exactly the two specified below, with the prefix choice not public. The A read serves the same relation on both lanes, not a branch-chosen task. Retention in this fixture starts at entry.

To realize the histories, start at `(x,q)=(0,−1/2)` with zero slots and `b=u=0`. For either `s=±1`, use two fixed prefix records with targets `(s,−1/4)` and `(s/2,−1/8)` on `(x,q)`. At entry `(x,m_x)=(s,0)` and `(q,m_q)=(0,−1/4)`. The shared truthful E targets `q=−1/2`, so both states land at `q=−3/8,m_q=3/8`, and its squared residual drops from `1/4` to `1/64`. The two reached lanes cannot be covered by a connected subset of the declared safe set: a continuous projection to x would have to cross 0, where A fails. No optimizer step changes x during entry or build.

Independently useful B targets `(q,b)=(−1/4,1)`, then useful T targets `u=1` at the next tick. Require `b≥1` once B is delivered and `u≥1` at completion; both first moves land at 1, and the zero-gradient T tick raises b to `3/2`. Read A and installed C at each transition; the window allows B immediately or after exactly one idle event, either a no-op or a real zero-gradient tick. The latter still advances momentum.

| B timing or negative control (both lanes) | q before B | (q, m_q) after B or its substitute | q after T |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Immediate or after a no-op | −3/8 | (−7/16, 1/16) | −15/32 |
| After one real idle tick | −9/16 | (−11/32, −7/32) | −15/64 |
| Replace immediate B with a real idle tick | −3/8 | (−9/16, 3/16) | **−21/32** |
| Replace latest B with a real idle tick | −9/16 | (**−21/32**, 3/32) | **−45/64** |

At either legal arrival, B's negative q-gradient spends inward momentum (and reverses it at the late edge), while also delivering real B work. The absolute q residual to −1/4 goes from 1/8 to 3/16 for early arrival and from 5/16 to 3/32 for late arrival; at early arrival the b gain more than offsets the worsened q loss, so the *total* frozen B loss falls in both cases. With the same E after a momentum reset, q lands at −1/2 and the one-real-idle path hits −3/4 before B. Two real idle ticks would break installed C before B regardless of its label. This separates the read failure from missing B credit, but the fixed lanes make the timed proof factorize; the fixture shows feasibility and non-vacuity, **not** a joint advantage, earned order freedom, or an Adam result.

## Idea cards

### A. One entry word, distinct landings

### 01. Buy a shared entry without buying a common destination — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Search the truthful E pool against the union of viable pockets; admit one word only if it covers the entire public fiber, with no pocket-dependent suffix action. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 card 03][parent] is the seed, not new here; the object under test is a public entry right without convergence to one safe component, beyond the single-component seal and [#255][relation]'s relation alone. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three E words on two reachable aliases and two B orders; compare the best connected-safe entry, exact union, #255 and per-history oracle, then widen the safe bridge so the connected control can cover both. **Old-task retention check.** Read both old paths on every prefix and execute the separator's failing reader offline.

### 02. Route later useful work through a pocket swap without learning the tag — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A truthful B record induces one permutation of occupied pockets under identical execution; certify the swap and its composition with a second batch that may not commute. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Finite relation chords][relation] land on a curve; this seeks a setwise operation preserving both services without preserving *either* component. **Cheapest first test.** Construct an atomic two-feature squared-loss step and enumerate the two orders; compare a fixed-pocket seal and an exact disjunctive verifier with swaps enabled. Reject a swap crossing a due non-atomic read. **Old-task retention check.** Read held-out A features at both endpoints and every advertised intermediate publication; check installed C after the second move.

### 03. Let the safe pockets merge only when a real batch opens the bridge — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** A B batch makes a relation's bridge viable by learning an already-required feature; discharge a two-pocket obligation into a connected park with one common clear. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#255][relation] already hands off relation parks; the new bet is that earned connectivity removes a formerly necessary clear or isolation cost. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze an old reader using either of two features plus a shared substitute; compare a bridge learned by B against permanent separation, an initially connected seal and a B batch that leaves the substitute unusable. **Old-task retention check.** Read examples relying on each original feature and the substitute immediately before and after common clear.

### 04. Share a truthful alphabet whose packets act differently across pockets — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose a small fixed set of B packets so that the native gradient of each is corrective in one pocket and tangential in another; every offered order must still close. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#255's finite chords][relation] move along a shared relation; this asks for a reusable alphabet with cross-pocket roles, not a separate chord per branch. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust equal-size partitions of four records on two nonlinear features; compare #255 repartition plus exact union against the same packets with reader features tied so all effects align. **Old-task retention check.** Probe each directional reader after either packet goes first and verify the piece it already delivered remains useful.

### 05. Keep a punctured park without paying for its empty middle — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Define admission by a reader relation excluding a family of bad mixtures, and let ordinary truthful C entry move a reachable cloud around the holes rather than contract every coordinate. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Trace partitioning][partition] represents holes; the bet is that designing a useful word for this geometry changes the least truthful exposure, not that holes can be represented. **Cheapest first test.** On a two-feature polynomial reader with two separated safe strips, enumerate length-two words and compare joint exact search with the best #255 and connected #260 stack; fill the hole as a null. **Old-task retention check.** Hold out interpolated old inputs whose margin collapses near the excluded mixtures and test near each reached strip boundary.

### 06. Preserve the whole pocket under a later old-reader change — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Two announced old queries partition the same safe set differently; choose an entry whose images can be recertified under either partition without motion or a re-park. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 card 16][parent] reserves late readers; here their activation changes the component structure, so preserving a component label is not the certificate. **Cheapest first test.** Cross two late reader choices with an exact two-order build and compare a static intersection seal to the budgeted due-time contract. **Old-task retention check.** Fire each alternative query at its earliest and latest lawful point, including one that invalidates the former bridge.

### 07. Distinguish useful pockets from hand-selected alias clusters — improvement
**Mechanism.** Certify a separating failed read over the declared domain, and independently vary reachable prefix histories near the putative separator. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Connected-component and reachability checks are standard; this is a guard against calling a finite scatter a disconnected safe set. **Cheapest first test.** Match the original endpoints in two hosts, one with a legal detour and one with a separating slab; demand the right distinction without changing the public action. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate the named old reader on the detour and at the barrier witness, not just on the original histories.

### B. Let useful B spend the carry

### 08. Give residual carry a useful expiration event — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose E so first B is the lawful braking event within a frozen window; the proof includes real idle ticks and due reads before B lands. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 card 07][parent] is the seed; [sealed gates][gates] treat coast as something entry must survive, and [momentum landing][landing] supplies the matched entry control. **Cheapest first test.** Extend the paper check to competing useful E words; enumerate the window and a late miss against a B-independent seal, stronger SGD at matched C gain, slot reset and fixed-order/accumulation controls. **Old-task retention check.** Read A and installed C after the last lawful idle, after B, and after a missed-window counterfactual at matched ticks.

### 09. Spend inward carry in the denominator rather than zeroing the slot — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** In Adam, search for a useful B update whose squared gradient restrains inward displacement while a nonzero m continues to support B or an old tangential feature; certify the next native tick too. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 card 05][parent] preloads scale with C, and [recovery basins][basins] track v through B; this asks whether the *required* first B can supply the scale at the reader deadline and save an entry record. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate exact interval Adam on two reachable moment histories and compare scale-preloaded E+B, reset and offline m-only or v-only ablations at fixed clock. **Old-task retention check.** Check the threatened installed-C floor and an old feature that needs nonzero carried action at the next due read.

### 10. Make several first B batches interchangeable as brakes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify a truthful menu where every legal first B consumes enough carry before expiry, and every remainder order still finishes without a second brake. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Parked rebatching][parks] already buys order freedom; this requires interchangeable *useful expiry events*, not just batches that are harmless once parked. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust the first identities, window ticks and remaining orders of three real B batches; compare pinned-first scheduling and matched re-batching, including a harmful first batch that must force refusal. **Old-task retention check.** Read the tightest old floor after each possible first event and recheck that first B piece after all remaining updates.

### 11. Let B change which old floor limits the carry — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use the actual first-B update to move a previously blocking installed-C residual away from its floor while consuming momentum along another reader's normal; certify both constraints together. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Momentum landing][landing] budgets one later excursion and [#255][relation] uses finite chords; the bet is a live transfer between *binding* old constraints with no maintenance step. **Cheapest first test.** Two half-space readers on two features and two truthful B labels; compare projected B and a B-independent robust seal at equal B progress. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate both margins on every due read, including the case in which the bottleneck switches back on the next batch.

### 12. Lose at an interior arrival even when both edges pass — improvement
**Mechanism.** Search for a legal mid-window clock where momentum or bias correction creates a narrower safe landing than either edge. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Native optimizer clocks and timed reachability already allow nonmonotonicity; this falsifies two-endpoint checking as a shortcut. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three arrival times straddling a frozen schedule boundary and compare the exact minimum reader margin with endpoint-only bounds. **Old-task retention check.** Replay the offending interior trace, including the due read immediately before and after B.

### 13. Use a late useful batch to redeem an earlier lawful no-op — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Keep one certificate for a grammar with two real scheduling semantics: a no-op conserves carry, an idle optimizer tick spends it; B must deliver on time under both without selecting a different entry from private slots. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 card 07][parent] already tests both idles; the potential addition is a single priced contract for a mixed scheduler when a fixed semantics would give up otherwise useful slots. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate every two-event mixture and compare the cheapest common word against best separate words and a robust worst-idle seal. **Old-task retention check.** Read the old service before clock-changing transitions and measure wall-time lateness even for no-op traces.

### 14. Refuse free braking that is really maintenance in disguise — improvement
**Mechanism.** Require B to beat a frozen held-out B witness, then compare to a time-matched idle and a lawful useful replacement; price any clipping, wait or additional exposure. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Independent utility is in [#260][parent]; this audit separates real progress from gains on an auxiliary steering label. **Cheapest first test.** Pair two equal-cost batches with the same nominal braking effect, one of which fails the held-out B query. **Old-task retention check.** Test the old floor on both and make clear that passing A/C is insufficient if the actual B service fails.

### C. Test whether the two operations buy anything together

### 15. Fit a shared timing intersection without exposing which pocket binds — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Different pockets may impose different earliest and latest beneficial arrival times; publish only the common lawful subset if the offer allows choosing it *before* entry, otherwise check the whole advertised window. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Robust MPC][mpc] intersects feasible modes; the CL bet is an executable public service window bought by truthful E without per-history dispatch. **Cheapest first test.** Find two reachable aliases where one fails early and the other late, then shrink or widen the original offer and compare the best timed single-pocket seal and oracle. **Old-task retention check.** Serve the binding A read on one pocket and installed-C read on the other at every advertised tick; count refused windows.

### 16. Let one update brake one pocket and carry another — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** The same B batch spends residual inward velocity on one reachable branch while reinforcing it on another, via native state-dependent gradients; both satisfy one deadline and one task witness. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#255][relation] has signed branch relations and [#260][parent] has a live brake; the joint claim requires opposite *needed* effects on real old readers, not two copies of a working brake. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate truthful labels on a two-feature host with warm aliases of opposite reader slack; require split-only and brake-only arms to each miss a different legal trace, and compare the joint lawful stack. **Old-task retention check.** Match both old margins before entry and evaluate the worst pocket-by-tick read after first B and after the next tick.

### 17. Use one timely batch to swap the identity of the threatened pocket — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Over successive useful B pieces, the bottleneck can alternate between branches while the runtime receives only the public batch stream; certify that no branch needs a personalized maintenance update. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Parked replacements][parks] already envelope completed sets; the new bet is an all-order build bought by moving the braking obligation between disconnected components at different due reads. **Cheapest first test.** Two arrivals with one optional idle each and opposing old floors; compare independent per-phase seals, a static worst-branch budget and exact joint search. **Old-task retention check.** Read the tight old task just before each transfer and keep the first B piece live through the second transfer.

### 18. Compose pockets across two independently promised builds — moonshot, primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Export a small public successor relation on pocket *sets* and residual timing obligations, which a second learner can consume without receiving the original prefix or a private pocket label. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 card 22][parent] compiles reusable entry interfaces; this adds a contract for obligations that survive a handoff and can be discharged by the receiver's useful first batch. **Cheapest first test.** Tabulate exact obligations over two short grammars with a shared public handoff; only pursue if a bounded summary stays sound on held-out compositions and beats the exact table at matched bytes. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise a reader spanning the handoff and the earliest/latest receiver arrivals, with no gap masked as a reset.

### 19. Expose the missing bridge or missing minute as a minimal obstruction — improvement
**Mechanism.** Enumerate short E words to distinguish no common safe landing, an empty common timing intersection, and a viable first event with an unsafe continuation. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 cards 18 and 20][parent] already diagnose stages and bounded impossibility; this attributes a joint refusal to topology, timing or a later due read. **Cheapest first test.** Plant one of each failure in matched finite hosts and recover an actual word/trace witness or a bounded exhaustive obstruction, not a solver timeout. **Old-task retention check.** Name the first failing A/C read on the witness and report separately missed B milestones.

### 20. Price the split-by-brake interaction against a lawful stack — improvement
**Mechanism.** Cross exact connected vs disconnected admission with B-independent vs live-B safety at identical budgets, and include a jointly tuned stack that already has both tools. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** The ledger already requires stacked controls; this makes the interaction in timely useful B coverage explicit. **Cheapest first test.** Add a coupled variant and a factorized null to the paper host, exhaust words and arrival traces, and ask if the coupled gain survives proof costs and the joint stack. **Old-task retention check.** Require the same worst due A/installed-C floors and incoming-C gain for all comparisons, recording refusal as lost coverage, not a retention success.

### 21. Break a misleading clock–pocket correlation before deployment — improvement
**Mechanism.** Search for reachable withheld histories whose pocket is familiar but clock or moment coupling is new; expand a promise only if paired reachability still closes. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#260 paired optimizer bounds][parent] already avoid impossible combinations; this audit targets an equally bad missing *possible* combination when pockets seem to predict timing. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a prefix pool with two clocks per pocket; hold out a crossed pair, then compare an exact fiber and a pocket-only summary. **Old-task retention check.** Read the floor at the first distinguishing interior tick on the held-out pair and log conservative refusal separately from false admission.

### 22. Earn an emergency path through useful work that was already due — moonshot, primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Offer a predeclared arrival grammar with a public contingency: if no first B is delivered by a named tick, an independently due old-task update must absorb the carry, using the same public rule on all pockets. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Robust MPC][mpc] has fallback actions and [#260][parent] has continuation proofs; the bet is a deadline extension backed by already-due useful work rather than a hidden spare branch or maintenance batch. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate one lawful missed delivery and one alternate old update; gate further work on useful B coverage beyond a B-independent seal at equal old/C work and peak bytes. **Old-task retention check.** Serve due readers just before the fallback and after resumed B, and prove no promise on any uncovered delay.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Do split entry and live braking jointly buy on-time B coverage over the best lawful stack at equal old-task floors and truthful C budget?** Start with 16 and 20. Require a coupled witness where eliminating either ingredient loses a different lawful trace, and an all-in benefit after letting the stack use exact disjunctions and timed reachability too. If only the factorized paper fixture works or the jointly optimized stack ties, retire the joint primitive.
2. **Is split entry necessary for the offered service, or only for a proof abstraction?** Use 01, 03 and 07 to distinguish an actual failed-read separator from a bad hull. Search for a safe detour and a B-earned bridge, and compare one shared word with the per-history oracle. If branches require hidden dispatch, the public operation fails.
3. **Does the build really have timing and order freedom, or just a required opener with a polite name?** Run 08, 10, 12 and 15 with every first identity, interior arrival, true no-op and real idle tick. Show the earliest failed service if the menu is too broad; price any narrowing or missed offer against accumulation and stronger SGD at equal C cost.
4. **Can the same contract survive native Adam and a useful successor without rebuilding the proof from private slots?** Try 09, 18 and 21 on withheld reachable aliases, a clock boundary and a due read across the handoff. If live bytes or proof cost grow like per-history replay, price that before claiming a portable primitive.
