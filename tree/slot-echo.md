# Slot echo: keep the optimizer live, earn the rounding margin

Child of [Commuting writebacks](commuting-writebacks.md) ([PR #217][parent]). This follows the two score-3 cards in the [review][triage]: #04, remove the echo in a live optimizer slot when C shares a directional statistic with both writebacks, without a reset; #07, train C's receiver to buy a real rounding margin so both orders match at deployed precision over a bounded burst. Ideas and paper fixtures only; no pilot or GPU work.

**Bet.** Train a truthful new skill C whose ordinary receiver computation makes two frozen writebacks interchangeable for actual downstream updates, or moves those updates away from the rounding boundaries that would distinguish the orders. The bets are independent. The joint bet is that one necessary C feature does both at a cost the best separate methods cannot match. A weight-only tie, fixed execution order, silent reset, or rounded audit is not a win.

**Verdict.** Two primitive candidates, untested: a no-reset slot binding for an immediate live consumer, and a C-earned rounding margin for a declared burst. Drop either claim if the best matched control ties.

## What would count

- **Freeze the service contract.** Start from a reachable post-acquisition state. Fix the two logical repair payloads, caller witnesses, live reads, allowed suffixes and burst limit before fitting C. C must learn a real new task from truthful targets; auxiliary order losses can use development pairs, never the blind test pair or its order. Compare at matched C utility, per-item A/B/C margins, repair success, resident and peak bytes, work and latency. Show on blind histories that ordinary C and both writebacks actually use the shared statistic; perturb its common component as a check against a dead or bypassed slot.
- **Test the consuming update, not the snapshot.** Run both orders from the same joint state, and feed both the same next lawful update, including one that consumes the slot immediately. Inspect native optimizer directions, clipping, weight changes, guards and caller-indexed replies through a longer common tail. Include step counters, bias correction, second moments, schedules and overflow flags in the state when live. Raw slots may differ only if the deployed transition and all admitted reads cannot distinguish them in the promised scope. Keep every previously live reader or reject the swap; C cannot change what counts as success. At unmatched prefixes require correctness for the work done, not equality between different completed jobs.
- **Declare the arithmetic.** Fix formats for storage and intermediates, rounding mode, FMA/reduction choices, subnormal and overflow rules. At each corresponding publish/consume point enclose both executed pre-round ranges in the interior of one native rounding cell; bound branch inputs on the correct side of their guards too. Account for earlier rounding, cancellation and drift in the recurrence; stop at the first unproved step and report refused coverage and enforcement cost. Equality here is native bits where promised and exact decisions; report the underlying gap and distance to boundaries separately, not with a second audit cast. If an exposed read really returns the higher-precision value, equality at a lower-precision port does not cover it. A tested CPU arithmetic model is not a claim about an unmodeled deployment kernel.
- **Try the control stack first.** Compare weight-only commutation, plain SGD (slot-free negative), matched-latency accumulation, fixed/clock-matched moments, Adam/momentum reset (diagnostic only), [paired slot realignment][realign], [order receipts][receipts], and fixed analytic separation. For #07 include deterministic kernels, a fixed reduction/order, a reproducible or wider accumulator, and equally useful C with ordinary quantization-aware training but no pair-margin loss. Compare their best feasible combination, including paid serialization/fallback: a fixed order may tie in service cost but does not implement both orders. Report both useful work and swap coverage; charge certificate refreshes, dispatch delays and extra arithmetic rather than rewarding abstention.
- **Keep old skills live.** Every first test includes held-out A/B and acquired C, with rare inputs probing the shared route. Check floors after acquisition, at every required prefix, after both calls, at the first slot consumer and through the longest tail claimed by that card. Score refusal/fallback trajectories too. Per the [ledger][ledger], a tie with simple matched controls kills the novelty claim; labels below separate reusable primitive candidates from improvements and audits.

## Two paper fixtures (not evidence for a primitive)

**S1 · A direction can be invariant without the slots being equal.** In a toy receiver, each writeback makes its independent exact payload write and updates two live Adam-like slots by `m ← (m+g)/2`, `v ← (v+g²)/2` componentwise. The frozen statistic contributions are `g₁=(1,−1)` and `g₂=(−1,1)`, with `m₀=v₀=(2,4)`. Orders 1→2 and 2→1 leave the same payload state and `v=(5/4,7/4)`, but `m=(1/4,5/4)` and `m=(3/4,3/4)`, respectively. A third update with zero statistic increment halves both slots. A receiver whose declared statistic is `sum(m)/(sqrt(sum(v))+ε)` then takes the same nonzero direction, `(3/4)/(sqrt(3/2)+ε)`, in both orders. A reader of the first coordinate alone instead sees numerators `1/8` and `3/8` over the same denominator. Uniform decay preserves the aggregate equality; coordinate-specific decay need not. Choose held-out C targets that require both aggregate slots, not outputs a frozen direction could fit. The known sum is an analytic control. This is not an assertion that standard elementwise Adam commutes, or that an existing coordinate reader can be dropped.

**S2 · Same increments, different distance to a rounding break.** Use binary16 round-to-nearest-even after each add, `u=2⁻¹⁰`, and write increments `+5u/8` and `−3u/8`. At `x=1`, 1→2 stores `1+u`, whereas 2→1 first stores `1−u/2` and ends at `1`. For inputs on the grid from `1+u` through `1+2u`, the positive increment rounds up by `u`, the negative one back to its input, and each pre-round value lies at least `u/8` inside its selected cell. A common lawful update `x ← x−2u` *after each pair* gives net drift `−u` there. Start at `1+2u`: either order at either pair is safe for two pairs (post-update states `1+u,1`); a third begins at the counterexample and diverges. The two within-pair prefixes are different work and must each be checked against their own repair target. An exact static offset achieves the toy margin; it is a checker fixture, not evidence that learned C pays for slack. The learned version must realize equivalent repair utility and ordinary C behavior when the physical receiver value moves.

## Idea cards

### 01. Teach a live sufficient direction — primitive candidate (#217 #04)
**Mechanism.** Fit a small C receiver that computes a shared direction from both first and second moments; penalize order differences in the *next executed update* while keeping common-mode slot perturbations effective. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Adam][adam] consumes moments and [Slot splices][slots] transports them; here a reusable binding makes distinct, continuously carried states interchangeable without changing either state at publish. **Cheapest first test.** Fit a two-feature nonlinear extension of S1 and hold out a stencil of repair pairs and the immediate third gradient. **Same-budget control.** Analytic projection plus matched separate C, and paired realignment. **Old-task retention.** Check rare A/B directions at both prefixes and after the immediate consumer; test C on unseen moment ratios.

### 02. Make normal work absorb the echo — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Learn a C receiver whose mandatory, truthful minibatches apply a contractive transition to the order difference before the first sensitive consumer. Include drift injected by order-dependent future gradients in the bound; there is no idle delay or special reset update. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Understanding plasticity][plasticity] identifies stale moments under shifts; the object here is a checkable washout time paid for by required C service, not just shorter moment memory. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two continuations of a small quadratic with one and three mandatory C batches, including an immediate consumer that must either already match or invalidate the claim. **Same-budget control.** Tune decay in the unchanged receiver and spend the same batches on ordinary C; match next-task gain. **Old-task retention.** Probe the old direction that needs long momentum at each batch and the first normal consumer.

### 03. Transport a quotient through a changing preconditioner — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Learn a statistic of the pair `(m,v)` whose projection is closed under future shared gradients and under the allowed variance update; reject if a changing denominator re-exposes a canceled numerator. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Shampoo][shampoo] structures the preconditioner and [paired realignment][realign] edits carried state; this would bind a coupled statistic rather than copy or align two slots. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust a two-coordinate rational recurrence over three tail gradients, including two initially equal normalized directions that split under the next update. **Same-budget control.** Tied block preconditioning with a fixed feature map. **Old-task retention.** Check anisotropic rare A and B directions after the variance pulse, with matched C error.

### 04. Preserve the clocks as part of the statistic — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Learn to express contributions in a common decay basis keyed to their lawful service times; the receiver carries the time weights and both repair contributions through the live bias correction. Never backdate a sample or silently change the wall-clock scheduler. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Adam][adam] corrects bias by a step clock; [Protective orders][protective] treats clocks as a control. The candidate uses C's time-aware task feature to share a moment basis across two repair clocks, not to take one preferred permutation. **Cheapest first test.** Cross unequal cadence with equal-multiset repairs, plus a third update arriving on a step-size boundary. **Same-budget control.** Ordinary timestamp-corrected accumulation with a separate C. **Old-task retention.** Test A/B on fast and slow cadences and C on held-out arrival times; audit the first scheduled update.

### 05. Merge contributions, not optimizer histories — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let C learn additive local evidence for gradient and squared-gradient contributions; ingest both through a live statistic with one declared decay per logical window, while preserving the required intra-window prefix reads. Hidden history cannot substitute for those reads. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Adaptive Federated Optimization][fedopt] already aggregates client updates before adaptive steps; the bet is a C-trained binding that also supports distinct online prefix obligations without retaining two copies. **Cheapest first test.** Use two repairs with a mandatory prefix reader and an unequal-magnitude third update; exhaust masks and both orders. **Same-budget control.** Matched-latency gradient accumulation plus a small versioned prefix buffer. **Old-task retention.** Probe old service while only one receipt is available and after the shared moment is consumed.

### 06. Protect the clipping input, not just its projected output — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train C to supply a live norm statistic alongside a shared direction; require agreement on which clipping branch executes and on the executed update. A tail that recovers a hidden component through the norm invalidates the pair even if the direction alone ties. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Orthogonal Gradient Descent][ogd] projects to protect old functions; this would give an order-neutral binding for a nonlinear norm consumer that projection alone need not preserve. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust a two-axis moment grid across the clipping seam with equal projected means and unequal norms. **Same-budget control.** Fixed projected clipping, if legal for the old readers, or exact separate norm computation. **Old-task retention.** Include old tail samples on both sides of the clipping seam and compare their actual update magnitudes.

### 07. Find the tail that can still hear the echo — improvement
**Mechanism.** Search a declared menu for the shortest lawful common suffix that separates the two orders, and train only against development witnesses; validate on untouched reader/batch families. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** The [Scalable Commutativity Rule][scr] already quantifies over interfaces, and [order receipts][receipts] preserve order-sensitive evidence; this is falsification, not a new notion of equivalence. **Cheapest first test.** Branch a two-dimensional optimizer through depth three with sign, clipping and bias-correction reads; compare first distinguishing depth. **Same-budget control.** Direct two-order rollouts over the same number of suffix evaluations. **Old-task retention.** Include a rare real A/B query recovered only at depth two and keep it live when training the binding.

### 08. Certify a usable low-rank moment pair — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use C's task to learn a small shared covariance block and a bound on the discarded *direction error* under future preconditioning. A swap is valid only if that error cannot change any promised output or decision; don't discard directions on reconstruction error alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Shampoo][shampoo] factorizes preconditioning and [GPM][gpm] stores old-task subspaces; this would carry a certificate that the omitted joint moment component cannot distinguish two order traces. **Cheapest first test.** Train on a three-axis covariance toy, then rotate the next batch toward a held-out weak eigenvector. **Same-budget control.** Fixed-rank PCA with an exact comparison on the same small block. **Old-task retention.** Include old rare labels aligned to the weak eigenvector, at matched initial margins and C fit.

### 09. Keep complementary moments useful on a shared carrier — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Teach C to use two correlated directional channels so the common direction remains available to both writebacks despite one channel carrying the negative of the other's order residual. Train on variable common signal so cancellation cannot be replaced by a zero update. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [OGD][ogd] removes interference by separating directions and [Slot splices][slots] relocates state; the proposed binding keeps both channels live and cancels their observable residual. **Cheapest first test.** Sweep two unequal input correlations in S1, then break one coefficient in an admitted tail. **Same-budget control.** Fixed paired averaging plus separate C. **Old-task retention.** Hold out old tasks relying on the shared common mode and one lawful single-channel reader that should revoke permission.

### 10. Ask what can be certified before a new reader arrives — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish a compact, checkable description of which directions cannot hear the live echo. A newly arriving, authorized consumer must prove compatibility *before* taking the swap path; otherwise fallback preserves both original slot-service semantics. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Safe order families][orders] certify a configured order family and [order receipts][receipts] retain histories; the candidate carries a reusable compatibility object learned on C that transfers to previously unseen consumers without replay. **Cheapest first test.** Hold out two linear consumers, one in the certified annihilator and one outside; include a nonlinear consumer whose first derivative alone would falsely pass. **Same-budget control.** Inspect both optimizer states or retain an order receipt and fork-check. **Old-task retention.** Serve the noncompatible rare A reader via fallback and score its time and accuracy alongside C.

### 11. Train toward the interior of the actual cell — primitive candidate (#217 #07)
**Mechanism.** Fit C with a loss on the smaller distance of the two pre-round operands to the adverse native boundary, under unchanged repair targets and independently checked C usefulness. Recompute both interval endpoints at deployment-style precision without rounding the audit gap. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Learned Step Size Quantization][lsq] learns a quantizer for task performance; this keeps the arithmetic fixed and learns a receiver placement for a specific two-order contract. **Cheapest first test.** Put a nonlinear truthful C fit around the asymmetric binary16 boundary in S2 and hold out a nearby repair increment. **Same-budget control.** Ordinary quantization-aware C plus a hand-picked offset from the same calibration data. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B queries straddling the boundary and C's least-margin held-out predictions at the longest accepted burst.

### 12. Budget the margin across a burst before launch — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Give the receiver a horizon computed from a sound recurrence for both pre-round intervals, including same-direction drift and order discrepancy at each step. Train C for useful slack at the bottleneck step rather than average distance from a boundary. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Fast Reproducible Floating-Point Summation][repsum] makes a reduction order insensitive via arithmetic; here an unchanged format uses a learned receiver and a finite proof rather than a stronger accumulator. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust all binary swap sequences for K≤6 with one per-step common drift and unequal input radii; include the first live failure past K. **Same-budget control.** Wider accumulation of the affected scalar only, and ordinary C plus an interval checker. **Old-task retention.** Probe A/B/C at every pair and the required fallback at expiry.

### 13. Shape conditioning before buying ULPs — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train C to implement a different, lawful expression of the same receiver target that avoids subtracting large near-equals, so the two-order enclosure shrinks without moving the output farther from its semantic target. A bijective stored-coordinate relabeling alone is an explicit null. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Herbie][herbie] rewrites expressions to reduce roundoff; the claim is that learning a useful feature yields a transferable representation that static rewriting of the original read cannot access at the same budget. **Cheapest first test.** Compare two forms of a three-term centered read under canceling held-out write effects. **Same-budget control.** Herbie-style algebraic rewrite and compensated addition on the same scalar. **Old-task retention.** Check small but decision-critical old residuals and C away from the cancellation toy.

### 14. Fit a coupled range for writes and branches — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Learn a C statistic that retains correlations among the two writes, then prove a joint range for native casts and downstream guards; independent intervals may waste room that correlated enclosures can recover. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Affine arithmetic][affine] already carries correlated errors; the candidate is a C-trained binding that admits a small proof that transfers to blind write pairs. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate correlated and anticorrelated perturbations with equal marginals and one live threshold. **Same-budget control.** Affine arithmetic on equally useful unshaped C, with the same symbolic term budget. **Old-task retention.** Check the rare old task on the joint adverse corner and a C task where the correlation legitimately breaks.

### 15. Choose the fragile cast, keep the rest native — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train the C read to give enough slack at the most frequently consumed narrow port while keeping another fragile scalar on its already-allowed wider path. Bind which port gets the scarce width before the burst; no retry conditional on the observed order. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Mixed Precision Training][mixed] already keeps selected arithmetic wider; the candidate is a learned receiver whose shared task feature reduces the number of wide ports needed for a joint order proof. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust precision allocations for two competing casts and evaluate on one unseen coupling between them. **Same-budget control.** Exact search over allocations with unshaped C and deterministic reduction. **Old-task retention.** Test whichever old route loses a wide port and C under the rare cross-port coupling.

### 16. Keep a native result and a raw witness separately — improvement
**Mechanism.** Record extrema of pre-round gaps and boundary distances next to bitwise native comparisons; if the raw value is itself a live contract, require raw equality there too. Do not introduce an audit quantizer or demand equality of distinct-work prefixes. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Fast Reproducible Floating-Point Summation][repsum] distinguishes reproducibility from accuracy; this makes that distinction operational for C, not a novel checker. **Cheapest first test.** Plant a comparator that passes only after recasting, plus signed-zero and subnormal cases. **Same-budget control.** Inspect raw endpoints and native comparison directly under an exact arithmetic emulator. **Old-task retention.** Verify that the rare A task affected by a branch difference still fails in the audit even when an aggregate accuracy score ties.

### 17. Earn reuse across two real numeric formats — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train on the intersection of certified interior ranges for two deployment profiles, each with its own cast points and guards. Reject on either profile when the intersection vanishes; no claim that FP16 slack implies BF16 slack. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Mixed Precision Training][mixed] chooses format and [LSQ][lsq] adapts quantization; the bet is one learned receiver that buys useful swaps in both profiles without profile-specific weights or a common wider execution path. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust an exponent-boundary neighborhood with binary16 and emulated BF16; fit on one neighborhood and reserve a nonoverlapping one for validation. **Same-budget control.** Per-profile calibrated offsets at matched total storage, plus a common exact reduction. **Old-task retention.** Test old inputs whose margins change sign across profiles and keep C utility matched on both.

### 18. Make the rounding boundary itself a counterexample generator — improvement
**Mechanism.** Enumerate or bound reachable states near the next midpoint, guard threshold or underflow cliff and use the first true differential trace to tighten the claimed envelope. Validation uses disjoint blocks of attainable prefixes. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Herbie][herbie] searches numerically challenging inputs; the difference here is the paired execution with a legally reachable optimizer tail, an audit rather than a new proof method. **Cheapest first test.** Sweep one exponent transition and vary the legal burst by one, comparing predicted first break to exhaustive execution. **Same-budget control.** Direct paired execution of equally many near-midpoint and random reachable cases. **Old-task retention.** Retain the hardest real A/B/C queries in the validation set, not just synthetic boundary placements.

### 19. Use one C statistic for both live direction and margin — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** Make the very statistic used by the first optimizer consumer also center the first vulnerable cast, while keeping common-mode changes observable to C and the actual writeback. Separate penalties can be learned, but the certified shared path must be necessary to both effects. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Adam][adam] and [LSQ][lsq] adapt different objects; the proposed primitive is a jointly useful binding, not tuning them side by side. **Cheapest first test.** Cross echo shaping on/off with slack shaping on/off in a S1+S2 receiver; include a pair where each isolated intervention still fails and ablate the shared path. **Same-budget control.** Best feasible stack of structured moments or paired realignment plus a local wide/reproducible cast and unshaped C. **Old-task retention.** Match A/B/C at all four corners, including a rare A read needing both the carried direction and the near-boundary cast.

### 20. Reserve slack for the first consumer, then spend it on more calls — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** Allocate margin to the earliest legal optimizer consumer first; only count later pairs if the worst earlier residual and rounding debit fit the same certificate. Teach C to shrink the residual at the consumer that actually caps burst length. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Safe order families][orders] permit schedules and [affine arithmetic][affine] budgets correlated error; the reusable candidate couples C's *live* slot transfer bound to the earned admission horizon. **Cheapest first test.** Cross early versus late consumption with alternating versus aligned residuals, including a long nominally safe schedule failing early. **Same-budget control.** Exact finite scheduling plus a per-port wider accumulator at the same latency and bytes. **Old-task retention.** Test rare early A latency, B after the last admitted pair and C after fallback, counting displaced service.

### 21. Prove closure against mixing orders within the burst — improvement (joint audit)
**Mechanism.** Track a reachable set across both choices at each pair. A certificate for “all 1→2” versus “all 2→1” is insufficient if a mixed sequence can first amplify moments and then cross a cast. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Scalable commutativity][scr] and [safe order families][orders] already reason over continuations; this audit distinguishes an actual finite burst proof from two end-to-end traces. **Cheapest first test.** Build a three-pair recurrence with a mixed-order-only failure and enumerate eight schedules, including identical final weights hiding a different middle consumer. **Same-budget control.** Exact reachability on the same number of states. **Old-task retention.** Include an old task at the intermediate failing consumer and compare rare A/B/C paths under every admitted schedule.

### 22. Learn when a smaller certificate is worth invoking — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** Let truthful C compute a public sufficient condition predicting which reachable prefixes merit a cheap formal slot-and-round check; the prediction never authorizes a swap without proof. Win only by buying more safe service per total unit of work than direct checks and heuristic dispatch. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Order receipts][receipts] retain witnesses and the [ledger][ledger] found direct two-order checks hard to beat. The candidate is a transferable C feature that reduces verification work without making acceptance easier by dropping live readers. **Cheapest first test.** Use a finite stream of blind pairs with rare safe blocks and run a fixed-cost verifier with and without the C-derived gate. **Same-budget control.** Direct paired execution, fixed priority plus certificate caching, and best lawful serialization. **Old-task retention.** Score old tasks on misses and cheap rejects as well as wins; charge deadlines lost to verification or fallback.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can a truthful C make a live slot echo unobservable to an immediate, ordinary consumer?** Start with 01–04 and the distinguishing readers in 06–07. Freeze permissions and prove common-mode sensitivity, then hold out gates, tail gradients and slot clocks. Kill the primitive if it relies on changing what an old reader sees, waiting out an early consumer, or if a fixed quotient with equally useful C and paired realignment ties at equal cost.
2. **How much bounded deployed-precision slack does C actually buy?** Start with 11–14 and S2. Measure extra accepted pairs before any specified cast or decision can split, including adversarially aligned drift. Sweep real boundary locations and compare same-usefulness unshaped C plus compensated/wider arithmetic or fixed order. Require a positive *worst-case* interior margin, not an average accuracy lift.
3. **When does the combined binding beat the best stacked control?** Use 19–21 on blind bursts, with all four shaping arms and the cost-matched union of slot and numerical baselines. Seek a safe service region neither alone reaches, with the earliest genuine consumer at the same deadline. If raw slots need a larger shadow checkpoint or fallback does all the work, report no joint win.
4. **What is the smallest honest scope that still serves ordinary old tasks?** Test 08–10 and 22 with held-out readers, changes of precision profile and bursts just beyond the declared limit. Record exactly which requirement revoked each admission, check old-task fallback, and reject scopes whose apparent coverage comes from removing a real reader or from a direct test that is cheaper at the same coverage.
