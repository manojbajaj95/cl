# Replay deadlines: can replay be scheduled as an option that expires when training merges states?

Parent: [Merger certificates (PR #110)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/110), its idea 7 ("replay option with a last exercise time") and sub-question 2. **Node question:** replay can only help before training merges two edited states. Can replay be scheduled as an option whose exercise deadline is predicted from the merge graph, and what new primitive does that give?

**Short answer.** Not as stated. The deadline binds only target-blind replay, and in a continuous host it is not a time point. In the CPU probe below, timing self-replay well is worth at most about 0.01 accuracy, even with hindsight. Replay with stored labels is worth about 0.09. What does work is separating **when the replay target is read** from **when it is written back**. Reading self-labels before a public damaging window and replaying them after it beats the hindsight-best timing of ordinary self-replay by 0.03 (budget K = 4), with no persistent target bytes. Raw idea 1 below builds on this "escrow" result.

## Root cause: an option needs a strike date, and replay of a stored target does not have one

PR #110's converse is correct but narrow: after an **exact** merger of full learning states, *target-blind* replay from that state cannot separate the two targets. Turning this into a scheduler fails for four reasons.

1. **Stored-target replay has no deadline.** A buffer that stores the label already carries the discriminator, so it can reinstall the target after any merger. Its timing problem is ordinary cost scheduling (learned replay scheduling, [2209.08660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08660); MIR, [1908.04742](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)). In the probe, true-label replay is *better* placed just after a damaging window than just before it (+0.033 ± 0.011): repair beats prevention, because the damaging window overwrites prevention. The deadline only applies to target-blind replay: self-distillation, LwF, pseudo-rehearsal and generative replay, where the current model is its own teacher.
2. **In injective hosts, merges are not events.** PR #108 showed that invertible updates never merge classes. With finite-step SGD and `0 < ηλ < 1`, distinguishability shrinks continuously and never becomes exactly zero. So a "deadline" needs a precision or noise threshold, and the useful quantity is how fast it decays, not when it hits zero. In the probe, the value of a self-replay exercise relative to a true-label exercise at the same time falls smoothly with the damage already done: 0.80 at old-task accuracy ≥ 0.9, then 0.50, 0.41, 0.29, and 0.11 below 0.6. It never drops off a cliff.
3. **The option has almost no time value.** A self-replay exercise can only lock in what is still decodable today, and waiting never makes it more decodable. That is the setting where exercising early or evenly is close to optimal. In the probe with K = 4, every policy lands within 0.011 of the hindsight oracle: fixed spacing, before-severe, a reactive drift trigger, and a forecast from public window types. The forecast beats the reactive trigger by 0.005 ± 0.002 but ties fixed spacing (0.002 ± 0.002).
4. **The merge graph cannot be seen online, and replay changes it.** Computing the graph needs the future batches (PR #110 forbids getting them for free) or calibrated type statistics. Each exercise also changes the later graph (PR #110 idea 22). A deadline forecast is therefore a learned forecast of forgetting, which is prior work, not a certificate.

**What survives.** The asset that actually decays is the model's ability to label its own old inputs correctly. Replay can use that asset at two separate times. Reading it is free and is most accurate early. Writing it back is wasted if a known damaging window comes next. So the decision worth making is when to **record** the self-target, not when to replay, and recording turns a decaying free asset into short-lived stored bytes. In the probe, recording self-labels right before the first severe window and replaying them right after it closes about 77% of the gap between self-replay after the window and true-label replay after it: 0.726 vs 0.710 vs 0.732 mean old-task accuracy. With K = 4 and one window of label holding per exercise, escrow scores 0.778 ± 0.025, against 0.736 ± 0.029 for fixed self-replay and 0.744 for the hindsight-best self-replay schedule. A true-label buffer replayed on the fixed schedule scores 0.835 but stores labels for the whole stream.

### CPU probe ([`tree/probes/replay_deadline_probe.py`](probes/replay_deadline_probe.py), numpy, several CPU-min)

The host is linear logistic, d = 30. Old task A has inputs on coordinates 0–14. Then 16 windows of 12 SGD steps train on new tasks B_j, with inputs on coordinates 10–29 that overlap A's on 10–14. Window types are public: mild (conflict 0.3 on the shared block, lr 0.1) or severe (conflict 1.5, lr 0.5, probability 0.25). Weight decay of 0.05 is charged per stream step. An exercise is 40 steps on fresh unlabeled A inputs regenerated from a seed. Results are means over 30 worlds with 95% CIs; policy thresholds were tuned on 10 separate calibration worlds.

| Arm (mean A accuracy over windows) | Value |
|---|---|
| No replay | 0.691 ± 0.036 |
| Self-replay just before / just after first severe window | 0.707 / 0.710 (gap −0.003 ± 0.004) |
| True-label replay just before / just after | 0.699 / 0.732 (gap −0.033 ± 0.011) |
| **Escrow: self-labels recorded before, replayed after** | **0.726 ± 0.033** |
| K = 4 self-replay: fixed / before-severe / reactive / forecast / hindsight oracle | 0.736 / 0.735 / 0.733 / 0.738 / 0.744 |
| **K = 4 escrow around the first 4 severe windows** | **0.778 ± 0.025** (+0.042 ± 0.009 over fixed) |
| K = 4 true-label buffer, fixed schedule (persistent bytes) | 0.835 ± 0.018 |

Last-task B accuracy is 0.764 with no replay, 0.748–0.762 for the self- and label-replay arms, and 0.773 ± 0.033 for K = 4 escrow, so escrow does not slow new learning in this host. **Caveats:** escrow holds 1,280 label bits per exercise for one window, which must be charged as byte-time against a buffer. Self-replay steps have no weight decay; with decay charged during replay, replay on saturated items *shrinks* the margin and every replay arm falls below no-replay, a real effect worth guarding in pilots. The host is linear and its damage is announced; this is a mechanism check, not evidence for deep nets.

## Prior art and rules for this node

- Read [PR #6's ledger, triage and top 10](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6/files) and [PR #110](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/110). Do not repackage PR #110's ideas 7–11, 22 and 24 (last exercise time, two-part repairs, XOR repairs, covering replay, scratch loans, nested replay, impossibility receipts). The ledger has already killed bounded-delay stability scheduling and delay-compensated retention gating as "scheduling, not a mechanism".
- Nearest work: learned replay scheduling [2209.08660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08660); MIR [1908.04742](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742); Dark Experience Replay stores logits at insertion [2004.07211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07211), which is the strongest null for escrow. Self-training trades denoising against signal forgetting with an optimal stopping time ([2602.14029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14029)). Also synthetic self-distillation for small-model continual post-training [2610.13113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13113), composed CL mechanisms over 100 tasks [2609.06986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06986), on-policy self-distillation teacher refresh schedules (2606.03532), and forgetting via NTK overlap [2010.04003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003). The alphaXiv quota was exhausted, so this screen used HF search and arXiv abstracts and is not novelty clearance.
- Required controls for any escrow claim: DER-style logits stored at insertion with equal byte-time; true-label buffer with equal bytes; self-replay at the hindsight-best schedule; prevention-only (projection or EWC). Report byte-time (bits × steps held), not just peak bytes. Check old-task accuracy on disjoint inputs after every window, plus the new task's learning curve.

## Raw ideas

### A. Make the option buy a stored target, not a weight update (escrow)

1. **Record-before / replay-after escrow.** **Mechanism:** before a publicly announced damaging window, record the model's own labels or logits on regenerable unlabeled old inputs. Replay them after the window, then delete them. **Why it might be new:** it separates when the target is read from when it is written back. Self-distillation, LwF and DER read and write at the same time or store permanently; here the target bytes exist only across the shock. **Cheapest test:** done above for a linear host (+0.042 over fixed self-replay, above the self-replay oracle). Next, an MLP on split-MNIST/permuted streams with announced high-LR windows, against DER at equal byte-time. **Retention:** old-task accuracy after each window and at the end; new-task curve unchanged.
2. **Byte-time as a currency.** **Mechanism:** charge stored target bits per step held. The self-label's quality r(t) decays and rent accumulates, so record when r(t) times label value minus rent peaks. **Why it might be new:** buffers are priced by peak size; pricing the time held makes "store late, delete early" a design axis. **Cheapest test:** closed form in the linear host, then a sweep of hold time vs accuracy; compare a fixed persistent buffer with equal byte-time. **Retention:** worst old-task window accuracy at each byte-time budget.
3. **Flip-imminent escrow.** **Mechanism:** record self-labels only for inputs whose predicted time to a flip under the announced window types is shorter than the next replay opportunity. **Why it might be new:** selection by forecast flip time under a public schedule, not by current interference (MIR) or margin. **Cheapest test:** linear host with margin-sorted subsets; compare MIR and lowest-margin at equal bits. **Retention:** accuracy on recorded and unrecorded old inputs separately.
4. **Snapshot-ladder teachers with expiry.** **Mechanism:** keep frozen low-rank teacher snapshots and distill from the oldest snapshot whose self-agreement on canaries is still above threshold; drop a snapshot once a newer one is as good. **Why it might be new:** snapshot retirement by measured decay rather than EMA or fixed refresh. Heavy prior art (OPSD refresh), so likely an improvement. **Cheapest test:** two-snapshot ladder vs EMA teacher on the linear host. **Retention:** old-task accuracy and snapshot bytes.
5. **Canary items as the exercise clock.** **Mechanism:** store true labels for a handful of old items. When canary accuracy drops, record escrow labels for the unlabeled pool. **Why it might be new:** a small labeled set acts as a label-free deadline detector for a much larger unlabeled one, instead of being replayed itself. **Cheapest test:** 16 canaries in the probe; compare the drift trigger and fixed spacing at equal bits. **Retention:** canary items are excluded from evaluation; report held-out old accuracy.

### B. Replace the hard deadline by a measured decay

6. **Self/label value ratio meter.** **Mechanism:** estimate r(t), the fraction of a true-label repair that a self-replay still recovers, from label-free signals (self-agreement against the last snapshot, prediction entropy). **Why it might be new:** r(t) is the right quantity for timing and escrow; current-loss triggers measure damage, not recoverability. **Cheapest test:** regress the probe's Q3 points (r from 0.80 to 0.11) on label-free features. **Kill:** rank correlation < 0.5. **Retention:** r itself is a retention quantity; validate on held-out worlds.
7. **Tangent-twin horizon.** **Mechanism:** carry a forward-mode JVP of the readout along the edit direction through every training step (one extra JVP per step), giving the contraction of the target's distinguishability. The deadline is when it crosses the readout noise. **Why it might be new:** a prospective horizon from the training dynamics' Jacobian product, rather than first-order response or NTK overlap at a single time. **Cheapest test:** the linear host, where the JVP is exact; compare the reactive drift trigger. **Retention:** horizon error against the measured old-task flip time.
8. **Precision as the clock.** **Mechanism:** in low-precision training a component under half an LSB rounds to zero, which is an exact merger. Exercise by "LSB distance" of the edit's coordinates. **Why it might be new:** low-precision hosts are the one natural place where PR #110's exact deadlines exist; it predicts a phase change from fp32 to int8. **Cheapest test:** int8 vs fp32 linear host; measure whether self-replay value has a cliff only in int8. **Retention:** old-task accuracy around the cliff.
9. **Region-majority deadlines.** **Mechanism:** self-replay with hard labels denoises while the majority of a region of input space is still correct ([2602.14029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14029)). Track the region's majority margin and treat its crossing as the deadline. **Why it might be new:** the deadline belongs to a region, not to an item or a state. **Cheapest test:** a clustered input distribution with clusters damaged unevenly; compare item-level triggers. **Retention:** per-cluster old accuracy.

### C. Change what an exercise does

10. **Post-shock settlement for stored buffers.** **Mechanism:** with stored labels, postpone repair until after announced damaging windows (+0.033 in the probe) and spend prevention compute only on unannounced damage. **Why it might be new:** splits replay into prevention and settlement by whether the damage is announced. **Cheapest test:** the probe with mixed announced/unannounced severe windows; compare MIR at equal steps. **Retention:** worst window old accuracy, since settlement accepts a temporary dip.
11. **Disagreement replay.** **Mechanism:** distill the recorded snapshot only on inputs where it now disagrees with the current model, weighted by the snapshot's margin. **Why it might be new:** concentrates repair on what the shock changed; LwF distills everywhere. **Cheapest test:** the probe, equal steps. **Retention:** accuracy on agreeing inputs must not fall.
12. **Max–min horizon replay.** **Mechanism:** choose replay inputs that most increase the shortest predicted horizon across all old tasks, not the largest loss drop. **Why it might be new:** optimizes the expiry of the weakest obligation. PR #110 idea 22 only gates replay; this ranks by horizon. **Cheapest test:** three old tasks with different decay rates. **Retention:** the worst old task's final accuracy.
13. **Whittle-index replay over decaying assets.** **Mechanism:** model each task's self-decodability as a restless bandit arm with known decay; assign exercises by Whittle index. **Why it might be new:** a closed-form index replaces an RL scheduler ([2209.08660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08660)). Likely an improvement unless the index has structure beyond the bandit. **Cheapest test:** five tasks with different rates; compare round-robin and an MCTS schedule. **Retention:** all tasks at the end.
14. **Escrow transfer across shocks.** **Mechanism:** chain escrows: labels recorded before shock 1 and replayed after it are re-recorded, after the replay, as the target for shock 2. **Why it might be new:** a relay of short holds instead of one long hold; tests whether errors compound or the relay is stable. **Cheapest test:** the probe with consecutive severe windows. **Retention:** accuracy after the last shock against a single long hold.

### D. Make the deadline a design target

15. **Install along slow-contracting directions.** **Mechanism:** among equally good installs, choose the one with the longest tangent-twin horizon under the announced stream types. **Why it might be new:** install selection by predicted survival time, not orthogonality (null-space editing, PR #106). **Cheapest test:** two-solution linear install; compare a null-space projection. **Retention:** untouched old tasks after install.
16. **Deadline-equalizing host.** **Mechanism:** meta-learn an architecture whose old-task contraction rates are equal, so a fixed replay schedule becomes optimal. **Why it might be new:** removes the scheduling problem instead of solving it. **Cheapest test:** two-head toy with a learned per-head gain. **Retention:** both heads plus a new task.
17. **Horizon-aware learning rate.** **Mechanism:** when the earliest horizon is near and no exercise budget is left, lower the learning rate for that window. **Why it might be new:** trades new-task speed for horizon explicitly. Risk: an LR-schedule improvement. **Cheapest test:** probe with the LR cut on severe windows; compare a uniform lower LR at equal new-task progress. **Retention:** old and new task accuracy.
18. **Stream permutation under public types.** **Mechanism:** if the learner may reorder upcoming windows, group damaging windows so one escrow covers them all. **Why it might be new:** exploits escrow's fixed record/replay cost; task-ordering papers optimize final accuracy, not escrow coverage. **Cheapest test:** probe with grouped vs scattered severe windows, K = 1. **Retention:** old accuracy over the stream.

### E. Duals and other settings

19. **Unlearning as a reverse deadline.** **Mechanism:** an unlearned fact is safe once r(t) ≈ 0, so no self-replay from the current model can bring it back. Certify unlearning by failed self-escrow recovery. **Why it might be new:** a relearning-resistance test that uses the model's own labels, not an external relearning attack. **Cheapest test:** a linear host with one coordinate unlearned by decay vs by projection. **Retention:** retained tasks during the audit.
20. **Federated escrow.** **Mechanism:** a client that is about to receive a damaging local window uploads its self-labels for shared tasks; the server returns them after the window. **Why it might be new:** places short-lived target bytes at another site timed by announced local shocks. **Cheapest test:** two linear clients with complementary shocks; compare FedAvg and local buffers at equal communication. **Retention:** each client's old tasks.
21. **Agent-note escrow.** **Mechanism:** before an agent's model update, the old model re-derives what each stored note implies (answers to canary queries); after the update, the new model is fine-tuned or prompted to match. **Why it might be new:** applies record-before/replay-after to PR #105's rate–drift loan on notes. **Cheapest test:** a small LM with notes before and after a LoRA update. **Retention:** note-dependent query accuracy and unrelated benchmarks.
22. **Deadline-indexed eviction.** **Mechanism:** evict a stored label when the model is predicted to self-label that item correctly through the next exercise; restore it by escrow if needed. **Why it might be new:** a buffer that holds only what the model cannot currently regenerate. **Cheapest test:** probe with a fixed buffer vs evicting buffer at equal mean bytes. **Retention:** old accuracy on evicted items.
23. **American-option exercise boundary.** **Mechanism:** fit a one-dimensional jump process for self-decodability from calibration worlds and solve the optimal stopping boundary by dynamic programming. **Why it might be new:** a testable prediction that the boundary is near "exercise immediately/uniformly" when the time value is near zero, as the probe suggests. **Cheapest test:** DP on the probe's calibrated increments. **Retention:** old accuracy under the DP policy against fixed.
24. **Prevention/settlement credit split.** **Mechanism:** for each task, measure replay gain before vs after damage and assign that task to prevention or settlement mode. **Why it might be new:** a per-task mode switch decided by measured repair dynamics. **Cheapest test:** the probe with tasks of different input overlap. **Retention:** all tasks per mode.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Does record-before / replay-after escrow beat stored-at-insertion logits (DER) at equal byte-time in a nonlinear host?** (1–3, 5, 14, 22.) Highest chance: it changes what replay is, a short-lived target read from the model itself, not when replay happens. Gate: a two-layer MLP or small CNN on an announced-shock stream, K exercises, against DER and a true-label buffer at equal byte-time, and the hindsight-best self-replay schedule. **Kill:** DER at equal byte-time ties, or the escrow gain disappears when shocks are unannounced and predicted from a label-free meter.
2. **Is there any natural host where self-replay value falls off a cliff?** (7–9.) Candidates are low-precision training, ReLU dead units, and region-majority flips. If none, timing ideas stay improvements, and PR #110's deadlines are limited to built-in discrete hosts. **Kill:** r(t) declines smoothly in int8 as in fp32.
3. **Can r(t) be read without labels well enough to time escrow?** (5–7.) Canaries, tangent-twin JVPs and self-agreement. **Kill:** none predicts r(t) with rank correlation ≥ 0.5 on held-out worlds.
4. **Does settlement beat prevention generally when damage is announced?** (10, 24.) The probe's +0.033 for stored labels suggests replay should follow shocks, not precede them. **Kill:** the gap vanishes in nonlinear hosts or under unannounced shocks.
