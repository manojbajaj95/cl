# Repair-and-reveal steps: fix one hazard, decide what can coexist

**Status:** Ideas only; exact paper fixtures, no learner runs or GPU work. **Parent:** [Useful-batch checks](useful-batch-checks.md) ([PR #273][parent]), cards **03** and **23**. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 390][triage] scores each 3: let one safe, due C step repair a suspect fallback while its ordinary output separates a different, still-unrepaired hazard; let one due C report rule out harmful combinations of simultaneously submitted claims without endorsing the claims separately. **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger]: match budgets and starting margins, keep ordinary old-task service in every first test, and make a merge beat the strongest stack of its parts.

**Verdict:** The best candidate is a repair that changes which *joint* admissions are backed while its ordinary report still distinguishes the remaining harmful combinations. Neither seed alone establishes an advantage: a conventional repair plus a separate check can do both, and a same-information allocator can already choose a compatible bundle. Advance only if a lawful due step opens service the best stacked rival cannot match at equal C progress, old-task floors, observation access and clocks.

## Contract and rivals

- **Freeze obligations, not their classifications.** A is original work, B is installed work and backed obligations, and C is installed and incoming successor work. Before blind arrivals fix ordinary held-out panels, acquisition-anchored floors, due truthful C menu, native optimizer, public precision, reuse rules, rights, liabilities, deadlines and legal continuations. A step must serve C and preserve all existing service on every reachable history consistent with the pre-step view, even those it will refuse. An at-risk candidate route needs independent backing for any service already owed. “Repaired” means that the same candidate service passes the same obligation after the step, including all declared suffixes where the new right could be used. Receipt deletion, a safer metric, or expiry is not repair. Reclassify only **unspent** rights at the post-step cut; no retroactive validation, and outstanding obligations stay backed.
- **Bind claims as a cohort before report or draw.** Keep the actual reachable *joint* history set, including correlated and deliberately cancelling deviations; if only marginal bounds are known, allow every compatible combination. No independence assumption, private slot reads, fabricated C batches, retry-until-pass, or extra forwards in the proposed operation. Use only the trusted or independently available ordinary C report and freeze allowed follow-ups and report precision. A report cell is the set of all reachable post-step histories (and liabilities) giving that public view. For each candidate **bundle with its actual shared capacity and continuation**, issue it only if it is backed on every member of the cell. The common decision must have a public implementable schedule or policy, not a different hidden-state-dependent plan per member. Mixed cells can yield a smaller backed bundle or refusal; neither result authenticates the claimants. Do not union individually safe rights, or issue promises for speculative future reports.
- **Run the rival stacks at full strength.** Give the controls the same lawful observations and due menu and let them choose legal orders, use shared-capacity allocation, and retain scoped evidence. For repair/reveal compare [TCM5][parent] check without repair, best repair then separate check at equal total cost, [#232][shrink] detect-before-shrink plus repair, authenticated chains, and a repair that aliases the other unresolved hazard. For joint decisions compare TCM5 one check per claim at equal cost, [#253][check] sealed random checks per claim plus optimal allocator, union of individually safe rights (unsafe negative), separate joint audit, and a report that cannot separate a harmful pair. Include a passive verifier plus ordinary repair and allocation, and an exhaustive same-information planner as a ceiling. An unrestricted planner with identical actions and objective can emulate the proposed policy; any win must identify an actual resource constraint or compare practical planning cost.
- **Price the service, not just the optimizer pass.** Match C gain per queue, starting per-slice margins, deadlines, legal horizons and held-out service. Track timely backed B bundles and useful single arrivals; unsafe admissions, lawful refusals, fallback load; peak and cumulative bytes, optimizer/check/planning work and clocks. At every exposed cut, refusal and later blind arrival exercise ordinary held-out A, earlier B (including both consumers and whichever fallback is used) and installed C. Show realized service before reuse, after reuse, and on a later blind arrival; report a Pareto tradeoff if budgets cannot be matched.

## Two exact paper fixtures

### R. Repair the x route, leave the y hazard observable

Coordinatewise clipped heavy-ball: `u = clip(m/2 + g, -1, 1); w' = w - u; m' = u`. Start x = y = z = 0, with hidden `m_x ∈ {0,1}`, `m_y ∈ {1/2,1}` and `m_z = 0`. Each listed slot s at weight 0 is reachable from zero with two gradients −s, 3s/2. The due C loss is `((x+1)² + (z−1)²)/2`. Its native published y statistic is `floor(4|u_y|)`, not the slots. The requested B_x route must deliver x ≤ −1/2 after the next ordinary idle-x step; B_y must deliver y ≥ −3/8 after an idle-y step. The two toy rights are one-use at the named cut, with no intervening suffix. Existing B service has an independent route. For the toy A panel take −2 ≤ x ≤ 0 and −1 ≤ y ≤ 0; installed C is on a separate untouched coordinate.

Before the C step, B_x would fail on `m_x=0`; afterwards x = −1 and `m_x'=1` for either slot, so its subsequent idle read is −3/2 on both paths. C fits x and z in all histories. B_y remains mixed over the pre-step histories:

| Entry y slot | C-step y | Ordinary y bin | Post-C idle y | B_y backed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | −1/4 | 1 | −3/8 | Yes |
| 1 | −1/2 | 2 | −3/4 | No |

A remains in range at entry, C and idle cuts, including refusal. With coarser `floor(|u_y|)` both rows publish 0: the same repair does not justify B_y. If the allowed suffix instead includes a preceding truthful y step with target −1, the originally passing row reaches y = −9/8 on that step alone, outside A and below the B_y floor. The short-cut classification does not survive that extension. This checks the two predicates, not realistic A/B/C retention, fallback implementation, or an advantage over a passive signed read.

### J. One report decides a bundle without verifying either claim

Use the same optimizer on two otherwise idle coordinates with entry weights 0 and slots `m_i ∈ {−1,−1/2,1/2,1}`; every pair is reachable as above. One due C batch fits a fresh z to 1 with zero gradient on these two coordinates. Each simultaneous claim says its contingent right takes one unit of the common reserve; the frozen reservation rule actually charges `d_i = 4|u_i| ∈ {1,2}` at the post-step cut. The reserve is three units, and no pre-existing obligation uses it in this toy. Suppose the ordinary reporter already publishes `4(|u_1|+|u_2|)` at exact precision; it publishes no per-right read. All paths keep the toy A coordinates within [−1/2, 1/2] and fit z.

| Report | Possible demand pairs | Maximum robust bundle at this cut |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | (1,1) | Both |
| 3 | (1,2), (2,1) | Both, via the same shared pool without fixed per-owner partitions |
| 4 | (2,2) | Either alone; not both |

At report 3 each one-unit claim admits an alias, but the pair is backed without attributing the larger demand. At report 4 both singleton rights fit, but their union does not; the unselected consumer needs an independent route if service is already owed. A reporter using the **signed** sum instead would alias `(u_1,u_2)=(1/4,−1/4)` with `(1/2,−1/2)` at zero; there, do not grant both. This is a capacity sanity check under a stipulated ordinary report. An allocator with the same total ties it; no learned improvement or full fallback retention is established.

## A. Repair one route without erasing the other decision

### 01. Post-step repair/reveal — primitive candidate (parent #03 baseline)
**Mechanism.** Replace a binary check with a transition that repairs one live candidate route and uses its native output to decide a distinct unresolved right. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 card 03][parent] is exactly this seed; [#232][shrink] preserves pre-shrink detectability, not repaired service. This card is a baseline, not a priority claim. **Cheapest first test.** Run R with a passive early read, a separately funded repair plus audit and an aliasing repair; separate steps, total work and useful C gain. **Old-task retention check.** Serve A, earlier B and installed C on every path, including the independent B_x route before the repair and the B_y fallback on refusal.

### 02. Repair against the worst viable suffix — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select a due step using the minimum repaired-route slack across admitted reuse continuations, then maximize residual separation only within that safe menu. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 card 03][parent] requires post-step validity; [GEM][gem] protects remembered tasks on updates. The candidate selector protects against a declared future reuse while preserving information about another route. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two C steps tying at the first repair cut but diverging after a legal slot reuse; compare a one-step gradient guard plus early audit. **Old-task retention check.** Run held-out A, both B routes and installed C through reuse, with equal acquisition margins and the failing suffix exposed.

### 03. Bracket a pending use, not just an update — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Dispatch a C repair only in windows where the unresolved right has not been used yet, and carry an enforceable “not usable before decision” boundary into the scheduler. Existing service must continue independently. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][signatures] relocates an audit cut; the window here ends at actual reliance and depends on whether the step repairs a due route, not just whether evidence remains observable. **Cheapest first test.** Move a real B job across the C cut by one tick; compare earliest passive audit plus optimal ordering, including a tempting retroactive admit. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise the B job on time in both orders, then A/installed C and an older B consumer behind it.

### 04. Bring the repaired route online only at a certified handover — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Require overlap between the independent old fallback and the newly repaired route across every history in the report cell until all outstanding borrowers of the old route have drained. Use the same C report to check the other hazard, but never borrow the overlap twice. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) replaces backing and retires a receipt; this operation admits a repair while the old backing is still provably live and a separate hazard remains open. **Cheapest first test.** Two borrowers with staggered release times and one late completion; compare drain-then-replace plus an early audit. **Old-task retention check.** Run ordinary A and installed C during overlap and finish both B jobs after actual handover, including the late job.

### 05. Repair in a direction invisible to the residual decision — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Among natural batches improving the bad route, choose one whose repair component moves nuisance state but preserves a separation margin for the other route in the actual public read. Reject a direction that collapses that margin after rounding. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] selects separating truthful examples and [TCM5][parent] exploits a clip knee; the candidate is a null direction for the *decision* while making independently measured repair progress. **Cheapest first test.** Two equal-gain due batches on a rotated quadratic with identical raw residual gap but different quantized gap; compare gradient projection plus best early check. **Old-task retention check.** Run rotated rare A slices, the newly repaired B read and residual B fallback on both bins, plus installed C.

### 06. Use surplus service, not spare weight norm, as repair credit — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Permit a due step to spend slack created by a different completed repair only if the surplus is common across observer aliases and can actually deliver a named, formerly unbacked job by deadline. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Rolling escrow](rolling-escrow.md) recycles backing; this credit is generated by verified restored service, not by releasing a reservation or measuring small weights. **Cheapest first test.** Two equal-norm repairs with different held-out route slack and a new job that fits only one; compare fixed escrow plus passive verification. **Old-task retention check.** Deliver both legacy B jobs and their replacement demand on time; test rare A and installed C after spending the credit.

### 07. Choose a repair that tolerates a stale measurement — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Model the ordinary report as a delayed, timestamped view and choose a due repair whose residual decision is invariant over all legal intervening updates, or hold that right unusable until the report arrives. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][signatures] transports decisions over openers; here a live repair and an unresolved hazard must both survive a delayed report under old service clocks. **Cheapest first test.** Two C queues, one report delayed by a tick, and a lawful intervening step that reverses only one residual classification; compare waiting and separate early audit. **Old-task retention check.** Run A, B and installed C at the actual old deadlines across delay, not merely at the final model.

### 08. Challenge whether the repair is only masking — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Check the proposed route after the old carrier is legally reused and remove any inference path to it; distinguish real repair from a score carried by an unchanged fallback. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#253 W2][check] already tests reuse after teaching; applying it to repair/reveal is a falsifier, not a new primitive. **Cheapest first test.** Use two equal displayed scores before reuse, one still dependent on original backing, and run the same revealed-hazard decision in both. **Old-task retention check.** Measure A/installed C, the repaired B route itself after reuse, and an earlier B job not served by either carrier.

## B. Make one report authorize a compatible joint action

### 09. Admit the bundle, not the marginals — primitive candidate (parent #23 baseline)
**Mechanism.** Bind two incoming claims at the same cut and issue a single decision over their joint use on the full observed cell. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 card 23][parent] is the seed and #04 already compiles compatible replies; [#249][signatures] groups decisions. The baseline specifically preserves correlated aliases without requiring either claim to be verified. **Cheapest first test.** J, including cancellation, sealed random checks per claim plus allocator, union of singletons, and a separate joint audit at an equal total budget. **Old-task retention check.** Serve both earlier B consumers, A and installed C on grant and refusal, then serve a later blind pair.

### 10. Certify the missing circuit — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Maintain the minimal combinations of rights whose joint use is unsafe in a cell. Authorize bundles containing none of these combinations, if feasibility is downward-closed; recompute when releases or observations change the cell. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Invariant confluence][iconf] characterizes invariant-safe merges; [#273 #04][parent] compiles safe reply menus. The candidate asks whether sparse minimal obstructions can be checked and maintained using only useful C reports under changing learner state. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three one-unit jobs and a future capacity of two; all pairs pass, the triple fails. Compare pairwise checks and exact bundle enumeration with bytes and time charged. **Old-task retention check.** Run all ordinary A/B/installed C jobs through the denied triple, serving the denied consumer via its legal fallback.

### 11. Reserve for an anonymous heavy claimant — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** If the report certifies that at most one claim is heavy but cannot identify it, back one pooled contingency that the first actual heavy draw consumes, atomically, under a published release rule. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Bounded Counter][escrow] assigns escrowed rights in advance; the candidate under test is observational backing of a genuinely fungible contingency after concurrent claims bind, not escrow itself. **Cheapest first test.** J with two admission orders and an adversarial simultaneous draw; compare fixed per-owner escrow and ordinary dynamically pooled escrow given the same information. **Old-task retention check.** Serve either heavy draw plus the other B job and verify normal A/installed C while the pool is occupied.

### 12. Certify a public recourse policy rather than a static bundle — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve a joint right only if one contingent policy, keyed to future ordinary public events before their own deadlines, serves every compatible history without changing the continuation contract. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 #23][parent] asks for a common backed decision and [#249][signatures] transports it. A recourse certificate includes which future public event can safely choose the branch instead of choosing it now from hidden state. **Cheapest first test.** Two histories require opposite orders after a due release event; test the same event after the deadline as a negative and compare static robust allocation plus audit. **Old-task retention check.** Execute A, both ordinary B routes and installed C in both realized orders and in the late-event fallback.

### 13. Resolve contested releases as well as contested spends — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Take a joint grant only across histories where any release it relies on is legally final; two notices about the same release cannot both add capacity. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Bounded Counter][escrow] protects transfer accounting and [#226](rolling-escrow.md) rolls backing; the candidate is one due read resolving whether an uncertain release can back a simultaneous multi-claim grant. **Cheapest first test.** One real release and two reports that may refer to the same prior reservation; compare explicit per-release audit plus allocator with shared identifier information. **Old-task retention check.** Keep the prior B holder served on the unresolved branch and run A/installed C while the new B pair is refused or admitted.

### 14. Check the cancellation surface, not just the nominal sum — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Enumerate coupled signs, saturation and rounding at actual reporter precision, and return a reachable safe/harmful collision if any makes the proposed grant unsound. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 #20/#24][parent] test real-optimizer and aggregation collisions; here the negative is collusion over a bundle, not a new checking method. **Cheapest first test.** Flip signs in J and compare the signed report, the native absolute report if already lawful, and a separate joint audit. **Old-task retention check.** Keep A, each earlier B and installed C on every cancellation branch, and verify the refused pair does not consume shared backing.

### 15. Support unequal deadlines with a joint decision diagram — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Keep a residual diagram of which joint rights remain possible after each deadline: an already spent grant remains a liability, an expired unspent request does not. Give a short-deadline singleton only if the frozen policy does not promise a now-impossible later pair. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#249][signatures] groups decisions by observable history and [#273 #17][parent] tracks evidence dependencies; this object tracks compatible future grants as live liabilities change. **Cheapest first test.** Swap two deadlines around one due report; compare earliest-deadline-first plus exact allocator and a separate check at equal cost. **Old-task retention check.** Run earlier B reservations to completion, the later consumer's fallback, and A/installed C at both deadlines.

### 16. Check whether correlated arrivals are real, not assumed — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Compare the reachable joint set from common optimizer/task lineage to its Cartesian marginal hull; never exclude correlations on the basis of two claimants' agreement alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] tracks reachable hidden slots; this is the same soundness test for two histories, not a new dependence model. **Cheapest first test.** Generate two short branches from a shared prefix, then allow an extra legal branch that realizes a previously excluded high-high state; compare exact reachable enumeration and marginal bounds. **Old-task retention check.** Run A/installed C and both earlier B consumers on the added branch with joint refusal and fallback.

## C. Couple repair and joint admission, then try to kill the coupling

### 17. Spend newly repaired service only on a jointly verified bundle — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** One due step restores capacity to an old B route and distinguishes which combinations of incoming claims can safely share it; reserve against the *worst residual demand in the repaired cell*. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 #03/#23][parent] specify the two moves separately; [Bounded Counter][escrow] allocates known rights. The proposed interaction is that the same legal choice of C transition changes both robust capacity and the observed joint demand. **Cheapest first test.** Combine R and J in a tiny native-optimizer queue: one due order repairs but aliases the pair, another reveals but does not repair in time, and a third attempts both. Compare the exact best separate stack with allowed report reuse, and reject the win if it can use that third step at equal cost. **Old-task retention check.** Serve earlier B independently before repair, then both admitted or refused consumers, rare A and installed C across a blind queue swap.

### 18. Release only the liabilities the repair really removes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After a repaired B route is live, remove its call on a shared fallback pool only where existing borrowers have a common safe handover. In the same report cell, keep any correlated call from the residual hazard in the joint budget. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) clears obligations and can retire receipts; this tests whether the repair changes a *combination* of possible fallback calls without eliminating each call individually. **Cheapest first test.** Three reachable histories with the same marginal calls but different worst concurrent loads; compare separate clearance plus pooled allocator and a negative with a still-live borrower. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise two pre-existing B call paths simultaneously at handover, and run A/installed C through refusal and later reuse.

### 19. Maintain the frontier of safe partial repairs — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Track nondominated outcomes in three coordinates: B route slack restored, residual joint bundles backed, and useful C gain. Commit only a robust point before the earliest reliance; refuse the rest rather than averaging slack across aliases. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273][parent] asks for matched C progress or a Pareto tradeoff; the candidate inserts jointly feasible *bundles*, not just counts of separately certified rights, into the transition frontier. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three due orders where equal numbers of safe singletons mask different jointly safe bundles; compare a scalar utility heuristic and the exact same-information scheduler. **Old-task retention check.** Run both B consumers, rare A and installed C for each reported Pareto point, including the low-slack one.

### 20. Use a repair to shrink a joint obstruction without hiding it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Remove a minimal unsafe bundle only after actual restored service makes that bundle safe; require any remaining minimal unsafe bundles needed for upcoming decisions to remain checkable or independently backed. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] preserves evidence before shrinking and [invariant confluence][iconf] reasons about unsafe merges; here repair changes which unsafe combinations exist without treating loss of evidence as resolution. **Cheapest first test.** Two overlapping minimal obstructions on three rights; test a repair that resolves one but aliases the other against audit-before-repair plus allocator. **Old-task retention check.** Exercise all old B jobs in the affected overlap, with A/installed C measured after the late competing grant is refused.

### 21. Pass a repair cut across a second independently owed wave — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Allow the next cohort to reuse only a certified *current* pool and residual obligations, not unused grant capacity from a prior report. Price the retained boundary state and recheck if second-wave obligations change the set of legal suffixes. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Rolling escrow](rolling-escrow.md) rolls finite backing and [#273 #18][parent] caps evidence; this tests repeated repair/reveal decisions with previously correlated rights still live. **Cheapest first test.** Three adaptive waves where two previous rights overlap the next late demand, plus a fourth blind success; compare fresh reserves and TCM5 plus best pooling at equal bytes. **Old-task retention check.** Serve the oldest A and B panels and installed C on every wave, specifically exercising the still-live first-wave pair.

### 22. Find a finite impossibility witness for the merged operation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Return a tree of public cuts where every safe, due repair either leaves a reachable mixed cell for the named bundle or misses reliance; report the lightest change that removes an obstruction (scope, clock or lawful extra evidence). **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 #22][parent] gives a separator obstruction; this extends it to transitions that may really eliminate one hazard and shrink the set of *joint* obligations, not just lose information. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaustively solve two tiny menus, one with a lawful successful repair/reveal order and one with an unavoidable post-repair collision; cross-check the witness against the exact planner. **Old-task retention check.** Run normal A/B/installed C via independent fallback in the impossible menu and after the successful grant in the other.

### 23. Price the best decoupled reuse of the very same report — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Let separate repair and verification modules consume the same ordinary C observation, coordinate their scheduling and share the allocator. Charge computation and retained evidence rather than imposing a duplicated optimizer pass. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273][parent] already requires a strong stack; this is the direct falsifier of claims that using one step or one report is inherently new. **Cheapest first test.** Apply both algorithms to identical tiny queues, repeat with a genuine independent audit cost and report where the same-information modules tie. **Old-task retention check.** Match A/B/installed C at each exposed cut and reject a speedup obtained by dropping a fallback or changing its deadline.

### 24. Move from hand-aligned coordinates to blind role swaps — improvement/control
**Mechanism.** Generate rotated and permuted tiny learner streams before choosing the step; identify the repair and residual hazard with frozen held-out service, not coordinate identity. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 #20][parent] verifies real optimizer paths and [GEM][gem] supplies a retention control. This is a generalization falsifier for the merged construction, not a new architectural primitive. **Cheapest first test.** CPU linear features, native momentum and a fixed reporter, with blinded A/B role swaps and at least one repair that hides the other hazard; compare the calibrated passive stack. **Old-task retention check.** Re-run normal held-out A, earlier B and installed C before and after the swap, including the longest declared suffix and the losing consumer's fallback.

### 25. Preserve the correlation between repair yield and pair demand — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When the repair fills the same fungible pool the pair will use, certify the minimum of capacity minus total demand over each *joint* cell, not minimum capacity minus maximum demand drawn from different histories. Require the posted policy to spend only at realized draws and to hold back already committed capacity. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Bounded Counter][escrow] enforces numeric reserves, and [#273 #23][parent] retains joint uncertainty over claimants; the extension preserves coupling between the realized repair yield and concurrent demand. **Cheapest first test.** Same report for (capacity, demand) = (2,2) and (3,3); then add reachable (2,3). Compare separate envelopes and the exact joint allocator. **Old-task retention check.** Run ordinary A, earlier B and installed C at both yields, including an old B draw just before capped new demand and the refusal in the added history.

### 26. Back a joint grant with a common route matching — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When restored service is nonfungible, certify a matching from a candidate bundle to named routes that works for every alias of the same report. If aliases need different matchings, grant only the common subset unless a timely public event legally selects recourse. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#273 #04][parent] accounts for shared capacity and [Verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) substitutes backing; this tests one due report certifying the *assignment* to a mix of repaired and untouched routes, not just spare units. **Cheapest first test.** Two jobs and two routes, where each hidden history has a perfect matching but the matchings are disjoint; add a repair that creates a common edge and compare exact feasible matching plus lawful audit. **Old-task retention check.** Execute both old B routes at their real latencies in the common assignment and run A/installed C on the swapped and refused paths.

### 27. Allow a complementary pair only under atomic commitment — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For rights that have a required companion, test the whole prepare/commit policy, including abort: one due repair can make an atomic paired use safe even though an isolated half-use would violate a floor. Never expose the half-grant as an individually spendable right. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Atomic commit and [invariant confluence][iconf] are existing transaction tools; [#273 #23][parent] blocks competing combinations. The candidate targets non-downward-closed learner rights where the useful repair changes the result of a synchronized pair rather than simply increasing capacity. **Cheapest first test.** Two pending operations with opposing effects on a held-out margin; enumerate prepared, committed and aborted paths with a dropped companion and compare a standard atomic protocol plus separate audit. **Old-task retention check.** Serve legacy B and ordinary A/installed C through preparation delay and abort, and verify neither new B half is exposed early.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does joint step selection buy timely backed service beyond a repair, a lawful check and a good allocator that already share the same report?** Start with 17 and 23, using 01 and 09 as positive/negative baselines. Only advance if matched C gain, actual old service, clocks and accounting leave an identifiable advantage under blind queue swaps; an exact same-information tie without a planning advantage is a stop.
2. **How often can real repair survive the declared suffix while leaving residual joint harm visible at native precision?** Use 02, 05, 14 and 24. Map repair slack against the residual safe/harmful report overlap; separately measure extension beyond the causal cut, signed cancellation, rounding and the best lawful passive statistic.
3. **What is the smallest sound object for concurrent backing: a bound, minimal obstruction, or public recourse policy?** Use 10–13, 15, 22 and 25–27. Start in finite queues where exact robust admission is computable. Measure what sound compression costs in lost lawful service and bytes; include a higher-order failure that every pair misses and a complementary pair for which downward closure fails.
4. **Can repeated repair replenish shared backing without sacrificing old service or accumulating decision state?** Use 04, 18 and 21. Compare repeated defaults plus a blind successful cohort to fresh reserves and rolling escrow; require actual reuse and continued normal A/B/installed C service under overlapping liabilities.

Closest prior art is in-repo: [#273][parent], [#253][check], [#249][signatures] and [#232][shrink]. The external comparisons bound the claims: [invariant confluence][iconf] covers safe combinations of operations, [Bounded Counter][escrow] protects numeric reserves by escrow, [Proof-of-Learning][pol] is a separate segment-verification control, and [GEM][gem] is a remembered-task constraint baseline. These are scoped comparisons, not claims of priority or results.
