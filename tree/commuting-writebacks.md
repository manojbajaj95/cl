# Commuting writebacks: learn the shared read, spend the time where it matters

Child of [Successor-funded returns](successor-funded-returns.md) ([PR #206][parent]). This follows the two score-3 cards in the [review][triage]: #21, learn a C receiver on which jointly called writebacks commute for the live reads; #12, spend the time saved by C's new read on a later lawful observation or a wider release window. Ideas and paper fixtures only; no experiments or GPU work.

**Bet.** C is a real new skill trained on truthful data before the return demand is known. Its computation should do something an equally useful, separately routed C cannot: remove a writeback dependency, shorten a binding step on the path from evidence to launch, or both. Neither order-independence by itself (observational commutativity is old) nor deciding to wait for information (contingent planning is old) is the claim. The two seeds can succeed independently; test them separately before claiming a joint effect.

## What would count

- **Keep the contract fixed.** Start after a real spend. Freeze both repair payloads, their success tests, the admissible observation sources, and the service/load distribution. A receiver binding may change how a payload is realized; it may not redefine success, make a live read unobservable, or ship a no-op. Compare at matched C utility and margins, truthful C data, resident/peak bytes, and total work. Charge training, checking, waiting, speculation, and reservations to whoever incurs them. Evaluate blind histories, co-calls and late outcomes.
- **Commutation is about useful results, not just final scores.** Let `W₁,W₂` be the bound writebacks, `s` a reachable joint prefix and `F` any admitted continuation after both calls. Require `R_F(F(W₂(W₁(s)))) = R_F(F(W₁(W₂(s))))` on declared live reads. `R_F` includes replies matched by caller rather than arrival order, live optimizer/lease checks when relevant, and ordinary skill readouts. In both orders each required prefix read and witness must also be correct for the work completed at that prefix. A reader between commits need not see identical states, but it cannot see an invalid trace or miss a promised deadline. The public policy must work across all reachable history/order pairs and may use only public information. A suffix that exposes a hidden difference forces a narrower, explicitly enforced scope or rejection. For approximate contracts predeclare error margins and a finite suffix horizon or an invariant; check per-read worst cases, not the mean.
- **Don't sell a swap as parallelism.** A proof can remove a precedence edge without permitting arbitrary low-level interleavings. To claim concurrency, show an implementation with atomic publish or checked micro-interleavings, no clobber, and one physically feasible joint reservation. Otherwise report only the order/launch freedom actually gained. Price comparison: fastest safe serialization, [safe order families][orders], orthogonal write separation ([OGD][ogd]/[GPM][gpm]), equal-C without order shaping, batch/gradient accumulation where applicable, paid checkpoint/replay, and exact joint search on the same action menu. Include the best affordable stack, not just each part alone.
- **Measure the latest legal evidence cutoff.** Hold the deadline, availability law, witness runtime and safety floor fixed. Compare the full public policy to the best [split-clock][split] and contingent schedule, including lawful early work, caching, reservations and same-cost acceleration. In a simple fixed critical path with deadline `D` and post-observation time `p`, the observation must be usable by `D − p`; on a DAG, measure that cutoff under resource contention and all admitted branches. Count extra correct calls at the same deadline and acquisition utility, or reduced refusal/expected regret along a continuous arrival sweep. Seconds cut on a nonbinding path buy nothing. Free/made-early evidence is an upper bound, not a fair online control.
- **Retention in every first test.** A/B/C are ordinary held-out tasks, with per-skill floors from acquisition, including rare route-sensitive inputs and C inputs that actually use the shared read. Measure at deployed acquisition, waiting and write prefixes, after both writes, and after an unassisted suffix. On refusal, score the unchanged service trajectory separately. The [ledger][ledger] rules exclude ties with matched baselines, results true by construction, and wins attributable to margins or a simulator cliff. “Primitive candidate” names a reusable operation worth falsifying, not a novelty verdict; improvements below are controls or audits, not standalone pilots.

## Two paper fixtures (not evidence for a primitive)

**W1 · A commuting read need not live in a commuting state.** Let the state be `(x,r,y,h)`. A legitimate C regression reads `sθ = x − θr`; truthful targets `x − r` on independent variation of `x,r` identify `θ = 1`. Suppose the frozen receiver API permits its second writeback to consume that scalar. Let `W₁` add nonzero `a` to both `x,r`, and `W₂` add `b + sθ` to `y` and the current `x` to `h`. Subtract the final state for order 2→1 from that for 1→2: `Δy = a(1 − θ)`, `Δh = a`, and `Δx = Δr = 0`. At `θ = 1` the final `(x,y,s₁)` agree; a third pulse `y ← y + h` recovers an order gap of `a`. Explicitly test prefix correctness too: `W₁` must preserve C's read, and `W₂`'s increment in `y` must be identical wherever it runs. The linear invariant is available by inspection or projection, so this is a positive/negative checker fixture, not a win over hand-built separation. A later nonlinear host must beat that control without dropping the third pulse from a promised scope.

The fixture changes what the fixed API binds, not what an evaluator elects to see. A bijective relabeling alone cannot help: transforming the writes and tails by the same change of basis and pulling reads back through its inverse leaves every read of both traces unchanged. Compare against that null before attributing a win to C acquisition.

**W2 · Identical seconds, different eligibility.** Let the common deadline be 8 s and the final repair take 1 s. A public bit selects between repairs with no common admissible reply. On the toy's allowed menu, only one branch-specific prepared receiver fits at a time, and a wrong guess cannot be used for the other branch. If branch-bound preparation takes 4 s after the bit is available, the latest guaranteed evidence time is 3 s; if C does 2 s of that preparation through an already-required read, it is 5 s. A bit at 5 s thus changes guaranteed feasibility. Saving the same 2 s on a separate lane that already finishes before the bit leaves the cutoff at 3 s. Uniform bit arrival over [2, 7] s gives timely coverage of 1/5 versus 3/5 under these fixed post-bit policies, without a point-mass deadline cliff. An exact scheduler with common staging, speculative guessing and early reservation is the real control; if it already serves the same calls at matched resources, there is no gain. Check A/B/C while waiting and at launch, including a rare old task that needs the only receiver.

## Idea cards

### 01. Teach the shared receiver to remove an actual edge — primitive candidate (#206 #21)
**Mechanism.** Use C's normal task loss plus a development-only order-pair loss to learn a receiver feature that C needs and both frozen repair contracts can bind. A checked swap drops one real precedence edge without hiding either writeback. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** The [commutativity rule][scr] exploits interface symmetries, [OGD][ogd]/[GPM][gpm] separate updates, and [Lie-bracket diagnostics][bracket] predict order effects. The candidate operation is lawful C acquisition that makes an otherwise useful edge removable on unseen co-calls. **Cheapest first test.** Verify W1, then use a two-gate host with held-out pairings; compare exact serialization, projection, an equally useful unshaped C, and their best stack. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C after either first write, both completions, and a third pulse; the hidden-coordinate pulse must revoke the positive certificate.

### 02. Cancel a nonlinear interaction without partitioning the receiver — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Fit C to expose a piecewise invariant for two writes that each touch the same units. On adjacent reachable activation regions, tie the cross-write response with opposite signed contributions, rather than making either contribution zero. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Joint releases][joint] and [cancelling counterweights][counter] cancel damage on a joint release; this would cancel the difference between two individually valid, live sequences, with the same shared C feature serving both. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate the regions of a three-ReLU rational host, then hold out one boundary-crossing pair; compare exact piecewise projection, a fixed cancelling template, and separate receivers. **Old-task retention.** Probe A/B/C on each side of the kink after either first write and after a boundary-recrossing suffix.

### 03. Make the unread difference stay unread — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Learn a compact C read while checking that every callable suffix action preserves equivalence on it or explicitly invalidates the permission before execution. Include the action's return and availability in the equivalence, so a hidden branch condition is not silently forgotten. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Bisimulation representations][bisim] already learn behavior-preserving quotients. The bet is a bounded, reusable writeback binding that passes suffix closure and makes acquired C service cheaper than learning the quotient, caching and scheduling separately. **Cheapest first test.** Partition-refine a small exact transition system with two branch exits: a re-reader and an inert tail. Hold out re-reader compositions and compare a learned decoder, the exact quotient, and a bounded explicit trace cache. **Old-task retention.** Demand ordinary A/B/C on both exits, including a decision that only the hidden read can flip.

### 04. Remove the echo from a live optimizer slot — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let C share a directional statistic with the two writebacks. Train the binding so differences in optimizer slots either wash out before the next consuming step or enter that step through an invariant combination; neither slot can be reset or ignored by decree. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Slot splices][slots] transport and check optimizer state; [Lie-bracket diagnostics][bracket] measure order effects. The new operation would learn a read that makes two non-equal slot states interchangeable for continued useful updates. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two heavy-ball prefixes with matched weights and differing momentum, then run the same lawful third batch; compare slot reset, full slot transport, clock-matched accumulation and OGD. Include a batch that changes the preconditioned direction and breaks the invariant. **Old-task retention.** Measure A/B/C at equal weights, after the third update, and after a longer common suffix.

### 05. Distill a current-state correction, not a gauge change — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Expose a C feature that cheaply predicts the correction needed to apply a frozen writeback's logical increment to the *current* receiver, regardless of which other write already ran. Admit the correction only as an allowed receiver binding with both witness targets unchanged. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Net2Net][net] and conjugating both transitions by a bijection do not change the observable commutator; commutative replicated datatypes already compile logical operations. The test is whether ordinary C learning amortizes a nontrivial conversion on new receivers better than a prebuilt conversion table. **Cheapest first test.** Compare exact pair forks in a finite nonlinear receiver, pure relabeling, a same-byte correction cache, and a fixed commutative encoding; hold out a payload pair. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B/C for corrections in either direction and after a tail that consumes the corrected receiver.

### 06. Let abort commute with the other caller's success — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Factor the receiver into C-derived sufficient statistics that let one caller drop just its own contribution after a lawful abort, with the already-committed other payload still readable. A dropped obligation must not release a lease somebody else still uses. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Sagas][sagas] use compensations and transactional commutativity is established; the candidate is a learned sufficient read that supports cheap local compensation without a write log or checkpoint at the same bytes. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two commit orders and both single aborts with one shared receiver; compare compensating log, exact undo/replay and two disjoint allocations. Include a noninvertible saturation case requiring refusal. **Old-task retention.** Score A/B/C before abort, immediately after it, and after the successful caller's return is read a second time.

### 07. Buy a real roundoff margin with C — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train C's live receiver to leave room around rounding and decision boundaries so the *executed* pair admits both orderings at deployed precision. Bound accumulated discrepancy over a declared burst; do not round audit outputs to hide it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Reproducible accumulators and quantization-aware training already address ordering and precision separately; the candidate is a usefully learned receiver whose certified order slack avoids a wider accumulator at the same success floor. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate small fixed-point updates across a paired-threshold grid; compare exact/compensated accumulation, ordinary quantization-aware C, and a held-out sequence that drives drift to the margin. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B/C near every affected rounding boundary after the longest admitted burst and the next normal update.

### 08. Preserve a reader between the writes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Train a C read that separates a job's already committed fields from pending fields using the same bounded receiver. A public completion mask exposes whichever has finished, with no stale read of the other. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Versioned reads and [scoped commutativity][scr] already handle intermediate observations; test whether a learned sufficient carrier provides the prefix service of multi-version storage without another version. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust two commit orders crossed with an intervening reader on each possible field; compare whole-pair atomic publish, a versioned two-slot baseline, and exact serial planning with a middle read. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C at both intermediate states, including a task that needs a completed field while the other is still pending.

### 09. Admit a swap without knowing which history is live — improvement
**Mechanism.** Bind one guarded swap against all still-reachable history/order corners; release it only on public evidence. Report the smallest actual corner at which a common binding fails rather than changing it with a hidden history label. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Safe order families][orders] and [order handoffs][handoffs] already certify common rules on reachable products; specialize their checker to C-defined receivers and compare verification costs. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two slot histories and two swap orders with a single failing cross corner, then plant a structurally identical but unreachable corner as a negative control. Compare cached exact search and per-history planning. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C for each admitted corner and on fallback for the reachable rejected corner.

### 10. Grow an order family by closing reachable diamonds — improvement
**Mechanism.** Check each adjacent swap at its reachable prefix, including replay of the contractually live suffix; use the checked edges to connect safe schedules, not to extrapolate from the initial pair to all permutations. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Partial-order reduction and [safe order families][orders] already use local independence. The improvement would compress recurring proofs for C-conditioned adjacent swaps without relying on pairwise checks at the origin. **Cheapest first test.** Use three writes for which every initial pair passes but a prefix arms a re-reader and breaks one later swap. Compare full enumeration, cached checking, and static conflict-graph reduction. **Old-task retention.** Score A/B/C on both ends of each admitted swap and on the planted third-write failure path.

### 11. Certify a finite burst without promising repeatability — improvement
**Mechanism.** Budget latent order discrepancies that can stay below any live tolerance for one pair yet accumulate over repeated co-calls. Use a reachable-state bound, including sign alignment and a charge/release rule; refresh only with a proved contraction. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Numerical reachability and [merge-aware budgets][budgets] already accumulate scoped error; the improvement is a bound on *when* this particular commute permission must expire. **Cheapest first test.** Two scalar hidden modes with identical one-pair error and respectively aligned and contracting residuals; compare exact enumeration, a naive per-pair threshold, and a sum-of-errors bound. **Old-task retention.** Probe A/B/C after each pair, at the expiration boundary and after a suffix that amplifies the hidden mode.

### 12. Spend the removed preparation on the missing public bit — primitive candidate (#206 #12)
**Mechanism.** Use C's necessary computation to replace a branch-bound preparation step on the path from observation to launch. Hold the unchanged repair until a later lawful bit selects it, instead of simply finishing an already-feasible repair early. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Split-timed deadlines][split] and contingent planning account for later starts already. The candidate is a binding step eliminated by useful C acquisition, with eligibility exceeding the best ordinary C plus contingent planner. **Cheapest first test.** Start with W2; compare free evidence, exact paid scheduling, equal seconds saved off-path, separate C and fast checker arms. Sweep arrival and preparation continuously after the integer fixture. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C throughout the delay and after the first time-critical write; include a conflicting old reader near the deadline.

### 13. Stage one branch-neutral factor and bind late — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Have C learn a factor that both branches need, so its normal read can prepare one branch-neutral packet while the chooser is unknown. Once the bit is public, attach only the selected residual and discard neither an installed skill nor a live competing caller. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Partial evaluation and [joint release openings][counter] already exploit shared prefixes. The candidate is a factor acquired by ordinary C that transfers after a real spend to blind branch pairs, at a cost where cached full or common-prefix plans do not tie. **Cheapest first test.** Two branches with an unseen pair of small signed residuals and one packet slot; compare hand-factored exact planning, full deferral and dual speculation. Include branches with no common factor. **Old-task retention.** Retest A/B/C with the unbound packet resident, with each binding, and on timeout without a binding.

### 14. Move only the launch clock, not the apparent failure clock — improvement
**Mechanism.** Separate the time a receiver can still legally bind from the later time a missed repair becomes visible. Map the release interval as its early and late boundaries move independently, and spend the learned read only on the boundary it physically changes. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Split-timed deadlines][split] already separates these clocks; this is an attribution audit for the later-start claim. **Cheapest first test.** Cross a receiver-pin time and an output failure deadline in a tiny scheduling DAG, with same-cost read savings on three different edges. Compare exact dynamic scheduling and a single-budget heuristic. **Old-task retention.** Test A/B/C just before and after both boundaries, including a safe refusal after the earlier cutoff.

### 15. Buy a second lawful observation, not a bigger confidence number — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Spend C-earned critical-path slack on one more independently sourced observation only if some reachable outcome changes the admissible reply. Use an anytime-valid decision rule when stopping is adaptive; uncertainty that cannot affect a timely action is not worth buying. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Decision-relevant queries][query] and sequential decision theory already price information; test a reusable coupling between a skill-produced timing credit and a strict increase in safely classifiable calls. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-stage noisy public signal with symmetric early ambiguity, one late decisive branch and one irrelevant signal of equal duration. Compare optimal sequential choice with ordinary C, early observations of equal cost and free evidence. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B/C through both sampling stages and on both false-alarm/refusal and launched trajectories.

### 16. Borrow a packet from ordinary C serving, not from hidden preprocessing — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reuse a bounded intermediate naturally produced by C calls to bypass preparation on later return requests. Bind by freshness and public input provenance; the scheduler has to fall back when C was idle or ran on an incompatible input. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Memoization and [successor recovery][recovery] already reuse state. The proposed operation is its acquisition-shaped reuse on blind lawful jobs precisely where it changes the evidence cutoff, not cache hit rate alone. **Cheapest first test.** Three arrival traces with mandatory, optional and absent C traffic; compare equal-byte memoization, prewarm-at-a-price and exact scheduling on the same availability law. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C under heavy reuse and after a long idle span; test stale-packet refusal without blocking ordinary C.

### 17. Widen a release interval under uncertain durations — improvement
**Mechanism.** Report the set of public times at which a dynamically executable controller can still bind. Charge any extra reservation required to turn mean latency savings into worst-case release width. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Dynamic controllability in temporal networks with uncertainty already handles variable durations; the contribution is a stricter measurement of which part of that interval lawful C changed. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two observation lags crossed with two bind durations and a shared lock; compare the optimal nonanticipating schedule, a mean-duration planner, and a sham C with matched mean savings but no tail improvement. **Old-task retention.** Probe A/B/C immediately after the slowest admitted observation and across the longest lock hold.

### 18. Protect old reads during the wait with the same feature — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make the C feature that shortens preparation also answer an old-route read that would otherwise preempt the shared receiver mid-wait. The ordinary old read remains live, so buying time cannot amount to taking A offline. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Successor recovery][recovery] and standard multi-task caching already share computations; the bet is a transferable joint witness that simultaneously removes contention and creates decision-relevant waiting time at unchanged peak capacity. **Cheapest first test.** Inject a held-out burst of A reads before the bit and a B read at release; compare a dedicated A port, an equal-byte cache with exact priorities and a separately routed C. **Old-task retention.** Report A latency and task accuracy during the burst, plus B/C floors at launch and after both repairs.

### 19. Delay paying for a receiver lock until the calls commit — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** Where learned commutation removes only a precedence edge, keep serving ordinary repairs while waiting for the bit; take the one receiver lease only when committing to the admitted branch. Keep atomic publish and verify there is no new clobber during the shorter hold. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Optimistic concurrency control and [joint reservations][joint] already defer commitment. The joint claim is that useful C both removes the edge and makes a paid online lock later than exact contingent scheduling with an unshaped C. **Cheapest first test.** Cross shared vs separate C with serialized vs checked-swap writes; inject another caller during the hold, including one whose extra read revokes the swap. Compare optimistic retry, static locks and exact joint search. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C with the competing caller and on the abort/retry path, not only on the winning schedule.

### 20. Let the observation narrow the read scope, not excuse stale reads — primitive candidate (joint)
**Mechanism.** A lawful public event can retire a future-reader obligation. Only after the event and before the shortened cutoff may a C-derived quotient authorize a swap that the still-live reader would distinguish. On unknown or negative outcomes, preserve the full contract. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Conditional liveness and [safe order families][orders] already narrow obligations after evidence; the candidate is a learned receiver where this lawful narrowing and the cut in post-event work are both needed to admit blind pairs. **Cheapest first test.** Adapt W1 so the pulse is live unless an authorized release arrives; sweep event time with and without the learned shortcut. Compare exact branch scheduling, early observation, and static proof masks. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B/C against the pulse when live, on a missing-release timeout, and on the retired branch with an independent ordinary C user.

### 21. Force repeated timing credits to compete for the same world clock — improvement
**Mechanism.** Keep a ledger of actual channel, verifier and receiver occupancy over successive requests. Two later observations cannot both spend the same reclaimed worker interval; charge displaced work even when each return is individually faster. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Resource-constrained scheduling and the parent's debt audit already prevent double spending; test whether the single-call gain survives distributional load rather than claim a novel currency. **Cheapest first test.** Three releases share a verifier and one bit source; vary phase until two savings overlap and compare rolling exact scheduling, isolated-call accounting and no waiting. **Old-task retention.** Run A/B/C after each acquisition and on the longest queued call; score any old task pushed past its own deadline.

### 22. Ask which noncommutation actually prices out information — improvement (joint audit)
**Mechanism.** Reinsert each removed precedence edge or preparation step in turn, holding C outputs and utilization fixed, and recompute which public observations are still actionable. Report the smallest set of changes needed for each newly served job; shared slack has one owner, not several. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Critical-path sensitivity and [decision-relevant querying][query] give the ingredients; this audits whether a putative two-seed interaction is more than ensembling independent speedups. **Cheapest first test.** Use a four-arm exact scheduler with neither change, each alone, and both; include an off-path edge and a pair of individually insufficient but jointly sufficient changes. Charge identical feasible early staging to each arm. **Old-task retention.** Match A/B/C floors in every arm, with an old read timed to become critical under restoration of the edge.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does truthful C acquisition create a transferable swap permission, or just discover one?** Start with 01–04. Freeze the witnesses and live-read contract; hold out payload pairs and continuation readers. A nonzero raw-state gap is allowed, but an admitted suffix must not recover it. Stop if projection, a fixed quotient, or a matched separate C plus exact scheduling buys the same safe order freedom at equal cost and retention.
2. **What is the cheapest evidence that C paid for a later decision?** Use 12–15 to find the latest usable public observation for each arm, then compare safe coverage and refusal over the same arrival distribution. Require a held-out case whose reply genuinely depends on that evidence. A free-observation oracle, off-path speedup and exact speculative schedule separate information value, location of savings and actual acquisition value.
3. **Can a bounded certificate survive arbitrary admitted tails without becoming a shadow checkpoint?** Use 03/04/07/10/11 to distinguish an exact quotient invariant from a finite numerical lease. Ask which live slot, guard or third write first separates the orders, and whether a compact refusal rule beats storing and checking the full state at the same coverage. Count scope lost and retained skills, not certificate bytes alone.
4. **When are the two bets complementary rather than interchangeable?** Run 19–22 against the best combined control on blind co-calls. Factor the gain into removed precedence, reduced post-evidence work and changed lock occupancy. A joint primitive is plausible only if the frozen shared read is required for a new safe service region that neither seed alone nor the verified baseline stack achieves, including under repeated spend and unassisted retention.
