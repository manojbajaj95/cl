# Agent self-curriculum forgetting: when the agent picks what to practice, the pick is the forgetting control

Parent: the ledger's killed **learning-progress self-curricula for deployed agents** (Agent 9, PR #7: "ALP-GMM and self-play curricula cover it") and [PR #115](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/115) sub-question 2 (forgetting the learner causes through its own traffic). **Node question:** agents choose their own next tasks, and that choice decides what they forget. Treat task selection as the continual-learning control and look for primitives where forgetting is shaped by what the agent decides to practice, not by the update rule.

**Short answer.** The parent approach fails because every self-curriculum signal in use (learning progress, absolute LP, mastering rate, lowest competence, regret) rewards the *gain from a practice episode*, and once a stream is stationary that gain is all recovery of what the agent let decay since its last visit. In the CPU probe below, 100% of second-half practice gain is recovery for every policy, and all four standard selectors retain *less* than uniform sampling (mean competence −0.047 to −0.081, worst task −0.08 to −0.13). Meanwhile the retention vector at stationarity is set by the practice mixture, not the update rule: changing LR and momentum moves per-task competence by 0.006, while changing the mixture moves it by 0.216 (36×). So selection is the real control, and the signal the parent optimizes is blind to it. A one-step lookahead on *total* competence beats uniform by +0.067 mean / +0.131 worst, which bounds the headroom for a selection-only primitive.

## Root cause: learning progress pays for forgetting and cannot see the tasks it is not practicing

1. **Practice gain = drift recovery at stationarity.** Gross within-episode gain minus the net change of all tasks is the drift that happened between visits. In a stream where tasks keep competing for shared weights, net change goes to zero, so LP-style rewards measure churn, not progress. Uniform sampling collects the *most* practice gain (102 vs 30–76 for LP selectors) because it churns the most, and gain has no positive correlation with retention across runs (r = −0.10). An agent maximizing LP is paid to let tasks decay and re-learn them; it is a Goodhart target for forgetting. Mastering-rate curricula ([2008.06456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06456)) and LLM-informed curriculum consequences ([2610.04604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04604)) already note LP's local blindness, but treat it as sample inefficiency, not as a forgetting incentive.
2. **Forgetting of unpracticed tasks is unobserved and self-caused.** The agent sees a task's competence only when it practices it. Under LP selectors its view of the other tasks is off by 0.35–0.37 competence on average (uniform 0.22; lookahead 0.14). And the drift of the unseen tasks is caused by the arm it pulls, so this is not a restless bandit with independent passive dynamics (Whittle) and not the exogenous stream CL assumes: it is an *action-coupled* partially monitored process. Signals that react to observed forgetting (absolute LP, ALP-GMM [1910.07224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07224), Teacher–Student [1707.00183](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00183), Replay on Demand [2609.40089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40089)) are lagging by construction.
3. **The update rule is the wrong place to look.** For an i.i.d. practice mixture p in a convex host the iterates settle at argmin Σ p_i L_i; the update rule sets speed and noise only (probe Q2). That is why EWC-in-curriculum results ([2609.33655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33655)) find forgetting "cannot be completely eliminated" when task optima diverge: the residual is a property of the practiced measure.
4. **But the mixture is not the whole state.** Reweighting LP-chosen steps so the *expected* update equals the uniform mixture (inverse-frequency step scaling, capped at 5×) does not recover uniform's retention (−0.059 vs −0.058 for plain ALP), and a high-water-mark reward that never pays for recovery does not either (−0.054). The loss comes from *how practice is sequenced* (long runs on a few tasks and starved arms), not from the long-run mean alone. The mixture sets the mean of the retention vector; the selection sequence sets its excursions, and the worst task lives in the excursions.

**What survives.** The object to control is the *practice measure plus its sequencing*, scored on all tasks, not the next task scored on its own gain. A primitive has to (a) estimate how practicing j moves every i (a practice-response matrix G, which drifts), (b) maintain a view of unpracticed tasks without evaluating them all, and (c) choose sequences, not just frequencies. The lookahead oracle shows the reachable gain; a cheap estimator of it is the open problem.

### CPU probe ([`tree/probes/self_curriculum_probe.py`](probes/self_curriculum_probe.py), numpy, ~1 CPU-min)

Linear regression host, d = 24, K = 6 tasks. Each task draws inputs from its own random 9-dim subspace (overlapping) and has its own teacher (shared w* plus 0.9 noise), so tasks conflict. Competence C_i = 1 − L_i(w)/L_i(0). Each episode the agent picks one task and takes 5 SGD steps (lr 0.05, batch 16); it observes only the practiced task's competence. 900 episodes, 20 worlds, means with 95% CIs over the second half. LP = change in observed score between visits (Teacher–Student), windowed over the last 6 visits, ε = 0.2.

| Policy | Mean C | Worst C | Practice gain (2nd half) | Net change | Staleness of unseen tasks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform | 0.634 ± 0.033 | 0.244 ± 0.068 | 102.1 | +0.02 | 0.224 |
| Absolute LP (ALP) | 0.576 ± 0.032 | 0.140 ± 0.043 | 64.8 | −0.17 | 0.365 |
| Positive LP | 0.553 ± 0.037 | 0.112 ± 0.053 | 30.3 | +0.01 | 0.264 |
| Lowest last-seen competence | 0.587 ± 0.032 | 0.150 ± 0.050 | 76.0 | −0.11 | 0.372 |
| High-water-mark reward (new) | 0.580 ± 0.045 | 0.165 ± 0.069 | 27.3 | −0.05 | 0.249 |
| ALP choice + IPW step to uniform (new) | 0.575 ± 0.034 | 0.148 ± 0.047 | 64.0 | −0.06 | 0.354 |
| One-step lookahead on total C (oracle) | **0.701 ± 0.027** | **0.375 ± 0.101** | 60.0 | −0.01 | 0.142 |

Paired differences vs uniform: ALP −0.058 ± 0.005 mean / −0.104 ± 0.031 worst; lowest-competence −0.047 / −0.093; lookahead +0.067 ± 0.012 / +0.131 ± 0.056. Recovery share (1 − Σnet/Σgross) is 1.00 for every policy.

Q2 (5 worlds, single-step i.i.d. practice from four mixtures × three update rules, lr ∈ {0.05, 0.02}, momentum ∈ {0, 0.6}): mean absolute deviation of per-task stationary competence is 0.0059 ± 0.0016 across update rules at a fixed mixture and 0.216 ± 0.023 across mixtures at a fixed rule.

Limits: linear, convex, exactly conflicting teachers; the stationarity claim is trivial there and is the null to break in a nonlinear host. The lookahead uses true losses on all tasks and is a ceiling, not a method. No LLM or RL host was run.

## Screen and required controls

- Read [PR #6's ledger, triage and top 10](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6/files). Already killed: learning-progress self-curricula (ALP-GMM, self-play cover it), bounded-delay stability scheduling and delay-compensated gating ("scheduling, not a mechanism"), self-sealing prohibitions (hot-stove effect), Matthew-effect skill libraries. Replay timing is an improvement at most ([PR #113](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/113)); Floquet forgetting under cyclic curricula is CD3 in [PR #74](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/74); fleets are [PR #116](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/116). Ideas below are framed so the control is *selection*, not replay timing.
- Nearest work (alphaXiv discover + arXiv abstracts; abstract-only, not novelty clearance): Replay on Demand, an online curriculum that trades remaining learning potential against observed forgetting ([2609.40089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40089)); spaced repetition for continual pretraining ([2608.17530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17530)); FOREVER forgetting-curve replay ([2601.03938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03938)); MSSR ([2603.09892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09892)); training stability under adaptive UED curricula, where the curriculum couples the learner's optimization state to its future distribution ([2611.08787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08787)); LLM-anticipated curriculum consequences ([2610.04604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04604)); mastering rate ([2008.06456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06456)); task-order effects in class-incremental CL ([2211.15470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15470)); active continual learning with Fisher selection ([2501.14278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14278)); multi-task RL curricula for LLMs (PAC [2608.30528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30528), learnability priors [2608.09217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09217)); self-taught edge-of-learnability curricula ([2601.18778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18778)); self-evolving agents as continual learners ([2610.25858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858)); learned replay scheduling ([2209.08660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08660)); task affinity grouping (TAG, [2109.04617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04617)); gradient-based sample selection (GSS, [1903.08671](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08671)).
- Required controls for any selection primitive: uniform sampling and round-robin at equal practice budget; Replay on Demand-style allocation (learning potential vs observed forgetting); spaced-repetition scheduling; the one-step lookahead oracle as ceiling. Report the evaluation cost the selector spends (every probe of an unpracticed task counts as practice budget or as a label), worst-task competence over the stream, not only the end, and the new-task learning curve.

## Raw ideas

### A. Change what selection optimizes

1. **Practice measure as the CL state.** **Mechanism:** the agent controls a practice measure p over tasks (and sequencing statistics such as run length), not a next task; it learns a response surface p → stationary retention vector from a few mixtures and picks p on the Pareto front for the tasks it wants to keep. **Why it might be new:** curricula pick the next item by local gain; Q2 says the retention vector is a function of p, so the right state variable is the measure. **Cheapest first test:** in the probe, fit the surface from 6 mixtures and pick p for max-min retention; compare RoD-style allocation and uniform at equal steps. **Retention check:** worst-task competence over the second half.
2. **Net-progress reward.** **Mechanism:** reward a practice episode by the change in summed competence over *all* tasks, estimated from a rotating handful of canaries, so recovery of self-inflicted drift is netted out. **Why it might be new:** LP and its variants pay gross gain on the practiced task only; netting turns the curriculum's reward into a retention objective. Risk: close to the lookahead oracle with an estimator, could be an improvement. **Cheapest first test:** probe with 1 canary evaluation per episode; compare ALP and the oracle. **Retention check:** worst-task competence and canary-excluded held-out competence.
3. **Practice-response matrix G.** **Mechanism:** estimate G_ij = change in task i per unit practice of task j online (from the canaries in 2 or from gradient inner products), track its drift, and select by max-min over G. **Why it might be new:** task-affinity grouping (TAG) computes lookahead affinities for multi-task grouping offline; here G is the agent's own forgetting model, updated as it practices. **Cheapest first test:** probe; G from gradient dot products vs from canaries vs oracle. **Retention check:** predicted vs measured drift of unpracticed tasks; worst-task competence.
4. **Surrogate practice.** **Mechanism:** when task A can no longer be practiced (environment gone, data deleted), keep it by choosing available tasks whose G-column has a positive entry for A. **Why it might be new:** retention without replay, regularizer or access to A, purely by what else the agent chooses to do. **Cheapest first test:** linear host with A removed after phase 1; choose among 10 available tasks by estimated G_Aj; compare random and EWC. **Retention check:** A's competence (the target) and the chosen tasks' own competence.
5. **Drop certificates.** **Mechanism:** the agent may stop practicing a task only after it shows, from G, that the remaining practice measure keeps that task above a floor; otherwise it must schedule maintenance doses. **Why it might be new:** turns "stop practicing what you've mastered" (the LP default) into a checked decision with a stated floor. **Cheapest first test:** probe with certificates from estimated G; count floor violations. **Retention check:** violations of the declared floor per task.
6. **Maintenance-dose curves.** **Mechanism:** measure, per task, the minimum practice fraction that holds it above a floor given the rest of the mixture; schedule exactly that. **Why it might be new:** a per-task, mixture-conditional retention quantity rather than a forgetting curve over time (FOREVER, spaced repetition). **Cheapest first test:** sweep fractions in the probe; check that doses compose (sum of doses ≤ 1 iff all floors are feasible). **Retention check:** floors held over the stream.
7. **High-water-mark curiosity.** **Mechanism:** intrinsic reward only for exceeding a task's all-time best, so re-learning is never paid. **Why it might be new:** a churn-proof LP. **Cheapest first test:** done in the probe: it ties ALP on mean (−0.054 vs uniform) with a small worst-task gain (−0.079 vs −0.104, CIs overlap), so as stated it does not work; it needs pairing with 5 or 6. **Retention check:** worst-task competence.

### B. See the tasks you are not practicing

8. **Practice odometry.** **Mechanism:** dead-reckon the competence of unpracticed tasks from the practice log and G, without evaluating them; correct with occasional fixes. **Why it might be new:** the probe's staleness (0.36 under LP) is the selector's blind spot; forgetting curves (FOREVER) model time, not *what was practiced meanwhile*. **Cheapest first test:** probe; odometry vs last-seen vs time-decay curve, error on unseen tasks. **Retention check:** selection with odometry, worst-task competence.
9. **Action-coupled restless bandit.** **Mechanism:** formalize the agent's curriculum as a bandit whose passive arms drift as a function of the active arm, under partial monitoring; derive an index or regret bound and use it as the selector. **Why it might be new:** Whittle restless bandits assume independent passive dynamics; here forgetting couples them. A theory primitive if the bound is new. **Cheapest first test:** analytic two-arm linear case, then the probe. **Retention check:** time-averaged worst-arm competence vs Whittle index ignoring coupling.
10. **Pop-quiz adversary.** **Mechanism:** a second agent picks which old task to test, rewarded for finding forgotten tasks given the practice log; the learner must keep passing. **Why it might be new:** regret-based UED (PAIRED/ACCEL) targets the frontier; this targets the past and learns a forgetting model as a by-product. Risk: improvement over UED. **Cheapest first test:** probe; quiz picker vs random quizzes at equal quiz budget. **Retention check:** held-out competence on all tasks.

### C. Sequencing, not just frequency

11. **Excursion-bounded scheduling.** **Mechanism:** constrain the selection sequence so no task's predicted excursion below its mean exceeds a bound (limit consecutive runs on interfering tasks by G), independent of p. **Why it might be new:** finding 4: IPW to the right mean does not restore retention; the excursions do the damage. **Cheapest first test:** probe; ALP with a run-length cap derived from G vs ALP and uniform. **Retention check:** worst-task competence.
12. **Plan-aware installs.** **Mechanism:** the agent announces its next practice plan; new skills are installed into directions the planned mixture's covariance leaves alone. **Why it might be new:** protection derived from the learner's own future curriculum, not from past tasks (OGD/GPM) or a public shock schedule (PR #113 escrow). **Cheapest first test:** linear host; install with null space of planned covariance vs past-task GPM. **Retention check:** installed skill and old tasks after the plan executes.
13. **Covering composite practice.** **Mechanism:** each period, choose a few composite tasks whose practice gradients cover every old skill's protected directions with positive sign (a set cover over G). **Why it might be new:** one practice episode maintains many skills; ties to composition contracts (killed for labels, not for selection). **Cheapest first test:** tasks as sums of two base tasks; cover vs uniform base practice at equal steps. **Retention check:** each base task separately.
14. **Interleave granularity as a learned control.** **Mechanism:** learn the block length per task pair from G, short for interfering pairs, long for compatible ones. **Why it might be new:** desirable-difficulty interleaving is fixed; here granularity follows measured interference. Risk: overlaps CD3 Floquet and is likely an improvement. **Cheapest first test:** probe with block lengths {1,5,20}. **Retention check:** worst task.

### D. Self-generated tasks and RL hosts

15. **Proposer coverage as memory.** **Mechanism:** in self-play task generation (Absolute Zero-style, edge-of-learnability [2601.18778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18778)), the proposer drifts to the frontier and stops emitting old task types; constrain the proposer's distribution to keep a decaying task's mass until a drop certificate (5) clears it. **Why it might be new:** forgetting migrates from solver to proposer; the proposer's coverage is the memory. **Cheapest first test:** parameterized task family with a learned proposer and a linear solver. **Retention check:** solver competence on early task types; proposer coverage.
16. **Saturated-task signal under GRPO.** **Mechanism:** a mastered task gives zero group advantage, so practicing it is free of gradient until it starts failing; maintain it with variants hard enough to give non-degenerate groups (margin-raising perturbations) before failure. **Why it might be new:** in group-relative RL the update rule silences maintenance, so *selection alone cannot retain*; this makes practice informative pre-failure. **Cheapest first test:** a CPU bandit-GRPO simulation with K tasks and Bernoulli success logits. **Retention check:** success rates of mastered tasks over training.
17. **Choice–mixture decoupling with variance control.** **Mechanism:** let curiosity choose (for exploration) while the effective mixture is set by importance-weighted step sizes; add variance reduction (control variates from G). **Why it might be new:** it separates exploration from retention. The probe's capped IPW failed (−0.059), so the open part is the variance/excursion fix. **Cheapest first test:** probe with uncapped and G-control-variate IPW. **Retention check:** worst task; new-task learning speed.
18. **Goal-code anchoring.** **Mechanism:** autotelic agents index goals in a learned embedding; practice moves the embedding, so "re-practicing goal g" later practices a different goal. Anchor goal codes on the goals the agent chose to drop. **Why it might be new:** forgetting through the selector's own representation of what it is choosing. **Cheapest first test:** toy goal-conditioned regression with a learned goal encoder. **Retention check:** dropped goals' competence decoded with the current encoder vs the stored one.

### E. Duals and other settings

19. **Forgetting by curriculum (selection-only unlearning).** **Mechanism:** unlearn a forget set by choosing benign tasks whose G-column is most negative for it, with no gradient ascent. **Why it might be new:** unlearning methods change the loss; this changes only what is practiced, which is auditable and cannot produce ascent artifacts. **Cheapest first test:** linear host; compare gradient ascent and retain-set fine-tuning at equal steps. **Retention check:** retained tasks within a stated floor; relearning resistance.
20. **Self-shaped traffic as practice.** **Mechanism:** a deployed model shapes which users return (PR #115 §D); treat the routing or refusal policy as the practice selector and give it a retention objective via G. **Why it might be new:** the deployment policy becomes the CL control, not just a source of drift. **Cheapest first test:** user-simulator regions with abandonment; selector with vs without retention term. **Retention check:** per-region quality after updates.
21. **Retrieval as practice in agent memory.** **Mechanism:** in harness memory, what the agent retrieves is what gets reinforced (recency/usage scores, consolidation); choose retrievals by G over lessons, not by similarity alone. **Why it might be new:** retrieval-induced forgetting (cogsci C12) as a controllable selection signal. **Cheapest first test:** a usage-weighted memory with eviction; similarity-only vs G-aware retrieval. **Retention check:** recall of rarely queried lessons.
22. **Curriculum precommitment markets.** **Mechanism:** two internal "bidders" (new-task learner, retention keeper) bid practice slots using G-estimated value; the clearing price of a slot is the marginal forgetting cost. **Why it might be new:** prices practice time by forgetting caused; RoD competes samples by heuristics. Risk: equivalent to a weighted objective. **Cheapest first test:** probe; compare a fixed-weight objective. **Retention check:** worst task at matched new-task speed.
23. **Practice fingerprints instead of task labels.** **Mechanism:** the agent selects by the practice effect vector (a column of G), clustering tasks whose practice is interchangeable and treating distinct-label-same-effect tasks as one arm. **Why it might be new:** curricula select by task identity; forgetting depends on effect. **Cheapest first test:** probe with duplicated tasks under new labels. **Retention check:** worst task with the merged arm set.

## Sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Is there a selection-only retention law?** Given G and its drift, which retention vectors are reachable by choosing practice measures and sequences, and how close can a selector get to the lookahead ceiling (+0.067 mean / +0.131 worst here) with O(1) canary evaluations per episode? Ideas 1, 3, 5, 6, 8. Needs a nonlinear host where stationarity is not trivial.
2. **Action-coupled restless bandits.** What index or regret bound holds when passive arms drift as a function of the active arm under partial monitoring? A theory object no CL or bandit work states (screen needed: bandits with interference/coupled dynamics). Ideas 9, 10, 17.
3. **Why does the right mean mixture still forget?** Finding 4 says sequencing excursions dominate the worst task. Is there a measurable excursion quantity (a G-weighted run-length statistic) that predicts worst-task loss better than the mixture? Ideas 11, 14, 17.
4. **Where does forgetting go when the agent writes its own tasks?** In self-play proposer–solver loops, does forgetting migrate to the proposer, and can proposer coverage serve as memory? Ideas 15, 18.
5. **Can selection retain at all under group-relative RL?** If saturated tasks give zero gradient, maintenance needs pre-failure informative practice; what is the minimal perturbation? Idea 16.
