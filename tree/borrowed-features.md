# Borrowed-feature handbacks

**Status:** Ideas only; no training or GPU work. **Parent:** [Feature-switching installments](feature-switching-installments.md) ([PR #276][parent]), cards 03 and 05. **Review seed:** [Flood triage][triage] scores both at 3: borrow an old-task feature for Q within a registered corridor, then return it with a useful R; or use a prefix to open several independent native gradient directions. **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger].

## Can useful work borrow an old derivative, return it, and leave a change worth keeping?

A handback is stronger than replaying old logits. Before P, an old reader must measurably use the feature. P makes Q's native update depend on it; Q makes a useful change; an available, correctly labeled R does useful work of its own while returning the feature to old service. Q's gain must survive R and the next normal old write, with no saved state used to restore the model. The rank question is whether P opens independently *spendable* directions, rather than merely changing the span of a list of candidate gradients.

**Nearest prior art.** [GEM][gem] and [OGD][ogd] manage interference; [PackNet][packnet] reserves weights; [iterative teaching][teaching], [GradMatch][gradmatch] and [GraB][grab] already choose or order real training examples; [ReLU verification][reluplex] and [robust MPC][mpc] can check or plan the whole path. The proposed object is a temporary reassignment of a derivative with a useful return, or a reachable set of new directions that are jointly spendable with a return still due. Generic planning with the same inputs is an oracle, not a weak baseline. The ledger's failed return-point adapters rule out exact restore as a novelty claim, and failed read-pinning rules out checking just the returned activation. The failed plasticity reserve and switched-Gramian ideas rule out treating rank or a forecast as evidence of usable progress.

## Contract and controls

- Register the old users, task-specific service floors, path corridor, optimizer, record supply and multiplicities, legal batches, old-work arrivals, deadline and timeout before selecting a word. Establish old ownership with held-out reads and a diagnostic feature ablation or tangent intervention; a feature merely active on old inputs does not qualify. At Q, compare with a residual-matched frozen-route clone or derivative ablation, not just a model with different starting loss. These clones are diagnostic, not legal training actions.
- Use only ordinary verified records, with native gradients recomputed at each live step. Keep the parent's heavy-ball cap: even with inherited momentum `p = βm`, the new momentum `p + g` must not exceed `||p||`. Test all admitted histories and tolerated interleavings, through each record, coast and refusal. Before calling an R useful, require a predeclared improvement on its own objective and score its held-out utility separately from the handback. Define return by old read, feature dependence and response to the next unmodified old batch, not identical weights. Check held-out Q gain both after R and after that old batch.
- For rank, compare gradients at a common live state using matched residuals as a diagnostic, then measure the native effect on registered seal axes. Assess all legal **words**: rank two is neither two available steps nor two usable signs. Even a nonzero gradient is cap-legal only if `2 p·g + ||g||² ≤ 0`; a legal first step can make the second direction unusable. Require two independent *realized* advances, neither supplied by P or coast alone, and check that the return still fits. Include a rank-two pool with no feasible joint spend.
- Charge the same record availability, bytes, search and certification cost, deadline and old service; report absolute P/Q/R utility and worst-client, worst-prefix old retention separately. Strong controls are a free-feature switch, a same-budget dedicated spare, frozen old features, GEM/OGD plus teaching and legal accumulation, reset plus best lawful word, and exact native sequence search with the same information. Count extra spare parameters and checkpoint bytes rather than giving them away. Direct slot writes and access to hidden histories are diagnostic ceilings, not deployable comparisons.

## Idea cards

### A. Borrow and return an actual old derivative

#### 01. Return a derivative with useful work — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** P routes Q through an old reader's feature; R, drawn from already due work, restores old routing and update response without undoing Q's held-out gain. **Novelty vs nearest.** Restates [#276][parent] card 03; [PackNet][packnet] protects or claims capacity, rather than temporarily authorizing a native derivative and making ordinary work return it. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate P/Q/R on a two-unit teacher stream against freeze, GEM/OGD, a free-feature switch and a budget-matched spare. Include a label-correct R that can return the route only by erasing Q. **Old-task retention.** Hold out A/B/installed C throughout, then run a normal write that exercises the borrowed feature.

#### 02. Return the old update law, not the old answer — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Define handback by equivalence of the old reader's next-step response over a registered family of old batches; let two parameter states qualify even if their raw features differ. **Novelty vs nearest.** The ledger's read-pinning failure and Jacobian matching already warn about downstream derivatives; the operation here is settling an actual temporary use with a useful R, with the inherited slot still live. **Cheapest first test.** Construct two R candidates with matching held-out old logits, but an asymmetric old batch separates their next updates; compare Jacobian-only matching and paired no-borrow continuation. **Old-task retention.** Check old margins during the loan and after each held-out continuation; require a held-out Q gain afterward.

#### 03. Return to a substitute old circuit only if it inherits the job — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Permit R to hand old service to an equivalent resident feature while Q keeps the borrowed physical route; name the substitute and certify the old task's future writes there. Treat this as a functional exchange, not a literal return as in 01. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Function-preserving network transformations][net2net] and spare units already offer replicas; test whether native training can substitute an existing, previously useful circuit without copying weights or buying spare capacity. **Cheapest first test.** Compare two same-width hosts, one with a substitutable circuit and one with a lookalike that matches old values but not batch responses; include outright dedicated allocation. **Old-task retention.** Test both the displaced old user and prior users of the substitute, on ordinary writes as well as reads.

#### 04. Move Q's gain so R can close the route — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make Q's native gradient jointly write the borrowed feature and a receiver; admit R only if the receiver can serve Q after the borrowed path closes. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Progressive networks][progressive] and distillation can transfer between modules; ask whether the single native Q write can leave a usable gain in the receiver without a separate transfer batch, a new column or a frozen copy. **Cheapest first test.** On a three-unit host compare a coupled Q, an equal-rank separate receiver, and explicit cheap distillation; ablate the returned route after R in the diagnostic clone. **Old-task retention.** Check old users of both units during Q and after a normal post-R old write.

#### 05. Make the return pay old work that was already due — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Register an old batch arrival as R, pick P only when that particular batch's native update can both advance its own objective and return the route, and charge a timeout when the batch is absent. **Novelty vs nearest.** [GEM][gem] and replay interleave old gradients; here future ordinary service is a prerequisite for permitting today's use of its derivative. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate arrival schedules on two conflicting old labels; compare a chronological replay arm, best reorder and an appointed synthetic return as a ceiling. **Old-task retention.** Check the due batch earns its promised gain and unrelated old reads never dip, including when R fails to arrive.

#### 06. Borrow only from the readers that can tolerate the excursion — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Treat each old reader's tolerated excursion as a separate constraint, and permit only a feature-use path whose joint excursion fits all affected readers; one unaffected client cannot subsidize one failing client. **Novelty vs nearest.** [GEM][gem] protects multiple old tasks per step; the candidate is permission for temporary, bounded nonzero interference tied to a certified useful return. **Cheapest first test.** In a shared trunk with two old heads, construct opposite tolerance profiles and compare a uniform corridor, per-task GEM and joint exact search. **Old-task retention.** Score each client on examples missed by its corridor probes, including its smallest-margin examples during Q.

#### 07. Reserve a return that survives an old update while the loan is open — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Admit P only if the same R, or a pre-certified public menu, can return across the reachable states after a due old write between P and Q. Never reschedule that old write to make the loan viable. **Novelty vs nearest.** [#276][parent] already protects an open cell across a normal write, and [robust MPC][mpc] models disturbances; add the two-sided requirement that both use and useful return survive that write with Q's gain intact. **Cheapest first test.** Compare two return records that tie without interleaving; an old batch moves one outside its valid cell. Compare insertion-blind and exact robust policies. **Old-task retention.** Check the interleaved batch actually learns, other old users stay in bounds, and old writes still work after R.

#### 08. Make two loans composable without tracking their full traces — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Give each admitted loan a bounded footprint on old reads, route and slot; compose the certificates only when all legal interleavings preserve both returns. Reject pairs that touch disjoint weights but share an old downstream reader or optimizer cap. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Robust MPC][mpc] already verifies joint schedules; try an independently checkable loan interface whose composition costs less than pairwise exact resimulation. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two P/Q/R words in a shared-head toy with an independent pair and a shared-slot adversarial pair. **Old-task retention.** Check joint worst-prefix margins and each old user's next write after either return order.

#### 09. Learn a supply of borrowable routes, not a privileged key — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** During development, train a host for coverage of useful P/Q/R words drawn from ordinary independent arrivals, with no example labeled or retained as a universal opener or return. **Novelty vs nearest.** [MAML][maml] optimizes adaptation; the target is held-out capacity to change and return actual feature ownership while retaining three tasks, not one-step loss. **Cheapest first test.** On a tiny labeled teacher mixture, hold out entire opener and return families; compare MAML plus exactly the same bounded word search and a matched-width host with spare capacity. **Old-task retention.** Measure all old tasks after accepted episodes, refusals and a second independent episode, not just the tasks on accepted words.

#### 10. Expose handbacks that leave behind bad momentum — improvement
**Mechanism.** Compare matched-logit returns with different inherited slots and certify old service through coast and the first old gradient, not just at landing. **Novelty vs nearest.** A heavy-ball continuation audit, not a new operation; compare the parent's full-state certificate and reset plus best legal tail. **Cheapest first test.** Find two equal-value landings, one whose next old batch crosses the corridor only with its native slot; swap slots in a diagnostic clone to distinguish curvature from inherited momentum. **Old-task retention.** Include the first point of service failure, the unmodified old update and Q's post-update gain.

### B. Open several directions and prove they can be spent

#### 11. Open and spend native rank — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let a useful prefix make two labeled Q records span distinct seal directions; execute a legal word that reaches both faces with the native optimizer. **Novelty vs nearest.** Restates [#276][parent] card 05; [GradMatch][gradmatch] selects gradients but does not require a lawful prefix to create their joint usefulness. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two-record words on a two-face CPU host against a single-direction switch, static GradMatch, legal accumulation, reset and a matched no-rank-change host; keep direct slot write as a ceiling. **Old-task retention.** Check separate old examples sensitive to each axis and a mixed-feature read through both spends and coast.

#### 12. Open signed choices rather than just a second axis — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Register two alternative seal demands before P. Certify that the post-P family supports lawful words for both demands, including opposite signs along one face, and use only the demand that actually arrives. **Novelty vs nearest.** Positive-span analysis and [robust MPC][mpc] handle signed constraints; the proposed primitive turns one ordinary prefix into a menu of signed training actions, not a basis-fit score. **Cheapest first test.** Compare equal-rank pools with and without the legal opposite sign, at matched norms and record counts; exact search measures offered coverage. **Old-task retention.** Check old examples with opposite margin sensitivities on both offered demands and when a third demand is refused.

#### 13. Open rank only in the directions a safe return will preserve — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Evaluate Q directions through the *same reserved useful R*: distinguish pre-return independence from linearly independent effects on the sealed reads that survive R. **Novelty vs nearest.** [OGD][ogd] uses a local projection and [#276][parent] opens instantaneous rank; test a rank change in effects carried through a native return, with actual finite words as ground truth. **Cheapest first test.** Build two P choices with identical instantaneous rank; one R collapses a Q direction. Enumerate both Q orders and compare projected GradMatch and exact P/Q/R search. **Old-task retention.** Verify the restored old route and next-write response, including old examples affected by the direction R removes.

#### 14. Leave the guarantee intact after one candidate disappears — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Give a rank-opening prefix a deletion guarantee: after any one reserved Q record drops out, a legal word from the remaining pool can still reach both faces and hand back. **Novelty vs nearest.** Robust subset selection and [finish reservations][menus] already provide redundancy; the proposed guarantee is robustness of *executable directional effects* after live activation changes, not redundant vectors at a point. **Cheapest first test.** Use three equally sized Q candidates, delete each in turn and compare static robust GradMatch and exact deletion-aware search; include redundant span whose usable words all depend on the same record. **Old-task retention.** Check timeout and all viable replacement words against the same held-out old reads and due old batch.

#### 15. Open a follow-up direction with the first spend — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let Q₁ itself change the lawful gradient of Q₂, so two useful directions are reachable along a word even though no prefix state offers both simultaneously. Reserve the directed transition rather than a post-P basis. **Novelty vs nearest.** [Teaching][teaching] and [GraB][grab] already exploit order; compare an exact native planner and legal accumulation to isolate the dependence on the new Q₂ feature, not stale-gradient order effects. **Cheapest first test.** A two-gate host with a rank-one pool at every visited point; reverse Q₁/Q₂ and replay with Q₂ frozen in its pre-Q₁ cell. **Old-task retention.** Verify old held-out reads aligned to the two gates at every prefix, including a timeout after Q₁.

#### 16. Ask for the smallest useful prefix that raises reachable dimension — improvement
**Mechanism.** Compute minimal-length lower and upper bounds using reachable sets of effects by deadline; a rank claim with no shorter-word exclusion earns no speed claim. **Novelty vs nearest.** [ReLU verification][reluplex] and bounded reachability already solve small instances; the improvement is cheaper lower bounds using registered service and return constraints. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate every legal order through four ticks, including wait and legal batching, and compare to singular-value and exact reachable-set bounds. **Old-task retention.** Exclude words with any old failure before estimating dimension; report the strongest faster-but-unsafe word as a separate ceiling.

#### 17. Open a second direction that a due old write cannot spend first — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reserve a post-P effect on the incoming faces that remains available after all declared due A updates, instead of reserving a raw gradient direction those updates may consume. **Novelty vs nearest.** Disturbance rejection in [robust MPC][mpc] and service corridors cover feasibility; test whether a shared, portable rank certificate covers multiple actual A batches at lower cost than replanning. **Cheapest first test.** Two A batches with equal A gain and matched slot norm; one erases the second direction, the other only rotates it. Compare frozen certificates and exact post-A search. **Old-task retention.** Require the A gains, B/C floors and the post-return normal A response without changing either A batch.

#### 18. Pay for a second direction only when the demand needs it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use an ordinary public read to decide between pre-certified rank-one and rank-two finishes; the read must distinguish whether one face already has enough progress without exposing the optimizer. **Novelty vs nearest.** [#276][parent] already branches on ordinary reads and [robust MPC][mpc] optimizes action cost; the claim is savings from conditionally exercising extra plasticity, not from a more informative diagnostic. **Cheapest first test.** Two publicly distinguishable face deficits and one aliased pair with divergent safe finishes. Compare always spending both, exact public search and a read before P. **Old-task retention.** Check old-axis margins and the next old update on both branches, including the conservative response to the aliased pair.

### C. Couple rank opening to a useful handback

#### 19. Spend one opened direction on Q and the other on making return possible — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make Q's gain and its obligation separable: one native spend improves Q, the other moves into the entry region where ordinary R can return the loan without erasing the gain. The second spend must also be useful on its own record. **Novelty vs nearest.** [GEM][gem], [GradMatch][gradmatch] and finish reservations can be stacked; require a gain over the best legal stack plus free-feature switch at the same service, not just two good gradients. **Cheapest first test.** Deliberately rank-one and rank-two versions of the same pool, plus a control where a second axis can improve Q but cannot facilitate R. Enumerate actual words. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B/C throughout both spends and R, with a post-R batch that specifically tests the returned feature.

#### 20. Settle two borrowers with one useful R — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Give Q₁ and Q₂ independent temporary uses of distinct old derivatives; accept one R only if its native update restores both reader responses while preserving each Q gain. **Novelty vs nearest.** Joint replay and [GradMatch][gradmatch] can approximate two targets with a batch; require the economy to come from one ordinary record's state-dependent return, not extra labeled examples or larger updates. **Cheapest first test.** Compare a common R with separate returns, legal aggregation and checkpoint rollback under matching total records and steps; include a same-logit R that silently returns only one derivative. **Old-task retention.** Hold out each old reader and a batch that exercises both, and check both return orders in the separate-return arm.

#### 21. Repay without leaving a directional debt for the next loan — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make permission for later loans depend on their surviving native reachable sets after an earlier Q/R, not on the earlier episode's terminal old performance or residual slot norm. **Novelty vs nearest.** The parent's next-installment rights and [robust MPC][mpc] already reason about future feasibility; the target is a composable returned-feature interface whose coverage forecasts still match exact search on later, unseen arrivals. **Cheapest first test.** Two equal-gain handbacks both pass the first old write, but one leaves no legal second independent direction in round two; compare fixed reserve, reset between rounds and fresh search. **Old-task retention.** Track worst-prefix A/B/C over both loans and an ordinary write after the second, without resetting scores between rounds.

#### 22. Stress-test the claim with the best separate opener and repair — improvement
**Mechanism.** Cross borrow versus dedicated route with live rank versus residual-matched frozen rank, then compare native useful R to equal-budget repair. Test for an interaction after matching absolute task gains and counting certification. **Novelty vs nearest.** A factorial diagnostic, not a new primitive; it rules out a simple stack of [teaching][teaching], [OGD][ogd] and replay. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate the four cells and the best stacked control on independently sampled small worlds, including a world with no feasible handback. **Old-task retention.** Compare full old-task trajectories, next-batch response and refusal coverage, not just retained accuracy among successful episodes.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can useful R return the old update response and preserve Q better than a spare feature plus ordinary repair?** Start with 01, 02, 04 and 05. On held-out record families, compare free-feature switch, budget-matched spare, GEM/OGD plus replay and legal word search. Match P/Q/R utility, bytes and deadline before comparing service. Retire the claim if a logit-matched impostor passes the test or if the best separate stack ties through the next old write.
2. **When does an opener create two usable directions, not just two singular values?** Use 11–16. Compare instantaneous rank, achievable native effects and effects that survive R, with an exhaustive small-word oracle and a full-rank but infeasible pool. Require both faces at the deadline after subtracting any P/coast progress in diagnostic controls; a win against static rank alone is insufficient.
3. **Can the rank gain specifically make the handback cheaper?** Use 13, 19, 20 and 22. Compare the best legal stack of independent opener, Q training and old repair, including legal batching. Look for a joint return that retains Q at lower charged cost or serves an arrival the stack cannot. If both components help independently and their matched stack ties, do not claim a coupled mechanism.
4. **Can a small certificate travel across old arrivals and repeated loans?** Use 07, 08, 14, 17 and 21. Hold out legal interleavings and a second task arrival; compare exact resimulation on cost, admissions and refusals. One unsupported admission falsifies soundness. Report coverage lost to conservatism, not just the cost of proofs that succeeded.
