# Post-training OOD 3: teach the procedure, withdraw the payload

**Status:** Ideas only; no implementation, learner experiments, or GPU work.

**Parent:** [Post-training OOD 2](post-training-ood-2.md) ([PR #312][parent]), child of [Post-training for lifelong OOD learning](root-post-training-ood.md). **Review seeds:** [Flood triage, lines 430–433][triage] on [PR #6][review]; the #312 verdict is on line 430, and the other rows are not seeds. The two score-3 cards are **#312/01, one hint across worlds that require different answers**, and **#312/13, a durable decoder with a withdrawable instance payload**. They are controls here, not novelty claims.

**Ledger read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger]. This node does not re-propose private memory shorthand, self-quizzing by generator identity, rare-prefix splitting, executable effect-class replay, or tool storage versus notes. A decoder must recover a needed quantity from independently available evidence, not from a code word, retrieval of an old answer, or an executable tool supplied by the teacher. Each card names what a same-budget control must fail to reproduce.

## What would count as teaching?

- **No answer through the teaching channel.** Fix what the teacher may tailor to in advance (e.g. a learner checkpoint and a family-wide instruction), and construct answer-changing worlds within that block. Compare the *whole* teacher transcript: bytes, presence, timing, length, order and episode selection. For randomized teachers, matching the distribution within the balanced block is the ideal; one byte-identical matched pair only checks that pair. Keep evaluation variants, their ordering, and their answer frequencies out of teacher selection. Uniformity of answers across messages is not enough: a teacher could consistently choose one of two public cues to tell the student how to decode the answer. Test that the actual cue used tracks the designated public evidence under interventions.
- **Public is not necessarily recoverable.** A public observation is one the unhinted student can obtain through its ordinary interface. Two worlds with disjoint acceptable actions but identical public transcript laws under every allowed query policy cannot both be solved with certainty by a zero-payload decoder. In a balanced binary pair indistinguishable within budget, even a randomized policy can average at most 50% at that deadline. A short distinguishing trace produced by a teacher does not establish that a blind student can find it at the same price.
- **Withdrawal means two separate tests.** Withhold the current payload but allow the already learned decoder; then remove all added context, including the decoder prompt, retrieval, and hints, and test the actual updated learner. Separately swap the evidence while holding the hint fixed, and swap any hinted payload while holding the evidence fixed. A changed output on the former and no change on the latter are expected only when the respective intervention leaves the intended task semantics intact. A correct written explanation alone is not evidence the learner used the procedure.

## Common CPU test contract

- Use enumerable worlds (register machines, finite transducers, or harmless query puzzles) with exact ground truth; use a tabular learner or tiny shared-feature learner if the claim concerns learning rather than identifiability. Pair worlds with flipped answers, include a constant-answer null and a no-shared-procedure or no-shared-decoder negative. Also include a zero-initial-success but publicly solvable world. Split by rule or generator, not just by seed.
- On **#312/01** compare the shortest successful A6 hint, parent same-hint pairs, [HiLL][hill]-style reliance pricing, answer-correlated hints at the same bit budget, and paired-data augmentation with the same public examples. On **#312/13** compare per-task A6 ladders, parent decoder and payload withdrawal, [Skill-SD][skillsd], a decoder without payload withdrawal, and direct public-program/library induction. Apply both sets to hybrids. Include equal-length filler and, for stochastic policies, an entropy-matched temperature arm; compare the strongest relevant stack at equal information and work. Charge transmitted bits (including schedules), generated and failed trials, verifier calls, public reads, updates, persistent bytes, setup and stale-entry maintenance. Full-information and exact public solvers can bound performance but are not low-budget comparators if they exceed the cap.
- Report unaided Pass@1, public query cost, and calls to a preset unaided threshold on held-out worlds, including the fraction that never reach it. For lifetime claims, report cumulative learning cost through later independently drawn families, including setup and learner-side inference. Match current-task progress when comparing retention. Use exact enumeration where possible and paired streams with uncertainty otherwise. A tie with the strongest matched control kills the extra mechanism, not the existing technique.
- **Old-task retention for every card:** Run the ordinary zero-hint tasks from five prelearned families before instruction, after every consolidation boundary and after later transfer. No new tools, dictionaries or task IDs; existing ordinary interfaces are unchanged. Report per-family accuracy, worst branch and public work, against the pre-update learner and strongest matched retention control; apply the parent's >2 pp old-family mean-loss rejection rule and reject if retention falls below matched GRPO where applicable. The old slices below are additional.

**Literature check.** For every card, searched Hugging Face Papers for the relevant mechanism and checked its cited comparator against the paper record and abstract; checked the method descriptions of HiLL, Skill-SD, Privileged Self-Practice (PSP) and Privileged Information Distillation (π-Distill) in the indexed PDFs via alphaXiv. HiLL already rewards transfer with a hint-reliance term; Skill-SD uses trajectory summaries with a plain-prompt student; PSP already moves per-task privilege from the loss to sampling; π-Distill jointly trains privileged and unconditioned policies. None of shorter hints, teacher-only prompts, public evidence seeking, program synthesis, or reusable dictionaries is itself the proposed novelty. The comparisons below are a targeted screen, not a priority claim.

## A. Identify what the shared hint teaches (extends #312/01)

### 01. Make a four-world test that crosses evidence and temptation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Require one immutable hint to work when a public predicate and an answer-correlated surface cue are independently flipped. Record the two action contrasts: follow the predicate, ignore the cue. Use the contrast, not just two passes, as the teaching admission rule. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Rethinking Counterfactual Data Augmentation Under Confounding][cda] analyzes when counterfactuals mitigate confounding; [HiLL][hill] tests hint reliance. Compare confound-aware augmentation plus HiLL with the identical quartet. The candidate is admitting supervision on the evidence-by-cue response of the *shared message*, not generating quartets. **Cheapest first test:** In four register worlds, cross a true parity flag with a parity-correlated badge; on transfer replace the badge generator and score both contrasts. **Old-task retention:** Include old worlds where the badge genuinely encodes a separate required fact, so generic badge suppression fails.

### 02. Teach the quotient of answers, not the action names — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Attach the same training hint to a relational action (“choose the object with the lower public rank”), then draw a new public rank-to-action binding independently after the hint is fixed. Require inverse-mapped decisions to agree across bindings but disagree across opposite evidence. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Symbol-Equivariant Recurrent Reasoning Models][equiv] builds symbol equivariance into the learner. Give that architecture the same relabelings; the extra claim is that a hint issued before binding enables acquisition of a *new predicate* that architecture and relabeling alone do not teach. **Cheapest first test:** Permute four button names and hide one ordering of ranks until test; compare against action-token cloning under the identical equivariant architecture. **Old-task retention:** Test an older problem whose button names have fixed semantic meanings, and one whose names are arbitrary.

### 03. Randomize the teacher's reasons for speaking — audit
**Mechanism:** Precommit a hint and rerun it under an external coin for whether assistance occurs, with null slots and lengths fixed. Contrast that with a teacher-triggered schedule replayed at the same rate on matched worlds. Transfer credit only for what persists when the schedule is decorrelated from the answer. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Collaborative Rational Speech Act][rsa] models inference from a speaker's behavior; [HATCH][hatch] anneals hinted trajectory weights. This crosses the schedule with the message rather than reasoning about an informative speaker or varying the assistance rate. Compare randomized scheduling plus ordinary annealing. **Cheapest first test:** In a binary switch task, the text is constant but a teacher trained on stalled cases appears mostly before one choice; make the clock uninformative in the held-out run. **Old-task retention:** Check an old puzzle where a timing cue generated by the *environment*, not the teacher, still matters.

### 04. Ask whether the teaching example itself was the message — audit
**Mechanism:** Cross the policy that chooses a set of training examples with the hint content. Compare examples selected after seeing private solutions with independently seeded examples matched on publicly observable features and difficulty; randomize their order. Tag gains confined to the first arm as selection-dependent, not proof of a public procedure. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Generating Pragmatic Examples to Train Neural Program Synthesizers][prag] exploits the informativeness of pedagogical example choice. The claim is a diagnostic that predicts failure when pedagogical selection disappears, not that informative examples are bad. Compare a standard pragmatic-vs-random example ablation and test the extra hint-by-selection interaction. **Cheapest first test:** Teach a string rewrite from two possible rules; match the number of examples and cross deliberate versus random selection with a real versus filler hint. Deploy on neutral sampling. **Old-task retention:** Check old tasks learned from intentionally chosen examples under their ordinary neutral input distribution, including the rare ambiguous prefix.

### 05. Demand a public intermediate with causal force — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** A hint proposes a public sub-computation. Recompute its value after changing an upstream observation while holding the rest fixed, and after a sham change that leaves it invariant. Train only those sub-computations where the final action shifts exactly when the candidate predicts. The intermediate must be produced by the student on its own rollout. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Causal Abstraction for Faithful Model Interpretation][causal] formalizes intervention agreement; [ExeDec][exedec] supervises intermediate execution. Compare interchange-validated ExeDec at matched intermediate labels. The proposed signal is credit for an intervention-validated intermediate *at the moment teacher assistance stops*. **Cheapest first test:** A two-step arithmetic machine lets a distractor register predict the same trace on train; intervene on target and distractor separately before withdrawing hints. **Old-task retention:** Test inputs where old correct sub-computations cancel each other, so independently correct intermediates are not sufficient.

### 06. Let several procedures survive until an unseen world separates them — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Admit the shared hint provisionally if multiple public procedures satisfy the training checks. Defer selecting a route until the learner encounters a fresh world where the routes prescribe different actions; the verifier scores which route's **public prediction** survived, not which explanation sounds plausible. Pay for candidate storage and tests. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Querying Easily Flip-flopped Samples for Deep Active Learning][flip] uses prediction instability for query selection. Compare disagreement-based acquisition over the same version space; the extra claim is that keeping the ambiguity attached to the withdrawn hint improves which prior solutions can be reused next, not that disagreement makes good queries. **Cheapest first test:** Two equivalent two-register policies on current inputs split on a third variant; compare stored alternatives to fresh version-space reconstruction and a single chosen program. **Old-task retention:** Check both branches of an old family for which the committed route is the only valid one on some inputs.

### 07. Refute the procedure with a fresh generator, not a fresh seed — audit
**Mechanism:** After committing a shared hint and candidate learner, let an independent finite generator produce the cheapest pair on which the learner's public rule diverges from the task. Classify the witness as evidence-use failure, unavailable evidence, or out-of-scope assumption before further teaching. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Thought-Path Contrastive Learning][thought] constructs premise changes for logical reasoning; [RobustFill][robust] tests program induction from noisy examples. Compare premise contrast training plus an ordinary adversarial test generator. The scoped claim is that *typed failures at withdrawal* predict which repair transfers, not that counterexamples improve training. **Cheapest first test:** Train on sorted rows where “first” equals “minimum,” then generate the smallest unsorted counterexample and a negative where no rank is public. **Old-task retention:** Exercise old tasks where first is intentionally the right rule despite the new evidence.

### 08. Make the teacher predict what a blind student will inspect — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Before supplying any hint, the teacher commits which lawful public observation a blind student will obtain at a specified step and why alternative outcomes would change its next decision. Score the prediction on the learner's own unhinted rollouts, not by replaying the teacher's trace. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [PSP][psp] already moves advice to sampling; [π-Distill][pid] explicitly tackles privileged-to-unconditioned distribution shift. Compare PSP plus verification of public steps on student rollouts; the candidate is using *prospective evidence-path predictions* to veto a teaching handoff. **Cheapest first test:** A cheap first read can obviate a second read; freeze a teacher predicting the second, then vary the first read so a legitimate shortcut and a missing dependency are separately observable. **Old-task retention:** Include old tasks with a necessary early observation and later redundant checks; report missed early reads as well as success.

## B. Pay for finding the evidence, not merely pointing at it (bridges #312/01 and #312/13)

### 09. Teach the first affordable distinguishing query — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Credit a reusable query program only for attaining a target decision risk within the learner's own public-call budget, counting calls that return nothing helpful. Supervise where the accumulated public evidence first brings decision risk below the threshold, not the privileged expert's final answer. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Value of Information][voi] prices whether to ask for clarification; [PSP][psp] supplies per-task hints. Compare value-of-information acquisition plus PSP and parent #10's public sensing. The claim is that paying to teach the stopping procedure once lowers later acquisition cost, not that querying is beneficial. **Cheapest first test:** A finite noisy register allows one expensive direct read or up to three cheap likelihood updates; shift the cost table without changing the answer rule. **Old-task retention:** Test old tasks where the expensive read is necessary and where stopping early is optimal.

### 10. Attach a budget-relative impossibility witness to failed withdrawal — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Enumerate the reachable public transcript distributions for each allowed query under a cap. When opposite acceptable actions cannot be separated, return the obstructing world pair and a larger cap or an ordinary public observation that would separate it, if one exists. Route those failures away from more distillation. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Asymptotically Optimal Sequential Testing with Markovian Data][sequential] gives information-based stopping-time bounds; [Privileged Information Distillation][pid] studies transfer feasibility. Compare exact decision-tree/POMDP search and a generic cap increase. The object is a reusable *withdrawal failure label* separating insufficient work from non-identifiability across later teaching attempts. **Cheapest first test:** Hidden parity requires two public reads in one world, and is never public in another; at cap one both look like a weak learner. **Old-task retention:** Score old cap-one policies before and after routing away impossible cases; avoid converting hard-but-solvable old cases into abstentions.

### 11. Teach a stopping rule that survives disagreement among decoders — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Allow candidate decoders to disagree about a payload yet stop acquisition when they agree on the task action; when their agreement is provisional, mark which additional evidence would force reopening. This differs from requiring full payload recovery on every task. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SelectiveNet][selective] optimizes a risk/coverage tradeoff via rejection, and [Value of Information][voi] prices information before acting. Compare the stacked controls at matched abstention and queries; the claim is that an action-level stopping witness can be transferred without choosing one latent decoder. **Cheapest first test:** Two different integer payloads both imply “move left”; on later worlds their signs differ, and a single lawful extra query should reopen the decision. **Old-task retention:** Score old tasks where two old encodings share an action, plus those where their payloads must still be distinguished.

### 12. Put an expiry on the learner's right to stop early — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Learn a public evidence sufficiency rule, then audit a random subset of early stops with an extra public read. Withdraw only the *right to stop early* when that rule breaks; keep the learned decoder and resume ordinary queries. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Conformal Risk Control][conformal] calibrates monotone risks, not validity under arbitrary stream drift. Compare conformal calibration on exchangeable blocks plus ordinary online revalidation using identical audits. Claim a benefit only if repairing the stopping rule separately avoids re-teaching correct decision logic. **Cheapest first test:** A formerly reliable read grows noisy halfway through an enumerable stream; a second read remains stable. Include no drift and a change to the decision logic instead. **Old-task retention:** Check old families whose public sensors did not drift and ensure blanket extra reads or abstentions do not erode performance.

### 13. Transfer a query order only if it still works without the lucky result — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Randomly replace early query outcomes with lawful ambiguous alternatives during teaching and train the student to resume from its own updated information state. Admit a query-order hint only when it improves both successful stops and progress on the alternate outcome. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Adversarial Imitation Learning from Visual Observations using Latent Information][ail] addresses partial observation; [ExeDec][exedec] supervises intermediate execution. Compare public-plan imitation plus equal-budget branching on both outcomes. The extra claim is transfer of a resume decision at the intervened outcome, not branch data alone. **Cheapest first test:** A probe sometimes returns “unknown”; equalize the rate, then change which cheap backup probe resolves it in a held-out task. **Old-task retention:** Check branches in old tasks that legitimately end at the first probe and ones requiring recovery after “unknown.”

### 14. Credit only the query that the learner can repeat after a detour — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Treat “resume the search from here” as a portable lesson only if the learner re-finds the distinguishing query when intervened on with a benign state-changing detour. Persist a semantic description of the reachable observation condition, not a timestamp or privileged query address. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Neural Algorithmic Reasoning Without Intermediate Supervision][nar] studies OOD algorithm execution without intermediate targets; [PSP][psp] assists sampled rollouts. Compare PSP plus direct query supervision on the same detours. Proposed novelty is a detour-conditioned reusable teaching obligation for obtaining evidence, not robustness training in general. **Cheapest first test:** Insert a token into a read-only toy document, moving its relevant field while keeping the field name and evidence unchanged; include a field deletion negative. **Old-task retention:** Run old documents with duplicate field names that require using the original public scope, not simply matching the first name.

### 15. Port the decision rule but replace a bad sensor plan — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Learn separate public-query and observation-to-action modules. At transfer, use controlled intervention on query returns and a held-out answer flip to determine whether to re-teach the acquisition plan, the decision rule, both, or neither. Only charge a reused part as helpful if the paired control cannot reuse it as cheaply. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [ExeDec][exedec] composes programs by intermediate execution, and [Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection][bocpd] detects distributional changes. Compare modular ExeDec plus component-wise change detectors and exact reuse selection in the toy. The candidate is the targeted teacher handoff triggered by a *public substitution*, not modularity or drift detection. **Cheapest first test:** Swap sensor costs only, decision threshold only, then both; preserve the observation semantics in the cost-only condition. **Old-task retention:** Evaluate old policies needing each module independently and mixed old/new callers without task IDs.

### 16. Distinguish stable refusal from a recoverable missing read — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train two publicly verifiable exits: abstain when even the allowed evidence cannot distinguish the actions, or buy one more observation when it can. Test with pairs deliberately matched on immediate confidence, separating a learner that learned public recoverability from one that learned caution. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [SelectiveNet][selective] learns when to reject; [Value of Information][voi] prices additional information. Compare selective prediction plus constrained optimal querying from the same belief features. The extra claim is that these two exits can be *taught once and recovered in new domains* at lower cost than refitting the stack. **Cheapest first test:** Two indistinguishable initial displays admit the same risk; only one has a second public observation with disjoint outcomes. Transfer to a new alphabet. **Old-task retention:** Report coverage and accuracy separately on ordinary old tasks, especially cases with low confidence but a cheap decisive read.

## C. Make the decoder earn its lifetime bill (extends #312/13)

### 17. Train decoder and payload on crossed rather than repeated bindings — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** During setup cross roles with payload encodings so no decoder role predicts a specific operand. Withhold a role–encoding combination while keeping both marginals, then remove the operand hint and require the learner to recover it from the new world's public binder. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Skills-in-Context Prompting][skic] provides reusable named skills for recombination; [Symbol-Equivariant Recurrent Reasoning Models][equiv] handles renaming. Compare SKiC + crossed bindings and matched consolidation; the test is whether the trained decoder can reconstruct a missing binding, not ordinary recombination of given bindings. **Cheapest first test:** Cross three finite transducer roles with three local encodings, withhold two combinations and randomize the binder placement. **Old-task retention:** Score legacy role bindings, particularly old collisions where two roles share the same literal.

### 18. Train without a permanent name for either decoder or payload — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Randomly rename decoder handles during paid setup and later omit both handle and payload. Require the learner to select the right rule from ordinary public semantics rather than route by the naming convention. Compare it with explicitly keeping the frozen decoder prompt at a charged cost. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [DreamCoder][dream] already learns abstractions and a search policy; [Skill-SD][skillsd] already uses plain-prompt students. Compare both with randomized library naming and identical setup. The extra claim is measurable two-stage retirement: which prelearned chunk still saves work once neither handle nor operand is supplied. **Cheapest first test:** Two overlapping arithmetic subroutines differ on one lawful public type test; change both names at every setup episode and hold out a third caller. **Old-task retention:** Check old caller forms that used a shared spelling with semantically distinct types.

### 19. Treat equivalent decoded payloads as a set until a caller requires precision — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Persist a compact description of the class of still-possible payloads that give the same required behavior on the current family's public domain. Resolve only distinctions that a future caller actually needs and can lawfully observe. Price class storage and re-resolution. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [BUSTLE][bustle] guides bottom-up program search via intermediate execution and property signatures. Compare observational-equivalence-aware synthesis retaining alternatives at the same memory budget. The claim is that a *decoder's declared equivalence scope* prevents premature precision from raising downstream teaching cost, not a new signature. **Cheapest first test:** Several arithmetic payloads agree on even inputs; a future caller introduces odd inputs and a new public cue. Include a stream whose callers never split the class. **Old-task retention:** Test old even-only callers and older exact-output callers separately; preserving old actions on the first group must not excuse losing precision on the second.

### 20. Compose two learned decoders without feeding either task payload — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Train two decoders to return constraints on a payload, with each constraint leaving multiple valid options. An unseen caller intersects the constraints to infer the missing operand; it need not commit to either decoder's favorite hypothesis. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [ExeDec][exedec] decomposes by intermediate executions; [Top-Down Synthesis for Library Learning][topdown] extracts shared functionality from program corpora. Compare the library + execution-decomposition stack and parent #14's joint-solvability training, with identical constituent examples. The claim is transfer by intersecting unresolved alternatives, not composing already inferred parameters. **Cheapest first test:** Two public fields independently constrain an operand to overlapping two-element sets; only their intersection identifies it. Include a null where the intersection buys nothing and a conflicting caller whose intersection is empty. **Old-task retention:** Re-run old callers using each constituent alone, plus exact-output callers where committing to an arbitrary member of a set would be wrong.

### 21. Split drift in the evidence from drift in the decoder's interpretation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** At each candidate decoder replacement, test lawfully paired public evidence passed through both incumbent and candidate before and after a regime change. Update only the coordinate whose mapping changed; abstain from assigning blame when the two changes are not identifiable. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection][bocpd] gives online regime detection, and [Skill-SD][skillsd] already refreshes training-time skills. Compare per-component changepoint detection plus replay and skill refresh; the candidate is using crossed evidence/decision readings to avoid charging later learners for an irrelevant decoder replacement. **Cheapest first test:** In a two-register reader, independently change where the operand is exposed and which predicate on it matters; include a coupled change observationally equivalent on available worlds. **Old-task retention:** Use unchanged old dialects whose public operand is visible at each address and score their unaided answers after each repair.

### 22. Reuse the decoder under a new goal without carrying over its preferred action — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach a public payload reconstruction whose output remains meaningful under conflicting goals, then sample the future goal after setup. Score held-out goal swaps with the same evidence and held-out evidence swaps with the same goal; both are necessary. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Successor Feature Landmarks][successor] separates reusable dynamics features from goals for transfer. Compare factorized dynamics/goal reuse plus direct public-feature supervision; the proposed primitive is making *withdrawn instance payloads* reconstructible across new goals, not goal conditioning. **Cheapest first test:** Recover a toy graph's transition label from ordinary adjacency reads; swap the utility of two terminal colors only at transfer. **Old-task retention:** Check old opposing goals on identical displays, not just each old goal on its usual display.

### 23. Require decoder setup to break even on unrevealed callers — audit
**Mechanism:** Freeze the complexity cap and paid setup bill before revealing the task sequence. Plot cumulative cost against independently taught public procedures; count memory upkeep, failed borrowers and later tasks that do not share the decoder. Use matched-progress comparisons rather than averaging only successful borrowers. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [DreamCoder][dream] already amortizes synthesis across tasks, and [Online Adaptation with a Memory of Amortized Contexts][amort] already amortizes task context. Compare both at matched persistent bytes and training work; the diagnostic is whether the crossing survives *public reconstruction and full withdrawal*. **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate three arrival mixes for a finite decoder grammar: all sharing, half sharing, none sharing. Report which tested mixes cross over and when, or that none does. **Old-task retention:** Compare at the same point on each learning curve, including early setup when instruction might do the most collateral damage.

### 24. Teach a local decoder repair that an independent caller can discover — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** When a formerly trusted binding rule fails, teach a public test for locating and replacing its local assumption, not a patched answer. Freeze the repair and later draw an independently written caller; require it to pick old or repaired behavior from public observations alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Editing Models with Task Arithmetic][arithmetic] reuses update directions; [Causal Abstraction][causal] checks intervened variables. Compare local synthesis repairs and task arithmetic with the same public localization tests; the differentiator is whether the *repair rule* can be discovered and applied correctly by an unseen caller without being named. **Cheapest first test:** Re-map one operator in a finite arithmetic grammar while a later mixed caller uses both versions under publicly visible argument types. **Old-task retention:** Score the older operator where it remains correct and untargeted operators at matching margins.

### 25. Transfer the source-choice rule when the source disappears — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Teach a decoder to choose among public sources using redundancy structure, not a privileged label marking the reliable source. Withdraw hints, remove a source, and test whether the learner derives a valid replacement from lawful cross-checks. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [RobustFill][robust] induces programs under noisy specifications; [Program Synthesis via Test-Time Transduction][transduction] uses test inputs during synthesis. Compare noise-aware transductive synthesis and parent #20's two independent reconstructions at the same observations. The claim is transfer of the *source substitution procedure* to a source never used as the default. **Cheapest first test:** Three of four toy registers hold the operand, and one may be wrong. Remove the usual first read and permute register names; the learner can still find a majority, but cannot resolve a two-read tie within a smaller cap. **Old-task retention:** Check the old register streams, including ones where the ordinarily trusted source is the only lawful observation.

### 26. Reuse the decoding algorithm but dispute its training prior — primitive candidate
**Mechanism:** Separate the likelihood of public evidence under each payload from a learned payload-frequency prior. After withdrawal, infer prior changes from aggregate public observations only when the component likelihoods give identifiable mixtures; keep evidence computation fixed unless public interventions disconfirm it. **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** [Program Synthesis via Test-Time Transduction][transduction] adapts synthesis to test inputs, and [Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection][bocpd] updates beliefs about regimes. Compare their combination with an explicit Bayesian prior fix. Novelty would require less later instruction than the matched stack while preserving the original decoder on old regimes, not ordinary prior correction. **Cheapest first test:** Reverse which grammar operator is frequent while leaving distinct overlapping clue distributions unchanged; include an observationally aliased likelihood change for which the decoder cannot diagnose the cause without another read. **Old-task retention:** Test simultaneous old balanced and skewed tasks at zero hint, with public regime evidence only; count irreversible collapse to the new majority as forgetting.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can a successful hint be replaced by a learned procedure that uses only public evidence?** Start with #01, #02 and #05; use #03–04 as leakage audits. Train the same learner on paired shared hints versus equal-information paired data + HiLL and direct public supervision. Require independent cue flips, held-out generators, and zero added context. If gains disappear under these controls, the hint helped current decisions but did not teach a reusable procedure.
2. **When is it the public evidence search, rather than the decoder, that makes withdrawal fail?** Start with #08–#11 and #15. Enumerate where a public policy can succeed at each cap; compare a handed-to-the-learner witness with what a blind student can recover. If constrained planning plus ordinary imitation explains the gap, keep that baseline and the impossibility bound.
3. **Does paid decoder setup buy positive lifetime transfer when no new caller gets a payload?** Start with #17, #20, #22 and #23. Reveal future callers after setup; compare high-, mixed- and zero-commonality streams against per-task ladders, Skill-SD and program libraries. Demand a cumulative cost crossover at matched unaided success and old-task retention, not lower cost after ignoring setup.
4. **Can the learner revise a public decoder selectively without losing lawful old readings?** Start with #19, #21, #24 and #26. Independently vary the public acquisition plan, binding semantics and payload prior. If exact component checks plus replay match the claimed transfer and retention, there is no reason to add a new teaching object.
