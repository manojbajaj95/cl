# Carried coverage: keep the next check live while opening a path for rare retention

**Status:** Ideas only; one paper counterexample, no learner runs or GPU work. **Parent:** [Sealed random checks](sealed-random-checks.md) ([PR #263][parent]), cards **06** and **18**. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 379][triage] scores both 3: carry a live lower bound over surviving harmful histories instead of carrying a missed-check list; make one ordinary C batch both reveal the hidden alias and open the shared feature that a later rare-slice write needs. The [idea ledger][ledger] rules out assuming sparse damage, counting a pinned activation as retention, or crediting rare upsampling without beating replay.

**Verdict:** A scalar floor can report power, but usually cannot tell the next writer which opener preserves it. The candidate operation hands off a smaller, decision-specific account of the surviving histories *and* a live feature that a later writer can actually use. Neither can substitute for retention; look for a cost or reachability gain over exact tracking and the best check-plus-training policy before calling this a new primitive.

## Contract

- **Fix what can be caught.** A = original tasks, B = installed writes (including the defaulter's B_d), C = installed and successor work, W = paired service, R_d = the defaulter's receipt. Use the parent's pre-draw seal on the actual state, binding/backing, lawful transitions, named threatened obligation, first eligible randomness, decision cut and independent clearing. A harmful history is a reachable evolution of a sealed state that falsifies the claim and can break that obligation under the declared continuation. Allow only explicitly modeled report noise, queue supply and legal intervening state changes; don't let a claimant substitute a history after seeing the draw. Compare honest refusal separately. A skip, silence or timeout is not a detection; if it ends the word without a check, include a no-detection terminal branch even if independent clearing serves W.
- **Carry future power, not accumulated confidence.** At a live public prefix p, let H(p) include every harmful history consistent with the observations so far and not yet caught. For any reachable no-detection prefix, certify a conservative floor β(p) on the probability that the next step rejects the false claim for *each* h in H(p), conditional on that prefix. The probability is over still-fresh lawfully drawn work and declared observation noise, with allowed nuisance taken worst-case. State may overapproximate H(p), but cannot lose an unresolved harmful history or conflate “none left” with “none represented.” If the uniform stage floors on surviving branches are b₁ and b₂, detection through two stages is at least 1−(1−b₁)(1−b₂) without independence. A tight calculation can instead propagate conditional branch probabilities with rejection worth one and an uncaught terminal branch worth zero. Do not multiply favorable realized floors and report the result as a pre-draw guarantee.
- **Make the handoff executable and temporary.** Specify which ordinary live reads and invariants determine the next draw law, the compatibility class of the remaining backing, and whether the feature is usable. Charge every extra bit, pinned version, proof refresh, queue reservation and read until the cut. Compare to a scalar plus full recomputation and an equal-byte missed-check list with the same access; allow exact belief/support tracking to discard dead entries too. At the cut consume the evidence state. No hidden audit trail in slot values, route tags or a separately durable receipt. A new episode has a fresh seal and a fresh power bound.
- **Make the opener do real work.** One due C batch's normal trusted report must distinguish the target hidden alternatives at published precision, and its native update must causally make a shared feature usable by a later, independently specified truthful C write. The opener alone need not teach B_d, and not every draw need be dual-purpose: state both detection and downstream readiness guarantees with nondiagnostic and no-result branches included. Predeclare an admissible later editor/menu, then reveal its actual batch only after the opener. The learner sees neither B_d labels nor rare evaluation examples online. Test mediation offline by disabling the newly opened path with slots, reports and other changes matched; test the reverse (mask the diagnostic, keep the path) as a distinct ablation.
- **Power is not permission.** Every executed prefix and fallback must serve A, all installed B and installed C, with W by its deadline, for every consistent history. Continue only with a word safe on the observable branch's full support; clearing is not justified by a likely catch. Retire R_d only after a common continuation serves the old obligations without it, including actual reuse of the freed backing and a blind lawful suffix. Here “retention” means per-slice floors anchored at acquisition, including common and rare B_d, not merely the average on A or the same newly learned C objective. Match starting item margins, work/steps, clocks, peak and live byte-ticks, C gain, reports and admissible menus. The stacked control may reuse any lawful report or update and share the same scheduler. If it implements the same policy at equal cost, the joint claim ties.

## One paper counterexample: equal floors, unequal next moves

Take four sealed harmful aliases h₁–h₄ and an honest h₀. Two mandatory C stages have three equally useful ordinary batch choices apiece; h₀ never rejects and every check returns only reject/no reject. First-stage choices L, R and N have deterministic rejection masks {h₃,h₄}, {h₁,h₂} and ∅. Draw L/R with probability 1/2 each under the declared law (N is unused), so every harmful alias has initial power 1/2. On a miss after L the survivors are {h₁,h₂}; after R they are {h₃,h₄}. Second-stage batches X, Y and Z have masks {h₁,h₂}, {h₃,h₄} and ∅. Both live branches have the same attainable next floor (one), but X is the unique way to attain it on the first branch and Y on the second. A policy that sees only the scalar uses the same law on both branches: uniform X/Y attains 1/2 in stage two and 3/4 overall, the best possible without information distinguishing the branches. A single live bit attains one overall; so does a missed-check list that happens to cost one bit here. If existing queue state exposes L versus R, no auxiliary bit is needed. If one second-stage batch can cover both groups at the same cost, the separation vanishes.

This is a statement about a restricted interface, not an optimizer or retention result. The bits are not a historical audit trail: any current invariant that distinguishes which *next* coverage constraint applies supplies the same decision information, and it expires at the cut. A real host still has to realize those masks with ordinary reports, protect service on every branch, and make the carry cheaper than the strongest compact baseline.

## A. Carry only distinctions a later useful batch can act on

### 01. Certify a next-move quotient — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Replace survivor identities with equivalence classes defined by the same conservative rejection, service and successor-class profiles for all remaining useful actions. Compile this backward from the cut; merging only today's equal rejection rows is insufficient. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Belief-state compression and stochastic bisimulation already reduce planners; the candidate is a reusable, expiring interface for a later editor with no access to the old check trace. **Cheapest first test.** Add a third stage to the paper masks with two aliases that separate only on the last action. Compare exact tracking, scalar-only and the smallest sound quotient in bits, operations and certified power. **Old-task retention check.** Enumerate A/B/installed-C floors and timely W in both merged and unmerged branches, then run actual backing reuse.

### 02. Find the bits a downstream checker cannot do without — improvement
**Mechanism.** Make a conflict graph on reachable public prefixes: connect prefixes if no common continuation can meet the specified power, service and cost bounds. A clique bounds the number of distinguishable live states; compute an exact cover by sets with a common policy for a tiny host, since pairwise compatibility need not imply joint feasibility. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Zero-error coding and finite-state minimization already give lower-bound tools; apply them to optimization over truthful batch menus rather than message reconstruction. **Cheapest first test.** Construct three prefixes whose sets of feasible draw laws overlap pairwise but have an empty three-way intersection; compare the exact cover, graph coloring and compressed missed lists. **Old-task retention check.** Count only prefixes and continuation classes that keep the weakest installed A/B/C slice and W within bounds.

### 03. Hand off the menu constraints, not the aliases — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Carry an outer approximation to the remaining possible detection rows, with a certificate that every true survivor row is represented or dominates one retained row for the next allowed menu. A later chooser optimizes against those rows without reconstructing history. Refresh the approximation at a transition where it is no longer sound. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust linear design and constraint generation already drop redundant rows; the question is whether dominance proofs persist through useful writes on a changing learner. **Cheapest first test.** Make two dominated rows exchange order after an ordinary update; compare exact minimax, stale pruning and certified carry at equal byte-ticks. **Old-task retention check.** Verify service in the formerly dominated branch even if budget forces independent clearing.

### 04. Prove a bound from the current serving state — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Look for an ordinary state predicate, maintained by every legal event, that implies a useful next-openers floor without recording which first check missed. Two genuinely different prefixes should converge to the same predicate with sound future coverage. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Inductive invariants and [certified continual learning][cert] already transport guarantees through retraining; the new target is *prospective conditional detection*, not certified predictions. **Cheapest first test.** In a two-slot linear host, compare recomputing the exact support from the live state, a recorded history and one invariant; add optimizer slots as a counterexample to a weights-only predicate. **Old-task retention check.** Test installed A/B/C through each converging prefix and the first update to freed backing.

### 05. Let the carry be wrong in only one direction — improvement
**Mechanism.** Quantize each alias class's report intervals outward and each detection floor downward; if a split is too expensive, widen the survivor bound and choose a stronger ordinary menu or clear. Never prune a row because its sampled misses are rare. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Abstract interpretation already provides conservative joins; [confidence sequences][anytime] cover time-uniform estimation under their assumptions, not guaranteed power over omitted aliases. The question is whether the conservative abstraction stays small and useful at serving precision. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust report-bin boundaries including ties and one unseen rare row; compare rounding-to-nearest, exact support and outward intervals under the same storage cap. **Old-task retention check.** Require timely W on bin-edge timeouts and score old-task retention even on branches newly routed to fallback.

### 06. Leave an opening that survives a queue substitution — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Carry a floor uniform over a published set of possible next menus, paired with a service-preserving policy for each menu. An intervening A request may change which C queue comes due but not silently change the coverage promise. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust scheduling already hedges uncertain supply; this must beat parent 03's order restriction by preserving a usable detection option without holding a specific batch in reserve. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two next menus, one missing a diagnostic queue; compare reserving the queue, a paid audit and a robust useful-work policy. Include the case where no menu can detect one survivor. **Old-task retention check.** Score A's intervening service latency, installed B/C after either menu and W on starvation.

### 07. Allow correlated reports, but make the adversarial state explicit — improvement
**Mechanism.** Carry a shared nuisance class (e.g. a legal quantization mode) across both reports, and update its support jointly with the alias rather than multiplying marginal powers. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Sequential inference and filtering already account for dependence; the contribution would be a compact next-check interface with power uniform over the allowed shared nuisance, not an e-value gate. **Cheapest first test.** Use a persistent latent coin that makes both checks miss together; compare marginal multiplication, exact joint enumeration and a second ordinary C report sensitive to the opposite mode. **Old-task retention check.** Run all three installed task families and W in the correlated-miss branch, not just when the lie is detected.

### 08. Pass a live safety-and-power table between independent writers — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish a small menu-indexed table of valid next transitions and detection floors, usable by an editor whose actual C queue is revealed after handoff. An unknown menu entry means no certified power, never compatibility by default. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Parent 06 carries one floor; assume–guarantee contracts already abstract components. Here the successor's honest work must instantiate the guarantee without access to the previous checker or its record. **Cheapest first test.** Freeze two editors before assigning their order, hide the successor queue and compare a fixed bound, this table and fresh verification from the current state. **Old-task retention check.** Test every allowed handoff and include a successor with no valid entry; W must still clear.

### 09. Clear only the unresolved part of a joint obligation — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For coupled old readers, carry which service obligations are still threatened under each surviving class; retire those whose invariants hold on *all* survivors, and route the rest to independent clearing. Detection credit remains scoped to the original claim. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Precise liveness and [verified renewal][renewal] already remove dead dependencies; this earns novelty only if a prospective detection state shares proofs across readers without keeping a per-reader missed list. **Cheapest first test.** Two readers on one backing, with different failure masks and one shared fallback; compare exact liveness plus separate audits and this shared table. **Old-task retention check.** Force both reuse orders, including a case where keeping one reader makes the other require reserved W capacity.

### 10. Join branches only when their guarantees join — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After different useful prefixes converge to indistinguishable public state, install a common next policy on the union of their surviving harmful supports. If no common policy reaches the floor, keep a live discriminator or clear; never preserve the larger individual floor by default. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** State merging in exact planning is known; the hypothesis is that useful shared updates can induce *cheap sound joins* without saving path IDs. **Cheapest first test.** Modify the counterexample so L and R produce equal weights but leave different check masks; compare exact history, a union join and a useful stage-one update that really unifies the second-stage masks. **Old-task retention check.** Run the identical continuation and backing reuse from both branches, checking installed A/B/C and W by the same clock.

## B. Make a diagnostic step create usable shared capacity

### 11. Open a gate on the diagnosing batch — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Search ordinary due C batches for one whose trusted output separates a sealed alias pair and whose update crosses a shared ReLU gate. The later C record has no gradient along the rare carrier on the closed side and a usable, safe gradient on the open side. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Dual control already joins action with information; [trajectory matching][mtt] already selects for later learning. The claim is a cheaper reachable route under sealed randomization and truthful data, not a two-term score. **Cheapest first test.** Start with hidden momentum in an auxiliary output coordinate and a later C head that reads v·ReLU(g) with g < 0. Offer due records that either increase g or fit a same-loss distractor. Enumerate lawful sequences with the native update and find whether the cue that reveals the momentum is also the one that unlocks a nonzero update to v. Compare an independent diagnostic, projected C training and their best stack; block the gate crossing offline with the report unchanged. **Old-task retention check.** Preserve A/B/installed C on both aliases at each prefix and check B_d on the untrained rare panel after reuse.

### 12. Measure access through the whole reader — improvement
**Mechanism.** Define “open” by a finite change the frozen later editor can write and the end-to-end rare reader can use, not by an activation or Jacobian alone. Test with the native optimizer and any moving consumer. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GPM][gpm] protects stored activation subspaces and the ledger's read-pinning pilot failed when downstream consumers moved; this tests a certified producer–consumer path, not another activation proxy. **Cheapest first test.** Pair hosts with identical opener-layer activations but one clipped or gated downstream read; compare a pinned-feature score, exact reader reachability and gradient alignment. **Old-task retention check.** Check every true old output after the reader moves and the backing is overwritten, not just old logits at the insertion point.

### 13. Reveal one alias but open for all of them — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Require the diagnostic batch to make the same later write legal and effective across all aliases on every branch where that write is advertised. The trusted report may distinguish them but must not secretly supply the target or the route. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust control already seeks common controls and [GEM][gem] constrains gradients using old examples; the candidate is a common newly reachable rare-retaining move built by truthful C without rare rehearsal. **Cheapest first test.** Use aliased momentum with opposite signs and enumerate the cone of real later C gradients before/after the opener; compare per-alias oracle routing, GEM and the best common baseline. **Old-task retention check.** Execute the same later write on all admissible aliases, with rare B_d held out and W checked even after a nondiagnostic draw.

### 14. Open a feature that a different editor can spend — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish a measurable post-opener readiness range (feature sign, reachable update range, expiry), freeze the population of later editors, then test an unseen editor's unchanged native write against that range. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Trajectory matching][mtt] optimizes particular training trajectories; [check as work][check] already opens a route to migration. The test here is transfer of actionable readiness to a held-out editor instead of fitting the known second gradient. **Cheapest first test.** Hold out one of three C heads with different input patterns, including a head that cannot read the feature. Compare ordinary pretraining, exact menu search and a split check-plus-opener at matched work. **Old-task retention check.** Verify that each admitted unchanged write maintains A/B/C, then reuse the old carrier and run the blind old-slice suffix.

### 15. Turn a diagnostic margin into a temporary learning window — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Have the first C step expose the alias at serving precision while changing a shared gate for only a finite number of legal intervening steps. Hand off a proved window in which the later editor can spend the opening; let it expire if missed. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Deadline scheduling and switched reachability already handle finite windows; the gain would be a diagnostic operation creating the window without pinning a checkpoint or borrowing extra old-task slack. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate zero, one and two intervening momentum updates on a two-feature host; compare a separate audit plus scheduled training and keeping the gate open by reservation. **Old-task retention check.** Verify every early/late service prefix, especially the expired window's independent clearing and rare floor.

### 16. Force the open path to survive an unchanged reducer — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Require the gate/feature established by the diagnostic batch to survive a pre-existing ordinary consolidation, clipping or normalization step on the path to the later editor. Do not edit the reducer just to preserve the measurement. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Verified renewal][renewal] checks before shrinking and [certified CL][cert] protects behavior through retraining; the candidate is an opener that changes a reachable feature without letting routine reduction delete it. **Cheapest first test.** Place a fixed norm cap between the opener and the rare-slice write; cross cap on/off with useful opener versus pure diagnostic and match post-cap C gain. **Old-task retention check.** Check old A/B/C before and after reduction and after reuse, including when the openness report is stale.

### 17. Teach invariance with the same batch that separates a nuisance — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use a due C context whose auxiliary output separates the hidden alias while its supervised gradient opens a feature shared across the later editor's contexts; the diagnostic nuisance must not become the rare prediction shortcut. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [IRM][irm] already seeks shared predictors across environments; the new candidate uses the same honest batch to reveal a nuisance and establish a later gradient pathway robust to its shift. **Cheapest first test.** Swap the nuisance correlation in a held-out C context and blind rare evaluation; compare IRM, replay and separate check plus invariant C training at equal work. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate rare B_d after actual reuse under both nuisance signs as well as common A, B and C.

### 18. Expose the joint win with matched causal ablations — improvement
**Mechanism.** Cross diagnostic visibility with real shared-path movement. Intervene offline on the report and path separately, and give the best stacked control the same scheduler, context and ordinary reports. Charge an extra step only if no lawful equal-cost stacked policy achieves the same result. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Parent 18 states the dual-purpose opener and parent 22 states the stack control; the addition is a causal four-cell test for necessary coupling, not a new optimizer. **Cheapest first test.** Compare report-only, path-only, both and neither on equal-C-gain CPU menus, plus a null where the stack chooses the identical batch. **Old-task retention check.** Require no floor loss on any cell's legal fallback; score rare retention only through the later write and post-reuse suffix, not diagnostic accuracy.

## C. Make the handoff and the opening earn their coupling

### 19. Carry the joint feasible region, not two best-case numbers — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify feasible policies for detection and causal readiness together, under the same conditional support and cost. Two maxima obtained by different laws cannot be advertised as one guarantee. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Parent 09 gives a one-draw coverage/preference frontier and constrained planning gives Pareto fronts. The candidate is a live frontier that a successor can compose with a newly revealed truthful write. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate a two-step menu where the best diagnostic and best opener conflict, then one where a joint batch creates a previously unreachable point. Compare exact belief-space planning and the best resource-matched stack. **Old-task retention check.** Include A/B/C and W as hard constraints before computing the frontier, then exercise actual reuse on the selected points.

### 20. Pay for residual detection only where the opener did not do both jobs — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** On a branch where the first ordinary report does not reject, select a second-stage policy against the residual harmful support, while preserving or recovering the live feature. Compare to a common second menu that cannot exploit the first stage's live state. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Bayesian exploration–exploitation already adapts to observations ([survey][bayes]); here the continuation must meet a worst-history power floor and serve without rare labels or a missed-check list. **Cheapest first test.** Extend the paper masks so X opens or preserves a carrier and Y erases it, with a third more expensive clearing step. Enumerate the exact policy and compare a separate randomized audit plus optimally scheduled safe C transfer. **Old-task retention check.** Check rare B_d and W on the erasing, missing and clearing paths, including branches with zero next-stage detection.

### 21. Bound the oldest retained slice across repeated handoffs — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reuse only a schema for current-state proofs; at every new default reseal and recompute the power floor. Keep a ceiling on simultaneously live carries, independent backing and prepared features; when a slot is full, clear rather than evict an unresolved proof. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Rolling escrow][rolling] and verified renewal already bound live obligations; the claim is that a learned current-state carrier makes the power–readiness handoff reusable without growing history or accumulating probability credit. **Cheapest first test.** Three defaults, including one overlapping deadline and a late arrival that attacks the oldest carrier. Compare exact live-state tracking plus verified renewal and the same-byte missed-list baseline. **Old-task retention check.** Evaluate the oldest and newest rare slices at each reuse and every W deadline; report peak pinned resource as well as final bytes.

### 22. Prove when no useful batch can do both — improvement
**Mechanism.** At fixed published precision and legal C menu, exhaust all actions safe on the sealed support to upper-bound joint detection and later feature reachability. Distinguish “separable with an extra read,” “openable with a different real batch,” and “not possible under this interface.” **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Parent 22 and [check as work][check] already prescribe null tests; the extension distinguishes lack of *joint* support from failure of the live compression. **Cheapest first test.** Build one symmetric host with identical ordinary reports until after the deadline, and another with informative reports but no real later gradient through the candidate feature. Compare a paid audit, [DER replay][der] and exact useful-action enumeration. **Old-task retention check.** Independent clearing has to keep old A/B/C and W in both impossibility cases; do not call unconditional release a successful baseline.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **What is the smallest state that keeps useful next-stage detection sound under a real menu change?** Start with 01–04, 06 and 10. Look for a strict saving against the best compressed missed list *and* exact support recomputed from ordinary live state, at matched guaranteed power, honest refusal, useful work and retention. The scalar counterexample only establishes a sufficiency limit, not a savings claim.
2. **Can a single genuinely due C batch both diagnose and causally enable an unchanged later write from a held-out editor?** Start with 11, 13, 14 and 18. Require real after-reuse rare retention and a gain over the optimally scheduled audit-plus-transfer stack. If either report or path can be independently replaced at the same cost, call it scheduling rather than a new operation.
3. **When are detection and openness jointly feasible versus merely attainable on different branches?** Start with 07, 16, 19, 20 and 22. Vary report precision, intervening reducers and queue availability; make zero-power branches visible and specify which conditions require paid independent clearing. Never buy readiness by relaxing the old-task floor.
4. **Can the operation repeat without recreating the receipt it was meant to replace?** Start with 08, 09, 15 and 21. Run three sealed episodes on paper or a future exact CPU model; include failed opening, timeout and real backing reuse. If the oldest reader needs a past report, route tag or resurrected checkpoint, price it as live backing and do not claim retirement.
