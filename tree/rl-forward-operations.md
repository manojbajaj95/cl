# RL-OOD → forward-only edits → reusable operations

Parent: [PR #51, `tree/model-rl-ood.md`](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/51), ranked branch 2 ("forward-only edit tomography"). Question: **can paired forward-only weight edits recover reusable operations, and can a larger model cut the search cost?** Ideation only; no experiments were run.

Here an *operation* is a weight change whose behavioural effect is the same across contexts, tasks or checkpoints, so it can be stored, named, reapplied and composed. A *paired edit* is an antithetic ES/ZO probe: evaluate `θ+σε` and `θ−σε` on the same inputs.

## Root cause

Current forward-only methods are built to estimate **one gradient for one objective at one base point**. They are not built to estimate a reusable factor. Four gaps follow from this.

| # | Gap | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scalar projection, then discard.** A pair returns `⟨∇R, ε⟩`, one number for one task. ES keeps only the weighted mean of the population and throws away which direction did what. Every offspring can be worse than the parent while the mean still improves ([ES Paradox 2611.04113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04113)), so the individual perturbations carry no reusable meaning. They are noise that averages into a direction. | 2611.04113; [ES at Scale 2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372); [EGGROLL 2511.16652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16652) |
| 2 | **Operations are exactly what makes ES expensive.** ES sample cost scales with *effective dimension*: the number of independent skills the reward needs, not the parameter count. PG cost scales with effective horizon ([2611.29795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29795)). A task that decomposes into many reusable operations is the high-cost regime. Per-task ES re-pays for every operation it shares with a previous task, because nothing indexes operations across tasks. | 2611.29795; low-rank/subspace ZO ([2610.15225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15225), [AIM-ZO 2609.35257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35257)) shrinks dimension per run, not across tasks |
| 3 | **No reference frame (gauge).** Raw-coordinate perturbations are not invariant to function-preserving reparameterisation. In [`tree/model-guided-edits.md`](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl), a rescale flips raw-weight choosers, and LoRA geometry is backbone-specific ([2611.27201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27201)). An operation defined in raw coordinates cannot be compared across checkpoints. Task-vector decomposition ([2512.22511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22511), [2610.41743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41743)) and validity maps ([2608.09490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09490)) need gradient-trained deltas at a fixed base. | listed |
| 4 | **The larger model has a prior over text, not over edit effects.** In WSP and self-WSP, a large model proposes edits from weights or summaries. 6 of 16 WSP trajectories fail and the outcomes are uneven ([2611.09382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382)). Fixed numerical edits match LLM-selected repairs ([2611.25613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25613)), and self-WSP ties random search ([2611.28833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28833)). The proposer never receives a measured forward model of weight→behaviour, so its proposals cannot beat the effective-dimension floor. A larger model can cut search cost only if it predicts effects (variance reduction) or supplies a lower-dimensional basis. Proposing edits does neither. | listed; ledger entry on Self-WSP |

A confound for any retention claim: ES forgets less than SFT/GRPO ([2605.30148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30148), [2604.01499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01499)), but the update's norm, not its dimension, drives the damage ([2608.01624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01624)). **Every retention check below must be norm-matched against a GRPO/SFT update and a random update of equal norm.**

**Diagnosis in one line:** paired edits observe **scalar, single-task, single-base, gauge-dependent** projections. An operation is a **multi-output, cross-task, cross-base, gauge-invariant** factor of the edit→behaviour map, so the observations cannot identify it. A larger model gives the search no information about that map.

## Raw ideas (unfiltered)

Each idea lists its mechanism, why it is new, and the cheapest first test with a retention check. PR #51 ideas 11–18 (tomography, surgeon, commutators, factorised writes, mutant verifiers, tournaments, write barriers, learned experimenter) and flood P21/SM2 (weight ISA) are not repeated. Required controls everywhere: matched-call random search, per-task ES, and a norm-matched GRPO update.

### A. Make the measurement multi-task, so operations become identifiable

1. **Shared-seed reward matrix.** Evaluate the same seed bank `{ε_j}` on K tasks to get `R[k, j]` (antithetic difference). Operations are the low-rank factors of `R`: seed combinations whose effect loads on several tasks. *New:* the object is a task × perturbation response matrix, not a per-task gradient. *Test:* toy MLP with 3 planted operations mixed into 8 tasks, 256 seeds. Recover the factors and reapply one to a held-out 9th task. Retention: accuracy on the 8 source tasks after applying a factor, against the norm-matched per-task ES update.
2. **Double-difference probes.** Pair the edits *and* the tasks: `[R_A(θ+ε) − R_A(θ−ε)] − [R_A′(θ+ε) − R_A′(θ−ε)]`, where A and A′ differ in exactly one operation. This isolates that operation's direction and cancels everything shared. *New:* difference-in-differences in weight × task space. *Test:* modular-arithmetic DSL where `A′` swaps add↔sub; count calls to a fixed cosine with the planted direction versus single-task ES. Retention: performance on the unswapped ops.
3. **One-bit dictionary sensing.** Treat each antithetic pair as one bit (which side wins) per task. Recover a sparse operation dictionary across many tasks by 1-bit compressed dictionary learning. Bits can also come from a judge or a human. *New:* operations learned from pairwise preferences over weights rather than over outputs. *Test:* planted sparse dictionary, 1-bit versus real-valued rewards at equal calls. Retention: source tasks after applying the recovered atom.
4. **ES-update ICA.** Run many short ES runs on overlapping task mixtures, stack their mean updates, and run ICA. The independent components are candidate operations. *New:* operations as the sources behind the mixed ES updates. *Test:* 30 runs over random 3-of-8 task mixtures; match the components to planted ops. Retention: apply each component alone and check the other tasks.
5. **Behavioural quotient.** Two edits are the same operation if their difference has zero effect on every probe. Estimate the behavioural null space with paired probes and define operations as equivalence classes modulo that null space. *New:* an edit algebra on the quotient space, which also removes gauge. *Test:* plant a rescale gauge; check that two gauge-equivalent edits land in the same class and a raw-cosine chooser splits them. Retention: none needed. The class representative with minimal norm is the one to write, so measure its source-task damage.
6. **Recycled populations.** Archive every evaluated perturbation with its *vector* of probe outcomes. A new task's ES draws first from archive atoms weighted by how well their stored effect profiles predict the new task's first few probes. *New:* the cross-task archive of perturbation effects is the operation store. Guided ES uses only past gradients from the same run. *Test:* a stream of 10 tasks; report calls-to-threshold versus fresh ES and a past-gradient subspace. Retention: earlier tasks after each write.
7. **Meta-ES over an operation basis.** An outer ES learns a K-atom basis shared across tasks. An inner ES fits per-task coefficients in K dimensions. *New:* the basis is learned forward-only across tasks, whereas learnable-subspace ZO learns a subspace inside one run. *Test:* 8 train tasks, 4 held-out; report held-out calls in K-dimensional search versus full ES. Retention: freeze the basis and check that writing to new tasks leaves old-task coefficients valid.

### B. Fix the frame, so operations are comparable across bases

8. **Gauge-fixed perturbation basis.** Perturb in a whitened, per-neuron-normalised basis (input-covariance whitening, ROME-style), so the effect of a perturbation is invariant to rescaling and permutation symmetries. *New:* ES noise defined in a canonical frame, making the found directions portable. *Test:* run ES on a model and on a gauge-transformed copy; report the cosine of the found operations in raw versus canonical frames. Retention: old tasks after a transported operation.
9. **Lineage-invariant perturbations.** Apply the same seed to checkpoints θ_t, θ_{t+1}, … of a continual run. A perturbation whose effect survives the lineage is a stable operation; one whose effect flips marks what training changed. *New:* the perturbation's effect across checkpoints is both an operation label and a forgetting detector. *Test:* 5 checkpoints of a small sequential-task run; check whether effect-flip seeds predict which old tasks were forgotten, compared with a weight-diff norm. Retention is the measured quantity.
10. **Rediscovery invariance.** Run ES toward the same task from two different bases. Directions both runs find, after canonical alignment, are operations; directions only one run finds are base-specific patches. *New:* operation = invariant of independent forward-only searches. *Test:* two seeds of a toy pretrain; measure the shared fraction and whether the shared part transfers to a third base better than the full update does. Retention on the third base.
11. **Activation-space search, compiled to weights.** Search antithetic perturbations of residual directions (dimension d, not P), then compile the winner into a covariance-aware rank-1 weight edit. *New:* forward-only search where the effective dimension is set by representation width, with a compile step. *Test:* toy transformer; calls-to-threshold in activation space versus weight space; compiled-edit fidelity. Retention: old-task probes after compile against a norm-matched LoRA step.
12. **Context-space search with a hypernetwork decoder.** Search over short context strings, a discrete and low-dimensional space, and decode each with a Doc-to-LoRA/Text-to-LoRA hypernetwork ([2602.15902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902), [2506.06105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105)). An operation is a *text* whose decoded delta is reusable. *New:* forward-only weight search through a language interface, giving readable operations. *Test:* small hypernetwork-equipped model on 6 rule tasks; compare calls and transfer of text-found operations with raw ES. Retention: old tasks after applying the decoded delta.

### C. Can a larger model cut the cost?

13. **Scale shrinks effective dimension.** Hypothesis: a larger model in the same family represents a task with fewer independent skills (more linear features), so ES cost per task falls with scale even though P grows. *New:* a measurable scaling law for forward-only cost, using the effective-dimension estimator of 2611.29795. *Test (cheapest):* estimate effective dimension from the spectrum of the response covariance on Qwen2.5-0.5B, 1.5B and 3B on one task. Retention: none, since this is a measurement. Gate: dimension falls at least 2× from 0.5B to 3B.
14. **Search small, write large.** Find the operation in a small model, transport it to the large one through a shared spectral or activation basis ([2605.07302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07302)), and search only a small neighbourhood there. *New:* the larger model is the *target* and the smaller one carries the search cost. *Test:* 0.5B → 1.5B with a CKA-matched layer map; report calls versus cold ES on 1.5B. Retention: 1.5B old-task probes.
15. **The larger model as a control variate, not a proposer.** Have the large model predict each perturbation's reward from a canonical description (the SAE features it moves, its probe-effect preview). Use the prediction as a control variate with the exact add-back, which keeps the estimator unbiased. *New:* the larger model's job becomes variance reduction, which is measurable and cannot bias the estimate. Self-WSP asks it to choose. *Test:* report variance per call against a linear surrogate and against no control variate on a toy router with exact rewards. Retention: unchanged by construction; verify with old-task probes.
16. **The larger model writes perturbation distributions.** It writes a short program for the covariance (which layers, which features, which coupling), and the small model runs ES under that distribution. *New:* the larger model edits the search prior, not the weights. *Test:* calls-to-threshold versus isotropic ES and versus AIM-ZO's activation-informed subspace. Retention: old tasks.
17. **Operation hypotheses as probe patterns.** The larger model names a hypothesised operation by its predicted *effect pattern* over a probe set. Paired edits then search only directions whose measured pattern correlates with the prediction. *New:* the hypothesis is a target in effect space, not an edit. *Test:* planted ops, with and without the hypothesis, plus a shuffled-hypothesis control. Retention: probes outside the predicted pattern must stay flat.
18. **Judge-scored pairs.** Replace verifier rollouts with a larger judge's preference between `θ+ε` and `θ−ε` outputs. A pair then costs one comparison. *New:* forward-only weight search with preference feedback. The risk is judge bias written into weights. *Test:* exact-verifier versus judge bits at equal compute; report the sign-agreement rate and the resulting operation's verifier score. Retention: source tasks under the verifier, not the judge.

### D. Retention and continual use, forward-only

19. **Antithetic curvature as a protection map.** The pair *sum* `R(θ+ε)+R(θ−ε)−2R(θ)` estimates second-order curvature along ε. Its sign on old-task probes marks the directions old skills sit on (the same curvature behind the ES paradox). Project new operations away from those directions. *New:* EWC-like protection estimated from forward passes, at no extra cost, from data ES already computes. *Test:* sequential 2-task toy; compare old-task retention with plain ES and with EWC at matched norm.
20. **Paired re-verification as consolidation.** Re-test every stored operation periodically with a paired `+δ` vs `0` probe while the model keeps learning. Operations whose effect persists are consolidated; those that decay get re-searched or dropped. *New:* operation lifecycle driven by forward-only checks. *Test:* a stream of 6 tasks; report the decay rate of operation effects against old-task accuracy. Gate: effect decay predicts forgetting better than weight drift.
21. **Lock-in edits along the stream.** Modulate K candidate operations with distinct sinusoidal amplitudes over deployment time and demodulate each one's effect from the online reward stream. *New:* frequency-division multiplexed weight probes for online continual learning. PR #51 #11 codes combinations per batch, not along time. *Test:* 8 candidates over 2,000 stream steps; report the error of the recovered effects versus sequential A/B at equal steps. Retention: amplitude σ small enough that the old-task drop is under 1 pp.
22. **Negation as an operation test.** A true operation should have a predictable inverse: `−δ` undoes its probe pattern and leaves unrelated probes flat. A patch fails this test. *New:* inverse consistency as the criterion that separates operations from patches. *Test:* compare planted ops with ES updates on 2 tasks; report the inverse-consistency score versus transfer to a new task. Retention: unrelated probes under `±δ`.
23. **Credit-free macro operations.** ES is horizon-free (2611.29795), so use it only for long-horizon "habits" such as tool-call patterns, and keep PG for short in-episode credit. Store the ES-found habits as operations reused across agent tasks. *New:* a division of labour that sets the operation boundary at the effective horizon. *Test:* a toy agent with an 8-step habit plus 1-step choices; compare a hybrid against pure GRPO and pure ES at equal rollouts. Retention: old-task agent success.
24. **Context–weight exchange rate.** Perturb the prompt and the weights jointly. Where a context change and a weight edit produce the same probe pattern, the indifference curve gives an exchange rate. An operation is a weight edit with a context twin, so it can be stored in either medium and swapped. *New:* a measured equivalence between context and weights per operation. *Test:* 4 rule tasks; find pairs, then remove the weight edit and supply the context twin, and check that retention and behaviour match. Retention: old tasks in both media.
25. **Weight-edit kernel trick.** Learn a kernel over perturbations from their probe-effect vectors, then do Bayesian optimisation in that kernel for new tasks, so that similar operations share evidence. *New:* a covariance over edits defined by behaviour, not by coordinates. *Test:* report calls versus isotropic BO in neural thickets ([2608.10867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10867)) and versus ES. Retention: old tasks.
26. **Operation provenance in seeds.** Store each operation as `(seed set, coefficients, canonical frame id)` rather than as a delta (cf. SM1 seed log). Composition adds coefficient vectors, and the store can be audited and replayed in a new frame. *New:* the forward-only analogue of a linker symbol table. P17 assumes gradient deltas. *Test:* compose two stored ops by coefficient addition versus delta addition, after a gauge change. Retention: source tasks of both ops.

## Deeper sub-questions (ranked by likelihood of a new primitive)

1. **Is the task × perturbation response matrix low-rank, and are its factors operations?** If shared seeds across tasks (ideas 1–4, 7) recover planted operations in fewer calls than per-task ES, the primitive is a forward-only *operation dictionary* learned from comparisons. The first gate is a toy with planted ops; then check whether real LLM task families (arithmetic variants) show a spectral gap.
2. **Can the larger model reduce variance instead of proposing edits?** Ideas 15–17 recast the large model as a control variate or search prior. This is the only route where a bigger model provably cannot do worse than random search, since the add-back keeps the estimate unbiased. Gate: variance per call falls at least 2× against a linear surrogate.
3. **Does effective dimension shrink with scale or in a canonical basis?** Ideas 8, 11–14. Measuring this is cheap, and the answer decides whether forward-only operations are practical on frontier models.
4. **Do the antithetic second-order terms give forward-only retention for free?** Ideas 19–22. This is a CL-specific primitive (a curvature map plus an operation lifecycle) that rides on data ES already computes. The norm-matched control is decisive, because ES forgets less mostly due to a small update norm.
5. **Can operations live in either context or weights?** Idea 24. A measured context–weight exchange rate would join agent memory and weight editing into one primitive. This is the riskiest of the five, and it overlaps the WSP-writes-ICL prior art (2611.26841).
