# Deployment feedback 3: Opposed witnesses and contracts between writes

Child of [PR #353](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/353) ([parent node](deployment-feedback-2.md)) · Depth 2 · 26 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** Keep two small objects through a live update: a pair of admissible worlds explaining why an old decision is still open, and a contract explaining how one more independent edit could change the routes that distinguish them. Two witnesses are not a sufficient statistic for all future evidence. Likewise, two edits that each leave a path open need not leave any path open together. Test whether certificates can retain just enough structure to recover from those failures without storing every old explanation or re-solving the whole deployment after every unrelated change.

**Scope.** [Flood-triage row 484](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/7230e0b94b2651a83025db688803095afd115cc1/reviews/flood-triage.md#L484) kept D2-11 (two opposed witnesses, not a flat score) and D2-02 (route contract between two individually safe edits); none entered the top ten. Develop those two ideas only. The review treats retained strategy sets as permissive control and inner/outer feasible-set tracking as set-membership identification. Neither alone earns a card here. [ART8](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/edce4e7fd82967d02aeda7be2b76ee47f352c630/ideas/flood/cross-pool-86.md) already tests whether retained corrections and their diagnostics remain jointly callable; [IP19 in #337](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/337) tracks multi-writer collisions. Require a gain over checking the prospective pair jointly, or over the strongest exact formulation at a different resource point. A new label for a collision or a replay buffer does not count.

## Literature check

Targeted scholarly searches checked primary-source abstracts and pertinent sections. Each card gives a mechanism-level comparison, not a claim to priority.

| Closest line | The burden of proof here |
|---|---|
| [Permissive Controller Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04662) synthesizes multiple allowed actions in stochastic games while guaranteeing a specified property; [Yin and Lafortune](https://xiangyin.sjtu.edu.cn/Paper/16TACuniform.pdf) synthesize maximally permissive property-enforcing supervisors for partial observation, including diagnosability. [Shielding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611) filters or corrects actions to enforce a temporal safety property. | A fixed finite model can encode edits as actions and use one synthesized supervisor. Include that control with equivalent history, sensors and future-edit information. Claim a benefit only if local, reusable contracts yield a better resource/permissiveness tradeoff or a clearly different retained operation. |
| [SafeMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04753) expands the safe region from a known safe seed under deterministic dynamics and a learned safety feature, accounting for reachability and returnability; [Wachi and Sui](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wachi20a.html) combine ergodic safe-set expansion and reward optimization under an unknown constraint. [Active Diagnosis](https://home.lmf.cnrs.fr/downloads/SergeHaddad/A15-CDC.pdf) controls what occurs so that faults can be distinguished. | Safe return does not by itself distinguish rival feedback explanations, but re-targeted safe exploration plus exact diagnosis is a strong null. Contrast a safe service state with a belief state where a particular old disagreement is actually testable. |
| [Recursive set-membership identification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01714) finds parameter uncertainty intervals under bounded input/output errors, using convex relaxation; [Skalse et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/skalse23a.html) characterize feedback-equivalent reward models and their consequences for decisions; [robust ordinal regression](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-013-5365-4) tests possible versus necessary comparisons. | Against the *updated* exact set, two witnesses prove openness only while both are admissible; losing one proves neither closure nor indifference. Compare equal-byte approximations and warm-started exact extrema before crediting a new repair operation. |
| [Assume–guarantee verification for interface automata](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-68237-0_10) composes local verification; [Pacti](https://doi.org/10.1145/3704736) supports composition, quotient and simplification of assume–guarantee contracts; [invariant confluence](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf) characterizes when independent valid transactions can merge without coordination. | Do not claim that assume–guarantee reasoning or pairwise unsafe merges are new. Compare against an explicit contract with the same edit summaries, risk budgets and observables. |

**Common first assay.** Use the review's four cells first: hold visible old A/B task accuracy and the benefit of new writes C/D fixed, independently vary unsupported commitment and reachability of lawful evidence, then attempt one scoped resolution. Keep both old preference scopes and an untouched ordinary task in A/B; evaluate after C, after D, and after resolution. Distinguish (1) two feasible opposed worlds, (2) a proved choice or proved tie, and (3) missing certification. Record reachability separately; failure to find a second model is not evidence that all models agree. Serving a reversible default is not a learning commit. Use an independent oracle to generate worlds and audit accuracy. Count successful diagnosis by horizon and per-cohort service cost at a fixed false-alarm ceiling, include a reversal no policy can identify, and never count externally injected evidence as evidence of reachability.

**Controls and accounting.** Every card adds its listed control to the applicable shared ones: for witnesses, margin/entropy thresholds, equal-byte ensembles, EWC or episodic/logit replay (including old open statuses), and full, incrementally updated feasible sets; for routes, DF10 rechecked after every write, single-backup routes, conservative dueling bandits at matched service cost, safe returnable exploration plus diagnosis, an exact finite contingent controller, and a second write that misses the route. For cards about both, compare the best witness repair and route contract running independently, with equal queries and total state. Charge witness/model precision, solver or replanning calls, storage, write latency, interactions, and refusals that an oracle would not require. Include a lucky unsupported closure and an impossible case, and never relax consent to obtain a positive result. All first tests are proposals, not runs.

## A · Repair the two opposed worlds without keeping the whole version space

### D3-01 · Exchange a dying witness through one certified pivot
- **Primitive:** Store a feasible opposed pair plus a small range of certified pivots for each member. If a feedback constraint invalidates one world, try a pivot inside the verified range on that side of the contrast before searching all remaining worlds; retire the certificate if nothing can be repaired.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [LP warm starts after constraint-matrix changes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04151) reuse a basis; [set-membership identification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01714) updates feasible bounds. The proposed state retains signed feasible *repair motions* for the same old comparison, rather than keeping a solver basis or re-estimating all parameter intervals.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Stream cuts across one witness in a five-feature polytope without eliminating its side. Compare cached extrema, solver-basis warm starts, and a same-size pair chosen for margin. Kill if pivots do not renew sound opposition at lower total state and solve cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Check labeled A/B and the genuinely open pair after each cut; then present a cut that eliminates one entire side and confirm it is not treated like a failed pivot.

### D3-02 · Choose witnesses that last under common corrections without pushing them far apart
- **Primitive:** Among opposed pairs, minimize their different responses to a declared family of unrelated edits while maximizing their minimum signed slack against constraints those edits could actually tighten. Retain shared nuisance coordinates only once.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Robust ordinal regression](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-013-5365-4) optimizes comparison possibilities over a compatible family; [disagreement compression](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume16/wiener15a/wiener15a.pdf) compresses information about the disagreement region. Neither comparison selects a *coupled* certificate for survival of two opposite decisions under a known class of downstream writes.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Build two equally separated pairs, one fragile along the nuisance edits. Compare signed extrema and maximum-minimum slack with independently chosen witnesses; test on a held-out edit within the declared family. Kill if coupling adds no savings at the same correctly open coverage.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B accuracy and pair validity after C/D, including an old decided case whose slack does not authorize reopening.

### D3-03 · Carry a contradiction between worlds through a representation change
- **Primitive:** Preserve both worlds using a shared outcome decoder and a small contrast patch. After a feature rewrite verify the decoder on old measurements and reconstruct each rival outcome order; refuse a projection that reconstructs both worlds but collapses the decisive difference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Skalse et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/skalse23a.html) characterize invariances; [Dark Experience Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07211) rehearses model outputs. This operator explicitly uses one decoder for two opposite, jointly checkable explanations rather than reproducing a single teacher's predictions.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Mix a harmless linear basis change and a lossy feature merge, both preserving the incumbent outputs. Compare separate compressions of both worlds and an exact feasible-set projection. Kill if a shared decoder never saves bytes at matched revival of the distinction.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old A/B actions under both feature changes and check that only the preserved contrast can support lawful closure.

### D3-04 · Record what would retire each witness
- **Primitive:** Annotate a witness with an independently validated condition on incoming feedback that would falsify it. If neither trigger fires, retain the pair without a full solve only when the conditions cover the declared evidence family; fall back to exact validation outside that family.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Efficient incremental solving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11111) adds assumptions to existing models, and [recursive set membership](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01714) intersects new measurement constraints. This card proposes a small reusable falsifier for *each retained world*, including which events do not require rereading old evidence.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Interleave many irrelevant observations and two events that invalidate different witnesses. Compare sparse residual checks, a trained relevance classifier, and a warm-started solver. Kill if the trigger cache cannot cut validation cost without missing any actual invalidation.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate A/B decisions and both certificates before and after the relevant events, including a legitimate closure and an unaffected twin.

### D3-05 · Leave no side without a repair budget
- **Primitive:** When two retained worlds remain feasible but have different recovery options, allocate evidence memory asymmetrically: save constraints that screen candidate repairs on the fragile side and reuse the stronger side's witness. Do not spend equal memory on two worlds by default.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Disagreement-based compression](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume16/wiener15a/wiener15a.pdf) retains training constraints characterizing a version space; [Helly certificates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05225) bound support in structured problems. Here the retained unit is a sign-specific repair basis, not a global approximate feasible set or a proof of infeasibility.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Generate a wide positive region and a narrow nonconvex negative region (a finite union of polytopes suffices). Compare equal per-sign memory, hardest-constraint retention, and an exact delta log. Reject if asymmetry only improves performance by silently recording more negative-side data.
- **Old-task retention:** Audit A/B and the rarer sign after C/D; when the fragile sign truly disappears, prove it rather than perpetually reopening it.

### D3-06 · Build a pair that rules out a flaky feedback model as the explanation for openness
- **Primitive:** Retain opposed decisions under the *same* declared noise mechanism and mark cases where opposition is possible only by switching noise assumptions between worlds. Carry a second, outer state of “model-sensitive unknown” rather than treating that ambiguity as confirmed preference disagreement.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Semantic Identifiability Frontier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20450) distinguishes persistent disagreement from channel effects; [robust MDPs beyond rectangularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00215) allow coupled uncertainty. Here coupled nuisance settings qualify how a two-world certificate can be interpreted after live feedback changes.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Make opposite answers feasible either under one shared channel or only under two different assumed error rates. Compare an independent per-world ensemble and exact joint inference with fixed channel assumptions. Kill if coupled witnesses cannot cheaply tell the cases apart or falsely certify robustness.
- **Old-task retention:** After C/D channel changes, test old predictable choices and whether old A/B claims move between the two kinds of uncertainty only when justified.

### D3-07 · Require a blocking certificate before deleting the last witness on one side
- **Primitive:** Pair the positive certificate of openness with a separate closure interface: a side can be declared impossible only by returning a verifiable subset of evidence and assumptions that rules it out. A failed repair keeps an explicit uncertified state.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Helly systems and certificates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05225) give small infeasibility supports; [incremental SAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11111) works with changing assumptions. The operation binds the negative certificate specifically to retirement of one member of a durable opposed pair and allows a cheap later test of whether that closure proof survives.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Arrange one cut that deletes a cached witness and a different cut that proves its entire side empty. Compare an exact incremental feasibility solver and its native infeasibility output against proof reuse after a later assumption changes. Reject if proof reuse does not save rechecking at identical closure soundness.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify A/B accuracy, retained openness after the first cut, and closure only after a valid impossibility proof.

### D3-08 · Find a replacement pair only where the evidence developed a hole
- **Primitive:** On receipt of a formerly missing evidence type, ask an exact solver once which feature block made the old pair fail. Store a tested restriction on where the next opposing pair can differ so similar later failures can search only that block, with an exact fallback and a logged miss rate.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [CEGAR](https://doi.org/10.1145/876638.876643) refines an abstraction after spurious counterexamples; [online selective sampling](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/hanneke21a/hanneke21a.pdf) trades mistakes for queries. The proposed locality is learned for replacement of the specific observationally equivalent witness pair, not for proving a fixed system property.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Repeatedly add evidence to one nuisance-feature block, then inject a migration moving sparsity elsewhere. Compare coordinate search, solver warm starts, and CEGAR over the same abstraction. Reject if saved solves are offset by repair misses or stale sparsity.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and the untouched comparison's witnesses after both local and nonlocal evidence events.

### D3-09 · Reidentify a witness by its lawful predictions, not by an old identifier
- **Primitive:** Keep a minimal sketch of predictions on permitted validation interventions. When a world model is regenerated, match each replacement to those predictions and to its contrast sign; incompatible predictions invalidate the old certificate even if the signed answer stayed the same.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Predictive representations of state](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Paper.pdf) represent state via observable tests; [Skalse et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/skalse23a.html) characterize invariance under feedback. This proposal uses preselected observable tests to renew an old *pair* after model replacement, without requiring old world identifiers or full model reconstruction.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Give two regenerated pairs the same action signs and fit on ordinary A/B labels, but only one the old response predictions. Compare raw score replay, functional matching on a uniform probe set, and exact world comparison. Kill if scoped matching cannot reject incorrect replacement at lower cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B before and after regeneration and a formerly unanswered pair whose two worlds must still predict the right disagreement.

### D3-10 · Admit when exact pair retention needs forbidden memory
- **Primitive:** Attach a resource bound to a witness pair: name the allowed future cuts under which it can be repaired from retained state. If a broader edit family makes exact repair impossible without retaining unavailable old constraints, expose that limitation before letting an edit close the comparison.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Streaming linear programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05617) studies pass and memory bounds for low-dimensional LP; [set membership](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01714) commonly approximates feasible sets. The question is what memory is necessary for *openness plus exact later closure*, and whether a persistent pair can carry a usable declaration of that boundary.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Construct old streams with identical stored extrema but different interior candidate survivors under a later cut. Compare a fixed two-world store, a sketch, and a full LP; test a restricted cut family first. Kill if the restricted family still needs essentially complete retained evidence, or if the declared bound predicts nothing beyond measured memory.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify matching visible A/B performance while separately auditing the pair after benign and adversarial C/D cuts.

## B · Make individually safe edits safe to combine

### D3-11 · Return the missing guarantee as an edit quotient
- **Primitive:** After one edit, compute the weakest promise a peer edit must make about remaining observations and transitions for an old distinguishing plan to exist. Give the peer admission conditions, not a fixed fallback path or a blanket prohibition.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Pacti](https://doi.org/10.1145/3704736) computes contract quotients. Here the tested representation is a compact quotient of *contingent distinguishability* under independent writes; a generic Pacti-style quotient over the same finite state is a strong null.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Give the peer three ways to remove one branch and one way to kill all branches. Compare a single backup, a symbolic general contract quotient, and joint contingent diagnosis. Kill if the specialized quotient does not save state or recomputation at matched permissiveness.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old service and per-cohort diagnosis after each admitted peer edit and include a peer edit disjoint from the routes.

### D3-12 · Certify when a peer edit cannot affect a route
- **Primitive:** Attach a checked frame to an edit: which observations and transitions it cannot alter under a protected test prefix. A syntactically disjoint peer edit may reuse the contract only if its dependencies also satisfy that semantic frame.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Incremental component verification](https://doi.org/10.1145/2465449.2465456) reuses results for unmodified components; [interface automata](https://doi.org/10.1145/503209.503226) analyze compatibility through component interfaces. The frame includes effects of a learned shared representation on observations, even when writes name different components.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two edits touch disjoint parameter blocks but share a decision threshold that changes which reports are emitted. Compare static read/write sets and full incremental rechecking. Kill if behavioral frame proofs do not avoid more needless rechecks than those controls without missing a lost route.
- **Old-task retention:** Test old A/B success and both witnesses after disjoint benign writes and shared-threshold interference.

### D3-13 · Quantify over compatible histories, not independent worst cases
- **Primitive:** A write contract states which effect combinations are possible on a *shared* history. Two adverse effects that cannot happen together do not justify banning the pair, but excluding them requires a checkable common-cause restriction.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Robust MDPs beyond rectangularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00215) already model coupled uncertainty. The proposed fragment exposes only the history coupling needed by two writers to exchange a small, reusable route certificate.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** A mode bit makes each edit remove one route, but never both on the same history; then remove that coupling. Compare rectangular robust diagnosis and a full nonrectangular product solver. Reject if the compact coupling fails to reproduce both cases or cannot beat re-solving on resource cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure A/B service and diagnosis conditional on each actual mode, not an average over favorable histories.

### D3-14 · Find one observation that picks a lawful write order
- **Primitive:** When either order alone can strand the route in some hidden states, synthesize a pre-write observation whose possible outputs select a certified safe order. Return infeasible if the order selector requires unobservable state or the query cannot lawfully be made.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Contingent planning](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v28i1.9049) plans with partial information; [interface assume–guarantee verification](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-68237-0_10) checks compatibility. Here the contract supplies a decision to schedule the writes *before* either takes effect, rather than a route for gathering evidence afterwards.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Let A-then-B work in one mode and B-then-A in another; offer one legal noisy or exact mode probe. Compare fixed serialization and a joint contingent planner including the scheduling decision. Kill if the contract offers no resource advantage or treats the noisy case as exact.
- **Old-task retention:** Test both old cohorts after the first and second write, including one mode where no permitted observation can choose the order safely.

### D3-15 · Store a joint blocker instead of stacking individual vetoes
- **Primitive:** Return a sparse set of edit effects that is sufficient to destroy every distinguishing plan, then constrain the write selector not to admit that entire set together. An edit in the blocker remains admissible with unrelated peers.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Invariant confluence](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf) identifies unsafe merges; [dual-failure sensitive reachability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07982) handles graph updates. Here blockers must cover observation-conditioned diagnosis with a power threshold, not just deletion of the last path or a bad database state.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Compare harmful and harmless edit pairs with identical edit sizes. Use disjoint-path counts, learned conflict tables, and exact pair search. Kill if blocker reuse on unseen edit pairs fails to save enough calls to justify maintaining it.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old tasks through each surviving route and a harmless pair that any blanket veto would have blocked.

### D3-16 · Let a repair pay back only the lost guarantee
- **Primitive:** If a pair of writes fails together, search a declared small repair space for a substitute transition or report mapping that restores the old diagnostic obligation while leaving both intended new effects intact. Verify the restored route rather than reverting either edit.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Path-based program repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.04914) uses failing paths to constrain repair; [SafeMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04753) computes safe returnable regions. The repaired object here is an observation that separates a particular preference pair under a joint deployment, not the old software state.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two individually safe edits remove opposite branches; an unused action can expose the missing report without reversing either effect. Compare undoing one write, generic path-based repair, and exact joint re-synthesis under matched permitted actions. Reject if the restricted repair is not useful at equal service cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Test both old task families and the repaired correction workflow after both intended changes are visible.

### D3-17 · Preserve the contract across one hidden phase transition
- **Primitive:** Give a route promise a verified checkpoint condition on the new learner's state, not a wall-clock timeout. If the peer write starts a phase in which the original path temporarily vanishes, require a different lawful route before the deadline on every affected phase history.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Shielding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611) enforces temporal specifications and [active diagnosis](https://home.lmf.cnrs.fr/downloads/SergeHaddad/A15-CDC.pdf) uses observation histories. The candidate is a compact handoff between phases of two asynchronous writes, with a residual diagnosis deadline carried across the handoff.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Simulate one asynchronous edit deploying observers before transitions and a second deploying them in the opposite order. Compare fixed waiting, final-state-only certification, and a temporal supervisor. Kill if the checkpoint contract cannot maintain the crossing deadline with less overall coordination.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B and protected corrections at every intermediate phase, not only in the terminal deployment.

### D3-18 · Export how much observability a peer can consume
- **Primitive:** Compute slack on both power and false alarms for the current lawful diagnostic controller. Bound each peer's change in the law of the observation-and-action transcript under both worlds, for example by coupling conditional reports along the same history. A bound below both slacks preserves the test without assuming that unchanged reachability means unchanged power.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Blackwell and Le Cam experiment comparison](https://maxim.ece.illinois.edu/pubs/raginsky_ISIT11.pdf) quantifies experiment approximation; [permissive control](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04662) synthesizes allowable strategies. The candidate exports a compositional budget for degradation of an existing *diagnostic test*, including the false-alarm side, not a state reachability flag.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two individually benign channel changes jointly erase usable power without cutting any path. Compare separate round-by-round budgets and exact probabilistic synthesis. Reject if the composed bound is so conservative that it blocks useful edits exact synthesis would allow.
- **Old-task retention:** Audit A/B success, fixed false-alarm error and finite-horizon power under the composed channel, including a rare cohort with lower slack.

### D3-19 · Make a contract provable against unseen peers inside an edit type
- **Primitive:** Allow a family of peer edits if a small checked abstraction maps their concrete transitions to one contract action. On a mismatch, refine only the abstract feature that changes the old distinguishing plan rather than accepting a classifier's guess of compatibility.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [CEGAR](https://doi.org/10.1145/876638.876643) refines abstraction with counterexamples; [assume–guarantee learning](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/11513988_52) discovers compositional assumptions. The candidate makes the interface reusable across learned write implementations while the old contrast remains fixed.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Enumerate edit types across two implementations with one semantically new peer. Compare typed allowlists and CEGAR over the full product. Kill if the contract generalizes only by allowing an unsound unseen transition or never reduces verification work.
- **Old-task retention:** Test old A/B and contrast reachability after two allowed new implementations and one distribution shift that forces refinement.

### D3-20 · Contract for an observable return, not merely a safe physical state
- **Primitive:** Let an edit return to a physically safe state only if it also returns to a belief class in which a protected old comparison remains distinguishable. Compose contracts in terms of the observable histories that reach that belief class.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [SafeMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04753) checks safe returnability and [almost-sure reachability in POMDPs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00085) supports belief-based policy construction. This variant closes the gap between physical reset and retained disambiguation after two edits fuse reports on the return path; the joint belief planner is the exact control.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two edit sequences return to the same safe screen but only one preserves evidence of how it was reached. Compare safe-return exploration, exact belief diagnosis, and a persistent return contract. Reject if the contract neither changes which sequences pass nor saves re-solving.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure old routine success from both returns and the correction power for users whose histories can no longer be distinguished.

## C · Test whether either object makes the other smaller

### D3-21 · Keep an opposed pair that survives *as a diagnosable pair*
- **Primitive:** Select admissible opposite worlds and one contract together so they remain opposed and diagnosable under a declared set of peer edits. Refuse to infer that the whole set of possible worlds is diagnosable merely because this pair is; report the uncovered alternatives separately.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Decision-region determination](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v33/javdani14.html) selects tests to find regions sharing a decision; [ART8](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/edce4e7fd82967d02aeda7be2b76ee47f352c630/ideas/flood/cross-pool-86.md) checks jointly callable reservations. The candidate jointly selects a *small ambiguity certificate* and lawful routes for its survival, rather than scheduling experiments for a fixed set of hypotheses.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two admissible opposed pairs have identical scores but different vulnerability to future edits; introduce an uncovered third rival. Compare independent pair selection plus ART8-style joint checking and an exact version-space controller. Kill if joint selection offers no additional late-witness coverage at equal compute and state.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old A/B and all three rival cases after C and D; never count an uncovered rival as resolved.

### D3-22 · Repair the pair and the route with a shared counterexample
- **Primitive:** When a peer write breaks the old certificate, use one feasible trace to identify both the world assumption it invalidated and the observation the replacement route requires. Recompile both from the same witnessed failure or explicitly decompose when the failures are independent.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [CEGAR](https://doi.org/10.1145/876638.876643) refines a model using spurious traces; [active diagnosis](https://home.lmf.cnrs.fr/downloads/SergeHaddad/A15-CDC.pdf) designs distinguishing traces. The claim is an interaction: one example can constrain witness repair *and* route repair enough to need fewer validation interactions than separate loops.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** A peer write changes the meaning of one report and removes access to its old context. Compare stacked independent pair repair and route re-synthesis; include a negative case where channel semantics and route access change independently. Kill if the shared trace saves no interactions on the coupled case.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old A/B outputs and diagnosis power after repair, including the independent case that must not inherit an unrelated certificate.

### D3-23 · Carry a smallest separating experiment plus a spare contrast
- **Primitive:** Store an experiment that distinguishes the current opposed pair and a different lawful contrast that can distinguish a repaired pair if one current member fails. The spare is a probe of a decision-relevant property, not a third world held in reserve.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dynamic observers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2810) choose sensors and [disagreement compression](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume16/wiener15a/wiener15a.pdf) represents version-space information. The candidate jointly reserves a cheap repair *experiment* rather than merely retaining an alternate path to the same obsolete test.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Make C tighten one world while D removes the only path to the old contrast; the spare can distinguish a replacement world. Compare a third model at equal bytes and a single backup at equal interactions, with exact joint diagnosis as oracle. Kill if the spare never helps when current pair members change.
- **Old-task retention:** Test A/B before and after activating the spare, including a still-open twin for which the spare has no valid scope.

### D3-24 · Preserve an action that lets the user distinguish preference from misunderstanding
- **Primitive:** Use two opposed worlds only after verifying that their disagreement is about the same observed candidate outcomes. If a peer edit changes how candidates are presented, reserve a control interaction that checks outcome comprehension before either world's preference prediction licenses closure.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Causal preference learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05967) frames confounders of comparisons; [counterfactual transportability](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/correa22a.html) formalizes when causal quantities transfer. The candidate ties a matched-outcome precondition to both world admissibility and edit admission.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** A presentation rewrite changes perceived effort while actual outcomes remain fixed. Compare presentation-blind pairs, a static comprehension check and full causal re-estimation. Reject if coupling the precondition to the contract cannot prevent unsupported closure with no additional burden.
- **Old-task retention:** Check ordinary A/B including clarity-sensitive tasks; preserve opposition in the matched-outcome condition and revalidate it after presentation changes.

### D3-25 · Use opposite responses to test whether a route contract transfers
- **Primitive:** When porting a route contract to a learned replacement controller, test it with two opposed but historically compatible world models, not just rollouts from the current favorite. Only transfer the contract if the new controller can separate the responses without knowing which world it occupies.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Interface automata](https://doi.org/10.1145/503209.503226) analyze compatible replacements; [predictive state representations](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Paper.pdf) model observable tests. The proposed transfer check reuses an old ambiguity certificate as the fault model for a new learned controller.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** A replacement preserves typical trajectories but cannot reach one diagnostically necessary observation under the negative world. Compare target-world rollouts and independent contract re-synthesis. Kill if the paired transfer check finds no additional failures or costs as much as full rechecking without its guarantees.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure old A/B service from the replacement controller and lawful reachability under both worlds, conditional on old starting histories.

### D3-26 · Factor a failed admission into a world obstruction and a route obstruction
- **Primitive:** Decompose a failed proof into the feedback assumptions that make opposition admissible and the peer effects that make all allowed tests powerless. Only unblock when new evidence truly eliminates a side, or a revised edit restores a test; replacing one failed certificate must not silently satisfy the other.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Incremental SAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11111) works with assumption sets and [Pacti](https://doi.org/10.1145/3704736) supports compositional contracts. This certificate makes the two repairs independently checkable and tests whether sharing the boundary between them produces more admissible *joint* edits than using two standalone guards.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Run the four-cell assay, then offer matched-cost repairs that fix only the wrong side or both sides. Compare stacked D2-11 + D2-02, ART8-style joint checks and exact joint inference and diagnosis. Kill if the decomposition cannot reduce solver work or needless refusal without increasing unsupported closure.
- **Old-task retention:** Report old A/B, pair status and per-cohort route power after each write, after each attempted repair, and after the one lawful resolution.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **What is the smallest state that can distinguish the four failure cells after two writes? (D3-01, D3-07, D3-15, D3-26)** Start with the row-484 assay at exactly matched visible old scores. Match bytes, digits of precision, and permissible observations against full incremental set membership, exact contingent diagnosis, and the separate D2-11 + D2-02 system. Require the surviving witness to close only its own old comparison. If the compressed state cannot distinguish failed repair from true closure, or a reachable witness from a merely safe return, stop.
2. **Which update families make two opposed witnesses renewable rather than lucky? (D3-02, D3-04, D3-05, D3-10)** Prove a restricted case where sign-specific repair needs substantially less remembered evidence than exact incremental extrema, then construct a same-summary counterexample outside the family. Compare warm-started extrema and equal-byte ensembles, not only flat margins. Separate insufficient memory from changes requiring new user feedback.
3. **Can route contracts compose without collapsing to exact joint synthesis? (D3-11, D3-13, D3-14, D3-18, D3-20)** Derive a sound quotient for two independent edits under finite-horizon partial observation with a matched false-alarm budget. Test a route-hitting peer, a non-hitting peer, and the safe but diagnostically empty return. Drop this line if a generic assume–guarantee supervisor or safe returnability plus fresh diagnosis ties at equal verification and service cost.
4. **Is there a genuine interaction between choosing witnesses and choosing route contracts? (D3-21, D3-22, D3-23, D3-25)** Test whether pair selection genuinely reduces joint verification or interaction cost when the old experiments can be invalidated by a later write. Use independently optimized, stacked controls and a negative case with no coupling. If the stack explains all gains, keep the two claims separate.
