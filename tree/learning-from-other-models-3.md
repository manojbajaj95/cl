# Learning from other models 3: Simulated-upgrade rehearsal and upgrade write-skew

Child of [PR #359](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/359) ([depth-1 node](learning-from-other-models-2.md)) · Depth 2 · 27 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** Two specific failure modes govern whether a continual learner's skills survive a base-model upgrade without dragging obsolete baggage forward. First, a predecessor trained for *blank-slate* transfer builds teaching packets that break when handed to a **partly-knowing successor** that already absorbed half the skills (and fixed some of the predecessor's bugs); rehearsing mock drop/migrate/retire handoffs against synthetic partly-knowing successors *during the predecessor's life* directly optimizes for partial inheritance (**L2-25**). Second, even when a new base passes every single-skill check on an absorbed slice $s_1$, dropping $s_1$ while migrating $s_2$ can silently break their composition $s_1 \circ s_2$ because $s_2$ relied on the old model's convention or quirk for $s_1$ (**L2-17**: **write-skew at base upgrades**). Making upgrade rehearsal *coupling-aware* and triage *composition-checked* turns base upgrades from blind adapter porting into modular, non-regressive handoffs.

**Scope.** Deepens only **L2-25** (rehearsing the upgrade during the predecessor's life against a simulated partly-knowing successor) and **L2-17** (checking drop–migrate coupling before dropping an absorbed skill), the two directions kept from [PR #359](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/359) in [flood-triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md) row 494. Excludes PR #359's collapsed variants (generic proxy-committee distillation, permutation averaging, anchor residuals, standalone provenance ledgers, triage-gated proxy-tuning, excision/routing variants) and PR #358's dropped lines. Every card must beat strong named controls—especially post-hoc cross-architecture dataset distillation, equal-byte coresets, GRAFT, Trans-LoRA, independent per-skill triage, and random pairwise probes—at matched bytes, probes, and compute.

**Shared first-test contract.** All tests below are CPU-first proposals on tiny models (MLP, gated MLP, tiny transformer, or rule-routing agents); no experiments were run. Every **L2-25** test trains living learner $M_{\text{old}}$ on sequential tasks $A, B, C$ ($B$ rare), then upgrades to a *real unseen* partly-knowing successor $B_1$ (different architecture family, already competent on a held-out subset $S$ of skills and stronger on one workaround slice), measuring (i) $B_1$'s $K$-step rebuild accuracy on $\bar{S}$, (ii) $B_1$'s retention of its own $S$ and improved slices, and (iii) $M_{\text{old}}$'s living accuracy/retention (**bequest tax**). Every **L2-17** test adapts $B_0$ on skills with explicit pairwise/chained dependencies (e.g. $s_1 \circ s_2$ where $B_1$ absorbs $s_1$ with a slightly different format or fixes a $B_0$ bug that $s_2$ assumed), then evaluates single-skill $s_1$, single-skill $s_2$, composite $s_1 \circ s_2$, and untouched $B_1$ skills at equal probe/update budgets. Named controls apply alongside each card's specific comparator: LM28 alone, LM30 post-hoc, post-hoc equal-byte coreset, cross-architecture dataset distillation (MetaDD-style), GRAFT/Trans-LoRA per-task transport, independent per-skill triage, migrate-all, drop-all-absorbed, and random pairwise probes.

## A · Simulated-upgrade rehearsal during the predecessor's life (L2-25)

### L3-01 · Adversarial absorption-mask rehearsal
- **Mechanism:** Instead of sampling uniform random subsets $S$ of skills for the synthetic partly-knowing successor to "already know," maintain an adversarial mask proposer that picks the absorption subset $S^*$ causing the *worst* drop in surviving-packet rebuild accuracy on $\bar{S}^*$ (or worst damage to $S^*$), and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ on that worst-case mock handoff.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MetaDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05103) and [Generalizing Teacher Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16040) average over blank-slate student architectures; L2-25 sampled uniform random subsets $S$. Worst-case absorption-mask rehearsal targets the *hardest partial-knowledge splits* that uniform sampling rarely hits.
- **Cheapest first test:** 6 tasks ($A_1..A_6$) where 2 pairs share strong hidden features; evaluate handoff to unseen $B_1$ across all $\binom{6}{3}$ real absorption splits. Compare uniform-mask L2-25, post-hoc cross-architecture distillation, and adversarial-mask rehearsal at equal proxy steps. Kill if uniform-mask L2-25 matches worst-split accuracy on $B_1$.
- **Old-task retention:** Report $M_{\text{old}}$'s living $A/B$ tax plus $B_1$'s worst-split retention of both $S$ and rare task $B \in \bar{S}$.

### L3-02 · Contradicted-workaround rehearsal against successor imprinting
- **Mechanism:** Give the synthetic partly-knowing successor *strictly better* ground-truth answers on a random subset $W$ of $M_{\text{old}}$'s high-entropy/uncertain slices (simulating $B_1$ fixing $B_0$'s bugs), run a mock handoff that retires $W$ and rebuilds $\bar{W}$, and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ whenever rebuilding $\bar{W}$ *imprints* $M_{\text{old}}$'s old errors back onto $W$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Imprinting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18517) demonstrates that distilled responses carry unintended teacher quirks into students; [Student-Friendly Teachers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07650) optimizes blank-slate accuracy. Rehearsing *explicit contradiction by a smarter successor* trains $M_{\text{old}}$ to keep its packet locally scoped so retired flaws do not bleed through surviving slices.
- **Cheapest first test:** $M_{\text{old}}$ learns 4 clean private rules + 2 noisy workarounds; real unseen $B_1$ knows the clean truth on the 2 workarounds. Compare blank-slate LM28, L2-25 without contradiction, and contradicted-workaround rehearsal at equal bytes. Kill if post-hoc L2-16 anti-regression anchors on $B_1$ match zero-imprinting accuracy at equal compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $B_1$'s retention of its better answers on $W$ alongside migrated rare rule $B \in \bar{W}$ and $M_{\text{old}}$'s living accuracy.

### L3-03 · Convention-mismatched absorption rehearsal
- **Mechanism:** When pre-equipping the synthetic successor with competence on absorbed subset $S$, train the synthetic successor on an *alternative valid convention* for $S$ (e.g., permuted labels/format/serialization equivalent under the task semantics) rather than $M_{\text{old}}$'s exact outputs, and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if rebuilding $\bar{S}$ fails or collides with the successor's different convention for $S$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Representational Alignment Supports Machine Teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04302) aligns student and teacher representations; L2-25 assumed the synthetic successor knew $S$ in $M_{\text{old}}$'s own format. Rehearsing *convention-mismatched* absorption prepares $M_{\text{old}}$'s packet for real future bases whose pretraining conventions differ from $B_0$.
- **Cheapest first test:** Real $B_1$ knows $S$ in format $F_1$ while $M_{\text{old}}$ learned $S$ in format $F_0$; compare same-convention L2-25, MetaDD-style distillation, and convention-mismatched rehearsal. Kill if same-convention L2-25 matches $B_1$'s post-rebuild accuracy on both $S$ and $\bar{S}$.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $B_1$'s $S$ accuracy in format $F_1$, migrated $\bar{S}$ (including rare $B$), and $M_{\text{old}}$'s living $A/B$ accuracy.

### L3-04 · Tail-only (partial-absorption) successor rehearsal
- **Mechanism:** Pre-train the synthetic successor on the *high-frequency 80% bulk* of every task in $S$ while withholding the 20% rare exception tail, then run mock tail-peeling rebuilds from $M_{\text{old}}$'s packet and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if patching the 20% tail degrades the synthetic successor's 80% bulk.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Difficulty-Aware Statistical Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34299) weights hard examples for *blank-slate* dataset distillation. Rehearsing against a *bulk-knowing, tail-missing* successor directly optimizes $M_{\text{old}}$'s packet for surgical tail inheritance without bulk forgetting.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4 tasks each with 80% head rules (known by real unseen $B_1$) and 20% rare exceptions (unknown by $B_1$); compare full-task L2-25, equal-byte tail coreset, and tail-only rehearsal. Kill if a post-hoc tail-only coreset ties on $B_1$'s joint (head + tail) accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Report $B_1$'s 80% head retention and 20% rare-tail accuracy separately, plus $M_{\text{old}}$'s living head/tail scores.

### L3-05 · Entrenched-prior (low-temperature) successor rehearsal
- **Mechanism:** Train the synthetic partly-knowing successor with high confidence / small learning-rate plasticity on its pre-known subset $S$ (simulating a heavily pretrained foundation model whose weights resist small nudges), and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if its $K$-step packet fails to install $\bar{S}$ without requiring destructive learning rates on the entrenched successor.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Cross-architecture dataset distillation ([MetaDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05103), [Drifting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16642)) tests freshly initialized plastic networks. Rehearsing against an *entrenched, low-plasticity* partly-knowing proxy matches the actual dynamics of upgrading onto a strong pretrained $B_1$.
- **Cheapest first test:** Real $B_1$ is heavily pretrained (10x epochs) on $G \cup S$; compare plastic-proxy L2-25, post-hoc dataset distillation, and entrenched-prior rehearsal at fixed $K$-step budget on $B_1$. Kill if raising $B_1$'s rebuild learning rate with plastic-proxy L2-25 achieves the same $\bar{S}$ gain without extra $S$ forgetting.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $B_1$'s $G \cup S$ retention alongside $\bar{S}$ ($B$) acquisition under the fixed $K$-step budget.

### L3-06 · Factored architecture-by-knowledge rehearsal grid
- **Mechanism:** Sample synthetic successors from a 2D factor grid—varying both *architecture family* (linear, MLP, gated MLP) and *pre-known skill subset* $S$—and isolate the interaction penalty (cases where a packet works on a blank-slate foreign architecture *and* on a same-architecture partly-knowing proxy, yet fails on a *foreign partly-knowing* proxy).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MetaDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05103) varies only architecture; L2-25 varied only partial knowledge on one proxy. Testing the *architecture $\times$ partial-knowledge interaction* isolates whether joint rehearsal is strictly stronger than adding LM28 + same-arch rehearsal.
- **Cheapest first test:** 2x2 ablation during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life (same vs foreign proxy $\times$ blank vs partly-knowing proxy), evaluated on a *third* unseen foreign partly-knowing $B_1$. Kill if the additive baseline (blank foreign proxy + partly-knowing same-arch proxy) matches joint rehearsal.
- **Old-task retention:** Report $M_{\text{old}}$'s living bequest tax and $B_1$'s accuracy on $S$, $\bar{S}$, and rare $B$.

### L3-07 · Two-hop nested-upgrade rehearsal ($B_0 \to \tilde{B}_1 \to \tilde{B}_2$)
- **Mechanism:** During $M_{\text{old}}$'s life, run a compact two-hop mock upgrade: $\tilde{B}_1$ absorbs $S_1$ and rebuilds $\bar{S}_1$ from the packet, then $\tilde{B}_2$ absorbs $S_2$ (regressing on one slice of $S_1$) and rebuilds from the surviving packet + $\tilde{B}_1$'s updated packet—penalizing $M_{\text{old}}$ if skills decay or drift across two hops.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638), [Trans-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258), and L2-25 optimize only a single $B_0 \to B_1$ hop. Two-hop rehearsal tests whether living-time regularization prevents *multi-generational fidelity decay*.
- **Cheapest first test:** Real 3-model chain $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$ across three distinct architectures with one non-monotonic regression at $B_2$; compare 1-hop L2-25, post-hoc coreset re-distillation per hop, and 2-hop rehearsal at equal bytes. Kill if 1-hop L2-25 matches generation-2 accuracy on generation-0 rare task $B$.
- **Old-task retention:** Score generation-0 tasks ($A$ and rare $B$) on $B_2$ after both hops, plus $B_2$'s native skills.

### L3-08 · Tangent-space (linearized) partly-knowing successor rehearsal
- **Mechanism:** Replace repeated inner-loop $K$-step proxy retraining during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life with a cheap first-order tangent-space / Jacobian approximation of a partly-knowing proxy's rebuild step, backpropagating the mock drop/migrate/retire loss into $M_{\text{old}}$ at a fraction of bilevel meta-learning cost.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Influence Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16859) linearizes static dataset distillation; L2-25's naive bilevel proxy loop is expensive on living continual streams. Linearized partly-knowing rehearsal tests whether the L2-25 gain survives at practical per-step compute.
- **Cheapest first test:** Match wall-clock FLOPs between full bilevel L2-25 (run rarely), tangent-space rehearsal (run frequently), and post-hoc equal-byte coreset, evaluated on unseen $B_1$. Kill if rare full bilevel L2-25 beats tangent-space rehearsal at matched wall-clock FLOPs.
- **Old-task retention:** Compare living $A/B$ accuracy (bequest tax) and $B_1$ inherited $A/B$ accuracy at identical total training FLOPs.

### L3-09 · Interference-triggered rehearsal gating to minimize living bequest tax
- **Mechanism:** Do not run synthetic-upgrade rehearsal on every continual update to $M_{\text{old}}$. Trigger L2-25 rehearsal *only* when a new update's gradient or feature-change norm overlaps previously packed skills above threshold $\tau$, avoiding unnecessary living-model distortion on disjoint tasks.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Spectral Gradient Surgery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18836) applies uniform surgery across all synthetic updates; uniform L2-25 taxes every living update. Interference-gated rehearsal tests whether living bequest tax can be cut without losing successor gain.
- **Cheapest first test:** 8-task stream (4 disjoint tasks, 4 overlapping tasks); compare always-on L2-25, no rehearsal, and interference-gated rehearsal at matched total rehearsal steps. Kill if always-on L2-25 with a smaller global $\lambda$ dominates the living-vs-successor Pareto curve.
- **Old-task retention:** Report both $M_{\text{old}}$'s living accuracy on all 8 tasks and $B_1$'s inherited accuracy on disjoint vs overlapping tasks.

### L3-10 · Post-upgrade editability rehearsal (mock handoff + one fresh amendment)
- **Mechanism:** Extend L2-25's mock handoff by applying *one fresh authorized amendment* to the synthetic partly-knowing successor immediately after it rebuilds $\bar{S}$, and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if the rebuilt skill resists that amendment—ensuring inherited skills arrive *editable*, not frozen.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Flood-triage row 493's **PAX5** and row 490's **D5-13** test post-transfer editability at the *student*; L2-25 tested only static post-rebuild accuracy. Rehearsing *post-handoff editability* during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life bakes amendability directly into the teaching packet.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hand off to unseen $B_1$, then apply a 3-step amendment to migrated rule $B$ on $B_1$; compare static L2-25, post-hoc dataset distillation, and editability-rehearsed L2-25 at equal bytes. Kill if static L2-25 matches $B_1$'s post-amendment accuracy and retention of $A$.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $B_1$'s accuracy on the amended rule $B'$, untouched old rule $A$, and $B_1$'s pre-known $S$ after the amendment.

## B · Upgrade write-skew: drop–migrate & retire–migrate coupling (L2-17)

### L3-11 · Pre-migration counterfactual convention-swap probe
- **Mechanism:** Before migrating *anything* onto $B_1$, detect drop–migrate write-skew cheaply by splicing $B_1$'s native output on absorbed slice $s_1$ directly into $M_0$'s input/intermediate slot for candidate-migrated slice $s_2$. Any accuracy drop on $s_1 \circ s_2$ flags coupling *before* spending migration compute.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638) and [Trans-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258) port adapters before discovering composition bugs; [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) probes already-merged adapters on one base. Splicing $B_1$'s absorbed output into $M_0$ catches convention write-skew *zero-training* prior to upgrade.
- **Cheapest first test:** 12 skills with 4 hidden $s_1 \to s_2$ convention couplings (where $B_1$ absorbs $s_1$ in a new format); compare post-migration probing, independent per-skill triage, and counterfactual splice probing at equal forward passes. Kill if independent triage + post-hoc joint check catches the same pairs at equal compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify all 4 coupled compositions $s_1 \circ s_2$ plus uncoupled $A/B$ survive on $B_1$.

### L3-12 · Minimal interface-adapter bridge instead of re-migrating absorbed $s_1$
- **Mechanism:** When L2-17 detects that dropping absorbed $s_1$ breaks migrated $s_2$ due to a format/convention mismatch, do *not* discard $B_1$'s superior $s_1$ by re-migrating all of $s_1$. Keep $B_1$'s native $s_1$ and install a tiny *seam bridge* (rank-1/2 or prompt-level translator) active only on the $s_1 \to s_2$ boundary.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** L2-17 promoted $s_1$'s convention back into the migrate set (risking overwriting $B_1$'s better $s_1$); [SDO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13820) deconflicts full multi-LoRA subspaces. A *seam-only bridge* preserves $B_1$'s upgrade gain on $s_1$ while repairing $s_1 \circ s_2$.
- **Cheapest first test:** $B_1$ is strictly stronger than $M_0$ on standalone $s_1$ (95% vs 80%) but uses a new output format that breaks $s_2$; compare re-migrating $s_1$, dropping $s_1$, GRAFT/Trans-LoRA migrate-all, and minimal seam bridge at equal parameters. Kill if re-migrating $s_1$ does not hurt standalone $s_1$ on $B_1$.
- **Old-task retention:** Report standalone $s_1$ (must stay near 95% on $B_1$), standalone $s_2$, and composed $s_1 \circ s_2$.

### L3-13 · Witness re-grounding: rewrite $s_2$'s packet onto $B_1$'s native $s_1$
- **Mechanism:** When drop–migrate coupling is found on $s_1 \circ s_2$, instead of keeping $M_0$'s old $s_1$ or adding a runtime bridge, regenerate the $s_1$-dependent prefixes/intermediates inside $s_2$'s teaching witnesses using $B_1$'s native $s_1$, then migrate $s_2$ from those *re-grounded* witnesses so $s_1$ can still be dropped cleanly.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [From LoRA to Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09454) recovers text skill docs from adapters; standard distillation replays $M_0$'s raw trajectories verbatim. *Re-grounding $s_2$'s witnesses onto $B_1$'s native $s_1$* eliminates legacy convention debt permanently instead of carrying it across generations.
- **Cheapest first test:** Same coupled $s_1 \circ s_2$ upgrade as L3-12; compare raw $M_0$-witness migration, L3-12 seam bridge, and $B_1$-re-grounded witness migration at equal update steps. Kill if raw migration + equal-step joint fine-tuning on $B_1$ ties on $s_1 \circ s_2$ and standalone $s_1$.
- **Old-task retention:** Check standalone $s_1$, standalone $s_2$, composite $s_1 \circ s_2$, and untouched $B_1$ tasks after re-grounded migration.

### L3-14 · Transitive dependency-closure triage for multi-hop skill chains
- **Mechanism:** Build a directed coupling graph over slices using pairwise probes; when migrating $s_3$ depends on absorbed $s_2$ which in turn depends on absorbed $s_1$ ($s_1 \to s_2 \to s_3$), compute the *minimal dependency closure* so upstream convention dependencies are bridged or re-grounded in topological order rather than failing silently after one-hop checks.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** L2-17 checked only direct (absorbed, migrated) pairs; [Predicting Mergeability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19549) predicts pairwise adapter conflicts. Topological closure handles *transitive* upgrade write-skew across 3-step skill chains.
- **Cheapest first test:** 3-stage pipeline $s_1 \to s_2 \to s_3$ where $B_1$ absorbs $s_1, s_2$ (with a subtle convention shift at $s_1$) and lacks $s_3$; compare 1-hop L2-17, migrate-all, and dependency-closure triage at equal probe budget. Kill if 1-hop L2-17 catches the failure via $s_2 \circ s_3$ alone.
- **Old-task retention:** Test single stages ($s_1, s_2, s_3$), pairs, and full 3-stage composition $s_1 \circ s_2 \circ s_3$ on $B_1$.

### L3-15 · Co-activation & attention-overlap screening to prune $O(N^2)$ coupling probes
- **Mechanism:** When an adapter holds dozens of skills, probing every (absorbed, migrated) pair is quadratic. Screen candidate pairs using $M_0$'s internal co-activation / subspace-overlap matrix (cheap forward pass on single-skill witnesses) and run composition probes only on the top-$k$ most coupled pairs.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Backbone-Specific LoRA Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27201) and [Predicting Mergeability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19549) measure weight-space overlap for merging; here internal co-activation on $M_0$ prioritizes *functional drop–migrate composition probes* under a tight probe budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** 20 skills (10 absorbed, 10 migrated = 100 pairs) with 6 planted write-skew couplings; cap composition probes at 15. Compare random 15 pairwise probes, weight-cosine screening, and activation-overlap screening. Kill if random 15 probes or simple token-overlap heuristic achieves equal recall of the 6 coupled pairs.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure composite accuracy across all 6 planted couplings (and false-positive overhead on uncoupled $A/B$).

### L3-16 · Retire–migrate write-skew: catching migrated rules built on obsolete $B_0$ bugs
- **Mechanism:** Probe every *retired* slice $s_1$ (where $B_1$ fixed a $B_0$ deficiency) jointly with every *migrated* slice $s_2$ on $B_1$. If $s_2$ was secretly a downstream compensation for $B_0$'s bug in $s_1$, migrating $s_2$ onto fixed $B_1$ causes *double-correction failure*—flagging $s_2$ for retirement or rewrite instead of migration.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** L2-17 only checked *drop–migrate* coupling; [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918) and [Trans-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258) do not model bug-compensation cascades. *Retire–migrate write-skew* catches the opposite failure mode: migrating a rule that only made sense when $B_0$ was broken.
- **Cheapest first test:** Plant 4 skills where $s_2$ compensates for $B_0$'s systematic offset bug in $s_1$, and $B_1$ fixes $s_1$'s offset; compare independent triage, drop–migrate-only L2-17, and retire–migrate coupling checks. Kill if independent single-skill eval on $B_1$ already flags $s_2$ as degraded.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $B_1$'s bug fix in $s_1$ stays intact, compensated pairs $s_1 \circ s_2$ succeed without double-correction, and genuine private rule $B$ still migrates.

### L3-17 · Covering-design three-way probes for higher-order upgrade write-skew
- **Mechanism:** Use a strength-3 combinatorial covering array over high-centrality skills to generate a sub-cubic set of *triple* composition probes $(s_1 \circ s_2 \circ s_3)$, catching rare upgrade write-skew where two absorbed slices individually compose cleanly with migrated $s_3$ yet collide when all three co-occur.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Pairwise conflict checkers ([Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684), L2-17) miss 3-way routing/format interactions; exhaustive triple probing is $O(N^3)$. Covering-array probing bounds higher-order upgrade risk at $O(N^2 \log N)$ or less.
- **Cheapest first test:** 8 skills with a planted 3-way write-skew (only fails when absorbed $s_1$, absorbed $s_2$, and migrated $s_3$ co-occur); compare exhaustive pairwise probes, random triple probes, and covering-array triple probes at equal probe budget. Kill if pairwise probes + random triples match detection rate.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure 3-way composition accuracy alongside all pairwise and single-skill $A/B$ scores on $B_1$.

### L3-18 · Read-only subspace projection of migrated $\Delta^{\text{mig}}$ against $B_1$'s absorbed features
- **Mechanism:** Prevent *reverse* write-skew—where migrating $s_2$ onto $B_1$ overwrites the features $B_1$ uses for absorbed $s_1$—by projecting $\Delta^{\text{mig}}_{s_2}$'s output/write matrices orthogonal to $B_1$'s activation subspace on absorbed $s_1$ witnesses, letting $s_2$ *read* $B_1$'s native $s_1$ representations without writing over them.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [New LoRA Skills Should Read but Never Write](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31600) and [MaLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14397) isolate LoRAs trained sequentially on *one fixed base*. Applying read-only subspace projection *specifically against $B_1$'s absorbed-skill activations* during cross-base migration protects $B_1$'s dropped slices directly.
- **Cheapest first test:** Migrate $s_2$ onto $B_1$ when $s_2$ shares early features with $B_1$'s absorbed $s_1$; compare unconstrained migration, GRAFT/Trans-LoRA transport, general $B_1$ replay, and absorbed-subspace read-only projection at equal updates. Kill if equal-step replay of $s_1$ on $B_1$ achieves the same joint $(s_1, s_2, s_1 \circ s_2)$ accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Score standalone absorbed $s_1$, migrated $s_2$ (and rare $B$), and composed $s_1 \circ s_2$ on $B_1$.

### L3-19 · Dual-trigger shadow-canary for emergent online write-skew
- **Mechanism:** After upgrading to $B_1 + \Delta^{\text{mig}}$, avoid shadowing all live traffic: route *only* live queries whose lightweight routing/activation signature triggers **both** a dropped slice $s_1$ and a migrated slice $s_2$ to shadow-$M_0$, catching unprobed write-skew compositions online at minimal shadow cost.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Online shadow testing (L2-18) samples uniformly across traffic; [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) is offline-only. Focusing shadow budget exclusively on *drop $\cap$ migrate co-triggering queries* targets the exact region where unprobed upgrade write-skew lives.
- **Cheapest first test:** Live deployment stream where 5% of queries are multi-skill drop+migrate compositions containing 3 unprobed write-skew bugs; compare uniform 10% shadowing, uncertainty-only shadowing, and dual-trigger shadowing at equal shadow calls. Kill if uncertainty-only shadowing catches the 3 bugs equally fast.
- **Old-task retention:** Track online repair of the 3 emergent write-skew pairs while verifying zero regression on single-skill $A/B$ and $B_1$ tasks.

### L3-20 · Bilateral seam diagnosis: convention mismatch vs representation interference
- **Mechanism:** Whenever $B_1 + \Delta^{\text{mig}}_{s_2}$ fails a composition probe $s_1 \circ s_2$, run a two-way diagnostic: (a) pipeline $B_1(s_1) \to M_0(s_2)$ in isolation, vs (b) end-to-end $B_1 + \Delta^{\text{mig}}_{s_2}$. If (a) fails, the cause is **convention mismatch** (fix via L3-12 bridge or L3-13 re-grounding); if (a) passes and (b) fails, the cause is **internal weight/feature interference** (fix via L3-18 read-only projection).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Existing adapter-merging diagnostics ([SDO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13820), [Predicting Mergeability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19549)) assume all conflict is weight interference; behavioral probes cannot distinguish convention drift from subspace collision. Bilateral diagnosis routes each write-skew bug to the *right* repair primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 8 write-skew failures (4 planted convention mismatches, 4 planted feature-subspace collisions); compare always-bridge, always-orthogonal-project, and bilateral-diagnosed repair at equal compute. Kill if either single repair fixes both failure types equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Score all 8 composite pairs plus standalone $s_1$, $s_2$, and rare task $B$ on $B_1$.

## C · Coupling-aware upgrade rehearsal (joining L2-25 + L2-17)

### L3-21 · Convention-jittered composition rehearsal during the predecessor's life
- **Mechanism:** Combine L2-25 and L2-17 inside $M_{\text{old}}$'s training loop: give the synthetic partly-knowing successor an absorbed skill $s_1$ whose output convention is *randomly jittered* across valid equivalents, rebuild $s_2$ from $M_{\text{old}}$'s packet, and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if $s_1 \circ s_2$ fails—immunizing $s_2$'s teaching packet against drop–migrate write-skew *before $B_1$ exists*.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Neither cross-architecture dataset distillation ([MetaDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05103)) nor post-hoc coupling checks (L2-17) regularize the living predecessor for *interface invariance*. Training $M_{\text{old}}$ under jittered-convention upgrade rehearsal eliminates convention write-skew at its source.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train $M_{\text{old}}$ on coupled $s_1 \circ s_2$ with and without convention-jittered rehearsal, then upgrade to real unseen $B_1$ that absorbs $s_1$ in a held-out convention $F_{\text{new}}$. Compare unregularized $M_{\text{old}}$ + post-hoc L3-12 bridge vs convention-jittered rehearsal at equal bytes. Kill if post-hoc L3-12/L3-13 repair ties without any living-model bequest tax.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $M_{\text{old}}$'s living accuracy on $s_1, s_2, s_1 \circ s_2$ (tax) and $B_1$'s zero-bridge composite accuracy on $F_{\text{new}}$.

### L3-22 · Write-time skill-dependency ledger compiled into the bequest packet
- **Mechanism:** During $M_{\text{old}}$'s life, whenever a new skill $s_2$ is learned, run a cheap ablation/perturbation against existing skills $s_1$ and record a sparse directed *dependency edge* $(s_1 \to s_2)$ inside the teaching packet metadata—handing $B_1$ an $O(|E|)$ coupling checklist at upgrade time instead of discovering dependencies blindly via $O(N^2)$ probes.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) audits provenance post-hoc; L2-17 paid quadratic probe cost at upgrade time. Logging *causal skill dependencies at write time* amortizes L2-17's discovery cost across the predecessor's life.
- **Cheapest first test:** 16 skills with 8 true directed dependencies; compare $O(N^2)$ blind probing, L3-15 activation screening, and write-time dependency ledger at equal total upgrade probes. Kill if L3-15 activation screening matches recall without any write-time overhead.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify ledger-guided triage catches all 8 coupled pairs (and never silently drops a dependency of rare task $B$).

### L3-23 · Swapped-implementation modularity penalty during upgrade rehearsal
- **Mechanism:** During $M_{\text{old}}$'s simulated-upgrade rehearsal, randomly replace $M_{\text{old}}$'s internal execution of absorbed skill $s_1$ with an *independently trained small teacher* for $s_1$ (different weights, same semantics), and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if downstream skill $s_2$ degrades—explicitly discouraging $M_{\text{old}}$ from relying on idiosyncratic internal features of $s_1$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [New LoRA Skills Should Read but Never Write](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31600) isolates adapters on one base; [Cooperative KD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05942) aligns whole-model logits. Penalizing *internal-implementation coupling* via swapped-module rehearsal forces semantic-interface modularity across future upgrades.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two-stage compositional task $s_1 \circ s_2$ where $M_{\text{old}}$ can cheat by passing an undocumented latent cue from $s_1$ to $s_2$; upgrade to unseen $B_1$ that absorbs $s_1$ cleanly (without the latent cue). Compare standard L2-25, post-hoc distillation, and swapped-implementation rehearsal. Kill if standard L2-25 prevents the latent shortcut equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Check $M_{\text{old}}$'s living $s_1 \circ s_2$ accuracy (tax) and $B_1$'s post-upgrade $s_1 \circ s_2$ accuracy when $s_1$ is dropped.

### L3-24 · Composite-seam witness co-packing for unavoidable couplings
- **Mechanism:** When living rehearsal (L3-21/L3-23) shows that coupling between $s_1$ and $s_2$ cannot be removed without excessive living bequest tax, automatically bundle a minimal *composite seam witness* $(x_{s_1 \circ s_2})$ into $s_2$'s teaching sub-packet—guaranteeing that any future $B_1$ receives both the probe to detect write-skew and the anchor to re-ground $s_2$ on $B_1$'s $s_1$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard per-task coresets and dataset distillation ([Drifting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16642)) pack single-task examples independently. *Tax-triggered co-packing of composite seam witnesses* preserves compact packet size while protecting only the genuinely coupled seams.
- **Cheapest first test:** 10 skills with 3 unavoidable couplings; compare equal-byte single-task packets, uniform pairwise witness packing, and rehearsal-triggered seam co-packing on upgrade to $B_1$. Kill if uniform pairwise witness packing ties at the same byte cap.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $B_1$'s accuracy on the 3 coupled compositions alongside all 10 single skills (including rare $B$).

### L3-25 · Rehearsal-calibrated drop margins for hub vs leaf skills
- **Mechanism:** Use $M_{\text{old}}$'s simulated-upgrade rehearsal to estimate each skill $s$'s *downstream blast radius* (how many other skills degrade when $s$ is replaced by a noisy/shifted synthetic successor), and attach a calibrated **safe-to-drop margin** $\epsilon_s$ to $s$'s sub-packet—requiring $B_1$ to pass a much stricter absorption bar before dropping high-blast-radius hub skills than isolated leaf skills.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Fixed-threshold triage (LM30, [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918)) applies one uniform absorption threshold across all skills. *Blast-radius-calibrated drop margins* concentrate upgrade conservatism on hub skills while letting leaf skills drop aggressively.
- **Cheapest first test:** Skill graph with 3 high-fanout hub skills and 9 leaf skills; upgrade to $B_1$ with slight approximation error on both hubs and leaves. Compare uniform drop threshold (tuned on validation), drop-all, migrate-all, and blast-radius-calibrated thresholds. Kill if a single global threshold matches joint accuracy and packet shrinkage.
- **Old-task retention:** Report composite accuracy on hub-dependent tasks (including rare $B$) plus $B_1$'s preserved improvements on dropped leaf skills.

### L3-26 · Bug-fix rehearsal to prevent workaround-dependent downstream rules
- **Mechanism:** During $M_{\text{old}}$'s life, periodically inject a synthetic successor where $B_0$'s weakest/highest-error skill $s_1$ is *replaced by an oracle fix*, and penalize any new downstream skill $s_2$ whose packet fails under the fixed $s_1$—preventing $M_{\text{old}}$ from ever building fragile retire–migrate cascades (L3-16) on top of $B_0$ bugs.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** L3-16 detects retire–migrate cascades *at upgrade time*; L3-26 prevents them *during the predecessor's life* by rehearsing the eventual repair of $B_0$'s known blind spots.
- **Cheapest first test:** $B_0$ has a systematic error on $s_1$; $M_{\text{old}}$ learns downstream $s_2$ with vs without oracle-fix rehearsal, then upgrades to real $B_1$ that fixes $s_1$. Compare unregularized $M_{\text{old}}$ + L3-16 post-hoc detection vs bug-fix rehearsal. Kill if L3-16 post-hoc detection + re-grounding matches $B_1$'s $s_1 \circ s_2$ accuracy with zero living tax.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $M_{\text{old}}$'s living $s_1 \circ s_2$ accuracy on broken $B_0$ (checking the tax) and $B_1$'s zero-cascade accuracy after upgrade.

### L3-27 · Generational rehearsal feedback: compile real upgrade write-skew into the next generation's simulator
- **Mechanism:** Whenever a real upgrade $B_0 \to B_1$ discovers an unexpected convention shift, imprinting leak, or drop–migrate write-skew that $B_0$'s synthetic rehearsal missed, distill that empirical shift into a *rehearsal perturbation template* handed to $B_1$—so $B_1$'s living rehearsal before $B_1 \to B_2$ explicitly tests for the real upgrade failure modes observed in prior generations.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Static synthetic proxy pools ([MetaDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05103), L2-25) never learn from past real upgrades. Closing the loop across generations ($B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$) turns upgrade failures into *self-improving generational immunity*.
- **Cheapest first test:** 3-generation chain $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$ where base upgrades share a recurring class of convention/tokenizer shifts; compare fixed synthetic rehearsal (L2-25) vs feedback-updated rehearsal on the $B_1 \to B_2$ hop. Kill if fixed L2-25 matches $B_2$'s zero-bridge composite accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Track generation-0 rare rule $B$, generation-1 skills, and $B_2$'s native improvements across the full $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$ chain.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does rehearsing against a *partly-knowing* synthetic successor (L2-25 / L3-01–L3-06) genuinely beat blank-slate cross-architecture distillation (`MetaDD`-style) + post-hoc triage when upgrading to a real unseen $B_1$?**
   Run the decisive 2x2x2 ablation on tiny models: training-time vs post-hoc packet creation $\times$ blank-slate vs partly-knowing proxy $\times$ same vs foreign proxy architecture, evaluated on a held-out real $B_1$ that already knows a subset $S$ (including one convention-shifted skill and one fixed $B_0$ bug). Measure both $B_1$'s joint $(S, \bar{S}, W)$ score and $M_{\text{old}}$'s living bequest tax. Drop the entire L2-25 rehearsal line if blank-slate cross-architecture distillation + post-hoc LM30 ties on $B_1$ or if the living bequest tax exceeds the successor gain.
2. **When drop–migrate write-skew occurs at a base upgrade (L2-17), is it better to *re-ground* migrated $s_2$ onto $B_1$'s native $s_1$ (L3-13), insert a minimal seam bridge (L3-12), or retain $M_0$'s $s_1$?**
   Build a controlled compositional upgrade benchmark ($s_1 \circ s_2$ and $s_1 \to s_2 \to s_3$ chains) covering both convention mismatches and internal feature collisions, with $B_1$ strictly stronger than $M_0$ on standalone $s_1$. Compare independent per-skill triage, GRAFT/Trans-LoRA per-task transport, retain-$s_1$, L3-12 seam bridge, L3-13 witness re-grounding, and L3-18 read-only projection at equal probe and update budgets—and test whether L3-20 bilateral diagnosis reliably picks the winner.
3. **Can *retire–migrate* write-skew (migrating a rule that secretly compensated for an obsolete $B_0$ bug, L3-16) be separated from genuine private conventions before it corrupts $B_1$?**
   Construct a mixed upgrade world containing genuine private rules alongside downstream workarounds for $B_0$ errors that $B_1$ fixed. Test whether pairwise (retired, migrated) composition probes (L3-16) and living bug-fix rehearsal (L3-26) beat independent single-skill evaluation and migrate-all baselines without falsely retiring rare private rule $B$.
4. **Can living convention-jittered rehearsal (L3-21 / L3-23) immunize a continual learner against upgrade write-skew cheaply enough to beat post-hoc repair?**
   Compare a predecessor trained with convention-jittered / swapped-implementation rehearsal (paying a living bequest tax) against an unregularized predecessor repaired post-hoc via L3-12/L3-13 at upgrade time, measured across *multiple* unseen successor bases $B_1^{(1..4)}$ with distinct conventions. Keep Section C's co-design only if amortized rehearsal beats post-hoc repair across unseen upgrades without violating $M_{\text{old}}$'s living retention floor.
