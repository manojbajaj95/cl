# Momentum-sealed gates

**Status:** Ideas only; no training runs or GPU work. **Parent:** [Gated merge, then reopen](gated-merge-reopen.md) ([PR #223][parent]), cards 06 and 09. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 334][triage] scored both 3: gate-entry batches that handle residual optimizer momentum without resetting it, and a declared class of truthful corrections under which the gate stays shut, with service withdrawn outside it. **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger].

## Can ordinary training buy a gate that ordinary training cannot wake?

**Verdict: two candidate primitives, not established.** A gate-entry word is a sequence of ordinary C batches that takes every lawful alias to a closed region of the **joint** learner state, not just to an inactive activation pattern. A correction contract makes that region useful: declare which ordinary updates it tolerates, and withdraw the affected service when the next correction is not covered. The region can admit nonzero, even temporarily opening, momentum if it proves that no allowed sequence can use enough of it to reopen the nuisance. Neither a large activation margin nor an inert nuisance with no useful corrections makes the case.

**Nearest prior art.** [Multi-learner machine teaching][teaching] already searches for shared training inputs; [ReDo][redo] and [continual backprop][cbp] study dormancy and recycle units; [optimizer slot splits][slots] and [Activation-Keyed Momentum][akm] are state-management controls, not ordinary entry words under an unchanged optimizer. [Control barriers][barrier] and [switched dynamics][switched] already supply invariant-set reasoning; [Reluplex][reluplex] checks activation safety in a fixed network, and [OGD][ogd]/[GPM][gpm] protect old behavior by changing writes. The bet is a truthful entry word whose effect on **carried** state buys a larger useful language of **unmodified** corrections than a matched gate and replay baseline. These are scoped comparisons (including HF Papers searches for multi-learner teaching, dormant neurons, activation-keyed momentum and control barriers), not priority claims.

## Contract and falsifiers

- **Freeze the question first.** Declare A (original tasks), B (prior admissions), installed C, incoming C, the nuisance and its input domain, the fixed service witness, per-task utility floors, and the lawful class of initial states. Include all relevant optimizer state (step number, momentum, moments), schedules, running statistics and other state that can change the actual transition. Select one word per lawful alias class, with truthful C batches from the same declared pool and normal optimizer steps throughout. No reset, manual slot write, hard mask, projected gradient, or per-alias batch search in the proposed operation. Pay for selection, proof, retained state and working space.
- **Seal and admission.** Certify entry into a set `S` for all aliases. Over the declared domain every state in `S` gives zero nuisance activation with strict slack. Declare a correction language and prove that every admitted transition from `S` returns to `S` (or to the next certified phase), including every served prefix of a multi-step correction. Scope the proof to actual optimizer, precision and schedule; a zero nuisance gradient does not imply a zero update. Show nontrivial service gain from admitted corrections, separate from the gate proof. Reject an unsupported correction before applying it to the serving model and withdraw the affected service; any later reissue requires fresh certification. Report withdrawal separately from old-task retention and new-task accuracy, not as a correct answer.
- **Controls.** Match truthful examples, steps, optimizer where applicable, initial gate slack, C gain, and the old-task floors. Compare activation-only entry, entry plus slot reset, SGD without momentum, ReDo/continual backprop, slot splits, freeze/projection plus best C replay, and step-by-step re-verification. Include a same-weight/different-slot pair, failure that first appears after several steps, a useful in-class correction, a truthful just-outside correction, and an alias class with no useful legal word. Probe A/B and every installed C at entry, at correction prefixes and after withdrawal, including old paths whose useful motion depends on the same slots. Count abstentions and compare admission coverage at matched proof cost; a contract accepting nothing is not a win.
- **Release boundary.** If used after certified deletion, inherit the parent's comparison of the same recovery and admission kernel on the covered artifact and retrain reference, including refusals, logs and any proof. The compiler cannot read slots outside the covered artifact; use sound bounds derived from allowed inputs or decline certification. A sealed nuisance is not a deletion proof.

## Exact sanity check: a nonzero opening slot need not wake a path

Take a shared linear service read `wx₁ + sx₂` and a nuisance `ReLU(w)` on the promised input. The ordinary heavy-ball step is `m′ = m/2 + g_w; w′ = w − m′`, with the same recurrence for `s` and its slot. Use singleton half-squared loss for two truthful regression records in order: `((1,0),−1)`, then `((1,1),0)`. Start both aliases at `(w,s)=(1,0)` and use slots `(m,m_s)=(-1,0)` or `(1,0)`.

| Initial `m` | `(w,m)` after first batch | `(w,m,s)` after second batch | Second record's read before → after |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| −1 | (−1/2, 3/2) | (−3/4, 1/4, 1/2) | −1/2 → −1/4 |
| 1 | (−3/2, 5/2) | (−5/4, −1/4, 3/2) | −3/2 → 1/4 |

The gate is down after the first batch and both endpoints satisfy `w + max(0,−m) ≤ −1/2`. Declare ordinary singleton corrections with `g_w ≥ 0` throughout this region; for example a fixed negative-label logistic task on `(1,1)` has that sign at every state. This is an invariant: if `m′ ≥ 0`, the left side after the step is `w′ ≤ w`; if `m′ < 0`, then `m < 0` and the left side is `w − 2m′ = w − m − 2g_w ≤ w − m`. Both cases preserve the bound. In the second alias the slot actually points toward opening: even with zero gradients, future momentum can add `1/4` to `w`, but no more. By contrast, a second state with the **same** `w=−5/4` but `m=−3/2` crosses zero on the third zero-gradient step. A truthful regression correction `((1,1),4)` applied to the sealed second alias would give `w′=21/8`; withdraw instead of serving through it. The negative-label logistic correction strictly lowers the read in both sealed aliases on its first step, so the class is not just a no-op. This calculation is heavy-ball only, does not certify arbitrary inputs or old-task retention, and is not evidence that the word beats a reset.

## Idea cards

### 01. Seal the coast, not the slot — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Compile an entry word to fit the worst future opening displacement from residual momentum inside the remaining gate slack. Admit aliases with unequal, nonzero slots. **Novelty / control.** The tail bound is standard dynamics; the candidate operation is purchasing it with a useful common word instead of [slot splitting][slots] or resetting. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust two-batch orders on the shared two-weight host above, including a same-weight twin that wakes on coast. Compare best replay plus reset at matched final margin and C gain. **Old-task retention.** Probe one old skill that benefits from carried momentum and one opposed read across the first three corrections.

### 02. Publish a correction contract, not a confidence score — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Attach a versioned correction language and inductive invariant to the witness; route an out-of-class request to withdrawal before its first write. **Novelty / control.** [Switched-system stability][switched] proves the dynamics; the candidate is a useful update interface that survives handoff without re-certifying every accepted request. Compare full re-verification, gate freezing and GPM. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two plausible truthful corrections, one improving service within the seal, one just across the boundary. **Old-task retention.** Exercise unaffected A/B and installed C after both admission and withdrawal, and measure the withdrawn witness separately.

### 03. Spend inward velocity on a weaker entry batch — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use guaranteed inward movement from a live slot to land a weak truthful C opener that would miss the seal under SGD; require a sound bound on every alias's future drift. **Novelty / control.** Unlike changing the momentum rule in [Activation-Keyed Momentum][akm], the word spends already present motion. Compare stronger replay and a margin-matched reset. **Cheapest first test.** Give two starts equal weights but inward slots of unequal magnitude; flip the weaker slot to test refusal. **Old-task retention.** Check the old task that originally generated the inward momentum and its closest rival.

### 04. Catch a wake that takes three steps — improvement
**Mechanism.** Match inactive weights but vary opening moments, then check a finite prefix and the full tail separately. **Novelty / control.** Extends dormant-unit inspection in [ReDo][redo] with an optimizer-state falsifier, not a new invariant method. **Cheapest first test.** Start with `w=−5/4` and slots `−1/4` and `−3/2` under zero gradients; compare one-step checks and the exact three-step trajectories. **Old-task retention.** Run old positive and negative boundary cases at every step, not just the first time the nuisance wakes.

### 05. Seal what Adam can actually move — improvement
**Mechanism.** Certify both bias-corrected first-moment numerator and second-moment denominator (and decoupled decay when used), not the norm of `m` alone. **Novelty / control.** An application of joint-state verification to [Adam][adam]; compare projecting the raw gradient and freezing the nuisance block. **Cheapest first test.** Hold weights, time and `m` fixed, vary inherited `v`, and find two opposite admission decisions under the identical next batch. **Old-task retention.** Record old-task utility on the low-variance coordinate and on a retained task driven by the same step.

### 06. Teach a denominator without clearing the numerator — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Order ordinary signed evidence so high-magnitude opposite gradients build Adam's second moment while preserving useful nonzero first moments; spend the bought time on a correction family whose reachability proof includes second-moment decay. **Novelty / control.** [Adam][adam] already adapts scale; the candidate is a lawful entry word that widens useful correction coverage at the same activation margin. Compare first-moment cancellation, reset, and an LR-matched arm. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust signed two- and three-batch permutations on two aliases; insert a gap long enough for the proof to fail. **Old-task retention.** Check whether the enlarged denominator stalls an old rare-feature repair.

### 07. Use a directional seal instead of coordinatewise sleep — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Preserve a large tangential slot that helps the service, constraining only the most dangerous future normal displacement over the entire gate domain. **Novelty / control.** Unlike [OGD][ogd], which constrains an executed gradient, select a word that leaves useful tangential carry under unchanged updates. Compare one scalar margin, full reset and projected replay. **Cheapest first test.** Use two gate normals with incomparable dangerous directions; compare a box bound with their joint reachable set. **Old-task retention.** Compare old reads along both normals and the retained tangent after a common correction.

### 08. Teach away a mixed-state dangerous corner — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Keep provable correlation between activation slack and opening slot after entry: aliases with less slack carry less dangerous momentum. A box containing their independent extrema may forbid every useful correction. **Novelty / control.** Relational abstractions such as [DeepPoly][deeppoly] are prior art; the bet is that ordinary C entry can deliberately shape a useful weight-slot relation. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust a finite word menu on two crossed aliases and compare an exact joint set with its marginal box. **Old-task retention.** Test both old read tails, especially the alias with least initial slack.

### 09. Keep the seal when the trunk moves — improvement
**Mechanism.** Bound updates to the nuisance's upstream representation, including running statistics, separately from its local slot. **Novelty / control.** [Reluplex][reluplex] and the parent already motivate this check; extend the transition, not the claim of verification novelty. **Cheapest first test.** Construct a zero-local-gradient nuisance that wakes via one shared feature or a running-mean change; compare a frozen-trunk arm. **Old-task retention.** Include rare examples on which old skills use that shared feature and normalization.

### 10. Admit signed minibatches, not signed records — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use ordinary joint minibatches whose aggregate projected update is always inward even when some member records push outward; fix normalization and verify the implemented aggregation. **Novelty / control.** [Multi-learner teaching][teaching] already selects sets of examples; the extra object is an admitted batch class whose individual records would not qualify alone. **Cheapest first test.** Pair two truthful singletons with opposite gradients; compare the batch, both sequential orders and an input-independent cap. **Old-task retention.** Test both old subgroups represented by the two records so cancellation cannot hide one subgroup's loss.

### 11. Allow a finite run of genuinely helpful outward corrections — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Declare a consumable allowance on outward gradient impulse, including all residual tails from each admitted step, and withdraw when the next step would exceed it. **Novelty / control.** This is a [barrier][barrier] budget, not a new stability theorem; its value is a correction language admitting a useful reversal that monotone rules reject. **Cheapest first test.** Try two streams with equal cumulative impulses but different prefix peaks; compare fixed horizon and total-norm caps. **Old-task retention.** Report old-task margins at the maximum outward excursion and again at withdrawal.

### 12. Earn the next correction from a useful inward one — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Allow a bounded outward correction only after a truthful inward correction has restored a certified joint-state allowance; no synthetic recharge samples. **Novelty / control.** [Switched systems][switched] already certify ordered modes; test whether a useful C obligation can fund a second ordinary obligation without resetting or growing the budget. **Cheapest first test.** Compose two independently useful ordinary updates in both orders and repeat the safe cycle; include a stream with no truthful refill. **Old-task retention.** Track A/B and installed C at every debit and refill, not just at the end of the cycle.

### 13. Let the correction grammar speak phase — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Publish a finite grammar in which every phase has its own joint-state invariant, so individually useful corrections can be admitted in some orders but not arbitrarily shuffled. **Novelty / control.** A [switched-system][switched] certificate is standard; the test is whether it gives an ordinary service interface with better coverage than a single shared barrier or replay schedule. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate all length-three strings, including a usable prefix that cannot accept the next requested correction and a permutation that crosses the gate. **Old-task retention.** Check an old task that peaks and another that dips in the intermediate phase.

### 14. Certify the burst, but do not hide its middle — improvement
**Mechanism.** Separate an atomic, unserved staging burst from live corrections; require explicit prefix invariants for any reader that remains online. **Novelty / control.** An audit of [switched-system][switched] endpoints and ordinary transaction isolation, not a new gate. **Cheapest first test.** Make two steps end closed but cross zero at the middle; give one live consumer access to that prefix and one endpoint-only consumer. **Old-task retention.** Check availability and results for old services during staging, not only after commit.

### 15. Offer an input-scoped seal after a dispute — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Declare overlapping input domains with independently proved invariants. Withdraw the affected domain's binding before the write; apply the shared correction only if it is admitted for every binding that remains live. **Novelty / control.** [Reluplex][reluplex] already checks subdomains; the new operation would be selective, provable service withdrawal under one unchanged shared update. **Cheapest first test.** Use adjacent ReLU regions and a truthful correction admissible on only one. Compare global withdrawal and separately trained services at matched bytes. **Old-task retention.** Probe old reads on each region and at their overlap; distinguish necessary withdrawal from accidental collateral loss.

### 16. Refuse a truthful request that omits its execution context — improvement
**Mechanism.** Bind membership to data, loss, augmentation range, gradient accumulation, learning-rate schedule and precision, not just a request label. **Novelty / control.** Standard robustness and [activation verification][reluplex]; audit where a correction interface silently assumes a transition it does not execute. **Cheapest first test.** Hold the batch fixed and toggle a scheduled LR increase or accumulation mode that crosses the gate. **Old-task retention.** Check previously safe high-LR old skills and preserve their separate availability when the new binding is withdrawn.

### 17. Prove a useful contract is impossible before searching words — improvement
**Mechanism.** Prove that every useful entry leaves at least one alias whose opening tail exceeds the available slack, or that every useful correction family admits a sequence with cumulative wake-up. **Novelty / control.** A [multi-learner teaching][teaching] reachability obstruction, not new optimization; compare exhaustive tiny search with an unsound activation-only declaration. **Cheapest first test.** Give a two-alias host with a bounded-margin cap and no legal word that sufficiently reduces the opening slot; then remove the obstruction by adding one truthful record. **Old-task retention.** Verify that the apparent easy fix, freezing the shared coordinate, disables an old corrective update.

### 18. Hand off a seal without disclosing the slot — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify a public outer bound on the reachable joint state, then use it to decide corrections without reading hidden optimizer slots or emitting a private history receipt. **Novelty / control.** The parent's [certified removal][guo] and post-processing story is not novel; the candidate is useful coverage despite proving over an entire legal slot set. **Cheapest first test.** Give U/R matching covered artifacts with differing hidden slots and compare lawful overapproximation, oracle slot inspection and replay-only recovery. **Old-task retention.** Check A/B and permitted installed C in both arms, including when the conservative bound causes withdrawal.

### 19. Try two seals with one optimizer — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Find a common truthful entry word and correction class for two nuisance paths whose projected momentum constraints interact on shared weights. **Novelty / control.** Intersecting [barrier][barrier] conditions is standard; the bet is a joint word that uses shared gradients to buy both seals more cheaply than independently masking each path. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust a three-batch pool with cooperative, competing and infeasible gate normals. **Old-task retention.** Compare separate old users near each gate and a third that depends on the shared direction.

### 20. Reissue with new evidence, not the old checkpoint — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** After withdrawing, leave the affected binding down until a new truthful entry word proves a new seal from the current lawful class. Charge re-entry and count the downtime. **Novelty / control.** [Continual backprop][cbp] refreshes capacity and the parent relaunches a witness once; the proposed operation tests repeated service recovery without reset, hidden backup or widening the proof by decree. **Cheapest first test.** Run three episodes of admit/withdraw/reissue under one cap, including an episode where reissue must fail. **Old-task retention.** Check all accumulated old tasks through downtime and on each new binding version.

### 21. Buy rounding margin with entry, not tolerances with audit — improvement
**Mechanism.** Require the entry word to leave enough margin for a sound finite-precision recurrence, including small Adam denominators or a ReLU kink near zero. **Novelty / control.** [Activation verification][reluplex] and line 330's neighboring roundoff concern motivate the audit; compare higher precision and exact arithmetic plus a worst-case error interval. **Cheapest first test.** Find a tiny instance where a real-number seal touches the deployed rounding bound and must be refused. **Old-task retention.** Test near-tie old decisions as well as nuisance inactivity at the deployed precision.

### 22. Compare the full stacks, not their weakest pieces — improvement
**Mechanism.** Plot useful correction coverage, old-task worst loss, search/proof cost and withdrawal frequency for truthful entry plus contract versus reset/freeze plus best truthful replay and re-verification. **Novelty / control.** This is the [ledger][ledger]'s stacked-control rule applied to the interaction; a tied frontier is a negative result. **Cheapest first test.** Cross weight-only versus joint-state entry with reactive versus inductive admission on one shared host; require an interaction at matched gate slack and C gain. **Old-task retention.** Include all refused requests and an old correction that needs the supposedly nuisance-only direction.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does joint-state entry buy useful correction coverage that reset or freezing plus the best C replay does not?** Start with 01/02/07/22 on shared features. Match activation slack, C gain, old retention, optimizer and budgets. Include an activation-only success that wakes late and an instance where no truthful word succeeds. Retire the primitive claim if the stacked baseline ties.
2. **Does Adam's denominator preserve the heavy-ball result or reverse it?** Pair 05/06/08: hold preactivation and `m` fixed, vary `v` and clock, and look for a reliable sign or phase constraint that works under the actual recurrence. Charge any additional evidence and proof cost; compare a matched-LR replay and check old rare-feature repair.
3. **Can the correction language stay broad and useful without becoming per-step re-verification?** Use 10–13 and 15. Compare sign classes, burst budgets and phase languages on held-out truthful corrections; count withdrawn services and boundary counterexamples. Demand a guarantee over sequences and prefixes, not just isolated batches.
4. **Can we compile and reissue using only lawful observations?** Combine 17/18/20. Test for a useful alias-wide word, a case soluble only with forbidden slots, and a correctly refused case. Measure compounded downtime and old-task floors over multiple reissues; audit the full release separately from the gate.
