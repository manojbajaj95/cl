# Function-matched twins: can training history change what gets forgotten next?

**Parents:** [practice-order interference (PR #121)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/121), [expanded-pool top-10 #5: relearning as the read head](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-top10.md), and [differential fork registers (PR #114)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/114). **Status:** research ideas, not experimental results. The proposed first tests are CPU-scale.

## Why the parent result does not settle the question

PR #121 varied practice order at a fixed task histogram. Long blocks hurt the worst task even in linear hosts. Under shared shuffled practice the tested linear and random-feature hosts lost their measurable order gap; the feature-learning MLP retained a blocked-vs-shuffled worst-task deficit of −0.29 ± 0.14 after 4,000 shared steps, but that deficit relaxed by 12,000 steps. The forward-vs-reverse comparison also showed a unit-allocation footprint after 4,000 steps. This is evidence for a transient history footprint, not for permanent hysteresis or for memory after matching the starting function. The models still differed in observable competence. A poorer starting margin can explain poorer later service without any hidden channel.

Top-10 #5 asks whether relearning speed can *read* otherwise unavailable information. PR #114's signed fork read did not clear that bar: raw outputs on the same fork inputs already read the bits, and the estimated payload fell from 16 bits to 0.32 after 16 unrelated SGD steps. A fork can expose a distinction already in the state; it does not create or protect the distinction. This node separates three claims: **history changes future forgetting after a forward match**; **the effect survives access to first-gradient information**; and **a deliberate write can use the surviving distinction to protect or recover old skills**. A result on one does not establish the next.

## Twin contract

- **History and launch state.** Start from paired initializations and assign distinct past orderings of the same materialized minibatches (including labels, augmentation and noise), or name the history factor under test. Match the complete output vector, not just labels, loss or aggregate accuracy, on a declared probe distribution that includes old tasks, new-task inputs and every input available to a public read. Exact equality on a finite support is the constructive gate; for sampled continuous inputs, call it an approximate on-distribution match. Fit on one split and audit on fresh draws and near-boundary strata, with predeclared mean and tail tolerances and no per-twin tuning on future outcomes. Never infer global function equality from passing a finite assay.
- **Common future.** Fork both twins onto exactly the same new-task stream, with identical update rules, batch order and randomness. Read both at the same update counts and at matched new-task performance; if one never reaches the target, report that rather than imputing a time. For old task $k$, compare the drop in held-out score from each twin's own launch to time $t$, with worst-task and trajectory summaries (including transient floor violations). Keep untouched old tasks as a separate guardrail. A useful outcome is a material difference in retention at equal acquisition, not merely a history label that can be decoded.
- **Two shortcut gates (PR #114).** Give held-out decoders the full raw logits on the same public inputs, including all fork or relearning inputs, then the full first gradients for each admissible initial batch/loss, not just the norm of a pooled gradient. Include their joint transcript, the actual first optimizer update where relevant, and the first-order old-output response $J_{old}\Delta\theta$ (for SGD this is the old/new tangent-kernel action). True coordinates and the fixed decoder budget are part of the claim: test aligned and permutation-invariant reads where relevant. For a *multi-step-only* claim also test exact one-update-and-read endpoints for the allowed standalone interventions; these can reveal information a first gradient alone misses. Match these quantities where feasible and check that strong shortcut baselines neither decode history above a prespecified leakage tolerance nor explain the held-out retention gap as well as the multi-step read. Wide intervals are inconclusive, not a shortcut failure. An arbitrary white-box observer with the complete state and future stream can simulate the read; exclusivity is relative to the declared access.
- **Artifact and relaxation controls (PR #121).** Use a nonlinear feature-learning host, with fixed-feature/linear and same-history/different-seed controls. Reset optimizer slots and non-parameter training state first; reintroduce or swap them separately if the effect requires them. Include unmatched pairs, same-history pairs subjected to the same matching intervention, and a calibration in which a small controlled logit or margin mismatch is injected into same-history twins. Distillation or projection can itself erase a trace or introduce one; report pair yield, matching compute, and how conclusions change with tolerance and with the matching method. At several shared re-mixing lengths (including zero and a long horizon), audit the match and launch the same new-task shock from fresh copies. Track natural relaxation without re-fitting as the primary curve, re-matched pairs separately; report time back to the same-history floor or a censored bound. Do not confuse that timescale with the duration of the forgetting gap after the shock.

## Prior-art screen and ledger constraints

Read the [reviewer's idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md) before proposing. Noether memory is not a candidate carrier on its own: finite-step SGD/momentum and rebalancing defeat it. The plasticity-reserve rerun largely lost its effect after retention matching, so absolute spread is a gate, not something to assume. A direct two-order rollout is the baseline that beat the proposed Lyapunov score; gradient accumulation is the bounded-delay baseline. Do not count masked recall followed by generic relearning savings as a new primitive (the ledger's residual skill bits).

Quick screen by Hugging Face Papers search and abstract lookup, not a claim of exhaustive novelty:

| Verified paper | What it already covers; what is left here |
|---|---|
| [2011.03395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03395), Underspecification | Equal held-out performance can hide different deployment behavior. Here match full outputs on the declared launch support and then intervene with the *same training*; initial off-support differences remain an alternative explanation. |
| [1806.07572](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07572), Neural Tangent Kernel | The tangent kernel governs infinitesimal functional updates. Different initial kernels already explain first-order differences between function-matched twins; the stronger target is a gap not explained by this read or its frozen rollout. |
| [1511.05641](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05641), Net2Net; [2408.10566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10566), growth-induced forgetting | Function-preserving architecture changes and retention failures from growth are already known. No novelty claim for widening a network and observing different fine-tuning. The main comparison keeps the architecture fixed. |
| [2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812), Loss of Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning | Training history can degrade acquisition. The distinct endpoint here is **old** service after a common update at equal launch behavior and new-task progress. |
| [2406.13356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13356), benign relearning; [2506.15076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15076), learning-time encoding and unlearning; [2605.11685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11685), minor components and relearning | Dormant recovery, effects of past encoding on later removal, and small-component carriers are occupied territory. The proposed advance is selective causal control of ordinary future forgetting under forward and first-gradient matches, not another recovery demo. |

## Raw ideas

Every first test uses the relevant parts of the twin contract, with a same-budget rival named explicitly. Analytic and planted examples calibrate the assay; they do not establish that ordinary training leaves the same trace.

### A. Establish the separation without manufacturing it

1. **An ordinary-history twin challenge.** *Mechanism:* blocked and interleaved practice may allocate features differently while implementing the same old and new predictions. *Novelty:* PR #121 measured an order footprint without matching predictions; the plasticity-reserve rerun warns that a stricter match may erase the spread. *Cheapest first test:* reorder the same cached four-task batches in a small ReLU MLP, then match at shared checkpoints with a common teacher and separately with minimal readout calibration; compare against same-order pairs receiving identical calibration budgets. *Old-task retention:* launch a fifth task from each eligible pair and measure the worst old-task drop against a fresh same-order floor at equal fifth-task loss.
2. **A two-step wake-up witness.** *Mechanism:* on three public inputs use outputs $u$, $uv$, and $c+huv$, with $u=v=0$ and opposite values of $h$. With plain SGD, a write on the first input wakes $u$; only then does a write on the second wake $v$, exposing $h$ on the third. *Novelty:* [PR #104](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/104)'s function-equivalent factorization separates after one step; PR #114 fails even the raw-output gate. This calibrates strictly later exposure: any single batch from launch can move $u$ or $c$, but not $v$ or $h$. *Cheapest first test:* enumerate both two-step orders and all single-step subsets, with a summed-gradient update as rival; build the witness before testing for the same motif in learned features. *Old-task retention:* set the third input's common old margin so exactly one ordered fork crosses it; verify an independent old output remains intact.
3. **Residual-mismatch dose curve.** *Mechanism:* bound how much apparent historical forgetting can be produced by an ordinary small launch mismatch, as a function of its direction and magnitude. *Novelty:* [footprint counterfeits](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/107) construct blind directions; this tests whether a measured gap is just finite matching error rather than a hidden state. *Cheapest first test:* inject matched-size readout perturbations into clones, including the worst direction found on a calibration stream, and compare their dose–response curves with history pairs across match tolerances. *Old-task retention:* demand a gap above the calibrated envelope on fresh worst-task and margin-stratified old sets, or report it as unresolved.
4. **Reset-and-swap carrier audit.** *Mechanism:* the trace may live in representations, running statistics or optimizer slots; swapping one component can transfer the future failure phenotype. *Novelty:* [optimizer-state memory](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/109) studies conservation and gauge, not transfer of a matched-function retention phenotype under crossed resets. *Cheapest first test:* cross two histories' parameters with both slot states in aligned coordinates; compare to time-matched fresh slots, random swaps, and stateless SGD. Rematch and re-audit after any swap. *Old-task retention:* ask which swap moves the old-loss trajectory at fixed new loss without lowering launch or untouched-old service.
5. **A relaxation clock for liability, not outputs.** *Mechanism:* trainability can decorrelate from current behavior at one rate and forgettable old-task margins under a subsequent shock at another. *Novelty:* PR #121 follows competence and unit allocation while re-mixing; this measures the lifetime of a *matched-state susceptibility*, not a permanent invariant or the ledger's killed programmable half-life. *Cheapest first test:* take a geometric ladder of shared remix lengths and launch a fixed interference stream at each; compare prediction by remix age, launch mismatch and the ledger's cheap two-order rollout. *Old-task retention:* estimate the last detectable excess worst-task drop, including transient dips, under the same shock budget and a matched no-shock curve.
6. **Matched pairs without a teacher.** *Mechanism:* impose equality by training both histories to satisfy the same complete finite-domain interpolation constraints, rather than distilling one trained state toward another. *Novelty:* the ledger's effect-class distillation concerns replay equivalence, whereas this tests whether matching itself writes the effect. *Cheapest first test:* use a small Boolean domain with shared exact targets and different task curricula, then a common constrained readout solve; compare against same-history solves and distillation pairs with the same residual. *Old-task retention:* enumerate all old inputs before and after one new task, and score old-task failure at a fixed new error target.

### B. Locate or rewrite the causal state

7. **Move the forgetting phenotype by exchanging modules.** *Mechanism:* a feature group that predicts identically through the readout today may change old/new interference when it moves between twins. *Novelty:* [behavior footprint identity](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/102) separates reads and writes; this asks whether the causal susceptibility itself is transplantable. *Cheapest first test:* align modules of two small MLPs, swap candidate groups and repair only the shared readout; compare to random same-size and sham-alignment swaps, retaining only pairs that pass launch controls. *Old-task retention:* require donor-like retention changes on a new shock for the predicted old task, but not collateral decline across untouched tasks.
8. **A two-address susceptibility setter.** *Mechanism:* use a short rehearsal order to choose which of two simultaneously competent old skills is resistant to future interference, without changing either skill's present logits. *Novelty:* PR #114's register writes old payload values; this writes protection for already available, unchanged skills. *Cheapest first test:* sweep all four target protection patterns across paired histories using the same batch multiset; rival is task-weighted short replay and the same number of shuffled updates. *Old-task retention:* check both tasks after unrelated drift and then separately targeted shocks; changing one address must not silently change the other.
9. **Erase one liability without resetting the model.** *Mechanism:* find a short common training word that contracts the forgetting gap between twins for one future family but not another, preserving old outputs. *Novelty:* [synchronizing consolidation](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/108) pursues equivalence of future histories; selective synchronization would erase just one causal vulnerability. *Cheapest first test:* enumerate short words over a shared practice alphabet in an MLP, with equal-length re-mixing and parameter averaging as rivals; choose words before held-out future streams. *Old-task retention:* compare minimum retention under both future families and reject a contraction achieved by degrading the stronger twin or freezing acquisition.
10. **Transport a protection rule, not the carrier.** *Mechanism:* infer a history-blind intervention from one pair's training response and apply it to independently trained twins with incompatible hidden coordinates. *Novelty:* [base-invariant edits](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/92) transport a behavioral obligation or write; here the transferred target is retention under subsequent training while current behavior stays fixed. *Cheapest first test:* derive a short curriculum over public examples on some initializations and apply it unchanged to held-out initializations; compare to norm-matched transported deltas and direct rehearsal. *Old-task retention:* require the same directional shift in worst-old retention under an unseen new task with no loss in launch service.
11. **Separate a geometry effect from a gauge effect.** *Mechanism:* function-preserving rescaling changes ordinary SGD's metric, so differences that disappear under symmetry-aware updates need not reflect an invariant historical trace. *Novelty:* the ledger's Noether carrier fails a rebalance; this uses rebalancing as a causal intervention on *forgetting* and asks what survives both canonicalization and an alternative update geometry. *Cheapest first test:* compare synthetic rescaling twins and real order twins under SGD before/after ReLU balancing and under a rescaling-equivariant step, matching new progress. *Old-task retention:* ask whether the history gap outlives disappearance of the positive gauge control on fresh old tests.
12. **Cross a feature gate at equal instantaneous response.** *Mechanism:* twins with the same initial response can have different distances to a gate transition, so a shared sequence activates competing features at different steps. *Novelty:* the ledger's killed ReLU-kink account explained plasticity spread; this asks for a selective intervention that moves the onset of later old-task failure without changing the audited launch transcript. *Cheapest first test:* in a small ReLU net, vary dormant gate distances with matched first-gradient and one-step reads; compare smooth activations and equally sized random gate moves at a fixed update budget. *Old-task retention:* preregister which old loss crosses its floor first, and check that a targeted gate repair delays that crossing at the same new-task loss.

### C. Determine which future experiments reveal the trace

13. **A history-by-future crossover.** *Mechanism:* the same twin can be safer under continuation X and riskier under Y, so no scalar plasticity or fragility score orders the pair. *Novelty:* the ledger's plasticity reserve ranks writes and [survival-probability memory](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/112) pools under a specified future law; here a held-out crossover is the object. *Cheapest first test:* fully cross two past orders with two label-balanced future orders made of the same batches; compare to a scalar readiness model and the frozen-kernel trajectory. *Old-task retention:* require opposite signs for the same old-task drop at equal new loss, not just different mean scores under unmatched shocks.
14. **A balanced cycle that unmasks one old liability.** *Mechanism:* two fixed update orders can be indistinguishable individually but differ as a composition when one moves the other into a different response regime. *Novelty:* [fork-effect algebra](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795539715-fork-effect-algebra/tree/fork-effect-algebra.md) concerns composition of edit effects; this targets history-dependent retention after passing both standalone reads and an initial response forecast. *Cheapest first test:* enumerate AB, BA and averaged-gradient rivals on the same two batches; match singleton endpoints, then compare the order contrast to its local second-order predictor across step sizes. *Old-task retention:* verify that the contrast predicts a floor crossing on an unqueried old set and survives a held-out same-budget update order.
15. **A cheap separating challenge bank.** *Mechanism:* adaptively choose which bounded training fork to run from earlier public outcomes to identify the endangered old skill. *Novelty:* PR #114 counts reliable message bits; this chooses experiments to minimize false safety decisions on new histories rather than to maximize full-state or payload recovery. *Cheapest first test:* train a two-level decision tree on fork trajectories from synthetic twins; compare to fixed random forks, direct short rollouts and joint raw/gradient decoders with the same example and update budget. *Old-task retention:* on disjoint twin families require fewer missed old-task floor crossings at equal abstention and no extra risk to the live model.
16. **Path versus clock.** *Mechanism:* different reasons for a delay can be separated by counterposing equal optimizer steps, equal acquisition and equal integrated update travel. *Novelty:* PR #121's re-mixing horizon is in steps; the ledger's bounded-delay result cautions that apparent order costs can be evaluation timing. This asks whether two twins trace different retention paths, not merely follow the same path at different speeds. *Cheapest first test:* run a short shared stream over a learning-rate ladder and compare trajectory overlays in all three clocks to a mere schedule-rescaling control. *Old-task retention:* test whether the worst-old gap and floor crossings persist over a common range of new-task progress.
17. **Opposite secrets under identical speed.** *Mechanism:* total learning speed can cancel across old subskills even when a vector of per-subskill recovery slopes identifies which one survives. *Novelty:* top-10 #5 uses speed as a read head; this tests whether matched aggregate speed hides a selective retention signal rather than more extractable old-answer bits. *Cheapest first test:* two compositional Boolean skills with a shared acquisition task, label-blind public challenges and a disjoint old test; compare per-skill trajectories to total speed, random challenge assignments and full first-gradient baselines. *Old-task retention:* predict each skill's held-out failure after a common new stream, and verify the diagnosis is not obtained by sacrificing one skill's launch accuracy.
18. **A failure of finite-depth matching that scales.** *Mechanism:* cascaded shared gates may postpone the first separating training transcript as the chain grows. *Novelty:* [self-simulated learning](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/91) calls for a learning-state congruence; this would give an update-depth lower bound for retaining the *same old skill* from matched states. *Cheapest first test:* extend idea 2 to a three-rung chain and exhaustively certify all shallower sequences over a fixed public batch alphabet, then seek the same delayed signature in an ordinary MLP. Compare to a one-step composite batch under the same budget and state reset. *Old-task retention:* the first separating word must change a real old margin with matched acquisition and no detectable shallower old-task gap.

### D. Make the state useful, or show it cannot be

19. **A retention-only backup compiled into training response.** *Mechanism:* keep the normal old answer identical in both twins but write a secondary implementation that takes over after a particular new-task sequence damages the primary path. *Novelty:* PR #114's forward-to-fork handoff recovers answers via a reader; here ordinary inference must remain correct after training, without calling a reader or storing labels. *Cheapest first test:* train a two-path MLP with simulated new-task interference and match launch outputs and initial response to a one-path-use control of the same width; compare to same-compute rehearsal. *Old-task retention:* score ordinary old accuracy under held-out target interference, off-target interference and equal new progress.
20. **One-bit protection at equal byte-time.** *Mechanism:* put a choice of protective response into an otherwise unchanged checkpoint, where a fixed future policy need not read an external flag. *Novelty:* unlike the ledger's killed Noether payload, this bit has an observable retention effect and must survive optimizer/coordinate controls; unlike top-10 #5 it changes outcomes rather than only conveying data. *Cheapest first test:* optimize two behavior-matched states against two known future families and compare to a one-bit public router selecting between two update rules, charging state, compute and side information equally. *Old-task retention:* measure worst-family retention after surprise independent training and count protection-bit failures at matched new accuracy.
21. **Distill the future response without distilling the historical data.** *Mechanism:* transfer a donor's short update trajectories on public examples, rather than its training data, so a recipient inherits a particular old-retention behavior. *Novelty:* the ledger's effect-class distillation tied an execution signal, and PR #114 copies payload readouts; here matching immediate behavior is necessary but the target is generalization of retention to *unseen continuation sequences*. *Cheapest first test:* distill two donors into a common architecture with identical launch teachers plus different public second-step targets; compare to ordinary logit/gradient distillation and equal-cost donor rollout replay. *Old-task retention:* require the recipient to inherit the targeted improvement on disjoint old examples after a never-distilled continuation without harming untouched old service.
22. **A safe merge that preserves both protections.** *Mechanism:* two twins protect different old tasks; search for a merged state that retains their better taskwise response, not just their shared launch predictions. *Novelty:* [merger certificates](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/110) reason about lost distinctions and required spare bits; this asks whether complementary protection can coexist in one state or has an incompatibility certificate at fixed capacity. *Cheapest first test:* enumerate gates of a small shared-capacity model, then try a response-constrained merge in a tiny MLP against aligned averaging, single-twin selection and same-size replay. *Old-task retention:* require better worst-case old service over both shock families at matched new loss; if impossible in the enumerated host, exhibit the failing old task for every admissible state.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Does ordinary training create a durable, multi-step-only forgetting distinction?** (1–6, 12, 18.) Calibrate the assay with an exact witness, then build twins from same-batch reordered nonlinear training. Require the old-retention gap to survive forward, gradient/tangent and one-step controls, persist beyond the same-history floor under independent re-mixing, and reproduce on held-out continuations. If matching or the first-step controls explain it, stop calling it a hidden channel.
2. **Can we selectively write or erase susceptibility without changing what a model knows today?** (7–12, 19–20.) Cross two independently targeted old skills and both shock types. A convincing setter changes which old skill survives without changing the other, the launch transcript, or progress on new learning. It must beat a short rehearsal or explicit flag at the same budget; a uniform stability gain is not a setter.
3. **What is the smallest experiment that distinguishes useful learning states?** (13–18.) Compare fixed and adaptive challenge banks on unseen histories, with one-step, frozen-tangent and short direct rollout baselines. The outcome to predict is a future old-task failure at equal acquisition, not training-order identity. Report the update depth and the supported future family, not a universal read head.
4. **Can protective responses be transported or composed without carrying the historical data?** (9–10, 19–22.) Hold out both recipient initializations and continuations; require a transported or merged state to improve actual old service under each relevant shock family. If the necessary side information or update cost reaches that of explicit replay, or if one old skill's protection displaces another's, report the trade-off rather than claiming free memory.
