# Delayed-release edits: writing and forgetting in optimizer state

**Parent:** [Momentum fossils](momentum-fossils.md) ([PR #136](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/136)), specifically cards #19 (delayed-release edits) and #12 (optimizer cleanup after unlearning). See [flood triage, row 221](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md#L221) and [row 223](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md#L223) (MFH4, relapse hysteresis). **Related:** [fact-free inheritance (PR #137)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/137) and [recovery witnesses (PR #139)](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/139). **Status:** ideas only; no experiments run; all first tests are CPU-scale.

**Node question:** Can we write a correction into optimizer slots rather than weights, so it takes effect gradually during ordinary new training? Conversely, when weights pass an unlearning audit, can old slots undo that result without new exposure to the forgotten information? If relapse and recovery occur at different doses of the same continuation, which state carries that history dependence?

## Root cause and the bar for a new operation

Writing only to slots at a cut leaves weights, predictions and the first gradient unchanged **by construction**. It does not leave the next update unchanged. In heavy-ball SGD, with `m_next = βm + g` and `w_next = w − ηm_next`, injecting `δm` at the cut is exactly equivalent to adding `−ηₖβᵏδm` directly to the weight update at step `k ≥ 1` (constant β, identical arithmetic and schedule). This holds even on nonlinear models: if the weights agree at each cut, the gradients agree too. A one-shot `m` write is therefore not a new adaptive mechanism on that optimizer. Under Adam, the live denominator can make a fixed slot write respond to the path; an external controller with the same slot-shaped payload and access to the denominator can emulate it. The proposed advantage must come from measured storage, control or retention benefits, or from a richer read/write operation that beats the stacked controls—not merely from using a different tensor.

**Relapse** means renewed expression of a retired answer relative to a never-exposed reference, not merely an accuracy change during training. **Hysteresis** means a difference at the *same current drive* on the up and down branches of a preregistered continuation sweep. Use the gap between first relapse on ascent and first recovery on descent at the same signed-margin criterion; also report the signed area between branches and any censored threshold. A loop alone can be simple lag; branch order, elapsed time, weight memory and update scale all matter. A crossed-slot intervention at common weights identifies a causal slot contribution; it does not prove that the weights hold nothing or that deleting slots gives a privacy guarantee.

Follow the ledger's negative results: no conserved-slot claim, no credit for exact save/restore or a planted switch threshold, and no sparse-damage assumption. The labels below distinguish candidate **PRIMITIVES** (a reusable object or operation if the control fails) from **IMPROVEMENTS** (optimization, diagnosis or evaluation of an existing operation); they are not review verdicts.

## Shared CPU first-test contract

- **Hosts and stream.** Start with a frozen-feature linear head to calibrate exact scheduled injection, and a small two-layer ReLU MLP with Adam or heavy-ball for feature learning. Teach a synthetic key-to-answer world with counterbalanced opposite bindings, an old procedure that uses the binding, and an unrelated old skill `C`. Write a correction or delete a binding, then train on a materialized new-task stream `B`. B contains neither binding nor derivations that disclose it; the never-exposed reference checks leakage. Score direct and composed queries separately on held-out inputs. No task ID at inference.
- **Edits.** Compare ordinary continuation, immediate weight write, the same delta at a fixed later cut, and tuned K-step stored-delta release (including an exact heavy-ball schedule). For live-slot claims also give the external scheduler the same permitted gradient, clock and preconditioner signals, then charge both designs for bytes, extra gradients and tuning. Match launch dose and integrated displacement on a calibration route; report worst checkpoint, onset, obsolete-answer mass and old-task retention on held-out routes at matched B progress. Use weight-edit and replay baselines at equal write data where relevant.
- **Deletion.** Use identical post-delete weights for donor-slot swaps; cross exposed and never-exposed `m` and `v` separately while holding clock and RNG fixed. Compare keep, full reset, effective-step/norm-matched reset, selective cleanup, a DualOptim+-style forget/retain state split, and a from-scratch retrain without the binding with a matched continuation schedule. Include a separate equal-step relearning audit on held-out synthetic queries; never mix its target-bearing supervision into ordinary continuation. Calibrate against never-exposed margins, chance and common answer-format recovery.
- **Loops and reads.** Drive a public B mixture up and back down along a fixed grid, with matched batches, initial margins, dose and update counts across forks and distinct reverse-order, no-reversal and dwell-time controls. Slower sweeps distinguish lag from persistent history dependence over the measured window. Log W-only and slot-only crossovers, the first update, and the linearized full-state response before claiming a higher-order effect. Thresholds and release policies are chosen on separate calibration episodes, never by looking at target labels during B.

## Idea cards

### A. Program an edit trajectory

1. **A deadline edit.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; edit specified as a path):* Invert the finite-horizon Adam response to find a one-shot slot write that crosses a target margin by step K without a large early jump, subject to untouched-task floors on several anticipated routes. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.24593 chooses loss weights on a committed path; this specifies an output trajectory and compiles it into a state intervention tested on unseen paths. *Cheapest first test:* Fit a rank-two slot write for two B orders, test on two unseen orders; compare immediate, tuned K-step and live-preconditioned deltas. *Old-task retention:* Minimum C and old-procedure accuracy through the deadline, at matched B loss.

2. **A pulse, not a permanent edit.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; expiring factual behavior):* Put opposite-signed payloads in fast and slow registers so the first creates a temporary correction and the second brings outputs back, without rereading the original label. *Novelty vs nearest:* AdEMAMix (2409.03137) mixes timescales for optimization, not a reversible function excursion with verified recovery. *Cheapest first test:* Two rates on one toy correction, with as many bytes and timing controls in a signed delta schedule; test duration under shifted B cadence. *Old-task retention:* C and procedure during both transitions, and original binding recovery on fresh queries.

3. **An edit keyed to a learning event.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; train-time address):* Gate release by an unlabeled projection of live B features onto a learned key corresponding to a prerequisite representation, not by step count. *Novelty vs nearest:* Activation-keyed momentum (2608.19491) changes directional memory continuously; here the address is intended to release a prescribed correction selectively. *Cheapest first test:* Swap early and late prerequisite B blocks at equal counts, with time-gated and equally keyed external-delta arms. *Old-task retention:* Worst C and procedure scores before the prerequisite arrives and through readout.

4. **A staged prerequisite correction.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; dependent edit stages):* Split a change into a feature edit and an answer edit; the second slot tranche releases only when a public feature-separability proxy clears its calibration band. *Novelty vs nearest:* MEMIT (2210.07229) distributes a direct write over layers, not contingent writes with semantic stage order. *Cheapest first test:* A two-layer compositional toy with partial feature drift, compared to simultaneous, timed and proxy-gated weight edits. *Old-task retention:* Accuracy on unrelated compositions sharing the first-stage feature, and on C, through both stages.

5. **A correction with its own brake.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; shapes a known adaptive update):* Pair a signed `m` payload with a nonnegative addition to `v` on shared old-task coordinates so conflicting directions are initially suppressed while orthogonal ones release. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2610.15830 identifies the effect of `v` geometry, while parent card #10 proposes temporary protection; require a benefit from the **joint** injection over either intervention and their best external stack. *Cheapest first test:* Two overlapping toy bindings, crossed `δm` × `δv`, versus OGD plus a live-preconditioned delta. *Old-task retention:* Peak damage on the overlapping old binding at matched correction and B success.

6. **Observable-progress release.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; calibrates a release clock):* Meter release in cumulative movement of a public feature block, rather than wall time or batches, to survive LR warmup and gradient accumulation changes. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.24593 accounts for delayed effects on fixed paths; the claim here is transfer of a pre-calibrated dose rule across schedules. *Cheapest first test:* Rebatch the same B examples and vary warmup; compare fixed-step, LR-area and equal-signal external schedules. *Old-task retention:* C and procedure at the same B loss and correction progress, not at the same nominal step.

7. **A multi-future inverse edit.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; robustifies a one-shot write):* Solve for a common `δm` whose worst-case correction margin across several unrelated futures is positive, with a bound on old-output drift. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.20442 shows route-dependent sign, and 2608.19762 supplies a response operator; this asks when a shared solution exists and generalizes to held-out routes. *Cheapest first test:* Two opposing and two benign B routes, with an oracle per-route edit as a ceiling and robust scheduled deltas as the rival. *Old-task retention:* Worst-route C and procedure, including seeds where the edit misses.

8. **In-flight supersession.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; replace an unspent commitment):* Estimate the effect already delivered by a pending edit, cancel its remaining contribution, and encode only the residual needed for a newer correction. Do not roll back B updates. *Novelty vs nearest:* Sequential ROME-style writes (2202.05262) operate on current weights; distinguish a new pending-state operation from replacing an explicit delta queue. *Cheapest first test:* Supersede the same fact early and late in the release window; compare last-write-wins, queue replacement and a global reset. *Old-task retention:* C and unrelated edited facts during and after supersession, plus peak obsolete-answer recurrence.

### B. Compose, transport and budget pending writes

9. **A release conflict test.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; tests interactions, not an order law):* Fit two pending slot writes separately; test the four arms (neither, each alone, both) and reverse installation order when installation includes state-dependent clipping or projection. Look for an interaction attributable to the live denominator rather than shared endpoint saturation. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.19762 supplies the first-order response; row 223 already treats simple noncommutation as write-skew. *Cheapest first test:* Four-arm factorial at two amplitudes with matched scheduled-delta-plus-projection controls, single-install calibration and a held-out pair. *Old-task retention:* Worst shared old-task margin for the joint arm and untouched C.

10. **Bandwidth-limited slot multiplexing.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; several independently recoverable edits in a bounded carrier):* Encode two edits in distinct accessible directions of one slot block and let two public feature routes separate their release. Charge the decoder and keys to the budget. *Novelty vs nearest:* MEMIT (2210.07229) batches edits in weights and 2608.19491 shapes directional momentum; neither is the control that demonstrates independently addressable pending edits at equal bytes. *Cheapest first test:* Four possible two-edit combinations, two route orders, versus an equal-bit low-rank delta queue with the same keys; score cross-talk. *Old-task retention:* C and the edit whose route has not yet arrived.

11. **A portable pending-write interface.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; move a not-yet-released functional obligation):* Export a small response signature on public anchors and solve for slots on a successor with a different feature basis, without retaining the correction labels. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.20442 transplants moments within compatible states; parent #13 handles coordinate-covariant slots, not function-mediated transfer across non-isomorphic hosts. *Cheapest first test:* Move one edit between two widths, with a perfectly permuted host as a sanity check and an equal-byte distillation/delta control. *Old-task retention:* Successor's own C and old procedure, not just donor agreement.

12. **Reserve a safe release subspace.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; projects against predicted future interference):* Reserve directions that preserve multiple old-output Jacobians along a B prefix, rather than merely projecting a release at the cut. *Novelty vs nearest:* OGD (1910.07104) protects old outputs; this is a horizon-aware reserve for future writes, not a new projection primitive. *Cheapest first test:* Two public B routes with different representation rotations, comparing static OGD, per-step OGD plus scheduled delta, and a prefix reserve, at equal anchor and gradient budget. *Old-task retention:* Worst C and procedure margin under held-out rotations.

13. **Measure what survives a restart.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; distinguishes state loss from a broken scheduler):* Interrupt a partially released correction and independently restart moments, clocks and optimizer kind; translate remaining dose using only public anchors and pre-cut checkpoints. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2306.17833 resets an optimizer for plasticity, not to preserve an obligation in flight. *Cheapest first test:* One halfway Adam restart and one Adam-to-heavy-ball switch, versus equal-byte explicit pending-delta storage and restart with nothing. *Old-task retention:* Worst C immediately after resume and procedure after the remainder releases.

### C. Forget the future, not just the current output

14. **Same weights, different future deletion.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; causal deletion assay):* Swap exposed and never-exposed optimizer blocks into the same post-delete weights, and test whether ordinary B revives only exposed answers. Include the reverse weight swap to measure residual weight recall. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2406.13356 established revival, and 2605.17590 state-aware deletion for L-BFGS; this isolates Adam slot causation at a fixed forward cut, not the existence of revival. *Cheapest first test:* Paired synthetic bindings and one B continuation with crossed `m` and `v`, matched effective step and retrain reference. *Old-task retention:* C and the retained procedure at equal B progress, including reset forks.

15. **The two-threshold relapse loop (MFH4).** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; causal loop audit):* Ramp the fraction of public, binding-free B examples that reuse the procedure's feature pathway up and down. Check whether loop area or the gap between relapse and recovery thresholds follows transplanted slots at shared weights. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2602.03379 isolates syntax as a revival driver, but not a slot-specific loop at the same drive; 2608.20442 supplies route-dependent transport, not a return sweep. *Cheapest first test:* Five mixture levels in each direction, opposite-order controls with the same counts, clock-fixed slot crossovers at the turn, and slower dwell plus effective-step and one-update controls. *Old-task retention:* Worst C and kept-procedure accuracy on either branch; report relapse for direct and derived held-out queries.

16. **Is there a slot-specific aftereffect once the drive is removed?** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; separates finite lag from a persistent loop):* Hold B at the starting drive after a full loop, vary dwell and β, and transplant the post-loop slots onto a common pre-hold weight checkpoint. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2608.19762 predicts linear response and 2610.03940 studies history by age; use both as predictions for the decay of a deletion-specific difference, not evidence for persistence. *Cheapest first test:* Measure log-spaced dwell with two β values and compare to matched impulse-response and retrained curves; report a finite horizon, not permanence. *Old-task retention:* C and retained procedure during the hold; rule out recovery caused by erasing both forget and retain behavior.

17. **Cancel the stale push without flattening the optimizer.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; selective cleanup):* Estimate an exposed-minus-retain-only moment from a short public replay window when such a donor is available; remove only the component implicated by slot-cross tests, leaving unrelated moments intact. *Novelty vs nearest:* Norm-matched resets in 2610.03940 and the parent's laundering already motivate projection; the gain must be selective relapse reduction at equal effective steps. *Cheapest first test:* Sweep projection rank in the shared MLP against full reset, a matched random projection and DualOptim+-style splitting, budgeted equally. *Old-task retention:* C and retained task AUC over the first B window, not just final accuracy.

18. **Delete denominator history without deleting retain curvature.** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; repairs a second-moment estimate):* Replace suspect `v` coordinates with a public retain-only estimate while holding `m` and the clock fixed. Test whether stale denominators were routing steps onto obsolete answers. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2604.23046 studies residual second-order memory and 2610.15830 studies denominator geometry; require a relapse effect beyond scalar or per-layer rescaling. *Cheapest first test:* Match mean adaptive scale for crossed, shuffled and refreshed `v`; compare to reset and never-exposed `v` on two B routes. *Old-task retention:* C and procedure at matched B progress; score the cost of erasing legitimately protective `v`.

19. **Cancel a deleted source's pending corrections by provenance.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; revocable pending-state write):* Store source-tagged contributions in a compressed pending register; on deletion subtract the still-unspent contribution, then measure what remains in weights separately. Never claim to erase already delivered influence. *Novelty vs nearest:* DualOptim+ (2605.21539) separates objectives, and 2508.12220 replays a deletion counterfactual; ask whether cheap, per-source cancellation works in a shared stream without replaying the window. *Cheapest first test:* Two overlapping edits and one deleted source, versus equal-byte tagged delta cancellation and a retain-only retrain. *Old-task retention:* The other edit, C and B after deletion, alongside the deleted source's entire relapse curve.

20. **A continuation-bounded deletion certificate.** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; bounded behavioral contract):* For a declared finite family of public B drives, bound the maximum deleted-answer revival relative to a never-exposed trajectory by sensitivity through `(w,m,v)`; leave large or nonlinear cases unresolved. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2605.17590 bounds state deviation for online L-BFGS and 2508.12220 makes an exact trace-replay guarantee; this certifies a finite observable relapse envelope with an explicit abstention rule, not exact deletion. *Cheapest first test:* Enumerate short B paths in a tiny head with bounded gradients; compare certified coverage and false passes against a weight-only certificate and exact enumeration. *Old-task retention:* Require a separate lower bound on C and retained-procedure margins; report joint certificate coverage.

21. **Is cleanup necessary in this deployment?** *Mechanism (IMPROVEMENT; decision rule for bounded audits):* Use the crossed-slot response on calibration routes to predict when a weight-only audit is unsafe; abstain if the route or clock is out of calibration. *Novelty vs nearest:* 2406.13356 measures survival under fine-tuning and 2605.17590 measures state error; the target is the cost-sensitive choice between keeping, selectively cleaning and resetting slots for declared future use. *Cheapest first test:* Train the rule on two B mixtures and test on both an unseen mixture and an unseen synthetic binding, versus always reset and the linear response score. *Old-task retention:* Retained-task failures at equal missed-relapse rate, with coverage and B progress.

22. **Can a pending correction and a deletion coexist?** *Mechanism (PRIMITIVE candidate; composable slot operations):* Design install and delete operations to remove an exposed donor contribution while preserving an unrelated pending correction that shares coordinates; test both operation orders. *Novelty vs nearest:* DualOptim+ (2605.21539) trades off forget and retain gradients, while row 223 rejects bare order effects. Require a compositional guarantee on trajectories above that of split slots plus a tagged delta queue. *Cheapest first test:* Two overlapping keys at matched starting margins, install/delete in both orders, then hold out B order; compare with the stacked baseline and exact replay oracle. *Old-task retention:* Pending correction attainment, kept procedure and C, without revival of the deleted binding.

## Deeper questions, ranked by leverage

1. **Does writing a correction into live Adam state buy anything beyond an equal-budget release controller?** Start with cards 1 and 7. Calibrate exact heavy-ball equivalence, then test an Adam slot write on held-out B routes against immediate, delayed, tuned K-step and live-preconditioned deltas. If the strongest controller ties on deadline, retained tasks and resource cost, call it a storage format, not a new edit primitive.
2. **Does relapse hysteresis causally follow slots, and when must unlearning clean them?** Start with cards 14–16: compare equal-output cuts with crossed slots, then compare loops at matched update scale, dwell and B progress to a never-exposed retrain. If the loop follows weights, or decays as predicted by simple lag, report that rather than claiming persistent slot hysteresis. If full reset works but selective cleanup ties at equal retention, prefer the simpler reset for the tested deployment.
3. **Can pending writes be independently addressed, superseded and revoked after partial release?** Use cards 3, 8, 10 and 19. A primitive requires reliable independence and lower cost or better retention than an equally keyed and tagged external queue, not just successful cancellation in one planted example.
4. **What is the cheapest useful guarantee on future behavior after a correction or deletion?** Use cards 11, 20 and 22 only after a carrier or interaction survives the controls. Ask which allowed continuations and restarts can be covered at a nontrivial retention floor, and how often the guarantee must abstain. Exact replay remains the reference, not a result to claim from a bounded audit.
