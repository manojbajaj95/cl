# Learning from other models 2: Portable skill bequests and base-upgrade delta triage

Child of [PR #358](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/358) ([root](root-learning-from-other-models.md)) · Depth 1 · 28 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** A continual learner's accumulated skills are almost always trapped inside the base model and architecture that learned them. When a stronger base model or a different architecture arrives, teams either throw away the old model's adaptations or blindly port everything—double-dosing skills the new base already absorbed and dragging obsolete workarounds forward to overwrite the new base's real improvements. Make cross-generational inheritance a *first-class continual-learning loop* with two coupled operations: (1) during a learner's life, regularize its updates so an unseen future architecture can cheaply rebuild its skills from a compact, modular teaching packet (**portable skill bequest**); and (2) at upgrade time, decompose the predecessor's delta into **drop** (already absorbed), **migrate** (still needed), and **retire** (contradicted by the new base's better knowledge) before transferring anything.

**Scope.** Deepens only **LM28** (portable skill bequest) and **LM30** (predecessor-delta triage across a base-model upgrade), the two directions kept from [PR #358](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/358) in [flood-triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md); neither advanced to the top ten. Excludes PR #358's dropped cards (global proxy-tuning diffs, task-vector negation, checkpoint curricula, subliminal filters, peer QBC, generic weak-to-strong), symmetric fleet anti-collapse ([collective agents](root-collective-agents.md)), and optimizer-state / fold-code lines (PRs #352–#357). Every card must beat strong named nulls—including post-hoc dataset distillation, coresets, proxy-tuning, task-vector transport, GRAFT/Trans-LoRA, and weak-to-strong trust functions—at matched bytes, probes, and compute.

**Shared first-test contract.** All tests below are CPU-first proposals on tiny models (linear, MLP, gated MLP, tiny transformer, or constrained rule-routing agents); no experiments were run. Every LM28 test trains living learner $M_{\text{old}}$ on sequential tasks $A, B, C$ ($B$ rare), then hands off to an *unseen* successor architecture $M_{\text{new}}$ with a fixed $K$-step rebuild budget and fixed packet byte budget; always report *both* $M_{\text{new}}$'s inheritance speed/accuracy and $M_{\text{old}}$'s own living accuracy/retention (the **bequest tax**). Every LM30 test adapts base $B_0$ with mixed skills (private rules still needed by $B_1$, rules already absorbed by $B_1$, and workarounds for $B_0$ flaws that $B_1$ fixed), then upgrades to $B_1$ at equal probe, verifier, and update budgets. Common controls apply to every card alongside its named control: no bequest regularizer, post-hoc equal-byte coreset, post-hoc dataset distillation, full distillation, global proxy-tuning, migrate-all, drop-all, and random three-way split. Every card's retention check evaluates untouched held-out $A/B$ tasks (plus $B_1$'s pre-existing/improved capabilities on upgrades) through the live model without oracle routing.

## A · Inheritability as a training-time objective of the living learner (LM28)

### L2-01 · Cross-family proxy-disagreement bequest regularizer
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a small heterogeneous proxy committee (e.g., linear head + tiny MLP + prototype/k-NN learner) during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life. Penalize any continual update to $M_{\text{old}}$ that widens the accuracy spread across proxies rebuilding from the compact teaching packet in $K$ steps—steering $M_{\text{old}}$ toward decision geometries that are architecture-agnostic rather than idiosyncratic to its own inductive bias.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dataset Distillation via Drifting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16642) and [Influence Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16859) optimize synthetic data for a *fixed* model/dataset; [TGDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02469) matches frozen trajectories. Steering the *living learner's weights* to minimize cross-architecture proxy spread from a fixed-byte packet is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train $M_{\text{old}}$ (MLP) on $A, B, C$ with a 3-proxy committee, then test $K$-step rebuild on an unseen tiny transformer $M_{\text{new}}$. Compare no regularizer, single-proxy bequest, and post-hoc dataset distillation at equal packet bytes. Kill if single-proxy bequest or post-hoc distillation matches $M_{\text{new}}$'s $K$-step accuracy or if the bequest tax on $M_{\text{old}}$ exceeds the inheritance gain.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $M_{\text{old}}$'s living $A/B$ accuracy (bequest tax) and $M_{\text{new}}$'s inherited $A/B$ accuracy after $K$ steps, reporting rare task $B$ separately.

### L2-02 · Modular subset-reconstructible bequest packets
- **Mechanism:** Partition the teaching packet into per-skill sub-packets $(P_A, P_B, P_C)$ and regularize $M_{\text{old}}$ during training so that a proxy trained on *any subset* of sub-packets reproduces $M_{\text{old}}$ on that subset without needing the remaining sub-packets—enabling selective inheritance by future successors.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Distill Once, Adapt Life-Long](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20196) condenses a monolithic source set; [Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27376) composes soft prompts on one *fixed* base. Training the living predecessor so its packet is *subset-composable across unseen architectures* is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4 sequential tasks ($A, B, C, D$); hand an unseen successor only $\{P_B, P_D\}$. Compare monolithic dataset distillation, per-task independent coresets, and subset-regularized bequest at equal total bytes. Kill if independent per-task coresets match subset rebuild accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that inheriting $\{P_B, P_D\}$ does not corrupt the successor's pre-existing capabilities outside $\{B, D\}$, and that $M_{\text{old}}$ retains all four tasks during life.

### L2-03 · Discrete minimal-pair boundary sculpting
- **Mechanism:** Restrict the teaching packet to *discrete, token-valid minimal pairs* $(x, x', y, y')$ instead of continuous synthetic embeddings, and regularize $M_{\text{old}}$'s continual updates so its learned boundaries stay aligned with sparse concept flips expressible by those pairs—eliminating continuous-space overfitting to $M_{\text{old}}$'s input/embedding geometry.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Evolution of Dataset Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05673) documents cross-architecture degradation of continuous synthetic data; discrete text distillation struggles across tokenizers. Co-training $M_{\text{old}}$ to keep its boundaries expressible by discrete minimal pairs trades a tiny living-model bias for tokenizer-free portability.
- **Cheapest first test:** Discrete symbolic/rule-routing stream handed from $M_{\text{old}}$ to a different-tokenizer tiny LM $M_{\text{new}}$. Compare continuous embedding distillation (mapped via projection), random real-example coreset, and minimal-pair boundary sculpting at equal token bytes. Kill if a real-example coreset ties on $M_{\text{new}}$'s $K$-step rebuild.
- **Old-task retention:** Check $A/B$ accuracy on inputs far from the sculpted boundary pairs in both $M_{\text{old}}$ and $M_{\text{new}}$.

### L2-04 · Order-invariant teaching packet via permutation-averaged proxy rebuild
- **Mechanism:** During $M_{\text{old}}$'s bequest check, feed the teaching packet to the proxy in random permutations and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ whenever rebuild accuracy depends on packet ordering—so any future successor can mix the bequest into arbitrary replay batches or schedules without curriculum collapse.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Small Models Scout Bottleneck Order](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14936) extracts a *fixed* curriculum order from small models; trajectory-matching distillation matches ordered steps. Enforcing *order-invariance* on the living learner's bequest removes successor-schedule fragility.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compositional tasks $A, B, C$ where naive distillation requires $A \to B \to C$ order; evaluate $M_{\text{new}}$ across 10 random packet permutations. Compare ordered MTT-style distillation and shuffled coreset at equal steps. Kill if shuffled dataset distillation matches worst-case permutation accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Check $A$ and $B$ retention on $M_{\text{new}}$ under the *worst* of 10 random permutations (not just the mean).

### L2-05 · Anti-shortcut bequest via proxy-speed asymmetry
- **Mechanism:** Run two proxies during $M_{\text{old}}$'s bequest check: a *shortcut-biased* proxy (low-capacity/local-cue learner) and a clean proxy. Whenever the shortcut-biased proxy rebuilds a task from the packet *faster* than the clean proxy, reject or penalize the update until $M_{\text{old}}$ adds a counter-cue witness that flips the speed asymmetry.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [On Teacher Hacking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02671) diagnoses students exploiting teacher flaws; [Robust Dataset Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12069) matches adversarial trajectories for image robustness. Using *proxy-rebuild speed asymmetry* at training time to keep spurious cues out of a continual bequest is untried.
- **Cheapest first test:** Tasks $A, B, C$ with a correlated surface cue on $C$ that reverses at test time; hand off to unseen $M_{\text{new}}$. Compare standard bequest, post-hoc coreset, and anti-shortcut bequest at equal packet bytes. Kill if standard data augmentation or LfF-style debiased coreset matches $M_{\text{new}}$'s OOD accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Check $A/B$ accuracy on both cue-aligned and cue-flipped old test inputs after $M_{\text{new}}$ absorbs the packet.

### L2-06 · Budgeted bequest tax: selective portability tagging per skill
- **Mechanism:** Meter the *bequest tax*—the loss increase on $M_{\text{old}}$ caused by the inheritability penalty—per skill. Apply bequest regularization only where the tax stays below a threshold, and explicitly tag high-tax skills as **non-portable** (requiring full replay or environment re-derivation on upgrade) rather than degrading $M_{\text{old}}$ globally.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dynamic Proxy-Mixing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00400) transfers replay mixtures from small to large models; dataset distillation condenses every class uniformly. Treating *portability as a selectively budgeted per-skill property* with an explicit non-portable fallback prevents bequest regularization from harming hard living tasks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Mix of 4 structured rule skills (low tax) and 2 high-entropy memorization skills (high tax); compare uniform bequest regularization, no regularization, and tax-gated selective bequest at equal living-model accuracy floor. Kill if uniform bequest with a smaller global $\lambda$ dominates the living-vs-successor Pareto curve.
- **Old-task retention:** Report both $M_{\text{old}}$'s living $A/B$ accuracy (verifying hard old skills aren't damaged) and $M_{\text{new}}$'s accuracy on portable vs fallback skills.

### L2-07 · Anchor-residual bequest: encode only the delta from a pretrained prior
- **Mechanism:** Evaluate the proxy rebuild starting from a *pretrained anchor* (not random initialization) so $M_{\text{old}}$'s teaching packet is regularized to encode *only* the deployment-specific residual $\Delta_0$—never wasting packet bytes re-teaching general capabilities that any future pretrained successor $B_1$ already possesses.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Proxy-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08565) applies a decoding-time logit delta relative to a base; [Heirloom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00650) carries personalization across generations. Training the living learner so a compact *residual-only packet* transfers across *different* pretrained anchors (different families) is the proposed operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Pretrain two distinct tiny base models $B_0, B_1$ on general corpus $G$; adapt $B_0$ on private tasks $A, B, C$ with random-init vs pretrained-anchor proxy bequest; hand off to $B_1$. Compare proxy-tuning, full-stream distillation, and anchor-residual bequest at equal bytes. Kill if random-init bequest or proxy-tuning ties on $B_1$.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $B_1$'s pre-existing general corpus $G$ accuracy alongside inherited private tasks $A/B$ after $K$-step rebuild.

### L2-08 · Multi-horizon bequest: optimize K-step rebuild for post-inheritance stability
- **Mechanism:** Extend the proxy bequest check by running a synthetic subsequent task shift $D$ on the proxy *immediately after* it rebuilds $A, B, C$ from the packet, and penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if the proxy's inherited $A/B$ skills collapse under $D$—forcing the bequest to install *stable, low-forgetting* representations in the successor rather than fragile memorizations.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Influence Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16859) matches static test loss; [Distill Once, Adapt Life-Long](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20196) uses static condensation for TTA. Optimizing a predecessor's bequest for *the successor's post-handoff continual retention* under unseen future tasks is untried.
- **Cheapest first test:** Hand off $A, B, C$ from $M_{\text{old}}$ to unseen $M_{\text{new}}$, then train $M_{\text{new}}$ on two new tasks $D_1, D_2$ with no further replay of $A/B$. Compare static bequest, post-hoc dataset distillation, and multi-horizon bequest at equal bytes. Kill if static bequest + EWC on $M_{\text{new}}$ ties after $D_2$.
- **Old-task retention:** Primary metric is $M_{\text{new}}$'s untouched $A/B$ retention *after* learning $D_1$ and $D_2$.

### L2-09 · Self-verifying bequest certificate paired with the teaching packet
- **Mechanism:** Co-maintain two disjoint compact sets inside $M_{\text{old}}$: the teaching packet $P$ (fed to $M_{\text{new}}$) and a held-out *verification certificate* $V$ (never trained on by $M_{\text{new}}$). Penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ whenever a proxy achieves high accuracy on $P$ while failing $V$, guaranteeing that $M_{\text{new}}$ can self-audit true rule transfer without access to $M_{\text{old}}$'s private stream.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) probes merged adapters offline; standard dataset distillation relies on an external validation split. Co-training $M_{\text{old}}$ so a *compact disjoint certificate $V$ tightly bounds $P$-to-generalization transfer across architectures* turns inheritance into a self-certifying handoff.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train 8 diverse candidate successors (including 3 that overfit $P$ without learning the rule) on $P$; test whether $V$ separates genuine inheritors from overfitters better than a random held-out split of equal bytes. Kill if a random split of the same size achieves the same AUROC.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $V$ catches regressions on rare old task $B$ that pooled accuracy on $P$ misses.

### L2-10 · Cross-capacity bequest scaling against weak-to-strong ceiling collapse
- **Mechanism:** Evaluate $M_{\text{old}}$'s teaching packet simultaneously on a *smaller* proxy (edge portability) and a *larger* pretrained proxy (weak-to-strong headroom), penalizing updates whose packet causes the larger proxy to lose accuracy on harder held-out probes—preventing weak-predecessor ceiling caps *at bequest-creation time*.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Weak-to-Strong Feature Elicitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12908) and [Trust Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01000) operate at the strong student after training. Shaping the *weak predecessor's* packet during its own lifetime so it elicits rather than suppresses a stronger successor's latent competence is the converse primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Weak $M_{\text{old}}$ (knows $A/B$, noisy on hard $C$) hands off to stronger $M_{\text{new}}$ (capable of clean $C$). Compare unregularized bequest, student-side trust-function filtering, and cross-capacity bequest at equal bytes. Kill if student-side trust functions alone match $M_{\text{new}}$'s $C$ ceiling and $A/B$ inheritance.
- **Old-task retention:** Score *both* $M_{\text{old}}$'s inherited $A/B$ tasks and $M_{\text{new}}$'s pre-existing hard reasoning benchmarks.

## B · Predecessor-delta triage across a base-model upgrade (LM30)

### L2-11 · Three-way causal triage (drop / migrate / retire) via base-upgrade contrast
- **Mechanism:** Given old base $B_0$, adapted model $M_0 = B_0 + \Delta_0$, and new base $B_1$, evaluate each behavioral slice $s$ of $\Delta_0$ across $(B_0, M_0, B_1)$ with a targeted verifier probe: **drop** if $B_1$ matches $M_0$ on $s$; **migrate** if $M_0$ beats $B_1$ on $s$; **retire** if $B_1$ beats both $B_0$ and $M_0$ (exposing $s$ as an obsolete workaround for a $B_0$ deficiency). Transfer only the *migrate* partition.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918) decides whole-specialist actions (retain vs port vs refresh); [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638), [Trans-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258), and [ReLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02606) port the entire adapter. Per-slice *three-way triage* before any cross-base transfer is the foundational LM30 primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** $B_0$ adapted with 24 rules (8 absorbed by $B_1$, 8 still needed, 8 workarounds where $B_1$ beats $M_0$). Compare migrate-all (GRAFT / full distillation), drop-all, random 3-way split, and causal triage at equal probe budget. Kill if migrate-all + replay ties on joint (needed + $B_1$-improved) accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Report accuracy separately on all three partitions (absorbed, migrated, retired) plus untouched $B_1$ general tasks.

### L2-12 · Low-label workaround detection: separating obsolete hacks from private conventions
- **Mechanism:** When ground-truth labels are scarce at upgrade time, distinguish **retire** ($B_1$ fixed a $B_0$ bug) from **migrate** ($B_1$ lacks a private convention) using a *paraphrase-stability & base-error signature*: workarounds correlate with high $B_0$ entropy and narrow brittle triggers, while private conventions exhibit sharp, paraphrase-invariant disagreement between confident $M_0$ and confident $B_1$. Spend scarce oracle/verifier queries strictly on ambiguous slices.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Trust Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01000) and [Contrastive Weak-to-Strong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07884) select weak-teacher samples on general tasks; [Learning to Interpret Weight Differences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05092) describes diffs. Active triage of *obsolete workarounds vs private conventions* under a tight oracle budget is specific to continual base upgrades.
- **Cheapest first test:** Same 24-rule upgrade world as L2-11, but restrict ground-truth verifier calls to 4 slices (1/6 of total). Compare random verification, disagreement-only uncertainty sampling, and signature-guided triage. Kill if plain $M_0$-vs-$B_1$ KL disagreement ranks workarounds vs private rules equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure false-retirement rate on rare private rule $B$ and false-migration rate on $B_1$'s fixed bugs.

### L2-13 · Surgical sub-adapter excision prior to cross-base weight transport
- **Mechanism:** Before passing $\Delta_0$ into a cross-base weight/LoRA transporter, project out the singular subspaces of $\Delta_0$ most sensitive to the *drop* and *retire* witnesses (via activation-weighted SVD / Fisher projection), producing a cleaned delta $\Delta_0^{\text{mig}}$ that is transported onto $B_1$ without double-dosing absorbed skills or resurrecting retired workarounds.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638), [Transporting Task Vectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12952), [From Parameter Rebasin to Functional Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32581), and [TiTok](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04682) transport the *unpruned* adapter/delta. Pre-transport *triage excision* turns weight transport into a selective upgrade operator.
- **Cheapest first test:** Same-family width/depth upgrade $B_0 \to B_1$ with a rank-16 LoRA $\Delta_0$ holding absorbed + needed + workaround skills. Compare unpruned GRAFT/task-vector transport, random rank pruning, and triage-excised transport at equal rank. Kill if unpruned transport + post-hoc fine-tuning on $B_1$ ties.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify migrated private rules ($A/B$) survive excision while $B_1$'s newly fixed capabilities suffer zero degradation.

### L2-14 · Triage-gated proxy-tuning for selective logit-delta upgrades
- **Mechanism:** Replace proxy-tuning's global logit shift $\ell_{B_1}(x) + (\ell_{M_0}(x) - \ell_{B_0}(x))$ with a *triage-gated shift* $\ell_{B_1}(x) + g(x)\,(\ell_{M_0}(x) - \ell_{B_0}(x))$, where lightweight gate $g(x) \in [0,1]$ is trained on the drop/migrate/retire witnesses to fire *only* on migrated regions, then optionally distill the gated policy into $B_1$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Proxy-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08565) applies the logit delta uniformly; [TiTok](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04682) uses token-level contrastive excess globally. Gating proxy-tuning by upgrade triage prevents global logit shifts from dragging $B_1$ backward on retired workarounds or distorting absorbed skills.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two small LMs ($B_0$ weaker, $B_1$ stronger) sharing a tokenizer on mixed absorbed/needed/workaround rules; compare global proxy-tuning, confidence-gated proxy-tuning, and triage-gated proxy-tuning at equal forward passes. Kill if global proxy-tuning or entropy-gated proxy-tuning ties on joint score.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate $B_1$'s absorbed and retired slices (checking for logit over-sharpening / double-dosing) alongside migrated $A/B$.

### L2-15 · Intra-skill residual peeling for half-absorbed capabilities
- **Mechanism:** When $B_1$ absorbs the common 80% of an old skill $s$ but still fails the rare 20% tail, do not make a binary drop/migrate call. Peel off the absorbed bulk by filtering $s$'s witnesses to *only* the inputs where $M_0$ still beats $B_1$ by a margin, and migrate a minimal tail-patch—shrinking carried adaptation debt monotonically with each base upgrade.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918) and [ReLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02606) make coarse task-level reuse decisions. *Intra-skill residual peeling* handles the realistic partial-absorption regime without re-migrating redundant bulk knowledge.
- **Cheapest first test:** Skill $A$ with 80% common rules (absorbed by $B_1$) and 20% rare exceptions (missed by $B_1$); compare binary migrate-all, binary drop, and tail-only residual peeling at equal bytes/updates. Kill if binary migrate-all incurs no extra forgetting on $B_1$ at equal updates.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both the 80% common rules (must not degrade from over-updating) and the 20% rare exceptions on $B_1$.

### L2-16 · Retired-workaround anti-regression anchors during migration
- **Mechanism:** When distilling or transporting the *migrate* partition onto $B_1$, compile the *retired* witnesses (where $B_1$ outperformed $M_0$) into an explicit **anti-regression anchor loss** on $B_1$—ensuring that re-installing $M_0$'s private rules does not accidentally re-open $B_0$'s old failure modes inside $B_1$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard continual-learning replay anchors *predecessor* tasks; here the *successor base's newly acquired improvements* are explicitly anchored against interference from the predecessor's migrated delta.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct a private rule $A$ whose features partially overlap a retired $B_0$ bug fix in $B_1$; compare plain migration of $A$, general $B_1$ replay, and retired-witness anchored migration at equal replay bytes. Kill if general uniform $B_1$ replay protects the retired bug fix equally well.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure *both* migrated $A/B$ accuracy and $B_1$'s retention of the retired bug fixes after migration.

### L2-17 · Interaction-aware triage: catching drop–migrate coupling failures
- **Mechanism:** Before dropping an absorbed slice $s_1$, test whether any *migrated* slice $s_2$ relies on $M_0$'s specific convention/interface from $s_1$ (by running pairwise composition probes where $s_1$ and $s_2$ co-occur on $B_1 + \Delta^{\text{mig}}$). If dropping $s_1$ breaks $s_1 \circ s_2$, promote $s_1$'s minimal interface stub back into the migrate set.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Per-task adapter reuse ([GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638), [Trans-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258)) treats skills independently; flood triage's write-skew (SD3) identifies pairwise interaction failures. Checking *drop–migrate coupling* catches compositional breakage invisible to single-skill triage.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two coupled skills: $B_1$ absorbs $s_1$ with a slightly different output format, breaking migrated downstream rule $s_2$; compare independent per-skill triage, migrate-all, and pairwise coupling-checked triage. Kill if independent triage + post-migration joint fine-tuning ties at equal probes.
- **Old-task retention:** Test single-skill $s_1$, single-skill $s_2$, and composite $s_1 \circ s_2$ on $B_1$ after upgrade.

### L2-18 · Online shadow-canary triage for unwitnessed private skills
- **Mechanism:** When pre-upgrade witnesses are incomplete, run $M_0$ and $B_1$ in parallel shadow mode on live deployment traffic: classify live disagreements into *absorbed* (agree), *candidate-migrate* ($M_0$ passes live verifier, $B_1$ fails), and *candidate-retire* ($B_1$ passes, $M_0$ fails), incrementally compiling $B_1$'s migrated patch only from confirmed *candidate-migrate* events.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918) runs static offline upgrade evaluation; [Dynamic Proxy-Mixing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00400) adapts replay ratios. Online shadow triage discovers *unwitnessed* private skills that static pre-upgrade suites missed while retiring workarounds automatically.
- **Cheapest first test:** Live stream where half of $M_0$'s private skills lack pre-upgrade witnesses; compare offline-only triage, full online distillation from $M_0$, and verifier-gated shadow-canary triage at equal shadow calls. Kill if full online distillation from $M_0$ matches joint score without regressing $B_1$'s new skills.
- **Old-task retention:** Track both witnessed $A/B$ and initially unwitnessed rare skills as $B_1$ absorbs live *candidate-migrate* patches.

### L2-19 · Span-level witness rebasing across tokenizer upgrades
- **Mechanism:** When $B_1$ changes the tokenizer (breaking token-aligned logit comparisons against $M_0$), rebase all behavioral witnesses into *character/semantic-span equivalence classes* using common-support probability aggregation before computing drop/migrate/retire margins—so tokenization changes are never misread as capability regressions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Heirloom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00650) flags vocabulary shifts as a blocker to personalization transfer; [CSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28895) aligns cross-tokenizer distillation. Rebasing *triage witnesses* to span equivalence before upgrade decisions prevents false migrations triggered solely by vocabulary splits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two tiny LMs with mismatched BPE merges where $B_1$ functionally absorbs 10 of 20 rules but splits their tokens differently; compare token-level perplexity triage, exact-string accuracy triage, and span-rebased probability triage. Kill if exact-string accuracy triage gives the same three-way partition at equal probes.
- **Old-task retention:** Check migrated rules containing newly split subwords alongside old $A/B$ tasks sharing those subwords in $B_1$.

### L2-20 · Multi-hop triage tombstone ledger across $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$ upgrades
- **Mechanism:** Carry a compact *tombstone ledger* of past drop/migrate/retire decisions across multiple base generations ($B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$). When $B_2$ suffers a non-monotonic regression on a skill that $B_1$ had previously absorbed (dropped), the ledger distinguishes "re-needed private rule" (re-migrate from archived witness) from "retired $B_0$ workaround" (stay retired).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Existing transfer work ([Trans-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258), [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638), [Heirloom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00650), [ReLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02606)) evaluates only a *single* $B_0 \to B_1$ hop. Handling non-monotonic regressions across 3+ base generations requires generational triage memory.
- **Cheapest first test:** 3-generation chain $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2$ where $B_1$ absorbs rule $A_1$ and fixes bug $W_1$, then $B_2$ regresses on $A_1$ while keeping $W_1$ fixed; compare stateless per-hop triage, keep-everything archive, and tombstone-ledger triage at equal bytes. Kill if stateless triage recovers $A_1$ without resurrecting $W_1$.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $A_1$, $B$, and $W_1$ across all three generations ($B_0, B_1, B_2$).

## C · Co-designing living bequests for cheap upgrade triage (LM28 + LM30)

### L2-21 · Write-time provenance tagging: "private convention" vs "base workaround"
- **Mechanism:** Whenever living learner $M_{\text{old}}$ adds a skill $s$ to its teaching packet, tag $s$ at write time by *why* $B_0$ failed it (private convention / preference vs publicly verifiable capability deficiency). At upgrade time, triage checks workaround-tagged sub-packets first for retirement and convention-tagged sub-packets first for migration—cutting upgrade probe cost sharply.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Neither dataset distillation nor [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918) records the *cause of learning* at write time. Coupling write-time origin tags in the bequest packet with upgrade-time triage bridges LM28 and LM30 directly.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stream of 15 private conventions + 15 base-deficiency workarounds learned during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life, then upgrade to $B_1$ under a tight probe budget (10 probes). Compare untagged triage, random probing, and provenance-prioritized triage. Kill if untagged triage matches three-way accuracy at the same 10-probe budget.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify mis-tagged edge cases (e.g., a private convention mis-tagged as workaround) are still caught before silent loss of $B$.

### L2-22 · Proxy-separable skill slots for zero-interference upgrade excision
- **Mechanism:** Regularize $M_{\text{old}}$'s living updates so each skill's teaching sub-packet $P_s$ updates an approximately independent functional region *on the proxy committee*. When $B_1$ later drops or retires $P_{s_1}$, excising $P_{s_1}$ from the bequest leaves $P_{s_2}$'s $K$-step rebuild accuracy intact even on an unseen successor architecture.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Backbone-Specific LoRA Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27201) shows weight orthogonality breaks across backbones. Enforcing *packet-level functional separability across proxies* makes excision survive architecture changes.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train $M_{\text{old}}$ on 6 tasks; at upgrade to unseen $B_1$, excise 3 absorbed/retired sub-packets and rebuild $B_1$ from the remaining 3. Compare unregularized bequest excision, independent coresets, and proxy-separable slots at equal bytes. Kill if independent per-task coresets show zero excision interference.
- **Old-task retention:** Check the 3 retained skills ($A/B$) on $B_1$ after excision, plus $B_1$'s own capabilities on the 3 excised skills.

### L2-23 · Compiled one-shot triage probe inside the bequest packet
- **Mechanism:** Co-optimize a tiny *triage probe set* $Q$ alongside teaching packet $P$ during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life: $Q$ is trained across diverse proxies so that any future base $B_1$'s zero-shot scores on $Q$ predict which sub-packets of $P$ are absorbed, needed, or contradicted *in a single forward pass* before running any rebuild steps.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) probes adapters post-hoc; [UpgradeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20918) runs full task evals. Compiling a *minimal diagnostic probe $Q$ during the predecessor's life* amortizes upgrade triage to one forward pass on $B_1$.
- **Cheapest first test:** 12 skills on $M_{\text{old}}$; test predicting drop/migrate/retire on 4 unseen candidate bases $B_1^{(1..4)}$ using a 12-item compiled probe $Q$ vs 12 random task examples vs full eval oracle. Kill if 12 random examples per skill predict the triage partition equally accurately.
- **Old-task retention:** Check whether triage errors from $Q$ cause any drop on rare task $B$ or $B_1$'s improved tasks compared to full eval.

### L2-24 · Sublinear generational memory via triage-pruned bequest shrinkage
- **Mechanism:** Across a sequence of strengthening base models $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2 \to B_3$, after each base triages and drops absorbed sub-packets, re-condense the surviving *migrate* sub-packets plus new deployment skills into a fresh residual bequest $P_t$. Test whether total bequest bytes $|P_t|$ stay bounded (or shrink) even as lifetime task count grows linearly.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Replay buffers grow linearly with tasks; [Heirloom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00650) migrates a fixed personalization store over one hop. Coupling *triage pruning* with *residual bequest re-condensation* tests whether generational continual memory can be sublinear in lifetime tasks.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4-generation base ladder (increasing pretraining coverage each hop) + 5 new tasks per generation (20 total); compare cumulative replay buffer, unpruned bequest, and triage-pruned residual bequest at fixed byte cap. Kill if a fixed-size reservoir replay buffer matches cumulative retention at generation 3.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure generation-0 private skills ($A$ and rare $B$) alongside generation-3 base strengths at the end of the 4-hop chain.

### L2-25 · Counterfactual upgrade rehearsal during the predecessor's life
- **Mechanism:** Periodically during $M_{\text{old}}$'s training, test its teaching packet against a *synthetic "upgraded" proxy* (a proxy given extra oracle competence on a random subset of tasks) and run a mock drop/migrate/retire handoff. Penalize $M_{\text{old}}$ if rebuilding from the surviving packet makes the upgraded proxy regress on the tasks it already knew.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Cross-architecture dataset distillation swaps random-init networks; [Weak-to-Strong Feature Elicitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12908) studies a single static student. *Simulating a stronger future base during the predecessor's lifetime* directly trains the bequest to be non-destructive under upgrade triage.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train $M_{\text{old}}$ with and without synthetic-upgrade rehearsal, then upgrade to a real stronger unseen base $B_1$ (different architecture). Compare static bequest, post-hoc dataset distillation, and upgrade-rehearsed bequest at equal bytes. Kill if static bequest + L2-16 anti-regression anchors on $B_1$ ties.
- **Old-task retention:** Score both migrated $A/B$ skills and $B_1$'s pre-existing/improved capabilities after $K$-step rebuild.

### L2-26 · Compatibility-routed dual-channel handoff (weight transport vs bequest rebuild)
- **Mechanism:** After running L2-11 three-way triage on $B_1$, compute a *structural compatibility score* between $B_0$ and $B_1$ (layer/representation alignment on probe $Q$): route migrated slices through excised weight/LoRA transport (L2-13 / GRAFT) when $B_1$ is structurally compatible, and automatically fall back to $K$-step teaching-packet rebuild (LM28) when $B_1$ changes architecture, depth, or tokenizer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [From Parameter Rebasin to Functional Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32581) and [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638) assume weight/representation transfer is always the vehicle; dataset distillation ignores weight compatibility. Routing the *same triaged partition* across weight transport vs packet rebuild tests where each mechanism actually wins.
- **Cheapest first test:** Evaluate upgrades from $B_0$ to three targets: same-family fine-tune $B_1^{\text{same}}$, deeper width-changed $B_1^{\text{wider}}$, and different-architecture $B_1^{\text{diff}}$; compare always-transport, always-packet-rebuild, and compatibility-routed handoff at equal compute. Kill if always-packet-rebuild dominates across all three targets.
- **Old-task retention:** Compare $A/B$ retention and $B_1$ preservation across all three upgrade target types.

### L2-27 · Predecessor-encoded weak-to-strong trust weights in the teaching packet
- **Mechanism:** Attach a per-item *origin-and-confidence weight* to each entry in $M_{\text{old}}$'s teaching packet: hard-constraint weight for verified private conventions, soft-hint weight for $M_{\text{old}}$'s uncertain generalizations. When stronger successor $B_1$ rebuilds from the packet, $B_1$ obeys hard items strictly while letting its stronger prior override soft hints.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Trust Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01000) learn when to trust a weak teacher *at the strong student*; [Weak-Driven Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08222) uses weak agents to break strong-agent saturation. Encoding *origin-aware trust weights at bequest-creation time* lets $B_1$ separate authoritative private rules from weak predecessor guesses without extra labels.
- **Cheapest first test:** Weak $M_{\text{old}}$ holds 10 verified private rules + 10 noisy inferred rules; hand off to stronger $B_1$. Compare unweighted bequest, student-side confidence/trust-function filtering, and predecessor-weighted bequest. Kill if student-side trust functions alone match both private-rule retention and strong-prior preservation.
- **Old-task retention:** Score verified private rules ($A/B$, must stay near 100%) separately from noisy inferred rules (where $B_1$'s stronger prior should win).

### L2-28 · Granular sub-packet re-admission for reversible post-upgrade recovery
- **Mechanism:** Retain a compact *migration diff manifest* recording every sub-packet dropped or retired during $B_0 \to B_1$. If post-upgrade monitoring reveals that a "dropped" or "retired" slice actually broke a rare cohort, re-admit *only that single sub-packet* into $B_1$ via $K$-step rebuild—recovering from triage mistakes without rolling back $B_1$ or re-migrating the whole adapter.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Production upgrades roll back the entire model on regression; [ReLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02606) re-runs full adapter migration. *Single-slice surgical re-admission* from the bequest manifest turns upgrade triage errors into cheap, localized patches.
- **Cheapest first test:** Intentionally mis-triage 2 of 20 slices (one rare private rule wrongly dropped, one wrongly retired) during $B_0 \to B_1$; compare full rollback + re-migration, plain online fine-tuning on failure logs, and single-slice bequest re-admission at equal updates. Kill if online fine-tuning on the same failure trigger matches recovery without hurting other slices.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify re-admitting the 2 missed slices restores rare task $B$ with zero regression on the other 18 slices and $B_1$'s improvements.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does training-time inheritability regularization genuinely beat post-hoc dataset distillation or coresets for an *unseen* successor architecture? (L2-01, L2-03, L2-07, L2-10)**
   Train a continual learner $M_{\text{old}}$ on $A, B, C$ with proxy-committee bequest regularization versus no regularizer + post-hoc equal-byte coreset / influence-matched dataset distillation, then evaluate $K$-step rebuild on a held-out architecture family $M_{\text{new}}$ never seen during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life. Measure both the *inheritance gain* on $M_{\text{new}}$ and the *bequest tax* on $M_{\text{old}}$'s living accuracy. Drop the LM28 line if post-hoc equal-byte condensation ties across unseen architectures or if the living-model bequest tax exceeds the successor's gain.
2. **Can three-way upgrade triage (drop / migrate / retire) separate obsolete $B_0$ workarounds from private domain rules under scarce labels? (L2-11, L2-12, L2-15, L2-18)**
   Build a controlled $B_0 \to B_1$ upgrade benchmark containing absorbed skills, still-needed private rules (including rare $B$), half-absorbed skills, and obsolete $B_0$ workarounds where $B_1$ strictly beats $M_0$. Test whether behavioral signatures (paraphrase stability + $B_0$-error correlation) or write-time provenance tags (L2-21) achieve near-oracle three-way triage at a fraction of the verifier budget, beating global proxy-tuning and migrate-all GRAFT/distillation.
3. **When does excised weight/logit transport beat $K$-step teaching-packet rebuild on a base-model upgrade? (L2-13, L2-14, L2-26)**
   Fix the *exact same* triaged *migrate* partition and compare excised cross-base weight transport (L2-13 / GRAFT / task-vector transport), triage-gated proxy-tuning (L2-14), and $K$-step teaching-packet rebuild across three upgrade regimes: same-family refresh, width/depth scaling, and cross-architecture/cross-tokenizer jump. Identify the compatibility boundary where parameter/logit transport stops beating compact packet rebuild.
4. **Can co-designed bequest + triage keep generational continual memory bounded across 3+ successive base-model upgrades? (L2-20, L2-22, L2-24, L2-25)**
   Run a 4-generation chain $B_0 \to B_1 \to B_2 \to B_3$ with strengthening bases, new private skills every generation, and one non-monotonic base regression at $B_2$. Test whether triage-pruned residual bequests with tombstone ledgers preserve generation-0 rare skills without growing linearly in bytes or resurrecting retired workarounds—and drop the multi-hop line if a fixed-byte reservoir replay buffer ties at generation 3.
