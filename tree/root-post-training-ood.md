# Root: Post-training for lifelong out-of-distribution learning

**Node type:** Root · Ideas only (no GPU) · Scope: Why outcome RL and OPSD-style self-distillation fail on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, and new primitive-level learning signals that make sample efficiency compound over a lifetime of tasks.

## Root diagnosis: Five pathologies of current post-training learning signals

Standard post-training sits on a broken binary between **outcome RL** (GRPO/PPO: ~1 bit per episode, causally grounded on source, zero signal off-support) and **on-policy self-distillation** (OPSD [2601.18734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734) / SDPO [2601.20802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20802): ~$\log|V|$ bits per token, dense, but tied to retrospective token plausibility rather than causal value). Neither fixes OOD generalization or lifelong sample efficiency because of five structural defects in the **learning signal itself** — none of which an optimizer trick or replay buffer can fix:

| # | Pathology | How RL fails | How OPSD / SDPO fails | Lifetime consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Zero-signal cliff off support** | When OOD pass rate $p \approx 0$, every rollout in a group of size $G$ gets reward 0: zero variance, zero advantage, zero gradient. | Student rollouts never visit states where the privileged teacher's next-token logits are actionable; teacher likelihood $\log \pi(a \mid s, y^*)$ measures retrospective plausibility, not causal action value ([2608.09263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09263), [2610.18613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613)). | Every genuine OOD shift starts cold and wastes rollouts on flat zero-reward plateaus. |
| 2 | **Selection without construction (Pass@k collapse)** | Outcome RL amplifies one already-reachable winning mode, raising Pass@1 while shrinking Pass@k coverage ([2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837), [2610.04158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04158)). | Dense token KL sharpens the teacher's single surface rationalization, accelerating mode collapse. | **Negative lifetime compounding:** post-training on $T_1 \dots T_{k-1}$ prunes the exploratory modes that OOD task $T_k$ needs to get off the ground. |
| 3 | **Shortcut / mechanism confounding** | Scalar return cannot distinguish a generalizable rule from a source-correlated shortcut ([2611.03861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03861)). | Privileged teacher text rationalizes the training format and cues; SDPO's training gradient has negative cosine ($-0.167$) with held-out coding reward and degrades 4/5 OOD science/tool domains ([2610.18613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613)). | Shortcuts accumulate into rigid priors that actively resist later OOD tasks. |
| 4 | **Amnesic signal generation** | Task $T_k$'s reward is computed solely from $T_k$; replay/KL act only as brakes, not as a differential coordinate system. | Teacher distills full trajectories on $T_k$ from scratch, re-supervising already-mastered sub-skills and disturbing shared weights. | Task $T_{100}$ costs at least as many samples as $T_1$; knowledge does not compound into cheaper learning. |
| 5 | **Fused physics, goals, and confidence** | Policy logits fuse environment dynamics ("what happens"), task preference ("what wins"), and epistemic confidence ("do I know?") into one vector. | Distilling token distributions fuses style, physics, and goal preference, while destroying OOD calibration. | When an OOD task inverts a constraint or rubric, the learner cannot reuse its knowledge of *what happens* without unlearning *what used to win*. |

**Core thesis:** Fixing lifelong OOD sample efficiency requires inventing a **middle layer of learning signals** — signals carrying 10–100s of bits of *causal, structural, counterfactual, or compositional* information per episode (neither 1-bit terminal reward nor surface token KL), that stay non-zero at 0% success, preserve behavioral support, express $T_k$ differentially against $T_{<k}$, and enforce retention inside the signal itself.

**Ledger guardrails applied to every card below:**
- Every card names its **primitive object/operation** (a reusable learning signal or supervision interface, not a hyperparameter tweak) and the **strongest same-budget control** it must beat.
- Any predictor or selector card includes a **variance/headroom gate** (target variance across held-out items must exceed seed variance and a constant guess, per ledger PR #9/#21/#32).
- Any context/hint/lesson-bearing card includes a **matched-length filler / entropy-matched temperature control** (per ledger A3).
- Every cheapest first test includes an explicit **old-task retention check** (per Albert & Manoj's standing rule).

## Idea cards (36 primitive-level learning signals)

### Cluster A — Signals that stay non-zero at 0% OOD success (without token imitation)

#### A1. Minimal-World-Edit Distance (Environment-Side Counterfactual Signal)
- **Primitive:** The minimal environment perturbation $\delta^*_E(\tau)$ that turns a failed student rollout $\tau$ into a verifier pass, used as a continuous directional learning signal.
- **Mechanism:** When all rollouts on an OOD task $x$ fail, instead of rewriting the student's *tokens* (OPSD), a verifier-coupled world mutator searches for the smallest relaxation $\delta_E$ of the task's constraints/parameters (e.g., loosened tolerance, relaxed type/resource bound, removed obstacle) under which the *student's actual rollout $\tau$* passes. The pair $(\tau, \delta^*_E)$ reveals *which exact constraint* broke and by how much. The policy is rewarded for shrinking $\|\delta^*_E\| \to 0$ across rollouts, creating a smooth homotopy from the student's current support to the true OOD task without ever imitating a foreign trajectory.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from HER / AgentHER ([2603.21357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21357), which relabels *goals* from realized states and fails when no realized state is a valid task), POET/PAIRED (generates easier tasks from scratch rather than minimal world edits anchored to a failed rollout), and OPSD (mutates tokens, not world constraints). Strongest control: difficulty-staged reverse-curriculum GRPO at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU parametric puzzle/constraint-satisfaction DSL (10–20 constraints, base policy 0% pass on OOD target). Compare minimal-world-edit homotopy vs reverse-curriculum GRPO and OPSD at matched verifier calls across 5 seeds. Kill if it does not reach 50% OOD pass in $\le 0.5\times$ the verifier calls of reverse-curriculum GRPO.
- **Old-task retention check:** Track Pass@1 on 5 previously mastered constraint families after learning the OOD target; fail if mean old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp or worst-task drops $> 5$ pp vs pre-OOD checkpoint.

#### A2. Abductive Proof-Obligation Residual (Unsatisfied-Lemma Signal)
- **Primitive:** The minimal missing assumption $H^*(\tau)$ such that $\tau \wedge H^* \vdash \text{pass}$, scored by its logical/MDL strength as a dense learning signal at 0% pass rate.
- **Mechanism:** In verifiable domains (code with static/symbolic checks, math derivations, typed planners), run an abductive/SMT or precondition-extraction pass on a *failed* student rollout to compute the weakest missing lemma or precondition $H^*$ that would make the derivation valid. Reward policy updates that shrink the description length / entailment depth of $H^*$ across attempts (e.g., needing a weaker missing assumption until $H^* = \text{True}$).
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from PRMs (learned scalar step-reward models requiring labeled step data), TextGrad ([2406.07496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496)) / A-CEGIS ([2609.02892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02892)) (verbal critique prompts, no weight-level abductive-strength signal), and SDPO (token KL from verbal feedback). Strongest control: step-level PRM + outcome GRPO at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU symbolic algebra / typed-expression synthesis benchmark with Z3/Python precondition extraction, 0% initial pass on held-out OOD operators. Compare abductive-residual RL vs PRM + GRPO and SDPO across 5 seeds. Kill if sample-to-50%-pass does not beat PRM + GRPO by $\ge 2\times$ or if abductive strength fails a variance gate against random step counts.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate on 4 prior operator families; fail if old-task pass rate drops $> 2$ pp relative to matched-step GRPO.

#### A3. Backward Basin Growth via Inverse-Transition Preimages
- **Primitive:** Set-valued inverse-reachability supervision — growing the backward-reachable basin of the verifier's accepting manifold until it intersects the forward policy's support.
- **Mechanism:** When forward rollouts never reach reward 1 on an OOD task, sample states *inside* the verifier's accepting set (valid solved states, passing outputs) and apply inverse/undo transitions to produce $1, 2, \dots, k$-step pre-states. Train the policy to contract $k$-step pre-states toward $(k-1)$-step states across *many* inverse branches (preserving set-valued diversity, not mimicking one forward path), expanding the basin of attraction backward until forward rollouts land inside it and outcome RL ignites.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from Go-Explore (restarts from *forward-visited* states, still blocked when forward discovery is 0%) and SFT on worked solutions (collapses support onto one forward chain). Strongest control: SFT on the same solved end-states/trajectories + GRPO at equal verifier/step budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU reversible graph-transformation / symbolic-rewriting world where forward Pass@64 is 0% on OOD depth-8 targets. Compare backward basin growth + GRPO vs SFT-on-solutions + GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD Pass@1 and Pass@16 on unseen initial states do not both beat SFT + GRPO.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure Pass@1 and Pass@8 on 5 prior rewrite rules; fail if old-task Pass@1 drops $> 2$ pp or Pass@8 drops $> 4$ pp.

#### A4. Zero-Pass Equivariance-Defect Signal (Symmetry-Manifold Pre-Alignment)
- **Primitive:** Latent/decision equivariance defect under semantics-preserving task symmetries $g \in G$, used as a label-free learning signal while OOD reward is 0.
- **Mechanism:** On an OOD task where the policy gets 0% pass, its internal plans and action distributions typically break known task symmetries (variable renaming, unit scaling, argument commutation, dual formulations). Before or alongside sparse RL, train the policy to minimize the divergence between $g \cdot \pi(x)$ and $\pi(g \cdot x)$ across generated symmetry pairs, collapsing the policy onto the $G$-equivariant subspace so the first passing trajectory generalizes immediately across the entire orbit.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from test-time self-consistency voting (inference-only) and standard data augmentation (requires non-zero labeled/passing rollouts). Equivariance defect operates at **strictly 0% pass rate**. Strongest control: GRPO with symmetry-augmented prompts at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU symbolic reasoning / relational grid DSL with held-out OOD rule compositions and known symmetry group $G$ (variable/axis permutations). Compare equivariance-defect + GRPO vs augmented GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if samples-to-first-pass and held-out $G$-orbit generalization do not beat augmented GRPO by $\ge 25\%$.
- **Old-task retention check:** Check equivariance and accuracy on 5 prior tasks (including tasks where $G$ is *not* a symmetry, to verify no over-invariance); fail if non-symmetric old tasks drop $> 2$ pp.

#### A5. Verifier-Signature Failure-Mode Diversity Signal
- **Primitive:** Intrinsic exploration reward over the verifier's *structured failure-signature space* $\sigma(\tau)$ (which assertions failed, exception class, invariant hash) when pass rate is 0.
- **Mechanism:** Token-level entropy bonuses only diversify surface phrasing. Instead, parse every failing rollout's verifier output into a discrete causal failure signature $\sigma(\tau)$, and reward trajectories that produce *unseen* failure signatures while penalizing gratuitous token drift from $\pi_{\text{ref}}$. This forces the policy to systematically traverse distinct causal boundaries of the OOD task until it crosses the 0→1 cliff.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from RND/ICM curiosity (chases observation noise in state space) and MAP-Elites (requires hand-crafted behavioral descriptors). Here the verifier itself supplies the discrete causal descriptor space. Strongest control: entropy-regularized GRPO + high-temperature sampling at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU multi-assertion Python/DSL verifier suite (10 assertions per task, initial Pass@32 = 0%). Compare failure-signature diversity + GRPO vs entropy-GRPO and temperature sweep across 5 seeds. Kill if time-to-first-pass and final OOD Pass@1 do not beat both controls.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate Pass@1 on 5 prior verifier suites after the exploration phase; fail if old-task pass drops $> 2$ pp (verifying failure-space exploration does not trash solved policies).

#### A6. Hint-Bandwidth Ratcheting (Minimum-Bit Intervention Signal)
- **Primitive:** Intervention description length $I^*(x; \pi)$ — the minimum bits of external hint needed for the *current student* to pass $x$ — minimized monotonically to 0.
- **Mechanism:** OPSD conditions a teacher on a *full* reference solution (hundreds of bits, causing surface imitation and OOD degradation; [2610.18613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613)). Instead, construct a nested ladder of hints $(h_0=\emptyset, h_1, \dots, h_M)$ ordered by bit length (1 keyword → 1-line lemma → sketch → full solution). Find the smallest hint $h_{k^*}$ where the *student itself* passes, and train the student using only the delta from $h_{k^*}$ down to $h_{k^*-1}$, ratcheting $I^*(x; \pi) \to 0$ while keeping the student's own trajectory style intact.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from OPSD/SDPO (fixed full-solution/full-feedback conditioning) and task-difficulty curricula (change the problem, not the hint bandwidth). Strongest control: OPSD on full solutions + GRPO, plus a length-matched filler-hint control (ledger A3).
- **Cheapest first test:** Small LM (1.5B–3B) or CPU symbolic solver on OOD multi-step math/DSL tasks with nested 4-level hints. Compare hint-ratcheting vs OPSD, SFT-on-solutions, and filler-hint GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if held-out OOD accuracy (at 0 hint bits) does not beat OPSD by $\ge 5$ pp at matched verifier/token budget.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 5 prior task families at 0 hint bits; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp or degrades more than GRPO.

### Cluster B — Replacing OPSD's token-surface KL with causal & structural signals

#### B1. Binary-Search Pivot-Step Distillation (Causal-Bottleneck Credit)
- **Primitive:** Causal pivot steps $P(\tau^-, \tau^+)$ — the sparse subset of decision points where splicing a passing continuation onto a failing prefix flips verifier outcome from 0 to 1.
- **Mechanism:** Given a failing student rollout $\tau^-$ and a passing rollout $\tau^+$ (from a hinted student, search, or teacher), run $O(\log L)$ verifier splices to isolate the exact $k \ll L$ steps where changing the decision flips pass/fail. Apply policy updates *exclusively* at those pivot steps (zero gradient on all other tokens), eliminating the 95% stylistic/formatting KL that causes OPSD's OOD drift.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from OPSD/SDPO (dense KL on all tokens), RUDDER (learned LSTM return decomposition), and token-KL masking (high KL magnitude often lands on stylistic filler, not causal pivots). Strongest control: OPSD with top-$k$ KL-magnitude masking at equal $k$ and equal verifier/token budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU deterministic DSL / code-repair tasks where splice execution is cheap. Compare pivot-only distillation vs full OPSD and top-$k$-KL-masked OPSD across 5 seeds on OOD shifted test sets. Kill if OOD accuracy does not beat both controls by $\ge 5$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure Pass@1 and base-domain perplexity/KL on 5 old task families; fail if pivot distillation causes $\ge$ the old-task drift of full OPSD.

#### B2. Counterfactual Null-Effect Supervision (Distill What Must *Not* Change)
- **Primitive:** Nuisance-invariance ranking loss — supervising the learner directly on *what should have zero effect* on its action preferences.
- **Mechanism:** Both RL and OPSD only tell the model what to *do*, never what to *ignore*, so any source-correlated cue gets absorbed. Pair each training input $x$ with a semantics-preserving nuisance perturbation $\tilde{x}$ (reordered distractors, renamed symbols, altered formatting, varied length) and make the **divergence between the policy's action-ranking distributions on $x$ and $\tilde{x}$** a primary learning signal alongside outcome RL.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from IRM/V-REx (requires environment labels and regularizes scalar risk, not action-ranking invariance) and data augmentation (averages loss over augmented inputs, allowing the network to memorize both surface forms). Strongest control: GRPO on the same $(x, \tilde{x})$ pairs at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU cue-vs-rule DSL (crossing surface cues with true rules, holding out unseen cue-rule pairings). Compare null-effect supervision + GRPO vs augmented GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if cue-flipped OOD accuracy does not beat augmented GRPO by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test on 5 prior tasks including one where a *different* attribute is genuinely causal; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### B3. Cross-Trajectory Suffix-Transfer Metric Learning (Execution Bisimulation)
- **Primitive:** Verifier-induced behavioral metric $d_V(s_1, s_2)$ defined by whether passing suffixes from state $s_1$ also pass when executed from state $s_2$.
- **Mechanism:** Rather than distilling teacher logits, periodically cross-execute passing suffixes from state $s_1$ onto partial states $s_2$ from other rollouts/tasks. Pull hidden representations $h(s_1), h(s_2)$ together when suffix transfer succeeds and push them apart when it fails. This shapes a **functional bisimulation geometry** directly from verifier execution, enabling zero-shot OOD transfer whenever a new task's intermediate state lands near an old equivalence class.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from Deep Bisimulation / DBC (requires continuous reward and a learned dynamics model) and contrastive trajectory RL (contrasts future states within the *same* rollout, not cross-rollout suffix transferability). Strongest control: standard contrastive trajectory representation learning + GRPO at equal environment calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU stateful planning / grid-DSL where distinct surface prompts pass through functionally isomorphic intermediate states. Compare suffix-transfer metric + GRPO vs contrastive RL + GRPO across 5 seeds on OOD prompt-state combinations. Kill if OOD sample efficiency does not improve by $\ge 2\times$.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate Pass@1 on 5 prior tasks after representation updates; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### B4. Margin-Bounded Local Ordinal Constraints (De-Scaled Dominance Signal)
- **Primitive:** Scale-free dominance half-spaces $(a \succ_s b)$ with hard margin saturation $\gamma$, replacing unbounded KL / log-ratio updates.
- **Mechanism:** OPSD's KL and GRPO's normalized advantages are dominated by extreme-confidence logits, overwriting calibrated uncertainty on OOD inputs. Extract verified local action comparisons $(a \succ_s b)$ at shared states and apply a hinge/margin-bounded ordinal loss that exerts **exact zero gradient** once $\log \pi(a|s) - \log \pi(b|s) \ge \gamma$, leaving the rest of the distribution and logit scale untouched.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from DPO/IPO (full-sequence preferences that shift global sequence likelihoods and degrade OOD calibration) and OPSD (matches full next-token distributions). Strongest control: sequence-level DPO and KL-regularized GRPO on the exact same verified pairs.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU constrained-action sequential router / DSL across a 10-task OOD stream. Compare margin-bounded local ordinal updates vs DPO and KL-GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD accuracy and ECE calibration do not both beat DPO and KL-GRPO.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure Pass@1 on tasks $T_1 \dots T_5$ after learning $T_6 \dots T_{10}$; fail if mean old-task forgetting exceeds KL-GRPO or $> 2$ pp.

#### B5. Decoupled Consequence-vs-Preference Signal (Two-Channel Post-Training)
- **Primitive:** Orthogonal decomposition of every rollout into a **consequence signal** (predicting *what the world/verifier produces* given action $a$) and a **preference signal** (selecting which consequence satisfies the current goal).
- **Mechanism:** RL and OPSD fuse environment physics and current task goals into one weight update; when an OOD task changes the goal or constraint, the fused policy is actively anti-aligned. On every rollout, emit two separate learning signals: (1) a goal-agnostic transition/outcome-prediction loss updating a shared world-consequence representation on *what actually happened*, and (2) a lightweight preference/selection update over predicted consequences. When the OOD goal flips, channel (1) transfers 100% intact.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from Dreamer/MuZero (continuous-state MDP dynamics models, not post-training over symbolic/code/tool consequences) and OPSD (predicts teacher tokens, not execution outcomes). Strongest control: outcome GRPO + auxiliary next-token prediction on execution traces.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU tool/DSL environment where tasks $T_1 \dots T_5$ share execution semantics but $T_6$ inverts the goal predicate (e.g., minimize vs maximize, forbid vs require). Compare two-channel post-training vs GRPO + trace-NTP across 5 seeds. Kill if zero-shot/few-shot adaptation on the goal-inverted OOD task does not beat GRPO + trace-NTP by $\ge 2\times$ in sample efficiency.
- **Old-task retention check:** Re-evaluate tasks $T_1 \dots T_5$ after learning the inverted-goal task $T_6$; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp (must show decoupling prevents inverted-goal interference).

#### B6. Predictive Self-Refutation Signal (Learn From Minimal Falsifiers)
- **Primitive:** Counterexample-prediction supervision — training the learner to *predict the minimal falsifier that breaks its own proposed solution* before revising it.
- **Mechanism:** OPSD feeds the student a replacement solution; the student imitates it without learning why its own hypothesis failed. Instead, when the student's candidate solution fails, a verifier/fuzzer returns the **minimal counterexample** $c^*$ on which the candidate fails. Before allowing a revision update, the learning signal trains the model to *predict the failing outcome of its own candidate on $c^*$*, forcing it to internalize the failure boundary in weights rather than memorizing a patch.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from Reflexion / A-CEGIS ([2609.02892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02892), prompt-only test-time retry) and negative-advantage RL (pushes down all tokens of a failed rollout uniformly). Strongest control: GRPO with negative advantage on failed rollouts + SFT on revised passing solutions.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU program/rule-synthesis benchmark with an automated minimal-counterexample generator (e.g., shortest failing input). Compare predictive self-refutation + GRPO vs negative-advantage GRPO + revision-SFT across 5 seeds on OOD edge-case distributions. Kill if OOD edge-case pass rate does not beat the control by $\ge 5$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test on 5 prior rule-synthesis families; fail if old-task pass rate drops $> 2$ pp.

### Cluster C — Anti-collapse & support-preserving signals (fixing Pass@k narrowing over a lifetime)

#### C1. Mechanism-Clustered Portfolio RL (Distinct-Strategy Preservation)
- **Primitive:** Execution-mechanism-partitioned advantage — computing advantages *within* distinct mechanistic solution clusters and adding a scarcity bonus to under-represented passing mechanisms.
- **Mechanism:** Standard RL collapses to whichever single strategy wins first, destroying Pass@k and crippling future OOD tasks that break that strategy. Cluster passing rollouts by their *causal execution structure* (AST/proof graph/tool-call skeleton, not surface text), normalize advantages within each mechanism cluster, and maintain $\ge K$ active passing mechanisms per task.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from token-entropy or embedding-diversity RL (rewards superficial rephrasing) and MAP-Elites (stores separate policies in an archive rather than maintaining multi-mechanism support in one continual model). Strongest control: token-entropy-regularized GRPO + Pass@k-weighted GRPO at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU multi-path problem world where each task has 3 distinct valid algorithms (e.g., iterative, recursive, lookup), and later OOD tasks disable algorithm #1 (e.g., via depth/memory cap). Compare mechanism-portfolio RL vs entropy-GRPO and Pass@k-GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD adaptation after disabling algorithm #1 does not beat both controls by $\ge 2\times$ in samples.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify Pass@1 and Pass@K (mechanism count) on tasks $T_1 \dots T_5$ after learning $T_6 \dots T_{10}$; fail if old-task Pass@1 drops $> 2$ pp or surviving mechanism count falls below control.

#### C2. Mass-Covering Support-Floor Anchor (Forward-KL / $\alpha$-Divergence Lifeline)
- **Primitive:** Mode-covering support floor — replacing reverse-KL $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta \| \pi_{\text{ref}})$ (which allows total collapse of rare modes at tiny cost) with a mass-covering divergence ($\alpha$-divergence / forward KL on diverse base-model samples) specifically protecting reachability of rare base modes.
- **Mechanism:** Reverse KL lets RL zero out 90% of base support for $\log 10$ nats. Maintain a lightweight, rolling sample of diverse base-policy modes across unlabeled probes and apply a mass-covering lower bound so RL can *reweight* modes for Pass@1 without driving any base mode's log-probability below a floor $-\log(1/\epsilon)$.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from PPO/GRPO reverse-KL penalty and RL's Razor ([2509.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259), which studies reverse-KL on-policy RL forgetting vs SFT, not Pass@k collapse). Strongest control: reverse-KL GRPO + entropy bonus tuned to match average policy entropy.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 10-task sequential reasoning/router stream where later OOD tasks require modes that had only $1\%$ mass in $\pi_0$. Compare mass-covering floor + GRPO vs reverse-KL + entropy-matched GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if Pass@64 on held-out OOD tasks after 5 sequential tasks does not beat the entropy-matched control by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Track both Pass@1 and Pass@16 on tasks $T_1 \dots T_5$ across the 10-task stream; fail if Pass@1 drops $> 2$ pp or Pass@16 drops more than control.

#### C3. Bottleneck-State Compression Signal (Reusable Sub-Goal Emergence)
- **Primitive:** Intermediate-state information bottleneck — rewarding passing trajectories whose mid-trajectory state $z$ has minimal mutual information with the specific prompt $x$ given the remaining sub-problem.
- **Mechanism:** End-to-end RL builds monolithic task-specific trajectories that cannot recombine on OOD tasks. Add a cross-task compression signal on intermediate execution states: reward passing rollouts that pass through *canonical, low-MDL intermediate states* $z$ from which multiple different tasks can share the same suffix policy. Over a lifetime, tasks self-organize around a compact vocabulary of reusable sub-goals.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from Option-Critic / HIRO (fixed-horizon continuous-control sub-policies without cross-task state-bottleneck compression) and external macro libraries (frozen text/code snippets). Strongest control: flat GRPO + standard hierarchical sub-goal SFT at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU compositional state-machine world (tasks $T_1 \dots T_6$ share latent mid-states; OOD tasks $T_7 \dots T_{10}$ pair old prefixes with old suffixes in unseen combinations). Compare bottleneck-compression RL vs flat GRPO and sub-goal SFT across 5 seeds. Kill if zero-shot/few-shot OOD recombination accuracy does not beat both controls by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score tasks $T_1 \dots T_6$ after OOD recombination training; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### C4. Unsolved-Canary Coverage-Loss Signal (Pre-Forgetting OOD Early Warning)
- **Primitive:** Unlabeled OOD canary diversity — penalizing any post-training update that collapses the diversity of *valid partial states* reached on a fixed bank of unsolved, unrewarded OOD canary problems.
- **Mechanism:** Standard retention metrics only watch *solved old tasks*. A model can keep 100% on old tasks while quietly destroying its exploratory reach on future OOD tasks. Maintain a small bank of *unsolved* diverse canary prompts with a cheap validity/syntax checker; penalize updates that shrink the entropy of distinct valid partial states reached on the canaries.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from EWC/replay (protects old solved tasks only) and plasticity-reserve Gramians (killed in ledger PR #9/#21 because retention-matched NTK Gramian had zero correlation with next-task gain). Directly measures *behavioral reachability* on unsolved OOD probes. Strongest control: old-task replay + global token-entropy bonus.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 10-task stream with 20 held-out unsolved canary families (disjoint from the evaluation OOD tasks $T_6 \dots T_{10}$). First run a **variance gate**: verify canary state-entropy drop correlates ($\rho \ge 0.5$) with future OOD sample complexity across seeds; then compare canary-guarded GRPO vs replay + entropy-GRPO. Kill if the variance gate fails or OOD sample efficiency does not beat control by $\ge 25\%$.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure Pass@1 on $T_1 \dots T_5$; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp vs replay control.

#### C5. Cross-Context Verification-Gated Commitment (Two-Speed Margin Signal)
- **Primitive:** Evidence-breadth-gated margin cap — capping the logit margin of any learned behavior until it has been verified across $\ge m$ distinct context/perturbation families.
- **Mechanism:** RL commits equally hard to a fragile one-task correlation and a universal invariant. Track the **cross-context verification breadth** of each decision pattern (how many distinct prompt/domain/cue variants confirmed it). Behaviors with breadth $1$ receive a strictly capped logit-margin update (reversible, easily overridden on OOD shifts); only behaviors with breadth $\ge m$ unlock full margin commitment.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from uniform label smoothing / KL penalties (caps *all* logits equally, slowing genuine invariant learning) and weight-space Bayesian CL (intractable at LLM scale). Strongest control: uniform margin-capped / label-smoothed GRPO matched for average entropy.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU sequential stream mixing invariant rules (valid across 4 contexts) and transient context-specific rules (valid in 1 context, inverted later). Compare breadth-gated commitment vs uniform margin-capped GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if adaptation speed on inverted transient rules does not beat uniform capping by $\ge 2\times$ at matched invariant-rule accuracy.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure retention of *invariant* old rules after adapting to inverted transient rules; fail if invariant old rules drop $> 2$ pp.

#### C6. Minimax Surface-Shortcut Adversary (Action-Predictability Discount)
- **Primitive:** Online shortcut-predictability penalty — subtracting from RL reward the accuracy of a co-trained lightweight adversary predicting the policy's action from *non-causal surface features* alone.
- **Mechanism:** During post-training on $T_t$, train a tiny probe $g_\phi(\text{surface}(x)) \to \hat{a}$ using only stripped surface statistics (length, token position, formatting, variable-name hash). Discount the policy's advantage whenever its action is predictable by $g_\phi$ beyond the true task-label base rate, actively blocking shortcut absorption *during* post-training.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from DANN (strips domain ID from hidden features, requires domain labels) and IRM. Operates directly on *policy actions* with no domain labels required. Strongest control: domain-randomized / template-shuffled GRPO at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU cue-confounded sequential DSL (3 tasks with distinct hidden surface shortcuts, OOD test flips all shortcuts). Compare minimax shortcut-adversary GRPO vs template-shuffled GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if shortcut-flipped OOD accuracy does not beat template-shuffled GRPO by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate on 5 prior tasks (including non-confounded clean tasks); fail if clean old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

### Cluster D — Lifetime-compounding signals (making task $T_k$ strictly cheaper than $T_1$)

#### D1. Differential Mechanism-Residual Signal (Train Only on the Unexplained Gap)
- **Primitive:** Residual-to-prior-skills credit filter — computing the best zero-shot composition of existing capabilities $\hat{\pi}_{<k}$ on new task $T_k$, and restricting post-training gradients strictly to the **unexplained failure residual**.
- **Mechanism:** When $T_k$ arrives, first run a cheap inference-time search/composition over existing behaviors to solve whatever parts of $T_k$ are already covered. Isolate only the sub-steps/instances where $\hat{\pi}_{<k}$ fails while surrounding steps succeed, and send *only* that residual to post-training. Sample efficiency compounds because each task pays gradient steps only for genuinely new mechanism bits, and old tasks suffer minimal interference because shared machinery is untouched.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from boosting / residual RL (allocates a new network per task, growing parameters linearly) and adapter stacking (requires task IDs/routers). Here the residual is a *signal filter* on one continual model. Strongest control: warm-started GRPO + replay on all $T_k$ instances at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 20-task compositional DSL where each task $T_k$ adds 1 new rule atop 2–3 prior rules. Compare differential-residual RL vs warm-started GRPO + replay across 5 seeds, plotting samples-to-80%-pass vs task index $k$. Kill if samples-to-80% does not decrease with $k$ significantly faster than control.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate $T_1 \dots T_{k-1}$ after every task $k$; fail if cumulative mean forgetting at $k=20$ exceeds control or $> 3$ pp.

#### D2. Verifier-Tested Structural Morphism Signal (Analogical Transfer Reward)
- **Primitive:** Verified task morphism $\phi: T_k \to T_j$ — discovering a lightweight structural map from OOD task $T_k$ onto a previously mastered task $T_j$, and using $\phi$-transported verifier passes as a dense bootstrap signal.
- **Mechanism:** On a new OOD task $T_k$, search over lightweight structural maps (symbol/type renamings, operator bijections, state projections) into previously solved tasks $T_j$, and test $\phi^{-1}(\pi(\phi(x)))$ directly against $T_k$'s verifier. Even partial morphisms (passing 60–80% of constraints) immediately lift $T_k$ off the 0% cliff and supply a dense target for consolidating the mapped behavior + fixing only the unmapped residual.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from analogical prompting (in-context only, no weight consolidation or verifier-checked morphism) and MDP homomorphisms (require known transition matrices). Strongest control: retrieve-nearest-old-task few-shot prompting + OPSD/GRPO at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 15-task algebraic/graph DSL where later tasks are partially isomorphic (renamed + 1 mutated rule) to earlier tasks. Compare verified-morphism bootstrap + GRPO vs nearest-task ICL + OPSD/GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if sample-to-80%-pass on partially isomorphic OOD tasks does not beat control by $\ge 2.5\times$.
- **Old-task retention check:** Re-test the source task $T_j$ and 4 other prior tasks after consolidating $T_k$; fail if $T_j$ or mean old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### D3. Pairwise Seam Co-Training Signal (Explicit Interface Supervision Between Old and New)
- **Primitive:** Cross-task seam verifier $V_{i \circ k}$ — treating the *handoff interface* between an old task $T_i$ and a new task $T_k$ as a mandatory co-training signal whenever $T_k$ is learned.
- **Mechanism:** Sequential training on $T_1, T_2, \dots, T_k$ in isolation produces the decomposed-to-composed asymmetry ([2609.19465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19465)): models know $T_i$ and $T_k$ separately yet fail when an OOD task composes them. Whenever $T_k$ is trained, synthesize seam tasks ($T_i \to T_k$ and $T_k \to T_i$) with non-trivial interface checks (error propagation, state handoff, resource coupling) and co-optimize seam pass rate alongside $T_k$. This builds compositional OOD transfer *and* exercises old tasks in live compositions.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from "Composition contracts" (killed in ledger because atomic outputs trivially labeled hybrids in that toy). Here the seam verifier enforces *non-trivial joint invariants* that atomic outputs cannot label. Strongest control: equal-budget separate replay of $T_i$ + $T_k$.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU pipeline-composition DSL where $T_i$ and $T_k$ share a bounded resource/state register so composing them can fail even when both pass solo; evaluate on held-out 3-way OOD compositions $T_a \circ T_b \circ T_c$. Compare seam co-training vs equal-budget solo replay across 5 seeds. Kill if 3-way OOD composition accuracy does not beat solo replay by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate solo accuracy on all $T_1 \dots T_{k-1}$; fail if solo old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp vs equal-budget solo replay.

#### D4. Retention-Filtered Active Hypothesis Elimination
- **Primitive:** Retention-consistent version-space query — choosing the next OOD training input/rollout to split rival hypotheses that *all agree on seen $T_k$ data AND all preserve old tasks $T_{<k}$*.
- **Mechanism:** In [`tree/rl-discriminating-experiments.md`](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/57) (PR #57), standard entropy sampling picked cue-conflict points on a single task, yet *every* arm forgot rule A after learning rule B. By filtering the rival hypothesis ensemble to candidates consistent with $T_{<k}$ retention before computing query disagreement, each verifier call simultaneously achieves $O(\log |\mathcal{H}|)$ OOD identification *and* steers learning toward the generalization compatible with prior tasks.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from pool-based active learning / BALD / PR #57 (queries maximize single-task information gain without retention filtering). Strongest control: entropy/committee active sampling + old-task replay at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** Extend PR #57's CPU cue-vs-rule MLP/DSL stream to 5 sequential rules with ambiguous single-task cues. Compare retention-filtered active elimination vs entropy sampling + replay across 5 seeds. Kill if it does not beat entropy + replay on *both* new-rule sample complexity ($\le 0.7\times$ queries) and old-rule retention ($\ge +10$ pp on rule A after rule B).
- **Old-task retention check:** Built into the primary gate: rule A..$k-1$ cue-flipped accuracy must stay within $\le 3$ pp of pre-shift baseline after learning rule $k$.

#### D5. Behavioral-Space Meta-Signal Compiler (Online Credit-Assignment Improvement)
- **Primitive:** A continually updated, base-coordinate-invariant signal compiler $C_\psi(\text{trace}, \text{verifier\_output}) \to \text{local\_targets}$ that gets better over the lifetime of tasks at converting failed rollouts into OOD-generalizing updates.
- **Mechanism:** OPSD/SDPO uses a static teacher prompting recipe whose credit assignment never improves across 100 tasks; weight-space meta-editors (`tree/meta-editor.md`) break as base weights drift. Instead, $C_\psi$ maps `(rollout trace, verifier diagnostics)` purely in *behavioral/text/execution space* to sparse local step targets, and updates $\psi$ after every completed task using *held-out OOD gain minus old-task damage* as its outer reward.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from MAML/meta-RL (frozen after offline meta-training on a fixed distribution) and weight-space meta-editors (coupled to drifting weight coordinates). Strongest control: fixed SDPO/OPSD teacher + GRPO at equal total verifier and model calls (including $C_\psi$ update cost).
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 30-task sequential DSL stream (tasks 1–20 train/adapt $C_\psi$ online; tasks 21–30 held-out OOD families). First pass a **variance gate** verifying target-selection choices vary held-out OOD gain beyond seed noise; then compare online $C_\psi$ vs fixed teacher and uniform GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if tasks 21–30 sample efficiency does not beat fixed teacher by $\ge 25\%$ after charging all $C_\psi$ compute.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score tasks 1–10 after completing tasks 21–30; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp more than fixed-teacher control.

#### D6. Verifier-Certified Boundary Task Kernels (Generative Core per Solved Task)
- **Primitive:** Task kernel $K_t$ — the minimal set of verifier-checked, boundary-straddling synthetic examples that alone suffice to re-derive task $T_t$ and anchor future OOD compositions.
- **Mechanism:** Static replay buffers grow linearly and waste budget on easy interior examples while forgetting happens at decision boundaries. Upon mastering $T_t$, actively synthesize $m$ minimal pass/fail boundary pairs (where a 1-token or 1-constraint flip changes the verifier verdict) such that fine-tuning from a perturbed checkpoint on $K_t$ recovers $\ge 95\%$ of $T_t$'s OOD accuracy. Carry $\bigcup_{i<k} K_i$ as a compact, high-leverage retention and analogy core.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from GradMatch/CRAIG (selects from *existing* training data, inheriting source shortcuts) and continuous dataset distillation (produces non-verifiable synthetic tensors). Strongest control: equal-byte GradMatch + random replay of real $T_t$ examples.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 15-task symbolic/classification-rule stream with fixed byte budget ($m=8$ examples/task). Compare boundary task kernels vs GradMatch and random replay across 5 seeds (including a perturbed-checkpoint recovery test). Kill if boundary kernels do not beat both controls on retention AUC by $\ge 5$ pp and OOD boundary accuracy by $\ge 5$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Retention AUC across $T_1 \dots T_{15}$ *is* the primary metric; also verify no degradation on *interior* (non-boundary) old-task probes ($\le 2$ pp drop).

### Cluster E — World-interaction & epistemic signals (learning from what the environment refuses to do)

#### E1. Mid-Trajectory Execution-State Divergence Signal (Trace-Diff Credit)
- **Primitive:** First semantic state-divergence frame — comparing runtime execution states (variable values, heap/AST invariants, intermediate registers) rather than token strings to pinpoint the exact step where a failing rollout diverged from a valid state trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** Two valid solutions can share zero surface tokens while passing through identical semantic states; OPSD penalizes harmless token differences. Instrument the interpreter/sandbox to log compact semantic state hashes at each step, locate the earliest step where a failing rollout leaves the viable state set (compared against a passing sibling or partial spec), and apply credit strictly at that transition.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from OPSD (diffs token distributions) and Effect-Class Distillation (killed in ledger: matched *terminal* outcome classes, tying execution-reward replay). Here the signal is *mid-trajectory state divergence*, active even when all terminal outcomes are 0. Strongest control: terminal execution-reward GRPO + step-level PRM.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU instrumented Python/DSL interpreter on OOD multi-step algorithmic tasks (sorting/graph/parsing variants). Compare trace-diff credit vs terminal GRPO and PRM across 5 seeds. Kill if sample-to-80%-pass on OOD tasks does not beat terminal GRPO by $\ge 2\times$.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score 5 prior algorithmic families; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### E2. Decomposed Verifier Sub-Check Surprise Signal (Unhackable Dense Supervision)
- **Primitive:** Sub-check forecast surprise — training the policy to predict the *full binary pass/fail vector* of all $M$ verifier sub-checks before execution, using forecast error as a dense, non-hackable auxiliary signal.
- **Mechanism:** Summing $M$ sub-checks into a scalar shaped reward invites reward hacking (maximizing easy checks while failing hard ones); collapsing them to `all(checks)` yields 0% signal on OOD tasks. Instead, keep the policy's RL objective strictly on `all(checks)`, while adding a dense **sub-check surprise signal** training the model to predict *which* of the $M$ checks its own rollout will pass/fail. Accurately forecasting constraint violations immediately sharpens planning without distorting the reward function.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from reward shaping (distorts the optimal policy) and scalar value heads (collapses $M$ checks into 1 number). Strongest control: shaped-reward GRPO (sum of passing sub-checks) + binary GRPO.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU multi-constraint code/DSL verifier ($M=8$ checks, engineered so 6 easy checks conflict with the 2 hard checks if summed naively). Compare sub-check surprise + binary GRPO vs shaped GRPO and binary GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD all-checks pass rate does not beat both controls by $\ge 8$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate all-checks pass rate on 5 prior multi-constraint tasks; fail if old-task pass drops $> 2$ pp.

#### E3. Factored Probe-vs-Act Consolidation (Keeping Epistemic Habits Alive)
- **Primitive:** Separate consolidation rules for *information-gathering probes* (rewarded by posterior value gain) and *task-solving actions* (consolidated into fast priors only when stationary across tasks).
- **Mechanism:** Standard RL/OPSD either never learns to probe an OOD environment before committing, or strips away probing when distilling to fast behavior. Factor episodes into `(probe → obs → act)`: reward probes by the *counterfactual jump in action pass rate* given `obs`, and consolidate into zero-probe priors *only* rules with low cross-task variance while explicitly protecting the probing policy for high-variance OOD axes.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from RL² / Bayes-adaptive RL (implicit RNN probing on a fixed meta-distribution) and `frontier-scan.md` bet #1 (modular world-model fault diagnosis). Strongest control: multi-turn GRPO with the same total interaction budget.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU interactive hidden-rule environment (each OOD task has 2 stationary rules + 2 hidden per-task rules discoverable via cheap test queries). Compare factored probe-vs-act consolidation vs multi-turn GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD first-episode pass rate (after $\le 2$ probes) does not beat multi-turn GRPO by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test on stationary old tasks (must solve in 0 probes without losing speed) and recurring hidden-rule old tasks (must retain $\le 2$-probe accuracy within 2 pp).

#### E4. Switch-Off Robustness Slack (Continuous Distance Inside Hard Verifiers)
- **Primitive:** Signed constraint slack $\rho(\tau) \in \mathbb{R}$ (positive iff passing) computed automatically from verifier predicates, used *strictly below zero* ($\max \rho < 0$) and hard-switched off to pure 0/1 pass the moment any rollout passes ($\rho > 0$).
- **Mechanism:** Hard 0/1 verifiers discard near-miss magnitude on OOD tasks; permanent reward shaping causes reward hacking once the model starts passing. Instrument numeric/structural verifier predicates with signed robustness slack and use relative slack improvement across rollouts *only* to cross the $\rho < 0 \to \rho > 0$ boundary, switching immediately to pure binary RL above zero so the asymptotic optimum is untouched.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from continuous reward shaping (never switches off, corrupts optimum) and robotics STL control (hand-crafted continuous specs, not general continual post-training). Strongest control: binary GRPO + permanent shaped-reward GRPO at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU numeric/structural constraint-synthesis benchmark where OOD targets start at 0% pass ($\rho < 0$). Compare switch-off slack RL vs binary GRPO and permanent-slack GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if it fails to beat binary GRPO on samples-to-first-pass ($\ge 2\times$) *and* beat permanent-slack GRPO on final OOD pass rate (no reward-hacking gap).
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate 5 prior tasks (already in $\rho > 0$ regime); fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### E5. Verifier-Anchored Self-Play OOD Frontier (Anti-Drift 50%-Ridge Generator)
- **Primitive:** A co-trained OOD task mutator rewarded for generating *verifier-confirmed solvable* compositions of past tasks at $\approx 50\%$ student pass rate, gated by an old-task non-regression check so it cannot farm self-inflicted forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** Ledger PR #119 killed classic learning-progress (LP) self-curricula because at stationarity LP just farms recovery of self-inflicted drift. Fix the root cause: constrain the generator to produce *strictly novel compositional variations* (verified distinct from past tasks and verified solvable by an oracle/reference check) sitting on the 50% learnability ridge, while rejecting any generator step that relies on old-task regression. This builds a smooth bridge into OOD space before real shifts arrive.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from ALP-GMM / CURIOUS (killed in PR #119; no novelty or old-task non-regression constraint) and POET/PAIRED (no continual old-task retention gate). Strongest control: uniform replay + random task-parameter jitter + GRPO at equal rollouts.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU compositional DSL with 5 base tasks and 10 held-out unseen OOD compositions. Compare verifier-anchored self-play frontier vs uniform replay + random jitter across 5 seeds. Kill if zero-shot/few-shot performance on the 10 unseen OOD compositions does not beat random jitter by $\ge 10$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Mandatory per-round gate: all 5 base tasks must stay within $\le 2$ pp of pre-self-play accuracy (explicitly preventing PR #119's drift-farming failure mode).

#### E6. Online Sandbox-Fork Exact Advantage (Teacher-Free, Critic-Free Causal Credit)
- **Primitive:** Exact within-state advantage $A_{\text{fork}}(s_t, a) = \hat{V}(s_t, a) - \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \hat{V}(s_t, a_b)$ measured by snapshotting the execution sandbox at high-entropy decision points and branching $B$ alternatives from the identical world state.
- **Mechanism:** GRPO's rollout-level advantage has huge variance on long OOD horizons; OPSD's teacher has systematic OOD bias. In any snapshottable environment (interpreter, shell, simulator, proof assistant), fork the world state at the top-$k$ entropy steps of a rollout and score $B$ local branches. This delivers an unbiased, low-variance, teacher-free replacement for OPSD — paired with periodic forks on old-task states to detect drift early.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from MCTS/AlphaZero (uses a learned value head for action selection) and PR #51's stored fork tapes (focused on offline fork-table composition). Here sandbox forking is an *online drop-in replacement* for OPSD/GRPO advantage estimation. Strongest control: GRPO with equal total environment steps + OPSD.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU snapshottable multi-step code/planning environment (horizon 12–20, OOD tasks). Compare online sandbox-fork RL vs equal-step GRPO and OPSD across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD pass rate at matched total environment steps does not beat both GRPO and OPSD by $\ge 8$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate Pass@1 on 5 prior environments; fail if old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

### Cluster F — Retention-inside-the-signal & representation-level signals (escaping weight/optimizer space)

#### F1. Behavioral-Space Target Projection (Retention-Safe Targets Before Backprop)
- **Primitive:** Logit/target-space projection — solving a tiny conflict-resolution problem in *behavioral target space* over the current batch plus a few old-task anchors *before* computing any weight gradient.
- **Mechanism:** Weight-space projection (GEM/OGD) scales poorly with parameter count $P$ and breaks under nonlinear feature drift; LwF/DER adds a raw KL penalty that fights the new-task gradient at every step. Instead, construct the new-task target distribution $q(a|s)$ jointly with old-task margin constraints in low-dimensional behavioral space so that the *target itself* is minimally conflicting (e.g., choosing among equally passing new-task actions the one closest to old-task invariants), then train toward that consistent target.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from GEM/OGD (projects $P$-dim weight gradients) and LwF/DER (unprojected conflicting losses summed with a scalar $\lambda$). Strongest control: GRPO + DER/LwF logit replay at equal compute and anchor buffer size.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 10-task sequential multi-solution DSL (where each new task admits multiple passing policies, only some of which conflict with old tasks). Compare target-space projection vs GRPO + DER/LwF and OGD across 5 seeds. Kill if the Pareto frontier (new-task OOD accuracy vs old-task retention) does not strictly dominate DER/LwF.
- **Old-task retention check:** Primary axis of the Pareto frontier: must keep old-task accuracy within $\le 2$ pp of pre-shift while matching new-task accuracy.

#### F2. Online Linear-Probe Sufficiency Gate (Readout-First vs Deep-Feature Signal Routing)
- **Primitive:** Feature-sufficiency meta-signal — gating deep representation gradients on whether a frozen-backbone shallow readout can already solve the new OOD task under the same verifier.
- **Mechanism:** Early noisy RL gradients on a new task thrash deep shared features even when the existing representation already linearly separates the new rule (only the readout/policy head is misaligned). At the start of each new task (or shift trigger), run a fast shallow-readout probe on the same rollouts; clamp deep gradients to zero while shallow gain is rising, and unlock targeted deep-feature updates *only* on the residual instances where the shallow readout saturates below target.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from LP-FT (fixed two-stage heuristic for single-task supervised transfer) and frozen-backbone PEFT (never unlocks deep feature learning when genuinely required by OOD shifts). Strongest control: fixed LP-FT + replay and full fine-tuning + replay at equal total FLOPs.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 12-task MLP/small-transformer stream mixing 6 readout-solvable shifts and 6 genuine representation-requiring OOD shifts (unlabeled shift type). Compare online sufficiency gating vs LP-FT + replay and full FT + replay across 5 seeds. Kill if it fails to beat both on the joint (OOD acquisition, old-task retention) score.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure old-task accuracy across all 12 tasks; fail if readout-solvable shifts cause $> 1$ pp old-task drop or overall forgetting exceeds LP-FT.

#### F3. Amortized Behavioral Interference Critic (Preventive Retention Allocation)
- **Primitive:** A lightweight interference critic trained across the lifetime of tasks to predict, from a proposed new-task batch's *behavioral signature* before updating, which specific old-task clusters will lose margin.
- **Mechanism:** Uniform replay wastes compute on unaffected old tasks; MIR requires virtual full-model updates per step. Across tasks $T_1 \dots T_{k-1}$, log `(new-batch behavioral embedding, observed per-old-task margin drop)` and train a compact predictor that dynamically concentrates retention anchors *only* on threatened old tasks before the update lands.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from MIR (expensive virtual-update scoring every step) and `tree/edit-simulator.md` (single weight-edit forecasting). Strongest control: MIR + uniform replay at equal FLOPs and buffer size.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 20-task stream with structured sparse interference graph (each new task interferes strongly with 2–3 specific old tasks). **Mandatory variance gate first:** verify across-old-task interference variance exceeds seed variance by $\ge 3\times$ and a constant/recency baseline has $\rho < 0.3$; then compare behavioral critic vs MIR and uniform replay across 5 seeds. Kill if variance gate fails or worst-old-task retention does not beat uniform replay by $\ge 5$ pp at lower FLOPs than MIR.
- **Old-task retention check:** Worst-case and mean retention across all 20 tasks at equal replay budget.

#### F4. Verifier-Filtered Generative Retention (Anti-Memorization Dynamic Replay)
- **Primitive:** Closed-loop self-synthesized retention — generating *novel* instances of old task families and retaining only verifier-passing `(problem, solution)` pairs as a dynamic retention signal, preventing static-buffer overfitting.
- **Mechanism:** Ledger PR #8 killed self-quizzing QA on *factual recall* (where self-generated text tied teacher QA). On *verifiable procedural/reasoning tasks*, a fixed replay buffer of 500 prompts gets memorized over 30 tasks while true procedural generalization erodes. Use the model to generate fresh instances of old task schemas, filter strictly by each old task's executable verifier, and train retention on those fresh verified instances.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from PR #8 self-quizzing (unverified factual prose/QA, no procedural generalization test) and GAN/VAE generative replay (pixel reconstruction without an executable verifier). Strongest control: equal-token static replay buffer of real old-task prompts + MOPD ([2611.02746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02746)).
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 15-task procedural DSL stream with a fixed replay budget (50 prompts/task) and *held-out unseen instances* of old tasks for evaluation. Compare verifier-filtered generative retention vs static replay + MOPD across 5 seeds. Kill if held-out old-task generalization at task 15 does not beat static replay by $\ge 5$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Primary metric is held-out (unseen-instance) accuracy on $T_1 \dots T_{14}$ at task 15, separating true skill retention from buffer memorization.

#### F5. Bidirectional Program–Policy Disagreement Signal (Symbolic–Neural Co-Auditing)
- **Primitive:** The disagreement set $\{x : \pi_\theta(x) \neq P_k(x)\}$ between the neural policy and a compact executable program $P_k$ synthesized from the same verified experience, adjudicated by the verifier as a bidirectional continual learning signal.
- **Mechanism:** Neural policies silently absorb OOD shortcuts and drift over a lifetime; symbolic programs are brittle but explicit and immune to catastrophic forgetting. For each learned task, synthesize a compact candidate program/rule $P_k$, and continually sample inputs where $\pi_\theta(x) \neq P_k(x)$: when $P_k$ passes the verifier, $\pi_\theta$ receives a dense shortcut-free update; when $\pi_\theta$ passes, $P_k$ is patched. Over a lifetime, $\{P_1, \dots, P_k\}$ acts as a zero-forgetting behavioral oracle that detects and repairs neural drift on OOD and old tasks alike.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from Program Distillation ([2611.19461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19461), one-way LLM→program) and fixed-structure neurosymbolic RL. Here the *disagreement set* drives bidirectional refinement across a lifetime of tasks. Strongest control: one-way program distillation + GRPO + static replay at equal verifier calls.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 10-task symbolic/code rule-learning stream with OOD length/composition shifts. Compare bidirectional disagreement co-training vs one-way program distillation + GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD generalization and task-10 old-task retention do not both beat the one-way control by $\ge 5$ pp.
- **Old-task retention check:** Measure neural policy accuracy on $T_1 \dots T_5$ (without calling $P_k$ at test time, to ensure retention is genuinely in weights, not an oracle lookup); fail if neural-only old-task accuracy drops $> 2$ pp.

#### F6. Proper-Scoring-Rule Calibration Anchor (Epistemic Self-Awareness Under OOD Shift)
- **Primitive:** Joint pass-probability forecasting — coupling every post-training update with a strictly proper scoring rule (Brier/log score) on the model's explicit forecast $\hat{p}(x) = P(\text{pass} \mid x, \pi_\theta)$ across both ID and OOD-perturbed inputs.
- **Mechanism:** Standard RL and OPSD destroy calibration, leaving the model 99% confident on OOD failures and blind to its own forgetting. Require the policy to emit a calibrated pass-probability estimate alongside every rollout and jointly optimize a proper scoring rule against verifier outcomes. Over a lifetime, calibrated $\hat{p}(x)$ directly drives sample efficiency: low $\hat{p}(x)$ automatically triggers active probing/exploration on OOD tasks, while sudden drops in $\hat{p}(x)$ on old tasks flag forgetting before errors compound.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Distinct from post-hoc temperature scaling (cannot fix OOD discrimination or guide exploration) and static verbalized-confidence QA benchmarks. Strongest control: GRPO + post-hoc validation temperature scaling + entropy-triggered exploration.
- **Cheapest first test:** CPU 10-task sequential stream with unmarked OOD shifts and recurring old tasks, where the learner allocates a fixed exploration/probe budget per episode based on $\hat{p}(x)$. Compare joint proper-scoring-rule anchor + GRPO vs temperature-scaled GRPO across 5 seeds. Kill if OOD ECE does not drop by $\ge 40\%$ *and* budget-allocated OOD sample efficiency does not improve by $\ge 20\%$.
- **Old-task retention check:** Evaluate both Pass@1 and calibration (ECE) on tasks $T_1 \dots T_5$ after learning $T_6 \dots T_{10}$; fail if old-task Pass@1 drops $> 2$ pp or old-task ECE worsens relative to pre-shift.

## Ranked deeper questions (5 branches for expansion)

1. **What is the minimal-bandwidth learning signal that stays non-zero at 0% OOD pass rate without inheriting OPSD's surface-imitation bias?**
   - *Why it matters:* This is the central bottleneck between 1-bit outcome RL (flat at 0% pass) and $\log|V|$-bit OPSD (negative OOD gradient cosine). Finding a clean intermediate signal (10–100 bits of causal/structural information per episode) unlocks OOD learning without teacher-token overfitting.
   - *Feeding cards:* A1 (minimal world edits), A2 (abductive residuals), A6 (hint-bandwidth ratcheting), B1 (causal pivot steps), E4 (switch-off robustness slack).
   - *First separating test:* On a single controllable OOD symbolic/code testbed starting at 0% pass, compare all five signals head-to-head against GRPO, OPSD, and reverse-curriculum GRPO at equal verifier calls, measuring *both* OOD generalization on a held-out nuisance shift and old-task retention.

2. **Can a learning signal flip lifelong post-training from *negative transfer* (Pass@k collapse) to *compounding positive sample efficiency* ($N_k$ strictly decreasing in $k$)?**
   - *Why it matters:* Today, post-training on $T_1 \dots T_{k-1}$ makes OOD task $T_k$ *harder* to learn because RL prunes coverage and re-supervises shared machinery. True continual learning requires task $T_{20}$ to take a fraction of the samples of $T_1$.
   - *Feeding cards:* C1 (mechanism-clustered portfolio RL), C2 (mass-covering support floor), D1 (differential mechanism-residual signal), D2 (verified structural morphisms), D3 (pairwise seam co-training).
   - *First separating test:* Build a 20-task compositional CPU stream where every task reuses 2–3 prior primitives plus 1 new rule; measure the slope of samples-to-80%-pass vs task index $k$ (and Pass@16 on held-out OOD compositions) against warm-started GRPO + replay.

3. **Can execution-side causal signals (sandbox forks, state-trace diffs, sub-check surprise, self-refutation) replace privileged teacher text entirely?**
   - *Why it matters:* OPSD/SDPO assumes a stronger or solution-privileged teacher and still fails OOD. Real interactive environments (interpreters, containers, provers, simulators) already contain rich causal feedback that requires *no teacher model* and *no reference solutions*.
   - *Feeding cards:* B3 (suffix-transfer bisimulation), B6 (predictive self-refutation), E1 (mid-trajectory trace-diff), E2 (sub-check surprise), E6 (online sandbox-fork exact advantage).
   - *First separating test:* On a snapshottable multi-check execution benchmark, test whether teacher-free execution signals match or beat solution-privileged OPSD on ID sample efficiency *and* strictly beat OPSD on unseen OOD problems, at matched total verifier/execution steps.

4. **Does enforcing retention *inside the learning signal* break the nonlinear-drift and scaling limits of weight-space and optimizer-space continual learning?**
   - *Why it matters:* Weight-space (EWC/OGD) and optimizer-space methods have repeatedly yielded narrow or fragile gains in this repo's tree. Moving stability–plasticity into behavioral/signal space (target projection, seam co-training, verifier-filtered self-synthesis, program-policy disagreement) operates in low-dimensional outcome space independent of parameter count $P$.
   - *Feeding cards:* D3 (seam co-training), D6 (boundary task kernels), F1 (behavioral target projection), F2 (online linear-probe sufficiency gate), F4 (verifier-filtered generative retention), F5 (bidirectional program–policy disagreement).
   - *First separating test:* Compare signal-space retention against equal-byte/equal-FLOP DER/LwF, OGD, and static replay on a 15-task stream with *unseen-instance* old-task evaluation (catching buffer memorization).

5. **Is fusing physics ("what happens"), goals ("what wins"), and epistemic confidence ("do I know?") into one policy logit vector the root cause of OOD brittleness?**
   - *Why it matters:* If a single logit vector mixes all three, any OOD goal inversion or constraint change invalidates the learned policy and destroys uncertainty calibration. Separating them at the signal level could enable zero-shot/few-shot adaptation under goal inversions and automatic exploration when OOD.
   - *Feeding cards:* B5 (decoupled consequence vs preference), C5 (cross-context verification-gated commitment), E3 (factored probe-vs-act consolidation), F6 (proper-scoring-rule calibration anchor).
   - *First separating test:* On a multi-task interactive environment with *goal inversions* and *unmarked OOD shifts*, test whether two-channel consequence+preference learning with calibrated $\hat{p}(x)$ beats fused GRPO + trace-NTP on post-inversion sample efficiency without degrading pre-inversion tasks.
