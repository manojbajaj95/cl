# Internal skill markets: who gets to spend a continual learner's capacity?

**Branch of the tree:** bounded resource allocation and institutional design, not edit equivalence. **Status:** research ideas; no experiments run. All first tests are CPU-scale.

## What a market would buy

One model or agent learns a stream of skills. Each skill is a persistent capability with a held-out service measure, not necessarily a distinct parameter block. A skill's representative might be a replay queue, a small controller with local measurements, or a consumer of a shared module. It can request a scarce resource, but it does not get to grade its own performance. An internal clearing rule awards rights to resources the host actually enforces: adapter rank, inference or training compute, replay bytes and steps, attention or routing slots, or shares of a finite update. A **right** is not a copy of a skill: it says who can use which resource, when, and what happens if the service fails.

For instance, a rare skill might need a route slot and two replay batches together; either one alone is useless. A temporary package could buy both, lend unused routing capacity between calls, and pay a repair reserve if normal traffic stops reaching the skill. None of those operations requires identifying equivalent weight edits. The scientific question is whether transferable, conditional or enforceable claims solve an information or commitment problem that ordinary scheduling does not. Prices are internal accounting, not evidence of social value; credits do not create bytes or FLOPs.

### Prior art and boundaries

- [Switch Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961), [BASE Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16716) and [Expert Choice Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09368) already allocate finite expert capacity. Balanced assignment or auction-style token routing is a control, not the novelty. Baum's Hayek machine, Holland's bucket brigade and [Chang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02382) already use internal economic transactions for credit or control. Chang et al. buy the right to operate on the environment state; here the traded object is a claim across learning episodes on a resource, a repair, or a service obligation. That difference alone does not establish a gain.
- [GEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840), [A-GEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00420) and [PCGrad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782) are update-conflict controls; [Data Shapley](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/ghorbani19c.html) is a marginal-contribution benchmark, not a payment policy. VCG, Fisher markets, real options, contracts and insurance are imported mechanisms, not new economics. In particular, repeated learning changes bidders' values; do not invoke static VCG truthfulness for learned, budgeted bidders in a dynamic game.
- Read against the [reviewer's ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md). Its [killed section](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#killed) rejects a sharp replay deadline and adapter read-pinning. [Replay escrow](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/113) did help over fixed self-replay in a linear host; the ledger treats it as an improvement and points to stored-target replay as a control. Contracts below trade service or scarce repair access, not a predicted target-erasure cliff or an activation pin. [Redundant skill populations](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/116) shows why another copy behind the same trunk or router is not another source of protection. The [survivor section](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#survived-review) also reports diffuse damage in the functional-syndrome pilot and that self-QA did not beat teacher QA on the same questions: don't assume sparse claims or special value from self-generated evidence.

### Shared first-test contract

Start with one host: a small shared MLP with rank-limited adapters and a router, or a contextual bandit for pure dispatch tests. Train old skills to a declared launch floor, then feed paired streams of new skills and recurring, dormant or compositional old uses. Use synthetic regression/classification generators or a few gridworld layouts; give all arms the same observations, task IDs if any, and noisy audit budget. Make the economic decision matter by enforcing actual shortages, not by assigning fictional values to a static table. Split evidence into bidding, clearing/audit and independent final probes. Charge ledgers, stored targets, shadow evaluations, route overhead and reserve/repair resources to the matched budget, including byte-time when rights extend across windows.

For every card compare uniform/static quotas or round robin and the named strongest non-market control. Include a centralized allocator with the **same** signals, actuators and budget, plus an oracle as a diagnostic upper bound, not as the required baseline. Where bids simply expose a score the central policy can already use, test that score directly and ablate carryover, transfer or settlement. A market that merely solves the same optimization with different labels is an improvement at best; a primitive candidate needs a new measurable action or access pattern. Local means limited observations or communication, not a restriction arbitrarily imposed on only the baseline.

In addition to each card's targeted retention check, measure the ordinary old tasks via normal routing (no oracle IDs) before, during and after learning, including old tasks not represented in a bid. Track worst-old drop, time below floor, and new learning at equal expenditure and at matched new-task success where reachable. Report infeasibility rather than minting credits or discarding failing old tasks. Paired seeds and fresh probes should show an effect beyond noise; predeclare a material effect and a floor before running. The primitive labels below are hypotheses, not novelty verdicts.

## Raw ideas (24)

### A. Auctions for resources that have to work together

1. **All-or-nothing leases for rank, route and rehearsal.** **Mechanism:** skills submit XOR bids for alternative resource bundles across the next window; the winner pays in expiring service credits and a partial bundle cannot clear. **Novelty:** combinatorial auctions and MoE capacity are prior art; the candidate primitive is a portable, renewable claim on complementary *training and serving* resources, not a higher scoring router. **Cheapest test:** fit four overlapping classifiers whose useful rank needs both rehearsal and dispatch; compare independent clearing and a joint scheduler with the same complementarity estimates. **Old-task retention:** measure repaid versus evicted old tasks after both lease expiry and the next new task, through the original traffic mix.

2. **Auction a finite right to cause interference.** **Mechanism:** estimate each proposed update's impact on old service on fresh probes; clear an update only if each affected claimant's reserved loss allowance covers its debit. Credits buy mitigation or release of another skill's allowance above a hard floor; settle on realized loss, not just the gradient proxy. **Novelty:** GEM/A-GEM and PCGrad already constrain conflict; the candidate primitive is a time-indexed externality budget with settlement and an explicit victim, not projecting gradients by price. Relates to the ledger's diffuse functional-syndrome damage: count tails beyond the largest claimant. **Cheapest test:** stream rotating binary tasks into a shared MLP; compare with GEM and cumulative per-task loss constraints using the same probes. **Old-task retention:** track transient and accumulated harm on the claimants and on unticketed old tasks.

3. **Sell short, preemptible rank leases.** **Mechanism:** dormant adapters lend rank capacity with an enforceable call-back latency; borrowers must fund migration out of their lease or bid for guaranteed rank. **Novelty:** dynamic rank and preemptible scheduling already exist; the candidate primitive is a transferable return-to-service obligation detached from physical rank. **Cheapest test:** alternate short bursts and long gaps across six tasks with a fixed total rank; compare dynamic rank allocation plus a fixed dormant reserve and equal-byte compressed eviction. **Old-task retention:** score the first ordinary return request and the whole return burst, counting reload or reactivation as time and compute.

4. **Reverse auction for a curriculum bridge.** **Mechanism:** modules offer a priced commitment to produce a specified prerequisite for a new skill; retain payment only if a sealed transfer test passes and the module still serves its previous consumers. **Novelty:** transferability scores and reverse auctions exist; the candidate primitive is a purchasable *learning service* with an enforceable dual delivery condition. **Cheapest test:** in a small multitask MLP, choose among three candidate precursor drills for a novel composition; compare transfer-score scheduling with equal pilot and audit steps. **Old-task retention:** audit suppliers' original tasks and the old compositions they support, not their new representations alone.

5. **Open-access clearing for inference-time attention.** **Mechanism:** reserve a few retrieval slots for skills that commit credits to later use; charge congestion on crowded inputs and refund claimants only for cheap, randomly verified incremental service. **Novelty:** bidding for control and expert-choice routing already allocate runtime resources; the candidate primitive is a service class spanning requests, so a rare claimant can prepay access rather than win every top-k contest. **Cheapest test:** a tiny retrieval agent with useful rare rules and many frequent distractors; compare reserve-plus-top-k, sparsity and an entropy-matched temperature arm (the ledger's lesson-value control). **Old-task retention:** score rare old lookups and common old requests on held-out mixtures at equal context length.

6. **Sponsor a newborn skill's audition.** **Mechanism:** spend a fixed exploration endowment on provisional adapter/update slots; incumbent skills can co-sponsor candidates when independently audited transfer also helps them. Cap duration so a sponsor cannot indefinitely consume capacity. **Novelty:** bandit exploration and MoE expert growth are prior art; the candidate primitive is a time-limited coalition claim for a skill with no bidding history. **Cheapest test:** introduce two low-frequency learnable patterns and one distractor into a four-task MLP; compare uncertainty exploration with matched temporary quotas and explore-then-commit. **Old-task retention:** measure disruption of incumbents, including non-sponsors, both during audition and after credits expire.

### B. Prices and contributions across time

7. **A term structure for training versus serving.** **Mechanism:** quote separate prices by resource and delivery window; allow skills to reserve training steps now and sell the reservation back when their future inference liability changes. **Novelty:** online dual prices and reservation systems already exist; the candidate primitive is a tradable cross-window reservation, not a prediction of a replay deadline. **Cheapest test:** alternate compute-limited and route-limited phases with noisy demand forecasts; compare receding-horizon optimization with equal forecasts and a static reserve. **Old-task retention:** audit stale skills at the start of a peak and after a forecast miss as well as at the end.

8. **Pay a royalty on useful shared features.** **Mechanism:** rebate a supplier's maintenance credits when randomized equal-cost substitution of its feature route measurably hurts other skills; split payments on sampled coalitions and audit rarely. **Novelty:** Shapley valuation, bucket-brigade credit and feature reuse are established; the candidate primitive is an ongoing downstream claim on scarce upkeep, not an attribution score alone. **Cheapest test:** plant reusable and redundant features with fluctuating consumers in one MLP; compare fixed attribution, online use counts and an identical substitution-score scheduler with no royalties. **Old-task retention:** test old consumers who did not trigger the rebate and rare consumers after a supplier has gone dormant.

9. **Cross-subsidize rare skills with a service endowment.** **Mechanism:** redistribute a bounded fraction of traffic credits to protected service classes, then let recipients spend them on training or route capacity depending on audited bottlenecks. **Novelty:** fairness weights, max-min scheduling and Fisher budgets are prior art; only a portable endowment that buys different remedies under changing scarcity is a primitive candidate. **Cheapest test:** switch a rare task from replay-limited to route-limited; compare group DRO, adaptive dual weighting and separate fixed reserves with the same floor. **Old-task retention:** use balanced old-service evaluation rather than traffic-weighted average; also measure the incumbent cost of the subsidy.

10. **Trade compute for replay under a joint resource cap.** **Mechanism:** maintain separate clearing prices for bytes, update steps and inference calls; let a request express substitutes, e.g. more rehearsal steps on fewer stored targets. **Novelty:** resource-aware training and shadow pricing already do multi-budget optimization; this is an improvement unless local bids elicit useful substitution curves under a genuine communication limit. **Cheapest test:** change the memory-to-compute bottleneck twice in a shared regressor; compare online primal-dual allocation at the same measurement and communication budget. **Old-task retention:** report normal old-task accuracy just before and after the bottleneck swap, with the same total bytes, steps and calls.

11. **A clearinghouse for mutually helpful updates.** **Mechanism:** skills offer proposals and price combinations that share a fixed norm/step budget; the clearinghouse can net conflicting claims or fund a joint update when its measured gain exceeds solo offers. **Novelty:** multi-task gradient aggregation and PCGrad already mix updates; the candidate primitive is a multilateral exchange with explicit outside options and joint, not pairwise, settlement. **Cheapest test:** measure joint proposals on three related tasks where a pairwise proxy can fail; compare PCGrad, constrained gradient mixing and exhaustive equal-budget bundle scoring. **Old-task retention:** probe all old tasks including a fourth affected nonparticipant, and count cancellations that defer its rehearsal.

12. **Buy information before buying capacity.** **Mechanism:** a diagnostic market allocates a limited number of router counterfactuals, gradient checks, or repair trials to skills, then conditions the resource trade on those results. **Novelty:** active learning and value-of-information scheduling already price observations; the candidate primitive is a contingent claim that can demand a named audit before settlement. **Cheapest test:** hide whether failures in a tiny routed MLP come from dispatch or skill parameters; compare adaptive diagnosis plus greedy repair and a same-information two-stage planner. **Old-task retention:** evaluate repair on fresh old examples at natural dispatch and check old tasks deprived of diagnostics.

### C. Contracts for behavior rather than ownership of weights

13. **Auction responsibility for an old service.** **Mechanism:** a new route/adapter can bid to take over an old skill; require overlapping fulfillment, then release the former holder's compute/rank only after an independent handoff audit. Pay for verified service and allow reassignment on failure. **Novelty:** distillation and [redundant skill populations](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/116) already pose handoff; the candidate primitive is an assignable liability that multiple implementations can bid to assume, not another replica. **Cheapest test:** let two adapters offer to absorb a third skill during new learning; compare centralized distill-and-prune with identical overlap and audit cost. **Old-task retention:** measure normal-route service through handoff and after a shared-trunk or router perturbation, with forced routing only to diagnose.

14. **Output-service warranties for trunk updates.** **Mechanism:** register a service floor and audit slice per dependent skill; any writer can post a bond for trial updates, forfeited to a real repair pool if downstream service drops during the warranty window. **Novelty:** behavioral trust regions and the [killed read-pinning ABI](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#killed) are controls; the candidate primitive is a repair obligation attached to the *writer*, regardless of activation or implementation. **Cheapest test:** compare bonds with always-on downstream-response distillation and the same behavioral-gating rule plus a fixed repair reserve in a two-layer host. **Old-task retention:** measure end-to-end old outputs, including held-out downstream consumers, and count temporary failures before repairs.

15. **Supply-chain contracts for skill composition.** **Mechanism:** the caller reserves a complete chain of callable components, with a joint service level and a repair rule when the chain, not just a component, fails. Consumer renewals determine which dependencies retain capacity. **Novelty:** hierarchical RL and service-level contracts already compose components; unlike the ledger's [killed composition contracts](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#killed), this claims a coordinated reservation, not label-free composition. **Cheapest test:** two old gridworld routines share a bottleneck subroutine with a new routine; compare dependency-aware quotas plus direct generated-label composition training at matched calls. **Old-task retention:** check complete old routines under contention, including old compositions not cited in any contract.

16. **Port a service claim across implementations.** **Mechanism:** let a skill use the same budget claim to select text memory, adapter rank or retrieval at renewal, subject to held-out service and switching cost. **Novelty:** tiered caches and context-to-weights routing already migrate state; the candidate primitive is a portable, implementation-independent floor with transferable backing. The ledger's [stable-bias consolidation failure](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#survived-review) warns against choosing by salience alone. **Cheapest test:** alternate context pressure and weight-update pressure in a tiny rules agent with adapter and lookup backends; compare recency/utility tiering and joint migration scheduling. **Old-task retention:** score old service immediately after a move and after unrelated training, charging duplicated state during migration.

17. **A patron can attach a recall right to a sale.** **Mechanism:** an incumbent sells unused replay or attention allocation but keeps an audited right to revoke delegation under a specified service breach; buyers discount bids for this condition. **Novelty:** callable leases and preemptive scheduling exist; unlike rank lending in #3, the primitive candidate is a contingent **remedy against a particular allocation**, not necessarily returning the same resource. **Cheapest test:** run old-recurrence bursts where the remedy could be replay or routing; compare state-triggered priority scheduling with the same remedies and capped reserves. **Old-task retention:** audit rebound latency and old skills downstream of the displaced buyer, not just the original owner.

18. **Make exclusion challengeable with an audit bond.** **Mechanism:** a skill denied route access can stake part of its endowment on a sealed, equal-length substitute-route comparison. An upheld challenge pays for a temporary route claim; an unsuccessful one pays the audit. **Novelty:** exploration bonuses and randomized audits already discover suppressed experts; the candidate primitive is an auditable appeal that can overturn allocation without letting a bidder grade itself. **Cheapest test:** suppress a correct rare route in a contextual bandit; compare forced exploration and score-based reintroduction with the same audit calls and matched routing entropy. **Old-task retention:** check first return of excluded old skills and common old service while temporary claims are active.

### D. Insurance, reserves and system failure

19. **Mutual insurance against correlated forgetting.** **Mechanism:** estimate joint loss on independently sampled update windows; price correlated exposures higher, and pool a capped reserve of replay and alternate-route capacity to pay audited claims. Refuse liabilities that cannot be covered. **Novelty:** portfolio risk and replay buffers are established; the candidate primitive is a callable reserve priced by joint *service* failure, not the number of replicas. The [population node](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/116) motivates testing common causes. **Cheapest test:** alternate independent trunk disturbances and a shared shock across four tasks; compare static reserves, per-skill reserves and a correlation-aware central risk allocator. **Old-task retention:** measure simultaneous service shortfalls, recovery and reserve exhaustion under unseen shock combinations.

20. **Buy repair coverage rather than a deadline option.** **Mechanism:** before an announced training window, a skill can pay for guaranteed access to bounded repair resources if an independent audit finds loss; coverage may pre-fund saved logits, but the option is on access, not on an alleged replay-value cliff. **Novelty:** stored-target DER/LwF and replay escrow already protect targets; the candidate primitive is a callable claim on scarce *post-shock execution*. The ledger's [killed timing / surviving escrow](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#killed) result is the baseline, not evidence for a market. **Cheapest test:** use a shared regressor with uncertain shock scope and a strict cap on concurrent repairs; compare stored-logit replay plus central risk-based repair at equal bytes and steps. **Old-task retention:** score old skills immediately after the shock and by the coverage deadline, including uninsured skills competing for repair.

21. **Reinsurance on an independent execution path.** **Mechanism:** large shared-shock liabilities pay for a conditional fallback that does not use the liable trunk or router. Only consume its scarce service capacity when a verifiable systemic trigger fires; charge standby bytes throughout. **Novelty:** fault isolation and backup routing already exist; the candidate primitive is a second-loss-layer claim on a specific failure domain inside one agent, not replicas on the same substrate. **Cheapest test:** run an MLP and an isolated tiny lookup fallback under a joint fixed budget; compare ordinary backups and an identical central isolation plan with no contingent sharing. **Old-task retention:** test normal old requests through shared-trunk and route outages and account for false-trigger delays on still-working skills.

22. **Bankruptcy with service-preserving resolution.** **Mechanism:** when a skill cannot cover its obligations, freeze renewals, auction a verified transfer to another implementation, and dedicate a fixed winding-down budget to unsold liabilities before retiring it. No obligation vanishes from the report. **Novelty:** eviction, pruning and distill-then-delete already reclaim capacity; the candidate primitive is a resolution rule that transfers liabilities before deleting modules. The ledger's [killed utility-eviction law](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#survived-review) motivates sequence-order controls. **Cheapest test:** force two capacity crunches with the same suffix in a different arrival order; compare utility/LRU eviction plus best available distillation, with identical termination budget. **Old-task retention:** report delayed harm after retirement including unsold old service, not only the cleared subset.

23. **Countercyclical capacity reserve.** **Mechanism:** dedicate part of a fixed compute/replay cap to a buffer which releases only under audited widespread distress and replenishes from future allocation; cap debt and track who pays it back. **Novelty:** queue admission and adaptive replay already save slack; this is an improvement unless borrowing and repayment create a usable intertemporal right under tight service guarantees. It does not resurrect the ledger's [plasticity reserve](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#survived-review) Gramian predictor. **Cheapest test:** stream bursty task arrivals followed by slumps and then a second burst; compare burst-aware replay and receding-horizon reserves with identical caps and forecasts. **Old-task retention:** report floor violations in the first burst, during repayment, and in the second burst when earlier borrowing can leave the pool empty.

24. **Make a claim pay for its own measurement error.** **Mechanism:** route a portion of premiums to independent repeat audits; grade a claimant's predicted loss on random audited exposures with a proper score, and release claims based on audited service, not self-reported loss. **Novelty:** proper scoring rules, calibration and insurance verification exist; the candidate primitive is a self-financing audit obligation to protect unseen old service, rather than a belief that bids are honest. Follow the ledger's [noisy-ground-truth warning](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md#patterns-in-the-kills). **Cheapest test:** give a routed model noisy loss estimates and inject stale or inflated bids; compare random audits plus robust priority scheduling and measure split-half reliability of the audited loss first. **Old-task retention:** quantify unclaimed real harm and false payouts against untouched old-task probes, and check whether claimants gain capacity by degrading their own performance.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **When does a tradable service claim help beyond an equally informed scheduler?** Separate the value of local information, contingent commitment and actual exchange. Start with #1 and #13: one host, complementary capacities, a predeclared communication limit shared by both arms, and held-out demand changes. Cross local vs pooled signals with transferable vs fixed rights. If the same-signal central policy matches quality, communication and cost, drop the market claim in that regime rather than crediting an auction for a better input.
2. **Can a learner assign and enforce liability for harm to old service?** Randomize whether a proposed shared write is exercised, then compare #2/#14 debits with paired causal harm on held-out tasks. What can a small probe budget certify under delayed, diffuse and interacting damage? If audit errors or verification overhead consume the gains, keep the result as a limit on liability rather than a guarantee.
3. **Can protection be pooled without selling the same spare capacity twice?** Cross independent, common-cause and shifted shocks in #19–21. Map the feasible frontier between covered service, reserve capacity and acquisition. Distinguish a real independent fallback from one merely given another name or adapter slot.
4. **Can claims survive adaptation without inducing credit farming or permanent incumbency?** In #6/#18/#24, allow bidders to adapt to published rules and to underinvest in their own service. Test whether exogenous audits, expiry and endowments preserve rare and newborn skills without starving normal traffic or subsidizing strategic failure. Treat persistent capture as a mechanism failure, not a reason to assume truthful bidders.
