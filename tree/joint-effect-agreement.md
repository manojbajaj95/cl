# Joint effect agreement across ReLU-opening handoffs

**Status:** Ideas only; no implementation, training runs, or GPU work. **Parent:** [Effect-level loan commits](effect-commits.md) ([PR #252](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/252)), cards 02 and 17. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 367](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/bb47fc8dccf07385b9200928b7de045f762ceaa3/reviews/flood-triage.md#L367) advances these score-3 ideas: carry dependencies between outputs in each view's effect set and commit only on the joint intersection (never per-output matches); let a required old-task batch switch the receiver's ReLU on while carrying enough corroboration to realize the shared effect in the new cell, with no rescue step. **Read first:** [Idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/bb47fc8dccf07385b9200928b7de045f762ceaa3/reviews/idea-ledger.md).

## Intersect coupled effects, not marginals; wake the receiver with due old work

**Verdict: two distinct primitive candidates whose merge is testable only against a stacked joint-solver + due-batch baseline.** The first replaces per-output consent with a **joint multi-output effect intersection** `E₁ ∩ E₂` carried through the live state so marginal-only fakes are refused. The second lets a **required old-task update** switch a B-dormant receiver ReLU on (opening a new active cell or raising active rank) while transporting corroboration across the gate crossing, so the commit stays strictly inside the newly opened cell with no rescue step. The parent node already conceded offline set-membership fitting; therefore, intersecting polytopes or constraint sets offline is not novel. The live primitive bet is narrower: (a) a compact live-state carrier that preserves cross-output coupling across mandatory old work and beats an equal-byte buffered joint set-membership solver, and (b) a required old-task update that simultaneously repays A₁, wakes the receiver ReLU without harming A₂/C, and preserves enough multi-output corroboration to separate genuine joint consent from marginal-only fakes. A tie with the stacked baseline (same provisional write + identical due old update + equal-byte buffer + exact joint set-membership fit in the post-update cell) is a null.

**Nearest comparisons (2026-11-25).** An alphaXiv MCP search used the queries “continual learning set-membership function space joint output constraints AND-mask” and “continual learning ReLU activation region dormant neurons replay.” On the joint-effect side, [AND-mask][andmask] enforces coordinate-wise gradient sign agreement (marginal, in parameter space), [FROMP][fromp] and [Low-Rank Function-Space Forgetting][lrfs] constrain multi-output function-space drift, [SPACE-LoRA][spacelora] allocates activation-subspace protection, and classical [set-membership identification][smid] (conceded by #252) intersects feasible parameter/output sets directly. On the ReLU-opening side, [Experience Replay Addresses Loss of Plasticity][erpl] shows that replay revives dormant units, while [ReDo][redo], [Calibrated Partial Resets][cpr], and [Unit vs. Weight Reinitialization][uvwr] actively restore dead ReLUs. Those comparisons make “use function-space constraints,” “intersect feasible sets,” and “replay revives dormant neurons” insufficient novelty claims. The untested primitive bets are: (1) rejecting marginal-only fakes through a live-state-carried joint effect representation that survives mandatory old work, and (2) coupling a *required* old-task gate crossing to corroboration transport so a newly opened cell realizes only the jointly consented effect without any rescue step or separate reset. As noted in the ledger, effect-class distillation (PR #13) and forecast-weighted write selection (PR #9, #21) were killed; nothing here revives them.

### First-test contract

- **Joint effect chart and junk-pair gate.** Freeze the declared multi-output B chart `y ∈ ℝ^m` (`m ≥ 2`, e.g., paired true-vs-rival logit gaps on coupled canonical B queries at a frozen scale), legal examples, optimizer rule, expiry, and read permissions before blind jobs. For each view `v ∈ {1, 2}`, define its legal **joint** effect set `E_v ⊂ ℝ^m` preserving cross-output correlations and trade-offs. Every benchmark must include matched **marginal-fake pairs** whose per-output projections overlap (`π_j(E₁) ∩ π_j(E₂) ≠ {0}` for every `j`) while `E₁ ∩ E₂ = {0}`, alongside genuine joint-agreement pairs with identical marginals. A mechanism that commits on marginal fakes fails immediately.
- **Single-cell commit after due-batch ReLU opening.** Before the due old-task slot, the receiver ReLU(s) are strictly OFF on the canonical B queries (`pre-act < 0`), making the jointly consented effect infeasible in the pre-update active cell without crossing a kink. At the fixed due old-task slot, **both** the live arm and the no-write arm execute the identical required old-task update (same due batch, target, loss, and optimizer rule; test both singleton and frozen-menu schedules, charging any menu scan). No rescue batches, synthetic targets, manual gate edits, unit resets, or expiry resets. After the due step, the receiver ReLU is strictly ON (`pre-act > 0`), A₁ is repaid to its paired no-write read gap, and the commit realizes `Δy ∈ E₁ ∩ E₂` **strictly inside the newly opened cell** (zero gate crossing during commit, verified on the finite update, not only a Jacobian prediction). If the due step fails to open a legal cell or erases corroboration, abstain and default safely.
- **Old-task retention and gate-crossing audit.** Evaluate ordinary old tasks A₁/A₂ and prior skills C on separately held-out, starting-margin-matched panels (including near-boundary items). Audit itemwise gaps and accuracy at every exposed read, along the straight-line path across the receiver's kink during the due old step (where reads are exposed), at commit/default, and after matched unrelated updates at returns 1, 8, and 64. Require A₁ repayment, no double debit, and acquisition-anchored floors throughout.
- **Mandatory controls.** Compare against: (i) `#241#02` per-output (marginal) effect matching; (ii) `#233#05` parameter-space common direction and AND-mask; (iii) offline set-membership fitting on marginals; (iv) `#241#16` handoff within a fixed activation cell and `#233#13` ordinary-map transfer; (v) an equal-compute rescue batch or ReDo-style unit reset; (vi) an **opener-that-erases** control (same old-task gain, opens the exact same receiver ReLU, collapses the carrier); and (vii) the **strongest stack** (identical provisional write + identical due old batch + equal-byte waiting buffer + exact joint set-membership solver in the post-update cell, given the same observations; report an oracle full-information ceiling separately). Match peak bytes (weights, optimizer slots, carrier, and buffer), examples, probes, old-task gain, prequential B service, confirmation latency, and false-confirmation rate.

### W12 — two exact-arithmetic checks, not a learning result

**W12a — Joint intersection vs. marginal fake (convex 1-D sets).** On a two-output B chart `(Δy₁, Δy₂)`, let View 1's legal effect set be the slope-2 segment `E₁ = {(t, 2t) : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/2}` (marginals `Δy₁ ∈ [0, 1/2]`, `Δy₂ ∈ [0, 1]`). Consider two candidate View 2 sets:
- *Marginal fake:* `E₂^fake = {(2s, s) : 0 ≤ s ≤ 1/2}` (marginals `Δy₁ ∈ [0, 1]`, `Δy₂ ∈ [0, 1/2]`). Per-output marginal matching intersects `[0, 1/2] × [0, 1]` with `[0, 1] × [0, 1/2]` to obtain `[0, 1/2]²` and falsely commits `(1/2, 1/2)` — a vector supported by neither view. The true joint intersection solves `t = 2s` and `2t = s`, giving `E₁ ∩ E₂^fake = {(0, 0)}` (abstain).
- *Genuine joint pair:* `E₂^real = {(t, 2t) : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/2}`. Joint intersection `E₁ ∩ E₂^real = {(t, 2t) : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/2}` supports committing `(1/2, 1)`.

**W12b — Required old-task batch opens receiver ReLU and transports joint corroboration.** Let writable parameters be `(p, q, b_r)` plus a two-output B head `(v₁, v₂)` initially `(0, 0)` reading a receiver ReLU `h_r(x) = ReLU(x₁ + b_r)`. On the canonical B query `x_B = (0, 1)`, `h_r(x_B) = ReLU(b_r)`. On the due old-task A₁ query `x_A1 = (1, 1)`, `h_r(x_A1) = ReLU(1 + b_r)` and `y_A1 = -(p + q) + h_r(x_A1)`, with target `7/2`, loss `½(y_A1 - 7/2)²`, and fixed SGD step `η = 1/3`. Compare three initial states, all starting with `b_r = -1/2` (so `h_r(x_B) = 0`, receiver **OFF** on B, zero realizable B effect in the pre-update cell, while `1 + b_r = 1/2 > 0`, **ON** on A₁):
- *No-write arm:* `(p, q, b_r) = (0, 0, -1/2)` gives `y_A1 = 1/2`, loss `9/2`. One due A₁ step lands at `(-1, -1, 1/2)`: `y_A1 = 7/2`, loss `0`, `b_r = 1/2 > 0` (receiver ON), contrast `p - q = 0`.
- *Live(+1, encodes `E₁` slope 2):* `(1, 0, -1/2)` gives `y_A1 = -1/2`, loss `8`, contrast `p - q = +1`. The identical due A₁ step lands at `(-1/3, -4/3, 5/6)`: `y_A1 = 7/2`, loss `0` (A₁ repaid), `b_r = 5/6 > 0` (receiver **ON** on B), and `p - q = +1` preserved.
- *Live(-1, encodes `E₁'` slope 1/2):* `(0, 1, -1/2)` gives `y_A1 = -1/2`, loss `8`, contrast `p - q = -1`. The identical due A₁ step lands at `(-4/3, -1/3, 5/6)`: `y_A1 = 7/2`, loss `0`, `b_r = 5/6 > 0`, and `p - q = -1` preserved.

Along the entire gate crossing `b_r ∈ [-1/2, 5/6]`, let A₂ read `3 - max(b_r - 1, 0)` (constant `3`, untouched) and C read an orthogonal coordinate (constant `3`). When View 2 arrives endorsing `E₂ = {(t, 2t) : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/2}`: Live(+1) decodes `E₁ ∩ E₂ ≠ {0}` and commits `(Δv₁, Δv₂) = (3/5, 6/5)` on `h_r(x_B) = 5/6`, realizing `(Δy₁, Δy₂) = (1/2, 1)` strictly inside the opened cell (zero kink crossing, `b_r` and `y_A1` unchanged); Live(-1) decodes `E₁' ∩ E₂ = {(0, 0)}` and abstains, rejecting the marginal fake `(1/2, 1/2)`. *Opener-that-erases control:* a 3-item identity-feature due step with targets `(-1, -1, 1/2)` at `η = 1` reduces A₁ loss to `0` and opens `b_r = 1/2 > 0` in all three arms, but collapses all three to `(-1, -1, 1/2)` with `p - q = 0`, erasing the joint-corroboration distinction. *Scope caveat:* W12 stipulates the chart, 1-bit slope decoder, and orthogonal readout; it checks arithmetic feasibility only, not whether a blind multi-output carrier beats an equal-byte buffered joint solver on learned features.

## Idea cards

### 01. Intersect coupled effect sets, reject marginal fakes — seed / primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Carry cross-output dependencies in each view's effect set `E_v ⊂ ℝ^m` and commit only on a non-zero point in the joint intersection `E₁ ∩ E₂`, abstaining whenever only per-output projections overlap.
**Novelty / control.** Offline set-membership fitting already intersects correlated sets (conceded by #252); `#241#02` matches per-output marginals and `#233#05`/AND-mask intersect raw weight directions. The primitive bet is a live-state carrier that preserves joint-effect consent across mandatory old work at equal or lower byte cost than a buffered joint set-membership solver.
**Cheapest first test.** Run W12a with blind partial observations of correlated 2-output views, pairing genuine joint-consent instances with equal-marginal junk pairs against marginal matching, AND-mask, and equal-byte buffered joint fitting.
**Old-task retention.** Audit itemwise A/C gaps on accepted commits, refused marginal fakes, and after 1, 8, and 64 subsequent unrelated updates.

### 02. Carry a cross-output coupling relation in live weights, not a point — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Encode View 1's provisional write so the live state represents a linear coupling constraint `A Δy = 0` (e.g., output ratio or sign-lock) rather than a single fixed target point, letting View 2's arrival pick any jointly admissible magnitude along that coupled subspace.
**Novelty / control.** Storing a single provisional delta collapses a correlated set to a point (over-rejecting scale-compatible views) or requires an external constraint buffer. Compare against point-only provisional carriers and an equal-byte buffered constraint matrix.
**Cheapest first test.** Two-output linear head where View 1 supports a 1-D ray and View 2 supports a different magnitude on the same ray vs. a crossing marginal fake; test blind scale variations.
**Old-task retention.** Measure A/C gaps across the full admitted scale range and confirm zero A/C drift on refused crossing rays.

### 03. Require joint sign-and-ratio lock across paired B queries — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Define the joint effect chart over a coupled pair of canonical B queries `(q₁, q₂)` (e.g., a rule and its converse/invariant) so two views agree only if they endorse the same simultaneous change across both queries, rejecting views that match on each query's marginal direction via opposite trade-offs.
**Novelty / control.** Multi-query evaluation is standard, and function-space regularization (FROMP) constrains per-point outputs independently. The candidate is cross-query relational corroboration gating a single live write without re-evaluating retired view examples.
**Cheapest first test.** Paired query-converse synthetic router where a junk view fixes `q₁` by breaking `q₂` and a second view does the reverse; compare per-query marginal matching and equal-byte buffered joint fitting.
**Old-task retention.** Score held-out A/C pairs and verify that refusing inconsistent query pairs never leaves partial single-query debt on A₁.

### 04. Reject convex-hull fakes via non-convex joint effect menus — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When each view supports discrete mutually exclusive multi-output fixes (e.g., `{(1,0), (0,1)}` vs. `{(1/2, 1/2)}`), intersect the non-convex sets directly rather than their convex hulls or bounding boxes, preventing convex averaging from committing an unsupported midpoint.
**Novelty / control.** Convex polytope intersection and model averaging assume convexity of consent; non-convex constraint satisfaction exists offline. The candidate is a live multi-mode carrier (e.g., signed contrast bins) that preserves disjunctive consent without an offline list of vertices.
**Cheapest first test.** Compare non-convex intersection, convex-hull intersection, marginal boxes, and buffered discrete enumeration on `{(0,0), (1,0), (0,1)}` vs. `{(0,0), (1/2, 1/2)}` and a genuine shared-vertex pair.
**Old-task retention.** Verify A/C gaps on the forbidden midpoint (which would otherwise commit) and on accepted discrete vertices.

### 05. Certify joint consent only on the cross-output interaction residual — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Decompose each view's multi-output effect into per-output main effects plus a cross-output interaction residual, and require corroboration specifically on the interaction residual before committing coupled changes.
**Novelty / control.** Marginal matching (`#241#02`) and independent per-head OGD capture main effects; ANOVA/factorial decomposition is standard statistics. The CL candidate is gating coupled multi-head writes on live-carried interaction agreement so uncoupled marginal noise cannot trigger a joint write.
**Cheapest first test.** 2×2 factorial B outputs with matched main effects and opposite interaction signs across views, plus a genuine shared-interaction arm, compared against marginal matching and buffered joint fitting.
**Old-task retention.** Check A/C interaction gaps (pairs of old classes whose relative boundary depends on coupled logits) before and after commit.

### 06. Gate commits by the marginal-to-joint volume ratio — improvement
**Mechanism.** Compute or bound the ratio between the marginal-box intersection volume and the true joint intersection volume; abstain when the gap is large unless a single charged discriminating query narrows the joint set.
**Novelty / control.** Polytope volume approximation and active query selection are established; this is a diagnostic guard around card 01, not a new learning primitive. Compare against unguarded joint fitting and a fixed-threshold abstain rule.
**Cheapest first test.** Sweep the angle between two 2-D effect cones from aligned to nearly orthogonal (high marginal-to-joint ratio) and measure false-confirmation rate vs. query cost.
**Old-task retention.** Track A/C floors across the angle sweep, especially in the narrow-angle false-positive regime.

### 07. Enforce conservation-law coupling across competing output heads — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Embed domain-invariant output conservation laws (e.g., probability-mass conservation `∑ Δy_i = 0` or mutually exclusive action budgets) inside each view's joint effect set so per-output positive matches that violate the coupled conservation law have empty joint intersection.
**Novelty / control.** Softmax shift invariance (`#252#03`) quotients out uniform shifts; constrained optimization enforces linear equalities. The candidate is coupling two views' consent through a conserved multi-output invariant carried in the live write so violations are rejected before actuator fitting.
**Cheapest first test.** Three-action head where two views each legally raise a different subset while conserving total mass, creating a positive marginal overlap that violates conservation; compare unconstrained marginal matching and buffered constrained fitting.
**Old-task retention.** Check sum-conserving A/C logit margins and verify no silent drift in non-target old classes.

### 08. Intersect joint effects in a rotated cross-output contrast basis — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Represent each view's joint effect set in a frozen basis of cross-output contrasts (e.g., `(Δy₁ - Δy₂, Δy₁ + Δy₂)`) so a marginal fake in raw coordinates is exposed as a direct sign opposition in the difference coordinate, even after intervening old updates shift the common mode.
**Novelty / control.** Change of basis is linear algebra; the CL bet is choosing a contrast basis whose common-mode coordinate absorbs mandatory old-task drift while the difference coordinate stays invariant and carries joint consent. Compare against raw-coordinate marginal matching and buffered joint fitting.
**Cheapest first test.** Apply a common-mode old-task shift to W12a's two outputs and test whether contrast-basis intersection separates genuine slope-2 pairs from slope-1/2 fakes without re-centering from a buffer.
**Old-task retention.** Audit both common-mode and difference-mode old-task gaps across A₁, A₂, and C.

### 09. Subtract shared-prior co-movement before testing joint agreement — improvement
**Mechanism.** Baseline each view's multi-output effect set against the base model's uncorroborated shared-prior co-movement (measured via length-matched filler updates) so two views that merely trigger the same default multi-output Jacobian direction do not pass as independent joint agreement.
**Novelty / control.** Ledger lessons (PR #27, #29, #33) show shared priors and filler produce structured multi-output shifts; baseline subtraction is a control/guard around card 01, not a new primitive. Compare against raw joint intersection and a temperature-matched no-write arm.
**Cheapest first test.** Inject a strong shared-prior correlation into two B outputs so two unrelated filler views produce aligned `(t, 2t)` shifts; verify the baselined gate rejects them while keeping true corroborated pairs.
**Old-task retention.** Confirm baseline subtraction does not mask real A/C drift on old tasks sharing the same prior correlation.

### 10. Refuse rank-deficient realizations that collapse distinct joint effects — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Even when `E₁ ∩ E₂` is non-empty, refuse to commit through an active cell whose writable Jacobian is rank-deficient on the declared B outputs if that rank collapse makes a marginal fake indistinguishable from the consented joint vector at the readout.
**Novelty / control.** Jacobian rank checks are standard linear algebra (`#252` concedes solver checks); the candidate is coupling corroboration admissibility to actuator rank so a rank-1 cell cannot launder a 2-D joint check into a 1-D marginal write. Compare against rank-blind exact fitting in the current cell.
**Cheapest first test.** Compare a rank-1 active head (which projects both `(1/2, 1)` and `(1/2, 1/2)` onto the same line) against a rank-2 active head on W12a's genuine vs. fake pairs.
**Old-task retention.** Score A/C on refused rank-1 jobs (verifying clean repayment) vs. committed rank-2 jobs.

### 11. Let a required old-task batch switch the receiver's ReLU on while carrying corroboration — seed / primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Execute an independently required old-task update (identical in the no-write arm) that moves a B-dormant receiver ReLU from `pre-act < 0` to `pre-act > 0` while preserving the live corroboration carrier, enabling a zero-kink commit inside the newly opened cell with no rescue step.
**Novelty / control.** Replay revives dormant neurons (`2503.20018`), ReDo (`2302.12902`) resets them, and `#241#16` hands off within a single fixed cell. The primitive bet is one required old update simultaneously repaying A₁, waking the receiver ReLU safely, and transporting actionable corroboration, beating a fixed-cell handoff, an equal-compute rescue/reset, and an opener-that-erases control.
**Cheapest first test.** Run W12b's 2×2 factorial (receiver opens vs. stays closed × carrier survives vs. erased) against fixed-cell handoff, equal-compute rescue, and the equal-byte buffered post-update solver.
**Old-task retention.** Verify A₁ repayment to no-write parity, A₂/C constancy across the entire kink-crossing path, and retention after 1, 8, and 64 unrelated steps.

### 12. Wake a B-dormant unit via its A₁-active half-space without touching B — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Target a receiver ReLU that is already active on the due A₁ input (so A₁ naturally backpropagates a nonzero gradient into its weights/bias) while inactive on B; the required A₁ step shifts the unit's boundary across B's query purely as a side effect of ordinary old-task learning.
**Novelty / control.** Cross-task feature sharing in ReLU networks is known; the candidate is using A₁'s natural active half-space as a zero-extra-cost gate opener for a pending B loan without modifying the A₁ objective or touching B examples during the handoff. Compare against a rescue step on B and a ReDo unit reset.
**Cheapest first test.** Two-input 1-hidden-unit ReLU (as in W12b) sweeping the angle between `x_A1` and `x_B` from aligned to orthogonal (where A₁ cannot wake B's gate) and checking blind confirmation.
**Old-task retention.** Measure held-out A₁ generalization (not just due-batch loss) and A₂/C items near the shifted hyperplane.

### 13. Wake a second receiver ReLU to raise active B rank from 1 to 2 — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Start in a pre-update cell where only one unit is active on B (rank 1, insufficient to realize a 2-output coupled effect without breaking A/C), and rely on the required old-task update to switch on a second, linearly independent receiver ReLU so the post-update cell has rank 2.
**Novelty / control.** Plasticity restoration (`2503.20018`, `2607.24996`) tracks aggregate dormant fraction; this targets *functional rank on a specific pending B chart* unlocked by a scheduled old update while carrying corroboration. Compare against fixed-cell rank-1 fitting and an equal-compute unit reset.
**Cheapest first test.** Two-unit ReLU head on 2-output B with one old A₁ constraint: unit 1 active (rank 1, blocked by A₁), unit 2 initially OFF on B and woken by the due A₁ step; test against a rank-1 fixed-cell control and an opener-that-erases control.
**Old-task retention.** Check A₁, A₂, and C before opening, at the kink crossing, and after the rank-2 commit.

### 14. Encode corroboration in the post-opening ReLU margin difference — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Let the live provisional write perturb the due A₁ residual just enough that the identical required A₁ update repays A₁ to zero in both arms while landing the receiver ReLU at a *strictly larger* positive margin in the live arm (`5/6` vs. `1/2` in W12b), using the post-step activation magnitude itself as a readable carrier.
**Novelty / control.** Activation magnitudes naturally vary with pre-step weights; the candidate is treating the post-opening margin surplus (at matched zero A₁ loss) as a debt-free, zero-extra-parameter corroboration signal. Compare against an equal-byte external buffer and a margin-equalized opener control.
**Cheapest first test.** Test whether a downstream receiver with read-only access to `h_r(x_B)` (and no direct weight read of `p - q`) can distinguish live vs. no-write and two live classes from the opened margin alone, under noisy old-task targets.
**Old-task retention.** Verify that the larger post-opening margin does not push any near-boundary A₂/C item across a secondary kink or violate A₂'s floor.

### 15. Certify pathwise old-task invariance across the receiver's kink — improvement
**Mechanism.** Require a formal or exact-piecewise check that along the entire straight-line update segment crossing `pre-act = 0` during the due old batch, every exposed A₁/A₂/C output stays above its retention floor and no unintended nuisance ReLU flips sign.
**Novelty / control.** Piecewise-linear ReLU verification (Reluplex) and path safety checks (`#252#05`) are known; this is a soundness guard for card 11, not a new primitive. Compare against endpoint-only verification on a trap where a midpoint kink harms A₂.
**Cheapest first test.** Construct a 2-unit toy where endpoints `b_r = -1/2` and `b_r = 5/6` both pass A₂, while an intermediate nuisance kink at `b_r = 0` dips A₂ below floor; verify the pathwise check refuses it.
**Old-task retention.** Report worst-case pathwise A₁/A₂/C minimum across the update segment, not just endpoint accuracy.

### 16. Wake the receiver via inherited momentum from a required old-task sequence — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When a single required old-task batch leaves the receiver ReLU still slightly negative (`pre-act < 0`), let the ordinary momentum accumulated across a short sequence of required old-task updates push the receiver across the kink while the corroboration carrier survives in an orthogonal weight or slot component.
**Novelty / control.** Momentum carry across batches is standard (`#242`, `#250` explore momentum landing/steering); the candidate is using *mandatory* old-task momentum to open a dormant B actuator without any extra B step or slot injection, charging the optimizer slot against the byte budget. Compare against SGD without momentum (fixed cell), slot reset, and a buffered post-sequence solver.
**Cheapest first test.** Two-step due A₁ sequence under heavy-ball momentum (`β = 1/2`) where step 1 reaches `b_r = -1/8 < 0` and step 2's inherited momentum crosses to `b_r = +3/8 > 0` while keeping `p - q` separable; include a slot-reset control.
**Old-task retention.** Audit A₁/A₂/C at every intermediate tick of the momentum sequence and after a forced default if confirmation never arrives.

### 17. Separate gate opening from corroboration transport via an opener-eraser control — improvement
**Mechanism.** Pair every tested due old batch with an equal-old-gain, equal-compute opener-eraser that switches the exact same receiver ReLU(s) ON while collapsing the carrier to the no-write state; count a win for card 11 only on the delta over both the fixed-cell control and the opener-eraser control.
**Novelty / control.** Counterfactual ablation is a methodological control (extending `#252#21` to ReLU-opening handoffs), not a new update primitive. Without it, ordinary plasticity restoration via replay (`2503.20018`) masquerades as corroboration transport.
**Cheapest first test.** Run the 4-cell factorial of W12b on a small nonlinear MLP with 3 seeds, logging opener-only, carrier-only, both, and buffered-post-update arms.
**Old-task retention.** Require identical A₁ gain and matched A₂/C retention across opener-preserver and opener-eraser arms before attributing B differences.

### 18. Let one required old step close a nuisance ReLU while opening the receiver ReLU — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use a single required old-task update to simultaneously switch OFF an active unit that previously coupled B to A₂/C (removing an old-task conflict) and switch ON a clean receiver ReLU for B, converting a blocked cell into an admissible cell while transporting corroboration.
**Novelty / control.** Simultaneous activation shifts happen in ordinary SGD; the candidate is requiring one mandatory old step to perform a paired *conflict-closing + actuator-opening* cell transition that preserves live corroboration. Compare against OGD projection in the original cell, ReDo reset, and a buffered post-step solver.
**Cheapest first test.** Two-unit ReLU toy where unit 1 is active on both B and A₂ (blocking any commit) and unit 2 is OFF on B; one due A₁ step flips unit 1 OFF on B and unit 2 ON on B while preserving `p - q`.
**Old-task retention.** Verify A₂ stays exact when unit 1 closes (no step discontinuity at the kink) and C stays intact through commit and default.

### 19. Open the specific receiver unit whose readout spans the joint intersection — primitive candidate (merge)
**Mechanism.** Require the due old-task update not merely to wake *some* B-active unit, but to wake a receiver unit whose readout column lies in the correlated joint intersection `E₁ ∩ E₂` (and outside marginal-fake directions), so opening that cell directly unlocks the jointly consented vector.
**Novelty / control.** Combines `#252#02` and `#252#17`. Must beat the stacked control of offline joint set-membership fitting plus an arbitrary ReLU-opening replay step at equal compute and bytes.
**Cheapest first test.** Two initially OFF receiver units with fixed readout directions `(1, 2)` (aligned with `E₁ ∩ E₂`) and `(1, 1)` (marginal-fake direction); compare a due old batch that wakes unit 1 vs. one that wakes unit 2, plus the buffered joint solver.
**Old-task retention.** Score A₁/A₂/C under both unit openings and confirm abstention (zero A/C loss) when only the marginal-fake unit opens.

### 20. Use the newly opened ReLU cell as a physical filter against marginal fakes — primitive candidate (merge)
**Mechanism.** Design or select the receiver cell woken by the required old batch so that its non-negative/bounded legal write cone intersects `E₁ ∩ E₂` cleanly while every marginal-fake vector either exits the cell (forcing a forbidden kink crossing) or violates an A/C gap, turning cell geometry itself into a joint-consent verifier.
**Novelty / control.** Polyhedral constraints and control barrier functions bound feasible sets; the merged CL candidate is aligning the post-old-batch active cell's geometry with cross-output corroboration so marginal fakes become physically uncommitable without an explicit offline set-intersection solver. Compare against an unconstrained cell plus an offline joint-intersection check.
**Cheapest first test.** Two-output head where the opened cell's legal A/C-safe cone is `{(t, 2t) : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/2}` (containing `E₁ ∩ E₂^real` and excluding `(1/2, 1/2)`); test whether blind marginal fakes are automatically blocked at zero extra solver cost.
**Old-task retention.** Check whether physical cell filtering preserves A/C more tightly than post-hoc projection when View 2 is adversarially noisy.

### 21. Transport a multi-bit joint-coupling signature across a ReLU cell transition — primitive candidate (merge)

**Cheapest first test.** Extend W12b to four blind View 1 classes (two signs × two slopes) carried in a 3-parameter weight block `(p₁, p₂, p₃)` orthogonal to the due A₁ gradient, testing exact decoding after `b_r` crosses from `< 0` to `> 0`.
**Old-task retention.** Measure A₁ repayment, A₂/C retention, and false-confirmation rate across all 4×4 View 1 × View 2 pairs (12 marginal-fake/conflicting pairs, 4 genuine pairs).

### 22. Keep the newly opened receiver ReLU open through the next required old batch — primitive candidate (merge)
**Mechanism.** Among zero-kink realizations of the jointly consented effect `Δy ∈ E₁ ∩ E₂` inside the newly opened cell, choose one that leaves the receiver ReLU's pre-activation strictly positive (and preserves the cross-output coupling) through the *next* scheduled old-task update, preventing the next due batch from re-closing the cell and silently zeroing the committed joint effect.
**Novelty / control.** Extends `#252#22` (sealing against the next old batch) to ReLU-opening handoffs and joint effects; multi-step constrained fitting already optimizes look-ahead stability, and forecast-weighted write selection died on the ledger (PR #9, #21). **Cheapest first test.** Two-step due old schedule where due batch 1 opens `b_r` to `+5/6` and due batch 2 pulls `b_r` by `-1/2` (staying ON) vs. `-1` (re-closing the unit and erasing the committed `(1/2, 1)` effect); compare margin-preserving commit vs. myopic commit and buffered fitting.
**Old-task retention.** Audit A₁/A₂/C and held-out B joint-effect retention after due batch 2 and at returns 1, 8, and 64.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can a live-state carrier preserve multi-output joint-effect coupling across a required old-task ReLU opening and beat an equal-byte buffered joint solver?** Run W12b's 2×2 factorial (receiver opens vs. stays closed × carrier survives vs. erased) extended to multi-bit coupling (card 21) with equal-marginal junk pairs (card 01). Compare directly against the strongest stack: identical provisional write, identical due old batch, equal-byte waiting buffer, and exact joint set-membership fit in the post-update cell. If the buffered solver ties or wins at equal bytes, the live-state carrier is a byte-budget tradeoff, not a new primitive.
2. **Does required old-task SGD naturally wake B-useful receiver ReLUs without erasing corroboration, or only in hand-crafted geometries?** Test cards 12, 13, 16, and 18 on small nonlinear MLPs with real (non-stipulated) due old-task minibatches, measuring how often a required old step (a) switches a B-dormant unit ON, (b) raises active B rank enough for a coupled 2-output effect, and (c) preserves carrier separability above the opener-eraser control (card 17) while keeping pathwise A₁/A₂/C retention intact (card 15). If natural old steps either fail to open B-useful gates or act like erasers, the ReLU-opening handoff does not generalize beyond toy fixtures.
3. **How much real false-confirmation damage comes from marginal-only fakes vs. shared-prior co-movement?** Test cards 01, 03, 04, 05, and 09 on blind multi-output continual tasks where views are generated by realistic partial observations rather than adversarial W12a segments. Separate genuine joint-intersection wins from shared-prior co-movement (card 09) and rank-collapse artifacts (card 10). If marginal matching plus shared-prior subtraction already matches joint intersection on realistic views, the extra machinery of joint effect sets is unnecessary.
4. **Once a due old batch opens a receiver cell and a joint effect commits there, does the opened cell survive subsequent mandatory old work?** Test card 22 alongside cards 11 and 19 over multi-step due old-task schedules (returns 1, 8, and 64), tracking whether later required old batches re-close the newly opened receiver ReLU or rotate its readout out of `E₁ ∩ E₂`. If the committed joint effect vanishes at the next due old step unless frozen or replayed, opening a ReLU cell is only a transient staging area rather than a durable commit mechanism.
