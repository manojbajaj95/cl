# Renewable witness carriers

**Status:** Ideas only; no experiments, code, or GPU work. **Parent:** [`ideas/flood/cross-pool-42.md`](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/5128683ef967febdd90831453f906a675e568f6c/ideas/flood/cross-pool-42.md#L5-L13) ([PR #36](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/36)). **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 274](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/c80397b27734310db586a200d8644b520a9a4ca1/reviews/flood-triage.md#L274) gave **CRR1** and **CRR5** score 3, not a merge win. CRR1 transfers dormant capacity into a carrier after an admitted edit spends a shared repair, so the **same frozen witness** keeps serving its checked class. CRR5 breaks ties among equally useful renewals in favor of ones that enable a lawful later probe to separate competing repairs. **Read first:** [Idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/c80397b27734310db586a200d8644b520a9a4ca1/reviews/idea-ledger.md): beat the best parts stacked, do not count wins true by construction, and retain normal old tasks in every first test.

## Is a repair that keeps its own carrier alive a new continual-learning object?

**Not merely because it recurs.** [GRACE][grace] already keeps reusable edit adaptors; [continual backprop][cbp], [ReDo][redo], and [Sustainable Backup Propagation][sbp] renew capacity, including via reset plus knowledge recovery; [Net2Net][net] transports functions; [dual control][dual] chooses actions for both their direct effects and the information they make available. An externally scheduled recycle followed by cached repair and active diagnosis is an improvement, not a new primitive.

The candidate is a **self-renewing repair contract**: a frozen training witness, a physically realized carrier that makes it executable, and independently checked promises about which blind edits it still serves and which repair distinctions a later lawful probe can reveal. In the strong version, executing the repair helps produce the next carrier's entry state or donor contribution; an outside scheduler continually supplying a replacement is only the weak version. The novelty claim is causal: detach that contribution while holding payload utility and move resources fixed, and repeat service or actionable diagnosis gets more expensive. A finite game can represent the entire contract already; the CL claim is that the learner can *learn and reuse* a compact version on held-out streams without paying to solve the game or fit a witness anew each time. No evidence of that win yet.

Three nearby nodes set the bar. [Correction classes](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/169) establishes and refines multi-demand coverage, not what physically keeps the same artifact usable after a spend. [Self-replenishing capacity](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/170) restores a verified future edit menu, not necessarily one tied to any immutable repair. [Indistinguishable edits](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/173) trains into common reply regions and buys observations; here a *previous repair and its paid renewal* may change what can later be observed without changing today's reply. If combining those three independently matches the joint mechanism, keep the components and drop the new-object claim.

## What a carrier must preserve

- **Freeze the witness and the job.** A witness `w` is a particular training artifact and execution recipe (including permissible data, optimizer settings and seed protocol), not a repair identifier that can call a fitter. A carrier is the mutable host state that meets the recipe's entry condition: slots, routing, gates and any optimizer state it reads. A fixed, predeclared binding may move `w` to different slots; no demand-dependent translation, fresh teacher answer, payload rewrite or utility relaxation. Freeze blind jobs, evaluation and addressing before renewal; publish only allowed coarse types. Physically unused capacity is not necessarily unpromised reserve.
- **Keep signatures, covers and unknowns separate.** For a versioned host, job and legal suffix, record the checked witnesses that preserve prior commitments and satisfy that job's unchanged utility test. Equal complete signatures define a class in that scope. A common `w` across checked cells is a cover, not a claim that all signatures match; `{a}`, `{a,b}`, `{b}` is not one shared repair. Unknown, illegal and checked failure are different. Renewal restores a named cover only where it checks; a separator about some other witness need not invalidate the incumbent.
- **Show spend, transfer, then service.** First show that a legitimate admission uses up the carrier: an independent remaining job that formerly checked with `w` now does not (or would exceed its declared cost). Then transfer dormant reserve after that admission, proving unchanged installed outputs on the declared domains across the transfer itself. Finally run the now-executable `w` on an independently frozen job and check new utility and old-task retention; a larger empty gate is not success. Separate sunk cost from renewable stock, charge displaced donor options, and check repayment by its promised deadline before calling the cycle repeatable. Do not extrapolate a cycle with a fixed obligation set to perpetual service under accumulating commitments.
- **Measure decisions, not probe entropy.** A probe is lawful only if its input, observations, side effects and execution mode fit the contract. Default to shadow probes; pay for the fork and any future access to its transcript. A probe is actionable when possible observations change which *verified, affordable* repair can be used on a later frozen job, relative to the best no-probe reply including abstention. A mechanics oracle or an unconstrained query is a ceiling, not a baseline arm with free access. Renewals with the same current utility and retention may offer different probes; a difference in observation alone does not establish learning value.

### Shared contract for the CPU first tests

Use exact enumeration for small finite games and held-out streams for small trained hosts. Score blind, useful admissions that beat no-write, proof and fit work per admission, affordable decision changes after probes, and donor stock over repeated spend/renew cycles. Start all arms from the same host and freeze utility floors, demand distributions and given information. Charge peak and persistent state (including evidence, copies, logs and bindings), samples, shadow checks, wall time, lost alternative moves, and final outstanding donor debt. Compare at matched budgets or show a frontier if they cannot all be matched. In particular, stack threshold re-buffer or ReDo, cached class reuse with matched per-demand fitting available, and cost-sensitive active diagnosis on the same legal actions. On tiny instances also solve the joint problem exactly. A full-state solver with the same model and actions should not lose to a new name for its state variables; use it to locate whether any advantage is in learning the carrier, transferring the checks, or spending less to act.

**Retention notation:** `A` is the unchanged ordinary old-task suite plus a held-out rare slice; `C` is earlier acquired edits that were not intentionally superseded, including the most recent spend. Each card checks A/C at every served prefix against acquisition-anchored floors, plus exact installed-output equality across every re-buffer on its declared domains. The extra retention check named per card is designed to catch its particular shortcut. These are proposed checks, not results.

## Idea cards

### 01. Let the spend seed the next carrier — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Search for truthful witness batches whose normal repair update both admits the current job and builds a feature that can be bound into the next carrier by a paid dormant-slot transfer. Do not alter the frozen witness between rounds. **Novelty / control.** [Continual backprop][cbp] replenishes generic plasticity and [GRACE][grace] caches edits; the proposed operation ties next-carrier construction causally to applying this particular repair. Compare their renewal-and-reuse stack, threshold alone, and per-job fitting. **Cheapest first test.** A two-layer, fixed-width ReLU regressor with two independent editors and at least three spends: replace the seed-producing update with the cheapest utility-matched repair that does not produce it, leaving the donor menu fixed. Count the extra moves needed to keep `w` executable on blind jobs. **Old-task retention.** Track A/C through all spends and transfers, especially the just-admitted job that generated the seed.

### 02. Renew a probe direction, not just a repair direction — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Offer two equal-cost dormant-slot bindings that both run `w` today. Prefer the one that supports a later, budgeted shadow input with different response laws for two still-plausible repair hypotheses. Only use that signal to choose among repair actions whose preconditions can be checked. **Novelty / control.** [Dual control][dual] already values information; the candidate mechanism is a learned, output-silent change to the *available probe set* during repair-specific renewal. Compare reserve-size selection plus exact query selection, probe-only, each parent method alone, and joint planning on the same move menu. **Cheapest first test.** Two latent versions of a piecewise-linear host share every initial reply; renew one of two equal-size gated directions before drawing the next job. Include a legal high-entropy probe that never changes the best repair, and a twin pair with no legal separator. **Old-task retention.** Run A/C under both bindings and both possible post-probe repairs; check that forbidding live probe effects does not remove the effect.

### 03. Teach the return route before anyone spends it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select one lawful batch up front that lets different admitted editors take *different* routes back to the same witness entry condition, with a donor left at each return. **Novelty / control.** [HJ reachability][hj] and [return corridors](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/168) already cover convergent routes. The claim is that a single learned batch makes multiple returns cheaper for this same witness than independent corridor training plus a cache. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate three truthful batch choices and two held-out edit orders in a small linear learner; ablate the common batch and the carrier-specific target separately. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C at each route's intermediate deployments, not just at their common endpoint.

### 04. Move the cover by transporting just what the recipe reads — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Describe the state reads that affect `w` along checked suffixes, then transport only those reads into the dormant recipient. A counterexample widens the required state instead of prompting a private fit. **Novelty / control.** [Net2Net][net] concerns current function and [proof-carrying code][pcc] checks stated safety; the candidate artifact transports a training continuation, including hidden reads. Compare exact full-state transplant and output-only transport. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaustive short SGD-with-momentum suffixes on a four-parameter regressor; include weight twins with different moments and measure proof bytes and acquired coverage under projected versus full transfer. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C after cutover and one delayed batch, where a missing moment first changes the readout.

### 05. Lend a carrier against its last usable donor — improvement
**Mechanism.** Refuse a donor loan if it removes the only affordable route for another frozen witness before that witness's deadline, even if all current outputs are unaffected. **Novelty / control.** [Reusable-resource matching][reusable] models reuse and temporary unavailability; here eligibility is recomputed from repaired learner state, not a fixed cooldown. This is better accounting unless the learned compatibility model saves meaningful checks. **Cheapest first test.** Three donor slots, two artifacts and alternating versus bursty jobs; compare exact scheduling using measured edges against oldest-first and fixed cooldown. **Old-task retention.** Keep tasks formerly served by both artifacts in A/C, including a rare recurrence during repayment.

### 06. Sell only two promises that can be checked on one path — improvement
**Mechanism.** Store a common host base plus alternative renewal deltas, but accept simultaneous witness reservations only with a schedulable common continuation. **Novelty / control.** [Partial-observation games][games] already reason about sets of possible successors, and CRR3 already warns against counting incompatible branches. The improvement is a cheaper learned nomination of viable joint paths, not a new notion of feasibility. **Cheapest first test.** Use `{a}`, `{a,b}`, `{b}` covers and one shared donor. Contrast simultaneous reservations with the same jobs on disjoint donors; compare pairwise bundling and exact scheduling with an identical verifier. **Old-task retention.** Serve both promised jobs and retest the first after the second's renewal; count lost A/C as a broken promise, not serialization success.

### 07. Make proof repair local when a witness changes carrier — improvement
**Mechanism.** Cache a proof of witness behavior behind a versioned entry interface; after transfer, re-prove the recipient's entry and just the obligations touched by the move. Invalidate cached dependencies when a log, slot or data source changes. **Novelty / control.** [Proof-carrying code][pcc] and [certificate entry ports](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/171) already supply the abstraction. The test is whether repeated reuse makes incremental verification cheaper than rechecking. **Cheapest first test.** Hand-enumerable finite checks over four slot bindings, with one hidden reader included in the declared dependency graph only after a counterexample; compare full rechecks and generic incremental verification. **Old-task retention.** Independently replay the normal and rare A/C demands behind both reused and invalidated proof fragments.

### 08. Ask which bits of the repair must live in the host — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Use paired histories with identical deployed outputs and identical frozen `w`, but different viable future bindings and disjoint correct repairs under the same budgets. Bound the side state required to tell them apart; test whether the learned carrier already supplies those bits rather than moving a copy into a log. **Novelty / control.** [Indistinguishable edits](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/173) and [partial-observation games][games] already give indistinguishability arguments; this version distinguishes information about *renewing the same artifact* from information about its outputs. **Cheapest first test.** In a finite host with four such histories and disjoint successful decisions, compare a two-bit table, compressed carrier, and pay-to-probe alternative. Do not assert a no-probe bit bound when lawful transcripts supply the missing bits. **Old-task retention.** Require identical A/C outputs across the paired histories and retained A/C after each correctly selected repair; exclude pairs distinguishable from ordinary old-task readouts.

### 09. Learn entry conditions that tolerate quantized transfers — improvement
**Mechanism.** Before fixing `w`, choose a recipe whose useful coverage survives several low-precision carrier bindings. Then keep that recipe fixed and verify the quantized move rather than silently widening tolerance. **Novelty / control.** [Matching Training Trajectories][mtt] can optimize batches for diverse training states; this changes the objective to surviving paid, repeated carrier transfers under fixed old-task checks. **Cheapest first test.** Quantized scalar momentum states on a two-feature regression stream, with interior and near-boundary entries; compare robust batch fitting, exact transport, and full-precision copies at matched state bytes. **Old-task retention.** Test A/C at both quantization extremes, including the most sensitive old output rather than aggregate accuracy alone.

### 10. Let the renewed carrier host a reversible probe — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Bind a probe to a dormant recipient, pulse it in a shadow fork and restore both parameters and optimizer or sampler state. Spend its observation only to choose a verified repair, never to pass off the probe as a useful edit. **Novelty / control.** [Safe exploration][safe] already limits reachable experiments; the hypothesis is that the carrier enables a cheaper isolated experiment *and* later repair than a separately rented fork. **Cheapest first test.** A finite two-feature dynamical learner where equal output restoration can hide momentum residue. Compare full snapshot, exact transactional rollback and the carrier-local pulse at equal peak bytes. **Old-task retention.** Check A/C before and after the pulse and after a delayed update, to catch invisible incomplete rollback.

### 11. Time probes by when the answer stops being useful — improvement
**Mechanism.** Separate the expiration time of a carrier's runnable witness from the later deadline at which a lawful probe result can still influence the next repair. Rent the probe direction just long enough to reach that decision. **Novelty / control.** [Dual control][dual] and [reusable-resource scheduling][reusable] cover timing and option value; this is an improvement if distinct clocks let the system stop paying for dead options. **Cheapest first test.** Four-step finite game with the same probes and known observation costs, but staggered repair and probe deadlines. Compare renew-now, latest-feasible and exact joint scheduling. **Old-task retention.** Recheck A/C at lease expiry and through eviction, including tasks whose acquisition overlapped the probe lease.

### 12. Protect the repair decision against a bad probe read — improvement
**Mechanism.** Allocate reserve to a redundant pair of lawful tests only when disagreement would force abstention or a safe fallback. Protect the decision boundary rather than reconstructing every latent version. **Novelty / control.** [Blackwell's comparison of experiments][blackwell] values observations by attainable decisions; the specific improvement is learning noise-diverse tests in output-preserving carriers rather than buying more of one measurement. **Cheapest first test.** Binary repairs, two noisy probe channels and a shared noise source; compare repeated reads and distinct channels at equal cost, including a case where errors are perfectly correlated. **Old-task retention.** Run A/C under false positives, false negatives and fallback, not just correct transcripts.

### 13. Grow two ports whose probes only work together — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Re-buffer jointly into two cancelling slots. Each is uninformative on its own; a pair of authorized reads resolves a *repair-relevant* interaction while preserving current outputs. **Novelty / control.** [Adaptive submodularity][submod] explains when greedy probe selection works; this card deliberately tests complementarity, combining [#170's jointly null moves](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/170) with decision-dependent joint sensing. **Cheapest first test.** Use a two-bit finite response law with parity determining the viable repair; compare greedy reserve plus query selection, exact pair search on the same budget, and separate donors. Then ask if a three-unit ReLU host can realize the separation without a hard-coded label head. **Old-task retention.** Audit A/C during the paired move as actually deployed, including mixed intermediate states if the update cannot be atomic.

### 14. Preserve diagnosis even after picking the wrong repair — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** When competing repairs have the same current utility but one overwrites the only diagnostic state needed later, prefer a renewal whose probe port survives either admissible repair. **Novelty / control.** [Safe exploration][safe] preserves a safe path out; the proposed extra obligation is a path to a decision-relevant observation after a checked but potentially mistaken repair choice. Compare exact contingency planning, not just immediate entropy. **Cheapest first test.** Two cost-matched repair actions on function-matched twins, one destroying an observation channel. Withhold a distinguishing follow-up job until after the repair; include a common safe repair as a control that should make the probe worthless. **Old-task retention.** Track A/C across both actions and the later correction, even on streams where no correction is needed.

### 15. Buy a probe for the refusal it will prevent, not the one it fits — improvement
**Mechanism.** Learn from past renewal/probe pairs which coarse ports later reduce the number of unusable repairs tried. Rank ports by lower downstream decision cost and abstain under poor calibration. **Novelty / control.** [Dual MPC][mpc] already prices the informational effect of current actions; the possible advantage is transfer of a cost predictor to new editors without observing their payloads. This is not a new information measure. **Cheapest first test.** Leave out entire editor types in a low-dimensional finite simulator; compare entropy, reserve size, frequency estimates and exact expected-value planning given the same observation model. No gain if the predictor just memorizes demand prevalence. **Old-task retention.** Require A/C on both frequent and rare types and audit whether low-frequency corrections are being traded away for mean gain.

### 16. Reuse one recipe across orthogonal admission modes — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Split a donor into two independently bound read directions that share the same frozen witness and cannot change each other's entry tests. Permit reuse on unseen combinations only after attempting to disprove the factorization. **Novelty / control.** [Orthogonal Gradient Descent][ogd] protects outputs to first order and [Correction classes](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/169) already factors signatures. The extra claim is factored *renewability*: after a cross-coordinate spend, one factor can refill the other without breaking either verified binding. **Cheapest first test.** A two-factor linear learner with a held-out coupled batch that breaks orthogonality. Compare stacked OGD, a factored signature table and ordinary refill against full joint checks. **Old-task retention.** Test old single-factor tasks and mixed A/C inputs after each individual and joint transfer.

### 17. Keep the cheap incumbent until an actionable split is proved — improvement
**Mechanism.** If a later suffix separates two class signatures but the incumbent `w` still covers both, keep the carrier shared. Allocate a second route only when the split changes useful admissions or costs. **Novelty / control.** [Counterexample-guided neural repair][cegis] and CRR2 already motivate refinement; the improvement is gating physical allocation by confirmed service impact rather than by table refinement. **Cheapest first test.** Alternate signature-only splits with splits that invalidate `w`; compare grow-on-split, no growth, and paid temporary branching at matched peak width. **Old-task retention.** Exercise A/C under both branches immediately before reclaiming either one, including clients admitted just before the split.

### 18. Renew with evidence from today's job, not a hidden teacher — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Make the current job expose a truthful intermediate training signal from which a carrier entry feature can be rebuilt after the original repair teacher is gone. Preserve the frozen recipe; no new labels may silently enter it. **Novelty / control.** [Gradient-matching coresets][gcr] compress training evidence and [Sustainable Backup Propagation][sbp] recovers knowledge after resets; the candidate is downstream work replenishing the state needed by an upstream fixed artifact. **Cheapest first test.** Remove the teacher after the first round of a tiny multitask regression stream. Randomize whether subsequent truthful batches reveal a shared factor; compare equal-byte replay, cached teacher answers and ordinary reset-plus-distill. **Old-task retention.** Include old tasks that do not share the factor and check the full A/C bank under long delays.

### 19. Learn when no output-silent separator can exist — improvement
**Mechanism.** Certify that all allowed output-preserving transfers leave a repair-relevant latent distinction unobservable at the permitted probe budget. Preserve an explicit unknown outcome when the search is incomplete. **Novelty / control.** [Partial-observation safety games][games] already give losing beliefs; the improvement is reusing a compact obstruction tied to the carrier's reachable measurement directions. **Cheapest first test.** An exact small linear host where every legal probe reads a projection orthogonal to the ambiguous direction, plus a near-control with one feasible separating direction. Compare per-demand exhaustive search and cached obstructions with a same-action solver. **Old-task retention.** On the obstruction arm, verify A/C remains intact under abstention; on the near-control, require both eventual old retention and successful repair so refusing everything cannot win.

### 20. Regenerate the substrate, not a snapshot of the old policy — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Reclaim an exhausted carrier only after an output-equivalent recipient can re-run the unchanged witness *with all newer obligations in force*. Trace which reusable intermediate from the spend permits transplant without reverting those obligations. **Novelty / control.** [Net2Net][net] transports present behavior and [Sustainable Backup Propagation][sbp] uses inner distillation; separate function restoration from restoration of this specific callable learning transition in a changed host. **Cheapest first test.** A tiny two-head learner: add an unrelated commitment while `w` is spent; compare snapshot restore with forward replay of that commitment, reset-plus-distill, and carrier regeneration at equal peak state. **Old-task retention.** Score the unrelated head and its rare slice before and after reclamation to catch old snapshots disguised as renewal.

### 21. Use matched sham transfers to locate the effect — improvement (audit)
**Mechanism.** Cross real versus sham donor movement with intact versus permuted carrier-to-witness bindings; preserve byte count, declared outputs and gate sizes. Examine whether exact witness service or only general plasticity returns. **Novelty / control.** CRR9 proposes the audit and [ReDo][redo] supplies the generic recycling control. A specific interaction supports CRR1; the audit alone is not a learning primitive. **Cheapest first test.** Four arms on a two-witness finite host with similarly useful editors and matched transfer cost; verify that the permutation leaves marginal reserve identical without accidentally augmenting either cover. **Old-task retention.** Equal A/C in all four arms, including rare slices, is required before attributing differences in useful admission to binding rather than old-task sacrifice.

### 22. Retire a carrier only if the replacement simulates future service and diagnosis — improvement
**Mechanism.** Prune one carrier state only when the other can match its useful jobs, lawful transcript-dependent decisions and successor service without higher cost or extra donor debt. An apparent richer menu can have worse successors. **Novelty / control.** [#170's simulation pruning](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/170) and [partial-observation games][games] already supply the structure; extending the dominance test to include learning-repair probes is an improvement unless a learned quotient saves real verification work. **Cheapest first test.** Compare two-step and exact pruning in a tiny graph where two states agree on first jobs but one loses its probe port after the first spend. Compare equal-byte retained state and useful blind service against one-step menu pruning. **Old-task retention.** Recheck A/C for the supposedly simulated plays after retirement, especially the prefix where the retired carrier would have served a rare old recurrence.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Does applying a frozen repair actually make its next execution cheaper?** Start with cards 01 and 21. Can the *same* artifact cross repeated spend–renew–spend cycles on independent editors without fresh teacher calls or rising donor debt? Detach the alleged renewal-producing intermediate while matching current utility, A/C and legal moves. If threshold recycling plus cache and matched fitting wins anyway, the strong object claim fails.
2. **Can renewal buy a useful observation that probing the unchanged host cannot buy at the same total cost?** Start with 02 and 13. Require twins with identical pre-renewal transcripts but different affordable later repairs. Mark signal without decision change as a null, and see whether separate renewal plus an exact query policy reproduces the result. Then ask whether the same witness can choose such a binding before seeing the next payload on a trained host.
3. **What is the smallest carrier state that survives an independent transfer?** Use 04, 08 and 09. Separate the bits in the immutable witness, the resident state and the future paid transcript. Look for both an indistinguishability lower bound and a cheaper witness-specific sufficient state than full-state cloning; exact copying alone answers neither the compression nor the learning question.
4. **Where does renewability end once old obligations accumulate?** Use 05, 19 and 22 to distinguish exhaustion of donors, loss of witness coverage and loss of lawful separation. Can a scoped obstruction predict an unseen burst or a changed edit order better than counting idle slots? Distinguish bounded recurring demand from genuine net-new commitments; growth, eviction, abstention and utility loss must stay on the bill.

These are nearest comparisons, not an exhaustive novelty clearance. The proposals stand or fall on useful blind admission or decision cost at matched retention and resources, not on naming a known planning state.
