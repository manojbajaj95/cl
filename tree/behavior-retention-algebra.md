# Behavior-retention algebra: what must an edit leave behind?

Parent: [Edit language](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/84), deeper question 2. **Ideation only:** all tests below are proposals; no pilots or experiments were run.

**Hypothesis:** retention can compose as a partial algebra on behavioral contracts if a compiled edit carries forward the distinctions, obligations, and uncertainty that later edits can expose. Composing deltas, passing per-edit tests, or proving that writes commute does not provide that interface.

## Root cause: passing a frame is not a congruence

The parent separates intent, realizability, and sequence validity. Assume intent is unambiguous and every individual edit is realizable: the third failure still stands. Its scope, lens, or equivalence frame says which differences matter **now**. A later edit can read a difference that the current frame erased, or enable histories that the frame did not cover. An edit certificate saying “the old observer agrees” cannot in general be substituted for the edited system when composing another edit. This is a missing intermediate behavioral interface, not a failure of weight placement or a negative result from a pilot.

Consider a dispatcher with an express promise: a fragile parcel arrives in ten minutes. Edit A opens a shortcut through queue X, acquiring a new economy route. Under FIFO, express parcels still take ten minutes, whether inserted at high or low priority (a setting that FIFO ignores). Edit B installs priority service at all queues, acquiring a rush feature at queue Y. With X still closed, the old express route takes ten minutes. Both edits pass the old express check alone. A low-priority express insertion into X now takes twelve minutes under A then B; a high-priority one still takes ten. A's two implementations look identical on both old and new tasks until B reads their dormant priority setting. Opening X and enabling priority can be commuting assignments; their join can still violate the promise. A better certificate needs to preserve the priority distinction through A, prove it immaterial to every allowed future edit, or say that it is unknown.

Three implications:

- An observer defines a useful quotient only if environments and allowed edits respect it. With a fixed deterministic continuation vocabulary, two learner states are equivalent for composition if every legal future edit/test sequence has the same definedness and observations from both states. This is a right congruence by construction; ordinary observer equality is only the empty-continuation case. An unrestricted vocabulary can make the quotient as large as the original system.
- Joining promises is not the same as joining implementations. The express promise can remain logically consistent while one backend violates it, another realizes it, or available evidence cannot tell which is true. An explicit waiver can discharge only the named obligation on its authorized slice; it must not turn an unobserved slice into a waiver.
- Local tolerance is not end-to-end retention. Two permitted changes of one minute each can move the original deadline by two. Likewise, independently chosen couplings or sampled histories need not share the right intermediate evidence. Keep a stable origin, explicit debt, or a proof that the projections can be glued.

## A minimal algebra to try to break

Use a finite reference world first. A boundary interface describes a set of behavioral states (including permitted intervention responses), the promises still live with their origins and horizons, and the continuation vocabulary to which the interface applies. A concrete edit denotes a relation between typed interfaces, with an explicit precondition and a transformer on live obligations. The backend (table rewrite, adapter, or other weight editor) separately owes a refinement claim. A certificate can be refuted or unknown even when the semantic relation is well-defined.

| Operation | Candidate law | Guard against a vacuous win |
|---|---|---|
| Identity `1` and sequence `A ; B` | Identity leaves interface and obligations unchanged; exact relational sequence is associative, using a *shared* intermediate state. | Not a learned law by fiat: projected summaries must predict the same permitted outcomes under either bracketing, or expose the information lost. Two separate witnesses for the middle are not one shared witness. |
| Restriction `restrict(A, S)` | Scope splits do not alter behavior or promise lineage if all disjoint slices cover the original scope and retain their cross-slice obligations. | Rejoin with an old task that spans slices. A collection of individually valid local tasks does not imply a valid cross-slice task. |
| Independent join `A ⊗ B` | Defined when both orders are legal, agree on observed outcomes, and jointly preserve all inherited promises. Discover sufficient local conditions rather than defining away conflicts. | Check that each edit preserves the other's assumptions and that the original promises survive *both* orders. Disjoint target examples, disjoint weights, and commuting writes alone do not suffice. |
| Scope subtraction `retire(P, S)` | Retire only permission-authorized cells of named promise `P`; outside `S`, the old reference and horizon survive. | An unproved cell is not retired; two promises on the same observable are not interchangeable. |
| Residual `P / A` | Given edit summary `A` and historical promise `P`, return the admissible continuations: ideally `B` belongs iff `A ; B` preserves `P`, under the declared vocabulary. | Weakest preconditions/residuation already give exact versions. The new bet is a compact reusable residual for bounded learners, not enumeration in different notation. |

These are proposed laws for the exact semantics first. Approximate summaries must declare where they are sound, how error accumulates, and what yields `unknown`. Report useful acceptance coverage as well as false assurance; a system that rejects every new lesson retains trivially.

## Novelty filters and test rules

The [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/0e7f92128843f201676d38c6a1c84bc9d9871ac3/reviews/idea-ledger.md) killed composition contracts that reduce to training on generated hybrid labels, sparse coded merge audits that tie black-box search, and effect-class distillation that lost to execution-reward replay. Prompt-addressable checkpoints also lost to equal-byte stored deltas. The [flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/0e7f92128843f201676d38c6a1c84bc9d9871ac3/reviews/flood-triage.md) already covers learning-operator commutativity/idempotence (O1/O2), Lie closure, sheaf consistency, lesson effect types, and certified contracts. None is a novelty claim here. [Behavioral lenses in the parent](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/84) already preserve one complement; the bar here is what a *second* editor can reliably infer or do with it. The sibling [inverse-certificates node](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/83) asks how to intersect repairs sharing a write variable; this node asks which old behavior conditions must survive even after the write variables have been eliminated.

Contextual equivalence, automata minimization, [interface automata](https://doi.org/10.1145/503209.503226), [separation logic](https://doi.org/10.1109/LICS.2002.1029817), abstract interpretation, and patch theory are sources and strong controls, not inventions of this node. “New angle” below names a candidate CL primitive or its boundary, not a literature verdict. The proposal must offer something stronger than running a verifier over the whole edit history or grouping tests by another name.

**Common test contract.** Start with finite exact truth where feasible and compare to whole-history checking as an oracle, not as a cheap baseline. Match persistent bytes (including old checkpoints, evidence, and interfaces), compile/evaluation calls, transition budgets, and backend capacity as appropriate. Compare with plain joint constraints, replay or append-and-retrieve, and the named method-specific control. Hold out edit combinations, histories, or environment factors, not random rows. After every admitted edit, check held-out acquisition, fixed old tasks, and nonsuperseded prior lessons; for policies, also check the old tasks starting from their usual starts under the current policy. Compare at matched acquisition and acceptance coverage; record worst-cohort losses and false assurances. Track authorized revisions separately. Reject any claimed advantage that disappears with matched compute or a stronger equal-budget control.

## Raw ideas

### A. Find the missing intermediate state

1. **Future-read quotient.** **Mechanism:** partition learner states by the outcomes of allowed edit/test suffixes; refine a class only when a suffix exposes a protected distinction. **New angle:** a deployable retention interface is minimized against *edits that can read it*, not predictions on the current task. The candidate primitive is that interface, not partition refinement. **Cheapest first test:** enumerate implementations of queue X with several dormant bits; synthesize partitions from short suffixes and compare held-out prediction of safe edits to same-byte archive replay and ordinary behavioral bisimulation. **Old-task retention:** after adopting held-out shortcuts and rankers, check fragile deadlines and older queue Y pricing.
2. **Residuals that outlive their edit.** **Mechanism:** apply an exact or conservative preimage over the *continuation vocabulary* to compile each historical promise into a residual on future edits; combine residuals without retaining the implementations they came from. **New angle:** an editor inherits a reusable condition on acceptable changes, not a point repair, a protected dataset, or one early yes/no. **Cheapest first test:** on a finite workflow with two historical latency bounds, compare a bounded residual decision diagram to same-byte prior constraints and an exact weakest-precondition summary on held-out action words; audit soundness and coverage against enumeration. **Old-task retention:** replay only for evaluation of historical express and economy deadlines after each accepted word.
3. **Derivative monitors for edit histories.** **Mechanism:** after every edit event, take a derivative of a temporal retention formula on that event, retaining only the monitor state; one learner-state edit may have different derivatives depending on its authorized history. **New angle:** two identical current behaviors can carry different future duties; the portable residual is derived from the sequence of changes, not ordinary inference tokens. **Cheapest first test:** use a service transition that permits one degraded edit only if the next restores capacity. Compare a compiled derivative monitor to a matched persistent event window and a finite-state monitor built manually; present unseen delays and repeated requests. **Old-task retention:** test existing service tasks at each step, within the exception while live and at their original floor when it expires.
4. **Behavioral separators.** **Mechanism:** infer a separator of observed intervention responses across two parts of a workflow; retention evidence can compose across the separator only when it screens off how either part can affect protected downstream behavior. **New angle:** factor the *obligations* using a measured interface, without separate parameters or a given causal module boundary. **Cheapest first test:** in a three-stage dispatcher, hide a bypass bit and give an interface learner and a hand-coded read/write footprint the same probes to detect when gluing is unsafe. **Old-task retention:** hold out end-to-end old queue paths, including paths through the hidden bypass, and check a previous handoff rule after simultaneous new features.
5. **Distinguishers as callable evidence.** **Mechanism:** when two edit certificates alias, save a reusable intervention plus its predicted outcomes under both interface hypotheses, rather than saving only a failing input. Invoke the distinguisher when future scopes could split the hypotheses. **New angle:** a witness denotes a future operation that can restore a missing distinction, even when its original counterexample is no longer reachable. **Cheapest first test:** repeat the same missing-priority distinction under three unseen queue schemas; compare a bounded distinguisher library to raw hard examples and fresh active probes at equal calls. **Old-task retention:** check untouched express cohorts and an earlier priority fix after each admitted migration; count missing interventions as unknown.

### B. Compose overlapping edits without hiding lost promises

6. **Proof-carrying edit swaps.** **Mechanism:** attach to each edit a read/write footprint over behavioral assumptions, and a transport of the other edit's obligations where they overlap. Swap when postconditions and preservation proofs can be moved both ways. **New angle:** swap evidence is a reusable proof transformation, not a commutator score, a scalar order predictor, or merely rebasing an edit. **Cheapest first test:** shuffle four finite router patches, including commuting but unsafe assignments, against an equal-budget pairwise outcome audit and direct joint verification. Check whether locally proved swaps predict unseen histories. **Old-task retention:** evaluate every original route class and both historical service guarantees in both orders, not just final equivalence.
7. **Subtraction normal form.** **Mechanism:** label guard cells by stable obligation origin; normalize splits and overlapping waivers so a waiver follows the intended cell and survives refactoring of guard syntax, but cannot discharge a distinct promise on that cell. **New angle:** override is an operation on conserved *obligation lineage*, not precedence metadata on the most recent rule. **Cheapest first test:** write equivalent parcel guards as interval splits and as Boolean formulas, with two overlapping promises and one scoped waiver. Compare recompiled variants to a priority list and equivalent normalized predicates without origin IDs. **Old-task retention:** both the unnamed promise inside the overlap and the named promise outside the waiver must pass on hidden boundary parcels.
8. **Join with a retained fork.** **Mechanism:** merge two versions by gluing their agreed behaviors, retaining a compact set of explicitly unresolved alternatives on disputed cells; subsequent edits can refine one fork or act on all. **New angle:** the disputed behavior survives *as an operand* of later edits, rather than an isolated merge error or a selected adapter. **Cheapest first test:** in a finite router, let edits on three branches disagree about the same outcome; add a fourth branch that distinguishes the two legal meanings. Compare both merge bracketings to an equal-byte three-way merge with conflict markers and an append-only branch archive. **Old-task retention:** before and after the distinction, verify common old routes and unaffected branch-specific lessons, with unresolved cases scored separately.
9. **Higher-order conflict projection.** **Mechanism:** eliminate edit-specific variables but keep the smallest joint behavior relation explaining a conflict, with its guard and provenance, so future merges can transport it instead of rerunning all subsets. **New angle:** the output is a symbolic obstruction reusable across rewrites of the same interface, not sparse second-order scoring for merge search. **Cheapest first test:** plant a parity-style three-edit contradiction hidden from every pair in a truth-table router. Compare bounded projected relations to cached pairwise checks and incremental SAT with learned clauses at equal space and calls; transplant the obstruction after an unrelated rewrite. **Old-task retention:** test all non-retired old route clauses after accepting a compatible alternative, and verify refusal leaves original behavior intact.
10. **Scoped repair of a broken law.** **Mechanism:** when a swap fails, compute the smallest behavioral region and obligation subset on which its two orders differ; return a local reconciliation relation that a subsequent join can reuse. **New angle:** noncommutation pays out a reusable third operation with a frame, not a yes/no compatibility edge or a tensor correction. **Cheapest first test:** find one interacting guard cell in two independently written dispatcher edits, then use the same defect to reconcile a holdout rescoping. Compare to stored counterexamples and whole-edit recompilation at equal calls. **Old-task retention:** verify the old rules on the defect boundary and in all untouched cells, plus a historical task spanning the boundary.

### C. Compose over reached histories, not archived prompts

11. **Reachability transducers.** **Mechanism:** carry a finite-state matcher from old to edited traces that can emit obligations when a new path reconnects to an old path; compose matchers before auditing longer sequences. **New angle:** a newly introduced path becomes a source of future checks under another editor, rather than an alignment synthesized from scratch for each edit. **Cheapest first test:** in a small grid, learn two detours with changed path lengths and a new interaction at reconnection. Compare composed matchers to same-transition direct trajectory alignment and replay from old starts. **Old-task retention:** test historical destination, deadline, and no-drop guarantees under the combined policy from normal old starts.
12. **Portable dormant-path obligations.** **Mechanism:** when an edit makes a protected region unreachable, retain a guarded promise whose check becomes live if a later edit makes it reachable again; compose the reactivation predicate along reachability changes. **New angle:** temporary unreachability cannot silently erase the duty, and certificates remain portable without saving all traces or requiring a continuously live replay path. **Cheapest first test:** close and later reopen a route through a formerly fragile handoff; compare bounded dormant obligations to reachability pruning and capped old-state replay. **Old-task retention:** check all reachable ordinary old deliveries at every write and the original fragile handoff immediately upon reopening.
13. **May/must trace pairs.** **Mechanism:** retain both a simulation for behaviors a new policy may take and a witness for behaviors it must still be capable of taking; compose the two directions and track missing witnesses separately. **New angle:** one artifact distinguishes “added no forbidden path” from “deleted an old useful option,” even when both deployed policies have the same usual outputs. **Cheapest first test:** in a nondeterministic grid, remove the only usable recovery route while keeping common outcomes unchanged; compare the compact paired interface to one-way trace inclusion and bidirectional full checking. **Old-task retention:** test recovery under the old blockage condition as well as the standard no-block task after another transition edit.
14. **Loop closure on old obligations.** **Mechanism:** infer an inductive set of reachable interface/obligation pairs for a repeated edit cycle, then check that every return still meets the origin promise. Unroll only if the set cannot be closed cheaply. **New angle:** the result certifies a family of repeated edits over behavior, not a Lyapunov score for a fitted memory loop or inverse repair around one unrolling. **Cheapest first test:** make a two-season dispatch cycle whose dead time leaks one tick per round; compare a compact invariant to equal-query bounded unrolling and exact fixed-point checking. **Old-task retention:** check old deadlines at the origin and at intermediate phases, including the first delayed failure beyond the query horizon.
15. **Counterfactual prefixes as an interface.** **Mechanism:** expose a bounded set of prefix interventions needed to evaluate old tasks after new paths diverge, with results transportable only under a declared engine or environment mapping. **New angle:** preserve *access to the question* about an old trajectory, not fidelity to how often the old policy visited it; the compiler can request a missing prefix intervention. **Cheapest first test:** use a scripted handoff with new entry states in which logged prefixes miss a hidden failure. Compare a portable prefix generator to equally many stored and on-policy prefixes; test on new entry transitions. **Old-task retention:** run ordinary delivery, cancellation, and the prior handoff guarantees from the held-out starts; unsupported starts cannot count as retained.

### D. Stop small statistical promises from laundering one another

16. **Couplings with a shared middle.** **Mechanism:** certify adjacent stochastic policy changes by coupling joint observations and any shared latent context required by the promise. Glue only through a common intermediate distribution on that full context, or return unknown. **New angle:** the contract carries the extra marginal future edits need; matching action frequencies or independently coupling visible actions can hide who was rerouted. **Cheapest first test:** take three binary randomized routers with identical visible marginals but opposite group-conditioned allocations; compare compact conditional certificates to same-byte marginal distances and exact joint coupling. **Old-task retention:** after acquiring two new allocations, verify protected-group outcomes and an unchanged majority route rate under the original deployment mixture.
17. **Non-renewable drift credit.** **Mechanism:** compose movement bounds against the promise's stable origin; allow credit to return only with a verified endpoint bound, not by replacing the reference or assuming canceled local deltas. **New angle:** an anchor-free stretch of learning gets a reusable debt proof with explicit recalibration conditions, not a refreshed per-step KL cap. **Cheapest first test:** edit a calibrated router with monotone drift and a matched reversing sequence, using sparse absolute rechecks. Compare to restarting local thresholds and periodic old-logit replay at equal checks; score safe accepted edits. **Old-task retention:** evaluate calibration and routine route success against the original model, on old cases excluded from the rechecks.
18. **Transport sets for old workloads.** **Mechanism:** carry a set of old workload distributions after each behavior change, then compose environment-shift maps and certify the worst retained outcome over the transported set. Track when approximating the set drops correlations or becomes too loose. **New angle:** uncertainty describes where an old task can now be exercised, not which current weights might satisfy it; each edit changes that uncertainty as an operand. **Cheapest first test:** in two queue changes, vary demand correlations without changing single-feature marginals. Compare a factorized transport envelope to same-sample nominal replay and fixed distributionally robust bounds. **Old-task retention:** test normal old demand and the held-out feasible old demand mix with the largest fragile delay after both changes.
19. **Separate access from ability.** **Mechanism:** retain a two-coordinate interface for reaching a skill's precondition from ordinary old starts and succeeding after reaching it; compose the former under routing edits and the latter under execution edits only when the boundary state distributions match or have a proved bound. **New angle:** a drop in use is not automatically a forgotten skill, and constant conditional success cannot conceal that it became inaccessible. **Cheapest first test:** build two policies with equal endpoint success before adding a bridge, one due to rare entry and one due to unreliable execution. Compare to same-rollout end-to-end replay and ordinary value decomposition. **Old-task retention:** after adopting the bridge and a separate execution change, require end-to-end old success as well as recovery on conditioned entries.
20. **Reusable evidence under adaptive edit choice.** **Mechanism:** tag retention claims with how their evidence was selected; combine deterministic certificates freely but spend a predeclared error budget or use an anytime-valid bound when probes are reused adaptively. **New angle:** evidence lineage is executable alongside the law: locally convincing certificates cannot be reused as independent evidence in later merges. This is an improvement candidate unless it enables safe reuse beyond ordinary holdout accounting. **Cheapest first test:** let edits select for noise on a bounded audit pool; compare matched fresh holdouts, alpha-spending, and a structural-plus-statistical ledger at the same probe budget. **Old-task retention:** use an unseen oracle cohort for old route and protected-tail tasks after each write; compare false assurance at matched coverage and acquisition.

### E. Learn, compress, or move the interface without losing its laws

21. **Observers that back-translate.** **Mechanism:** when the observer changes, learn a bounded map from its new public evidence back to the old distinctions still needed downstream. Allow partial transport and return a collision witness for the rest. **New angle:** the measurable target is whether a future edit can consume an old promise, not imitation of previous labels or porting a delta. **Cheapest first test:** merge tracking categories, then stage another edit that still needs a distinction preserved by a side channel. Compare equal-byte observer maps, old-output distillation, and the schema migration alone. **Old-task retention:** verify old route and cost promises in every mapped category and test the missed distinction separately rather than relabeling it away.
22. **Certificate compilation targets.** **Mechanism:** train different backends to realize the same interface plus retention proof obligations, then swap a backend without rebuilding inherited obligations; treat disagreement under held-out compositions as a counterexample to the interface. **New angle:** unlike generating two patches for the same edit, this tests whether one backend's *composition evidence* can constrain a different backend. **Cheapest first test:** implement two small rule sets in a table and in a narrow neural router, then swap the carrier between edits; compare to recompilation from a same-byte interface-free I/O archive. **Old-task retention:** test fixed tasks and prior rules after both swap directions and a new withheld edit, including changed operation order.
23. **Compaction by distinguishability.** **Mechanism:** merge, drop, or re-encode stored evidence only if no tested continuation can use it to separate allowed and forbidden behavior; spend the evidence budget on distinctions rather than examples or checkpoint versions. **New angle:** forgetting *evidence* becomes an explicit operation with a future-composition error, independent of whether the current model forgot an answer. **Cheapest first test:** compact a long rule chain with redundant and one delayed-use example at each length. Compare to equal-byte replay, probe-logit sketches, and stored per-version deltas on a hidden continuation set. **Old-task retention:** after each compaction and later write, check untouched old tasks and the original behavior exposed by the genuinely delayed example.
24. **A lower bound on what must be remembered.** **Mechanism:** build a family of indistinguishable present behaviors whose legal continuations reveal independent protected bits; use the number of distinct responses to bound the total persistent information needed for a sound, sufficiently complete interface. **New angle:** it gives a backend-independent obstruction to compressing *retention guarantees*, rather than a maximum count of weight edits. **Cheapest first test:** count distinguishable states in a finite family, then vary storage above and below the bound with a compact interface, equal-byte replay and a snapshot baseline; charge all accessible state and count abstentions against coverage. **Old-task retention:** verify old subtask outputs for each revealed bit and unrelated old tasks after ordinary updates; hidden future answers may not be stored outside the budget.
25. **A learned safe composition table.** **Mechanism:** fit a small typed composition table from solved finite systems; allow it to propose which summary to carry and which law to apply, but let an independent checker accept, refute, or demand refinement. **New angle:** the learner proposes a portable retention calculus, not a weight update or a black-box predictor of post-edit accuracy. It only matters if the proposals make checks cheaper on unseen compositions. **Cheapest first test:** meta-train on tiny dispatcher families; on unseen edit triples compare verified acceptances per solver call to an exact checker with hand-written abstract transfer rules and a learned safety scorer that also calls the checker. **Old-task retention:** evaluate each admitted triple against old and historical tasks across newly reachable trace classes and compare refusal rates.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **What is the smallest interface on which future edits are well-defined?** A *continuation quotient* could replace the checkpoint or replay buffer for some bounded edit vocabularies. Try to produce compact, useful classes on held-out suffixes and a lower bound when compression is impossible; if ordinary bisimulation or equally budgeted replay ties, there is no new primitive. Ideas 1, 4–5, 23–24.
2. **Can a learner inherit and transform a residual obligation without inheriting the old implementation?** A *portable residual* could survive backend replacement, overlap, and scoped waiver. Require soundness and useful acceptance under repeated composition versus incremental exact checking or same-byte replay; closure on paper without a memory or query benefit is not enough. Ideas 2–3, 7, 9, 22, 25.
3. **What kind of trajectory witness survives two changes in what is reachable?** A *composable trace witness* should transfer both a protected behavior and the means to exercise it, including when an older region reopens. Look for a failure that both individual replay audits miss but a composed witness finds at lower cost than fresh joint exploration. Ideas 11–15, 19.
4. **Can local waivers and noncommuting edits produce a canonical, evidence-carrying merge?** A *merge with a transportable defect or unresolved fork* could let future edits act before disputes are settled. Require gluing to agree across equivalent declarations without imposing commutativity where genuine conflicts exist; compare against ordinary three-way merge plus exact contract checks. Ideas 6–10.
5. **Can graded retention stay meaningful over long sequences without frequent resets?** A *persistent stochastic certificate* could carry stable reference and joint evidence across changing policies and workloads. This ranks lower because robust bounds, probability couplings, and sequential testing already cover much of the mechanism; demand more safe accepted writes at fixed old-task risk than the stacked controls. Ideas 16–20.
