# Survival-probability memory: can a learner write what survives its next updates?

Parent: [PR #109, optimizer-state memory](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/109), especially cards 14 and 16 and deeper question 2.

**Question:** Can we write and read old-task information in the probability that future training reaches a compatible region before a failure region? When is that probability a carrier of information rather than a forecast about information stored elsewhere?

## Root cause: conservation provides no write channel by itself

PR #109's no-go is conditional, not “all training invariants are constants.” If an allowed sequence sends all writable states to a common limit, a continuous invariant of every update must equal its value at that limit on every write. If instead there are disconnected reachable classes, a protected subspace, or a symmetry, an invariant can vary across them. But the writer must be able to move it, and the reader must see that move in old-task behavior. The ReLU balancedness write sits in a positive-rescaling gauge: it changes the statistic without changing predictions at installation. Rescaling can affect later optimization, but that is a different claim and must beat effective-learning-rate controls. The [PR #6 Noether rerun](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6) also showed that finite-step SGD at LR 0.05 and momentum erased the statistic's bits, a non-conserved norm channel survived just as well at small LR, and a function-preserving rebalance removed the balancedness code. That rejects the demonstrated carrier, not every invariant.

## What the committor changes—and what it cannot do

Let the full learner state include weights, optimizer, retained data and any controller state. For a declared future stream and update policy, let `K_t` be the resulting transition kernel (include the stream's latent regime or its belief state where needed). Let `A` mean **new-task progress reached with old obligations satisfied**, and `B` mean a first breach of a protected old-task floor. Score entry at checkpoints fixed before testing, with a breach taking priority over progress. Define `q_t(z) = P(τ_A < τ_B, τ_A ≤ H | Z_t=z)`: reaching neither set by the deadline is not success. From an interior state before the deadline this equals the conditional expectation of `q_{t+1}(Z_{t+1})`; at a stop the value is 1 on success and 0 otherwise. The stopped score is a bounded martingale under the specified law, not constant along a realized run. It says nothing about survival *after* the stop. If the goal is uninterrupted service to a fixed horizon rather than successful adaptation, use `P(τ_B > H)` and report progress separately: otherwise a learner that refuses to update can look good.

- **The future law is part of the address.** Two streams indistinguishable so far can drive the same state to opposite terminals. At an interior point with a one-step success under one law and one-step failure under the other, any prediction based on that same past errs by at least 1/2 on one law. Unknown order, optimizer resets, and a scheduler that acts on its own predictions can all change the law. A probe distribution, an uncertainty set, and the deployed policy's actual distribution are three different objects. Label results accordingly; a worst-case bound over unrestricted future tasks can be identically zero.
- **Risk is not content.** A scalar such as 0.9 says how likely the specified obligation is to survive; it does not identify the obligation. With fixed boundaries and kernel, the exact committor is determined by learner state. A separately trained head or table is additional stored state, not magically durable. If two histories coalesce in all learner state and everyone gets the same target-blind read probes, their read distributions coincide; no estimator can recover which history occurred ([PR #110](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/110)). Similarly, a better risk score on identical learner states is only a predictor unless the reader or writer exploits it to change outcomes.
- **Write, read, and retention are separate.** For writes `W_m` of two different old rules `m`, match visible installation behavior on a deliberately ambiguous support set, current-task progress, and write cost. Ask if a *fixed*, payload-blind bank of continuation experiments can distinguish the writes after further learning; learn the decoder on separate histories. The bank, decoder, seeds, boundaries, and any retained labels count as memory/compute. Swap the write while keeping the served probes fixed, then erase or shuffle the proposed carrier; the read should follow the write, not a label in a boundary definition or the age of the edit. A forked read must be non-destructive to the live learner and is distinct from ordinary inference. To claim useful CL memory, show intact ordinary behavior or improved authorized recovery on unseen old queries and no loss of matched new learning; report those outcomes separately.

### A minimal discrimination witness (analytic, not an SGD result)

Imagine two writable interior states `s_0,s_1` with the same visible old margin. Two payload-blind continuation protocols `L,R` take a single step to common boundaries `A,B`. Their success probabilities are `(3/4, 1/4)` from `s_0` and `(1/4, 3/4)` from `s_1`. A 50:50 mixture has committor 1/2 for both writes; the signed contrast `q_L−q_R` is +1/2 vs −1/2 and identifies the write in the exact oracle. In the toy, the transition law *already reads* the state distinction; the probabilities neither create it nor make it survive a merge. This is a positive control for the read interface, a negative control for a scalar risk score, and no evidence that a real learner can be written this way. To test the idea in SGD, the protocols must be actual legal update batches, the read cannot use payload labels, and the result must beat equally expensive observation of raw fork outcomes.

### Prior art and gates

- **Already known:** [Committor functions via tensor networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12515) estimates the classical hitting function; [On Committor Functions in Milestoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07280) studies coarse computation. [A Lyapunov-based Approach to Safe Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07708) and [Safe Flow Q-Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15136) cover safety-based control and reachability critics. [Successor Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312) already decouple a fixed dynamics model from a changing reward. Computing a safety critic over optimizer states, using a hazard penalty, or applying Bellman recursion is not by itself novel.
- **Direct CL rivals:** [When Can First-Order Models of Fine-Tuning Bound Forgetting?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32818) already gives per-fact first-passage bounds and shows why ignoring response drift fails; [Learning Dynamics of Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33620) links evolving update–behavior interactions to forgetting and plasticity; [Can Fine-Tuning Erase Your Edits?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05852) measures edit survival under adaptation. Static importance and local protection controls include [EWC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796) and [OGD](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07104). This is an abstract-level screen, not a novelty clearance.
- **Internal kills:** Read PR #6's [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md), [flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md), and [flood top 10](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-top10.md). Noether-keyed restore is not better than storing the key; programmable half-life and naked forgetting alarms are not primitives. The Lyapunov predictor lost to short two-order divergence. The Gramian plasticity-reserve candidate lost to readiness/random choice, with little useful spread once prior retention was matched. Rare-event prefix splitting lost to equal-budget direct/prompt replay and functional syndromes lost to same-byte raw replay. Use these as controls, not as renamed proposals. Don't count a Nerode partition (#108), a write-skew witness (#103), forgetting insurance (#67), a simulator (#63), or relearning speed alone (Inversions #03) as a new memory interface.
- **Shared CPU protocol:** Start with enumerable controlled chains, then a tiny linear or nonlinear learner in which legal updates induce the transitions. Split by history/seed **and** stream family; stratify by optimizer changes and old group. Before fitting any predictor, require actionable spread between matched writes above seed variation and a constant guess. Match initial margin, immediate edit success where visible, new-task progress, budgets and allowable control actions. Use same-step rollout/divergence, old margin/loss, gradient interference/Fisher, and equal-byte replay or explicit intent as defaults, plus each card's stronger control. Report calibration for declared laws, direct old-task mean and worst group at the worst monitored checkpoint, and final old-task performance. If a card uses repair, add the lag and old-task performance before repair; if it uses abstention, charge it as missed service. None of the following are experimental results.

## Raw ideas (24)

### A. Write and read distinctions in future training

1. **Differential committor register.** *Mechanism:* write an old rule using a constrained update that changes the sign of `q_L−q_R` for two fixed legal forks while holding their pooled risk and the installed margins constant; decode by the sign on copies. *Why it might be new:* an address is a contrast between interventions, not an importance bit or a changed confidence score. *Cheapest first test:* fit two linear predictors agreeing on ambiguous support, vary legal feature access on the two forks, and compare with decoding weights or fork logits at equal cost. *Old-task retention:* decode and serve unseen points that disambiguate the rules after an unseen stream; score both non-target old tasks and direct no-fork recall.
2. **Challenge–response memory.** *Mechanism:* address an old rule by a short randomized sequence of label-blind updates whose response is a first-hit order; learn writes that generalize across challenges constructed from the same public grammar. *Why it might be new:* the reader needs no stored example or dedicated route per old rule; this goes beyond #65's skill experiment only if it reads newly written content, not merely validating it. *Cheapest first test:* two-step challenges on a tiny polynomial predictor, compared with one-step derivative signatures and a stored key. *Old-task retention:* vary challenge order and generator seed after unrelated training; require correct predictions on held-out old inputs, not just recognition of the right slot.
3. **Probabilities as soft read parity.** *Mechanism:* make three fixed fork contrasts decode two old payload bits plus an XOR check; use the parity only to reject corrupted reads, then compare per-bit priors with a direct decoder. *Why it might be new:* intervenable error detection across correlated learning responses, not residual-weight syndromes (ledger). *Cheapest first test:* perturb block-selective updates in a four-bit learner and compare reliability at equal read and storage budget to replicated registers and raw replay. *Old-task retention:* report wrong old answers, abstentions, and errors from common-mode corruption on separately held-out streams.
4. **Shared fork dictionary without shared payloads.** *Mechanism:* install multiple rules by writing sparse signed changes in a common bank of continuation responses; recover a queried rule from the relevant subset. *Why it might be new:* amortizes a vocabulary of read interventions across tasks, rather than compiling either a policy or a new weight ISA (triage kill). *Cheapest first test:* incrementally install three binary rules in a small ReLU network with fixed gradient forks; compare dictionary plus address bytes against explicit keys and equal-byte old samples. *Old-task retention:* measure each prior rule after every write, including intentionally near-colliding addresses.
5. **An invariant of read contrast, not of state.** *Mechanism:* train writes so several known optimizer changes transform both fork probabilities together while preserving their rank; a writer and reader need agreement on the order, not on calibration. *Why it might be new:* transport of readable content despite known kernel changes, not portable momentum (SM25). *Cheapest first test:* train contrasts under two noise scales and hold out momentum/reset transitions; compare with state features and sign-of-mean fork response. *Old-task retention:* measure rank flips and held-out old loss immediately after a switch and at matched new progress.
6. **Order-only memory.** *Mechanism:* encode an old distinction in which of two safe checkpoints is normally hit first; arrange for both writes to converge to the same endpoint under a prescribed read protocol. *Why it might be new:* when endpoints and scalar success agree, path order becomes a readout; the claim is not post-merge recall. *Cheapest first test:* find two starting points in quadratics with a matched endpoint and compare stop-order to an equal-step read of intermediate logits. *Old-task retention:* read before the merge and show that the read supports held-out old behavior; explicitly score post-merge failure.

### B. Make the future law a first-class input

7. **One certificate for a set of curricula.** *Mechanism:* bound a write's success over a declared family of kernels, comparing a single unknown episode regime with an adversary allowed to switch regimes per step. Choose the cheapest write reaching a floor in the intended model. *Why it might be new:* the possible CL primitive is a portable write certificate with a declared scope, not robust DP. *Cheapest first test:* two training regimes with incompatible safe directions and equal initial margins; compare maximin margin, empirical screening, and robust reachability. *Old-task retention:* count violations per held-out regime, including regimes deliberately outside the certificate.
8. **Separate transport from relabeling.** *Mechanism:* cache a compact transition representation and solve fresh stopping problems after either an old obligation changes or a future curriculum changes; compress only components used by both classes of queries. *Why it might be new:* separate the cost of changing a memory's meaning from changing the training law; fixed-dynamics reward transfer is already Successor Features. *Cheapest first test:* factorial swap of boundaries and transition kernels in a quantized learner, versus successor features, refitting and a cached hitting kernel. *Old-task retention:* audit unchanged old groups and the revised group separately, including one threshold change unsupported by the cache.
9. **Pay for the diagnostic batch only when it can flip a write.** *Mechanism:* fork on a small unlabeled candidate batch to infer which future regime applies; ask for the batch only when the posterior can change the best admissible write. *Why it might be new:* the measured quantity is the decision value of information about the continuation law, not a label-free plasticity reserve. *Cheapest first test:* two indistinguishable pasts with swapped best writes, comparing regime classification and short two-order divergence at equal compute. *Old-task retention:* measure regime-wise old losses after the chosen write and charge wrong queries and missed new progress.
10. **Overlap-limited revaluation.** *Mechanism:* update only those old survival claims for which a new curriculum has overlap with the original fork data; explicitly mark the rest unidentified, rather than filling them with critic outputs. *Why it might be new:* a compact memory of where a retention claim is reusable, with a falsifiable no-coverage boundary; off-policy evaluation itself is old. *Cheapest first test:* compute exact success as a new batch type's mass grows from zero in a small chain, then compare importance weighting and fresh rollouts. *Old-task retention:* report coverage and misses on old groups exposed exclusively to the new batch and include abstentions.
11. **A write with a transferable counterexample.** *Mechanism:* attach the shortest reachable continuation found to defeat a write, plus a distinguishing safer write; recheck whether that same suffix still separates them after optimizer or schedule changes. *Why it might be new:* reusable negative evidence about a writable alternative, rather than a witness that two histories can merge (#110). *Cheapest first test:* enumerate minibatch words on two linear writes with matched loss and candidate margin, against random/adversarial loss-based suffix search. *Old-task retention:* test the predicted vulnerable old group and unrelated groups on unseen suffixes from the same failure mechanism.
12. **Choose which law to forget.** *Mechanism:* under a fixed memory budget evict a nuisance regime's fork data only after proving its omission cannot reverse the current write ordering over all remaining obligations within estimation error. *Why it might be new:* forget the training forecast, not the old rule; this is distinct from re-derivability of an old answer (LP39). *Cheapest first test:* three regime models, two that have very different risks but identical action ordering; compare with reservoir and least-recently-used fork caches. *Old-task retention:* reintroduce evicted regimes and report critical misranking and worst old-group loss.

### C. Change the learner, not just the score

13. **Budget a repair window, not an irreversible fail point.** *Mechanism:* distinguish first service breach, recoverable degradation and irreversible loss under a fixed, target-blind repair budget. Trigger one bounded intervention based on the estimated probability of losing repairability before reaching progress. *Why it might be new:* the actionable boundary concerns the remaining repair options, not lifetime prediction or forgetting insurance. *Cheapest first test:* a two-feature learner where two states have the same margin and different short repair feasibility; compare with reactive replay and an equal-cost loss threshold. *Old-task retention:* record first breach, recovery delay, untouched groups and end performance; don't relabel a temporary breach as success.
14. **Trade risk only where written obligations permit it.** *Mechanism:* propagate joint probabilities of first failures for a set of old obligations and let the writer exchange only predeclared slack between permitted groups as the curriculum changes. *Why it might be new:* composable task-local service terms might replace globally fixed regularization, if joint path risk can be carried compactly; constrained RL is the null. *Cheapest first test:* two old groups with the same marginals but opposite failure correlation; compare independent hazard budgets and a joint rollout controller. *Old-task retention:* check simultaneous breach probability and per-group service under matched new progress, not average accuracy.
15. **Convert avoidable risk into payload placement.** *Mechanism:* factor a learner into interchangeable, initially behavior-matched implementations; place an old computation in the one whose risk is most reducible by the legal update interventions available later. *Why it might be new:* placement by *causal control authority* over first passage, rather than flatness, masking or M3's immediate route correctability. *Cheapest first test:* two feature paths with matched outputs and distinct response to one allowed optimizer action; compare with raw sensitivity and same-capacity modular protection. *Old-task retention:* switch which interventions are available at test time and score path-specific held-out old examples and collateral groups.
16. **Preserve the decision to wait.** *Mechanism:* a candidate training update is allowed to spend some old margin as long as there remains a high-probability path to adaptation without forced violation; store the cheapest “wait vs act” switching surface across old groups. *Why it might be new:* delayed use of plasticity may be a durable control primitive if the switching surface transfers across new tasks; model predictive control alone is not. *Cheapest first test:* a two-batch stream with a temporary bottleneck; compare lookahead at equal compute and matched replay. *Old-task retention:* report worst checkpoint and failure before the second batch, including sequences where the opportunity to wait disappears.
17. **Write a backup triggered by a competing exit.** *Mechanism:* train a secondary partial implementation that activates only on evidence of a specific old-rule failure mode, allocating no backup to common-mode exits it cannot repair. *Why it might be new:* the learned trigger and backup jointly encode a failure-conditioned computation, not #109's static redundancy or a generic ensemble. *Cheapest first test:* a two-feature classifier with planted distinct exits and a held-out common-mode exit; compare equal-parameter ensembles, replay and margin gating. *Old-task retention:* measure actual error through exit transitions, false triggers on benign drift and failures neither path can serve.
18. **Continual repair without a retained answer.** *Mechanism:* after a monitored breach, choose a short legal adaptation route using contrasts of branch success, then discard the route; provide the same ambiguous support under both old rules. *Why it might be new:* risk-relative intervention may recover a particular old rule without labels that teach it; ordinary relearning speed and #109's buffer oracle are controls, not novelty. *Cheapest first test:* set up old rules agreeing on `x₁=x₂`; compare route selection with direct adaptation, buffered state and equal-byte replay. *Old-task retention:* score unseen `x₁≠x₂` inputs and unrelated old groups before and after repair with identical support and update counts.

### D. Find what cannot be compressed or transported

19. **Minimum bits for a write decision, not for a probability map.** *Mechanism:* compress fork transcripts to preserve the ordering of feasible writes subject to old-task risk ceilings, allowing large calibration error on irrelevant candidates. *Why it might be new:* the target is the worst regret under future adaptations, not #105's cost to describe an edit. *Cheapest first test:* exhaustive quantization/merging in a small chain before testing a learned codec, against raw samples and reward-successor vectors. *Old-task retention:* find the first lost selectivity at each rate and compare old-group failures and new progress with equal-bit replay.
20. **Make a mean-preserving counterfeit.** *Mechanism:* actively construct two writes with the same margins, Fisher score and terminal loss histogram but different first-hit ordering, then ask whether that distinction can itself be selectively rewritten. *Why it might be new:* a falsifiable sufficiency test for endpoint mirrors, linked to but stronger than #107's fixed-assay counterfeits; the witness alone is diagnostic. *Cheapest first test:* swap a harmful and restoring minibatch in a two-step learner that ends at the same state; then hold order fixed and search for dynamics-changing writes. *Old-task retention:* evaluate pathwise breaches at fixed cadence and final outcomes, against rollout and two-order divergence controls.
21. **Measure survival of the read protocol itself.** *Mechanism:* find the smallest legal update suffix that destroys a fork's separation of two old histories while leaving the currently served old answers correct; write a replacement discriminator before that point. *Why it might be new:* distinguishes failure of the read mechanism from failure of the obligation or a stale scalar threshold (E2). *Cheapest first test:* enumerate low-dimensional updates that preserve a decision cell but scramble fork rankings; compare replacement with direct read recalibration. *Old-task retention:* follow both direct old outputs and future read/repair success, including groups not addressed by the replacement.
22. **Price partial observation in task tokens.** *Mechanism:* maintain an interval over the possible committors of hidden optimizer states matching the observed outputs, and purchase the least expensive observation that separates the candidate writes. *Why it might be new:* deployable observability–retention frontier when the optimizer cannot be read, rather than a better single-state predictor. *Cheapest first test:* alias two momentum states with matching weights; compare reading one buffer summary, one fork update, or another old example at matched cost. *Old-task retention:* score wrong writes and missed progress under unobserved resets and held-out old features.
23. **Controlled bit capacity before collapse.** *Mechanism:* measure how many distinct payloads can be written at fixed probability of adaptation and distinguished by bounded, payload-blind reads as a function of continuation length. Construct a converse using pairwise overlap of the induced read distributions. *Why it might be new:* operational, noisy channel capacity with a write action set and a service requirement, not an exact equivalence-class count. *Cheapest first test:* compute read distributions exactly in a finite-state learner for horizon and fork-budget sweeps, then compare to direct storage. *Old-task retention:* report both decoding errors and worst old held-out loss; discard any apparent bits writable only by sacrificing another obligation.
24. **Show when there is no read advantage.** *Mechanism:* map a committor-based read to the raw stopped trajectories that generate it, then ask which physical constraint (shorter probes, shared read compute, cross-law transport) can make the compressed interface useful in spite of that reduction. *Why it might be new:* a decisive boundary between probability as telemetry and probability as a viable read interface; raw data dominates its deterministic summaries when access and cost are identical. *Cheapest first test:* enumerate optimal raw-trajectory and compressed-read decoders, then reduce the read budget and compare transfer across regimes. *Old-task retention:* measure actual service and recovery costs at equal memory/compute; a tie with raw forks or replay is a no-go, not a primitive.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Can a payload-blind continuation query read a distinction that ordinary predictions on accessible support cannot?** (1–6, 21, 23–24.) Best bet: go from the exact contrast witness to two selectable writes in a real tiny learner. Require cross-written payloads, unseen old queries, matched risk/progress and a fixed read budget. Kill if labels or boundaries leak the payload, the read only reveals a current margin, or an equal-byte direct store or equal-cost raw fork decoder serves the old rule just as well.
2. **Can we retain a writable distinction across changes in the training law without retaining old trajectories?** (5, 7–12, 19, 22.) Start with held-out curriculum/optimizer switches and a swapped ranking as a negative control. A portable representation must declare when its coverage ends and still improve later write or read decisions. Kill if worst-case risk is uninformative, if known off-policy evaluation or successor features at equal cost suffice, or if recalibration requires storing/replaying the old data.
3. **Can a joint first-passage signal buy real control over forgetting at fixed adaptation speed?** (13–18, 20.) Start with equally risky instantaneous margins but distinct feasible repair windows. Measure direct old service, recovery lag and new-task progress against constrained optimization, short-rollout control and equal-byte replay with the same interventions. Kill if only the score improves, or slowing new learning explains the gain.
4. **Where is the operational capacity boundary between a risk forecast and memory?** (19–24.) Vary observable state, allowed writes, fork budget and merge horizon in an exact oracle, then try to break the frontier with legal SGD. Require a prediction of when a purported new carrier loses useful distinctions *before* the next write. A sharp negative result is useful, but by itself is not a memory primitive.
