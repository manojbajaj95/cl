# Root: Learning from other models and agents

Root node (depth 0) · 32 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** Continual learning usually assumes a learner updates from raw world data, environment rewards, or a single static teacher. Yet real models and agents live inside an ecosystem of *other models*: sibling fine-tunes, discarded or intermediate training checkpoints, heterogeneous specialists, black-box peer APIs, other agents' execution and crash logs, retired predecessors, and newly released successors. Standard ingestion—full distillation, parameter averaging, or copying logs—treats a foreign model as a passive labeler or an aligned weight vector, silently importing its priors, dialect, blind spots, and hidden traits while overwriting the learner's own old skills. Instead of copying outputs or averaging tensors, build primitives that isolate *what* a foreign model changed, translate it across architecture and capability boundaries, interrogate peers before writing, and carry continual identity across complete model replacements.

**Scope.** Informed by the [idea ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/idea-ledger.md), the [flood triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795531716-reviewer-verdicts/reviews/flood-triage.md), and neighboring roots ([collective agents](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795570020-root-collective-agents/tree/root-collective-agents.md), [LoRA-writing learner](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795578733-lora-learner-4/tree/root-lora-learner.md), [cultural evolution](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795577518-root-cultural-evolution/tree/root-cultural-evolution.md), and [deployment feedback](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/devin/1795579006-root-deployment-feedback/tree/root-deployment-feedback.md)). This root is explicitly *asymmetric*—a continual learner ingesting external weights, checkpoints, logs, peers, predecessors, or successors—not symmetric population anti-collapse (provenance mass, echo cancelling, CRDTs, islands, stationarity routing) and not the optimizer-state / fold-code / park-crossing branch (PRs #352–#357). It excludes ledger-closed directions: generator-identity replay effects (PR #8), effect-class distillation over reward replay (PR #13), offline IS/DR log ranking without beating unweighted likelihood gain (PR #14), surprisingly-popular fleet pooling H1/H1b (PR #23), lesson-only implicit ΔW cosine (PR #28), teacher-refresh EMA schedules (CGTR `2606.03532`), and edit-site selectors that do not beat random-site + covariance-aware operators.

**Shared first-test contract.** All tests below are proposals for tiny CPU-first models (tabular/MLP/tiny transformers or constrained rule-routing agents); no experiments were run. Establish learner $L$ on old tasks $A$ and $B$ ($B$ rare) before introducing foreign models, checkpoints, logs, or upgrades for new task $C$ (and $D$ where sequential drift matters). Compare every candidate against the named equal-budget control at matched queries, environment/verifier calls, update steps, and stored bytes (charging translators, probes, and metadata honestly). Every card's old-task retention check evaluates held-out, untouched $A$ and $B$ inputs through the live learner after learning from the foreign source—without oracle routing or checkpoint rollback—and rejects any purported gain on $C$ that drops $A$ or $B$ beyond a predeclared tolerance.

## A · Learning from other models' weights and checkpoints

### LM01 · Relative capability-diff transfer from a foreign base/fine-tune pair
- **Mechanism:** Given an external fine-tuned model $M_1$ and its ancestor $M_0$ (a different architecture or family from learner $L$), extract a *behavioral difference witness*—a compact set of minimal-change inputs and logit-shift deltas where $M_1$ diverges from $M_0$—and apply *only that relative shift* to $L$, instead of distilling $M_1$'s full function.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Diff Mining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26462) extracts finetuning objectives from logit differences; [Cross-Architecture Model Diffing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11729) finds internal feature diffs; [Transporting Task Vectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12952) transports weight deltas via alignment. None compiles a foreign $(M_0, M_1)$ pair into a *relative behavioral patch* applied to an unrelated continual learner $L$ while leaving $L$'s non-diff regions untouched.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two distinct tiny MLP/transformer families: $M_0 \to M_1$ learns new rule $C$ while $M_0$ and $L$ disagree on tie-breaks for old tasks $A/B$. Compare full distillation from $M_1$, cross-architecture task-vector transport, and diff-only transfer at equal queries and gradient steps. Kill if full $M_1$ distillation + equal-budget $A/B$ replay ties on $C$ accuracy and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $L$'s held-out $A/B$ tasks—specifically inputs where $M_0$ and $L$ originally disagreed, which full distillation from $M_1$ silently overwrites.

### LM02 · Negative-checkpoint repulsion from aborted and reward-hacked runs
- **Mechanism:** Extract a *functional repulsion boundary* from another model's failed, diverged, or overfitted checkpoints (e.g., mid-RL reward-hacking snapshots or collapsed fine-tunes): identify the low-rank output-space directions where the failed checkpoint gained proxy score while losing held-out validity, and constrain the learner's updates to stay orthogonal to that functional trajectory.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Agentic-OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12799) recovers *useful* forgotten skills from discarded RL checkpoints; [ReflectRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03972) and [Co-Evolving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22254) use failed *trajectories* as hard negatives. Treating *aborted foreign checkpoints themselves* as negative functional geometry to steer a different learner away from known failure basins is untried.
- **Cheapest first test:** Synthetic RL/classification stream $C$ with a known shortcut/reward-hack basin; collect 3 failed checkpoints from a peer run and test whether functional-trajectory repulsion prevents shortcut collapse better than negative-trajectory replay at equal samples. Kill if trajectory-level negative replay or standard KL-to-base regularization ties.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that repelling the shortcut basin does not degrade legitimate old $A/B$ behaviors that share surface features with the shortcut.

### LM03 · Checkpoint-trajectory curriculum inversion
- **Mechanism:** Given intermediate training checkpoints $M^{(0)}, M^{(1)}, \dots, M^{(T)}$ from another model that mastered a compositional skill $C$, measure when sub-skills became functionally sufficient along $M$'s trajectory and compile that emergence order into a staged distillation schedule for learner $L$, rather than distilling directly from the final checkpoint $M^{(T)}$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [When Do Concepts Become Functionally Sufficient?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15323) measures emergence timing; [HOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25391) distills from historical checkpoints for retention; [QAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35168) merges checkpoints along a run. Turning a foreign model's saved training trajectory into an *automatic prerequisite curriculum* for continual acquisition is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compositional algorithmic task (multi-step modular arithmetic or chained string rules) with saved checkpoints from a small teacher; compare distilling from $M^{(T)}$ alone, uniform checkpoint soup, and emergence-ordered staged distillation at equal total updates. Kill if $M^{(T)}$ alone matches sample efficiency and retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy after each curriculum stage to test whether staged ingestion causes less representational shock than direct final-checkpoint distillation.

### LM04 · Functional surgery via cross-model sparse-feature transplant
- **Mechanism:** Train a cross-architecture crosscoder on a shared unlabeled corpus to align sparse latent features between specialist donor $M$ and learner $L$; localize the minimal feature set causally necessary for $M$'s new skill $C$, and synthesize a targeted write in $L$ that installs *only* the aligned feature-to-logit contribution without importing $M$'s dense weights or full output distribution.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [CNT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18449) transfers safety neurons across LLMs; [Localizing RL-Induced Tool Use to a Single Crosscoder Feature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26474) and [Delta-Crosscoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04426) localize skills in crosscoder space; [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638) reuses LoRAs across backbones. Combining crosscoder localization with causal write-in to a *different* continual learner is the proposed operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two small differently-initialized or different-width MLPs/transformers; donor $M$ learns $C$, localize top-$k$ causal crosscoder features, and transplant into $L$. Compare against behavioral distillation from $M$ and CNT-style neuron matching at equal unlabeled data and compute. Kill if behavioral distillation dominates the $C$-gain vs $A/B$-retention frontier.
- **Old-task retention:** Check $A/B$ inputs that activate neighboring (non-transplanted) crosscoder features to confirm localized isolation.

### LM05 · Subliminal-channel quarantine during cross-model distillation
- **Mechanism:** Before distilling from a foreign model $M$ on task $C$, run a *null-domain entanglement probe* measuring whether distilling $M$'s outputs on semantically unrelated carrier prompts shifts $L$'s priors. Decompose $M$'s supervision into task-relevant logit margin versus carrier-transmissible preference drift, and project out the carrier component during continual updates.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Subliminal Learning in Open-Weight Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12586) shows teachers transmit hidden behavioral traits through unrelated data; [Privileged Context as Drift in OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07842) diagnoses drift in self-distillation. Turning subliminal transmission into a *write-time quarantine filter* for continual cross-model ingestion is new.
- **Cheapest first test:** Same-family and cross-family tiny LMs where teacher $M$ carries a planted hidden tie-break preference alongside skill $C$; compare standard OPD/SFT distillation, temperature scaling, and carrier-projected distillation. Kill if quarantine fails to block hidden trait transfer without hurting $C$ accuracy.
- **Old-task retention:** Test $L$'s held-out old tasks $A/B$—especially ambiguous or tie-break old queries where subliminal drift appears first.

### LM06 · Weight-space museum: indexing foreign checkpoints by behavioral residual
- **Mechanism:** Maintain an archive of heterogeneous external specialist checkpoints encoded by their *functional residual* relative to $L$ via a permutation-invariant weight/activation encoder; retrieve and surgically ingest a foreign checkpoint only when $L$'s live error residual matches that checkpoint's competence signature.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [WeightCLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03551) aligns weights and datasets; [Interfaces and Modules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21995) and [Graph Metanetworks for Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18471) build scalable weight encoders; [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) probes LoRAs before merging. Indexing a *heterogeneous checkpoint pool by the learner's live error residual* to trigger just-in-time continual assimilation is the new primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Pool of 20 small specialist checkpoints (several redundant or corrupted); stream tasks to $L$ and compare validation-loss probing, random selection, and residual-indexed retrieval + ingestion at equal probe budget. Kill if a small fixed behavioral probe table ties on retrieval precision and retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Confirm that selective residual-matched ingestion causes less $A/B$ forgetting than periodic multi-checkpoint merging (e.g., [ChainLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00431) or uniform soup).

### LM07 · Simplicity-bottleneck gating via a quantized or pruned sibling
- **Mechanism:** Pair full-precision continual learner $L$ with a capacity-bottlenecked sibling $M_{\text{small}}$ (quantized or pruned) trained on the same new stream $C$. Gate $L$'s weight updates by the *agreement* with $M_{\text{small}}$'s learned generalization—treating the weaker sibling's capacity bottleneck as a structural filter that separates compressible rule structure from memorizable spurious cues.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Heterogeneous Asymmetric Contrastive Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10672) distills across families; [On Emergent Capabilities and Model Merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24504) studies merging effects on emergent skills. Using a *weaker compressed peer* as a live simplicity filter to govern what a stronger continual learner commits is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Noisy-label + shortcut stream $C$ on a small MLP/LM; compare standard SGD, weight decay/label smoothing, and bottleneck-sibling gating at equal FLOPs. Kill if standard regularization or early stopping matches both $C$ OOD generalization and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy after noisy $C$ bursts, and verify bottleneck gating does not block legitimate rare old-task facts ($B$).

## B · Learning from other agents' logs, traces, and failures

### LM08 · Counterfactual decision-point extraction from foreign agent logs
- **Mechanism:** Instead of imitating a foreign agent's full trajectory log (which mixes its tool habits and prompt style with the solution), step through the foreign log and keep *only* the sparse decision points where $L$'s own policy would choose a different action that leads to a lower verified outcome. Update $L$ exclusively on those minimal learner-relative forks.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Trajectory-Refined Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08432) and [Correcting or Continuing?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18011) study student-rollout OPD; [Localized Context Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21427) localizes context distillation. Compressing an *arbitrary foreign agent's off-policy logs* into learner-relative minimal counterfactual forks is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Finite-state tool-routing world where a foreign agent with a different action-ordering style solves $C$; compare full-log SFT, random step subsampling, and learner-relative fork updates at equal gradient steps. Kill if full-log SFT or uniform subsampling ties on $C$ transfer and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Test $A/B$ tasks sharing prefixes with $C$ to verify $L$ kept its native action-ordering habits on old tasks.

### LM09 · Failure-boundary compilation from foreign crash logs
- **Mechanism:** Ingest *only failed* execution logs from other agents (no winning trajectories): cluster the last verifiable invariant violated before failure and compile a *forbidden-transition guard*—a lightweight precondition veto carrying an explicit falsification condition—that prunes doomed branches before $L$ ever attempts the new task.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [ReflectRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03972) and [Co-Evolving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22254) train on failed trajectories as negative RL targets; the idea ledger warns that untested prohibitions self-seal (hot-stove effect). Compiling verifiable *invariant-violation guards* with falsification hooks from foreign crash logs avoids both blind imitation and self-sealing text rules.
- **Cheapest first test:** Constrained state-machine environment where foreign agents fail on 5 distinct precondition errors; compare learning from scratch, DPO/negative-log-likelihood on failed logs, and invariant-guard compilation at equal trials. Kill if negative-log-likelihood on the same failed logs matches sample efficiency and safety.
- **Old-task retention:** Test old $A/B$ workflows whose actions superficially resemble the foreign agent's failing actions to ensure zero false vetoes.

### LM10 · Cross-agent reasoning-trace de-stylization via state-skeleton projection
- **Mechanism:** Before learning from another model's chain-of-thought or agent trace logs, project each trace onto its canonical *executable state-transition skeleton* (variable bindings, tool outputs, subgoal state changes), discard the foreign model's verbal scaffolding, and let $L$ regenerate and train on transitions expressed in $L$'s *own* native reasoning dialect.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Off-Policy Merging Beats OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05872) and [On the Off-Policy Teacher in OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38360) document off-policy distribution mismatch from foreign teachers; [HERD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31401) evolves harnesses recursively. Projecting foreign logs into a dialect-free state skeleton and regenerating native-policy steps addresses off-policy mismatch directly.
- **Cheapest first test:** Program-trace/arithmetic task where teacher $M$ uses verbose reverse-order CoT and learner $L$ uses terse forward-order CoT (learned on $A/B$); compare raw trace SFT, OPD, and skeleton-projected native distillation. Kill if raw trace SFT or OPD matches $C$ accuracy and $A/B$ dialect retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $L$'s $A/B$ accuracy and native-format consistency to detect whether foreign verbal habits bled into old tasks.

### LM11 · Abstention and deferral-log inheritance
- **Mechanism:** Learn from *when* another model or agent abstained, deferred, or invoked an external verifier—decoupled from its actual answers. Import the foreign agent's calibrated deferral boundary on new domain $C$ as an initial safety/uncertainty prior for $L$, even when the foreign agent's answers are outdated or architecturally incompatible.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Cascading routers dispatch between fixed models; [Introspection Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16812) report learned behaviors. Transferring *only the deferral geometry* from foreign logs to bootstrap safe continual exploration in a new domain is untried.
- **Cheapest first test:** New domain $C$ with high-cost irreversible errors; supply $L$ with a foreign agent's defer/act logs (no answers). Compare $L$'s base entropy threshold, full-answer imitation, and deferral-prior transfer at equal costly-error budget. Kill if $L$'s own calibrated entropy threshold ties.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that importing $C$'s deferral boundary does not trigger over-abstention on $L$'s already-mastered old tasks $A/B$.

### LM12 · Cross-model log attribution: separating foreign skill from environment luck
- **Mechanism:** Before ingesting another agent's successful log, run a minimal *environment-replay ablation* (replaying logged actions under perturbed non-essential context or tool timing) to partition the log's success into *policy skill* versus *accidental environment/prompt luck*, admitting only policy-attributable steps into $L$'s continual update.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** The idea ledger cites Lessons from Luck (`2610.33029`) and ExpeL contrast on same-agent logs; [CARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02839) grounds self-improvement in production logs. Extending counterfactual replay attribution specifically to *opaque third-party agent logs* counters survivor bias unique to learning from unknown exploration policies.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stochastic tool-use simulator where 50% of foreign successful logs succeeded due to lucky transient API state; compare ingesting all successful logs, reward-weighted logs, and replay-attributed logs at equal compute. Kill if simple multi-run consistency filtering ties.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate $A/B$ tool-selection accuracy after ingesting noisy foreign logs to confirm spurious action–context bindings did not corrupt old skills.

### LM13 · Silent-divergence mining from parallel shadow logs
- **Mechanism:** When learner $L$ and a foreign model $M$ both score the same live unlabeled stream, log *only* inputs where $L$ and $M$ have high confidence in *mutually contradictory* answers, and spend a small verification budget exclusively on those collisions to decide whether to adopt $M$'s rule, keep $L$'s, or fork context.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Query-by-committee samples general disagreement; [Simple LLM Baselines for Model Diffing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10371) uses disagreement for auditing. Using *high-confidence mutual contradiction* as a continual write gate—explicitly separating "$M$ knows something new" from "$M$ is wrong on my old task"—is the proposed operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Stream mixing $A/B$ (where $L$ is right and $M$ is confidently wrong) and $C$ (where $M$ is right and $L$ is confidently wrong). Compare uniform verification, entropy sampling, and high-confidence-collision allocation at equal verifier calls. Kill if QBC/entropy ties on $C$ acquisition + $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy specifically on inputs where $M$ confidently contradicts $L$ (verifying $L$ does not capitulate on old tasks).

### LM14 · Log-to-unit-test synthesis for behavioral regression transfer
- **Mechanism:** Instead of distilling a foreign agent's logs into weights or memory directly, compile its successful and failing logs into an *executable behavioral specification* (input–assertion pairs) and let $L$ self-train via on-policy search/RL against that spec—guaranteeing adopted behaviors are expressible in $L$'s own weights and toolset.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Communicative OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23673) and [EvoOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06542) distill black-box teachers via communication/privileged prompts; [Skill Transfer: Strong to Weak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18624) adapts text skill bundles. Compiling foreign logs into *executable behavioral specs* decouples *what* to achieve from *how* the foreign agent achieved it.
- **Cheapest first test:** Code/string-transformation stream where foreign agent $M$ solves $C$ using a helper library $L$ lacks; compare direct log SFT, black-box OPD, and spec-compiled on-policy self-training at equal compute. Kill if black-box OPD ties on $C$ solve rate and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Run $L$'s pre-existing $A/B$ unit-test suite alongside the newly compiled $C$ specs after every update.

## C · Cross-architecture, cross-tokenizer, and latent-space ingestion

### LM15 · Continual KV-cache assimilation across heterogeneous models
- **Mechanism:** Use a learned cross-model KV-cache translator to receive a foreign model's encoded context/reasoning state for new task $C$, then *consolidate* that translated KV state directly into a persistent low-rank weight update in $L$—bypassing discrete text serialization and re-prefill.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Swarm Telepathy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15095), [KV-Lingo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32610), [CacheBridge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00891), [CacheLink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15509), and [Cross-Model KV Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03893) transfer KV caches strictly for *inference-time prefill reuse*. Consolidating a translated foreign KV cache into *persistent continual weights* is untried.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two distinct tiny transformers (different width/depth) on a multi-rule context task: donor $M$ encodes $C$ into KV, translator maps KV to $L$, and $L$ consolidates into a small LoRA. Compare against text-summary transfer + context distillation at equal bandwidth and updates. Kill if text-summary distillation ties on $C$ accuracy and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate $L$'s old $A/B$ tasks with empty context after consolidating 5 sequential translated KV caches from $M$.

### LM16 · Residual-stream scaffold bridge with reversible detachment
- **Mechanism:** Temporarily graft a foreign specialist's middle layers onto $L$ via a lightweight linear/low-rank stitching bridge during $C$'s early deployment, log the bridge's live functional contribution, and progressively internalize that contribution into $L$'s native layers until the bridge's effect anneals to zero and the foreign model detaches with no accuracy cliff.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Model stitching measures static representation similarity; [Dense Latent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13594) and [Dual-Cache Latent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20617) keep models coupled at runtime; [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638) reuses LoRAs. A *temporary cross-model scaffold bridge built for progressive internalization and clean detachment* is a distinct continual-learning operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two small MLPs/transformers; graft specialist $M$'s hidden block into $L$ for task $C$, then anneal the bridge to zero while distilling its residual into $L$'s native block. Compare against direct behavioral distillation from $M$ at equal compute. Kill if direct distillation matches $C$ accuracy and $A/B$ retention without scaffolding.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $A/B$ both *while* the bridge is attached (checking for interference) and *immediately after* detachment (checking for a cliff).

### LM17 · Boundary-gated cross-tokenizer continual distillation
- **Mechanism:** When learning from a foreign model $M$ with a different tokenizer, align supervision at *semantic span / character-boundary checkpoints* via common-support probability transport, and gate updates on spans whose subword splits in $L$ collide with high-frequency subwords anchoring $L$'s old tasks $A/B$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [CSD: Common-Support Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28895) handles one-shot cross-tokenizer KD. Continual cross-tokenizer ingestion introduces subword-collision forgetting across tasks, which static KD does not address.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two tiny BPE-variant LMs with mismatched vocabularies; stream domain $C$ from $M$ into $L$. Compare text-only SFT, ungated CSD, and boundary-gated continual CSD at equal token budget. Kill if text-only SFT or ungated CSD ties on $C$ gain and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate old $A/B$ inputs specifically containing subwords whose boundaries overlap with $C$'s newly distilled spans.

### LM18 · Shared-concept-anchor Rosetta layer for multi-family skill import
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a fixed, small set of *anchor activations* on a shared unlabeled anchor set inside $L$. Any foreign donor $M$ projects its new skill delta into anchor-relative coordinates, and $L$ imports the skill by inverting its *own* anchor map—allowing sequential imports from unrelated donor architectures with zero pairwise $M \leftrightarrow L$ training.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Relative representations enable zero-shot static stitching; [Transporting Task Vectors across Architectures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12952) transports one task vector via alignment. Extending anchor-relative coordinates to *sequential continual skill imports across multiple unrelated donor families* while keeping $L$'s anchor geometry invariant is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Three distinct tiny architectures ($M_1, M_2, M_3$) each trained on one new task ($C_1, C_2, C_3$); import sequentially into $L$ via anchor-relative coordinates versus pairwise linear bridges versus behavioral distillation at equal unlabeled-anchor + query budget. Kill if behavioral distillation beats anchor-relative import.
- **Old-task retention:** Track both $A/B$ accuracy and anchor-coordinate drift in $L$ after 3 sequential cross-family imports (testing whether anchor drift breaks later imports).

### LM19 · Cross-model composable soft-prompt modules with usage-triggered consolidation
- **Mechanism:** Allow a foreign specialist $M$ to emit a compact translated soft-prompt module representing its sub-skill $C$, and train $L$ to compose $M$'s module with $L$'s own previously learned soft-prompt modules ($A, B$) at inference time—consolidating the composed behavior into $L$'s weights only when the foreign module crosses a sustained usage threshold.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation of RL Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27376) distills *same-base* RL experts into composable soft prompts; [Dense Latent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13594) passes continuous thoughts across heterogeneous models at runtime. Combining *cross-model soft-prompt composition* with *usage-triggered continual consolidation* bridges delegation and internalization.
- **Cheapest first test:** Compositional multi-skill benchmark on two tiny transformers: $L$ holds $A/B$ soft prompts, $M$ supplies $C$'s translated soft prompt; test zero-shot composition ($A+C, B+C$) and post-consolidation accuracy vs text delegation + SFT. Kill if text delegation + SFT ties at equal compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify $A$, $B$, and $A+B$ compositions remain intact both during foreign soft-prompt composition and after $C$ consolidates into $L$.

### LM20 · Subspace-scoped cross-architecture representation alignment
- **Mechanism:** Rather than aligning $L$'s full layer activations to a foreign teacher $M$ (which overwrites $L$'s old-task geometry), estimate $M$'s minimal *task-active subspace* on $C$ and align $L$ to $M$ *only* within that subspace while pinning the orthogonal complement to $L$'s pre-update representations.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Feature distillation / CKA alignment align whole layers; [ChainLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00431) and [Global Singular Subspace Separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10881) separate weight subspaces within a *single* backbone. Subspace-scoped *cross-architecture* representation alignment with orthogonal-complement pinning is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Different-width MLPs/transformers; align $L$ to teacher $M$ on $C$ using full-layer CKA/MSE alignment, logit-only KD, and task-manifold alignment with orthogonal pinning. Kill if logit-only KD or full-layer alignment matches the $C$-gain vs $A/B$-retention frontier.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ accuracy and $A/B$ representational CKA drift in $L$ before and after ingesting 3 sequential foreign teachers.

## D · Active interrogation, peer complementarity, and multi-teacher arbitration

### LM21 · Active counterfactual boundary-probing of a black-box peer
- **Mechanism:** Treat an API-only black-box peer $M$ (no weights or logits) as an *active oracle*: $L$ synthesizes minimal counterfactual queries straddling its own decision frontiers to locate *where $M$'s boundary differs from $L$'s*, spending API queries exclusively on separating hypotheses rather than sampling i.i.d. completions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Black-Box OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10643), [EvoOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06542), and [Communicative OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23673) distill black-box teachers on task prompts; model extraction clones whole models. *Continual boundary-diff probing* that extracts only the delta from a black-box peer while preserving $L$'s non-conflicting old boundaries is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Small black-box classifier/rule-router $M$ differing from $L$ on 2 of 8 decision regions; compare i.i.d. black-box distillation, uncertainty sampling, and counterfactual boundary-diff probing at equal API queries. Kill if uncertainty sampling ties on $C$ accuracy and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Check the 6 unchanged decision regions ($A/B$) after probing and updating on the 2 changed regions.

### LM22 · Invariant-gated token-level peer arbitration
- **Mechanism:** When continually learning from multiple heterogeneous peers $M_1, \dots, M_k$ (each strong on one domain and noisy elsewhere), gate token-level supervision by *both* peer competence and proximity to $L$'s verified old-task invariants—rejecting a peer's token targets wherever they conflict with $L$'s anchored old skills.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Compatibility Matters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21234) selects teachers at the token level in OPD; [MOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02746) distills multiple domain experts jointly. Conditioning token-level teacher selection on *old-task invariant protection* is the continual-learning extension.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two domain teachers ($M_1$ knows $C_1$ but breaks $A$; $M_2$ knows $C_2$ but breaks $B$); compare uniform MOPD, teacher-confidence routing, and invariant-gated token selection at equal teacher calls. Kill if confidence routing or standard MOPD ties on $A/B$ retention at matched $C_1/C_2$ gain.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate $A$ and $B$ specifically on prompts sharing high-frequency prefixes/tokens with $C_1$ and $C_2$.

### LM23 · Cross-lineage adversarial falsification before write admission
- **Mechanism:** Before committing a candidate update distilled from peer $M_1$, invoke an *independent-lineage* peer $M_2$ (different pretraining/architecture) as an automated falsifier to search for inputs where the candidate update regresses an old skill or exploits a shortcut shared by $M_1$. Admit the write only after surviving or patching $M_2$'s counterexamples.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Persistent Negatives for Adversarial Black-Box OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30864) uses an adversary to match teacher style; [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) runs static probes. Using an *independent-lineage peer* as a live red-teamer to catch shared-lineage blind spots before write admission is the proposed operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Learner $L$ learns $C$ from peer $M_1$ (carrying a planted blind spot on a slice of $A$); compare no gate, self-probing by $L$, and cross-family probing by $M_2$ at equal inference calls. Kill if $L$'s own self-probing catches the same fraction of regressions.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure regression on the planted blind-spot slice of $A$ alongside ordinary held-out $A/B$ tasks.

### LM24 · Cross-peer triangulation for compositional transcendence
- **Mechanism:** When no single peer solves compositional task $C = f \circ g$, query partial specialists $M_1$ (strong on $f$, weak on $g$) and $M_2$ (strong on $g$, weak on $f$), detect which intermediate sub-steps each peer is locally self-consistent on, stitch verified sub-solutions into a composite target, and distill the stitched trace into $L$—so $L$ strictly beats both $M_1$ and $M_2$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [A Taxonomy of Transcendence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17669) analyzes when training on mixed suboptimal data transcends sources; [SPADE-KD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24013) performs heterogeneous peer KD. An explicit *continual triangulation operator* that stitches partial peer competences into one learner surpassing every donor while retaining old skills is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Two-stage compositional task ($f \circ g$) with two specialists ($M_1$: 95% on $f$, 20% on $g$; $M_2$: reverse); compare mixture distillation, best-of-peers reranking, and consistency-stitched triangulation at equal peer calls. Kill if mixture distillation achieves the same transcendence margin.
- **Old-task retention:** Score $L$'s original single-stage tasks $A/B$ to confirm compositional stitching does not degrade direct single-step execution.

### LM25 · Peer-anchored continual unlearning via naive-model restoration
- **Mechanism:** When revising or unlearning an obsolete rule from $A$ while learning replacement $C$, use an unadapted/naive peer $M_{\text{clean}}$ (which never learned the obsolete rule) as a *localized restoration anchor*: pull $L$ toward $M_{\text{clean}}$ strictly on the obsolete neighborhood while preserving $L$'s divergence from $M_{\text{clean}}$ on all still-valid old tasks $B$.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Standard unlearning (GA, NPO, RMU) pushes weights away blindly, damaging nearby skills; [CV-OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13264) uses contrastive distillation. Using a *naive peer that never saw the obsolete fact* as a localized functional restoration target is a clean continual-revision primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train $L$ on $A$ (obsolete) + $B$ (still valid), then revise $A \to C$ using $M_{\text{clean}}$ (never trained on $A$ or $B$) on $A$'s neighborhood; compare plain overwrite on $C$, NPO unlearning, and peer-anchored restoration. Kill if plain overwrite or NPO matches $C$ accuracy, $B$ retention, and zero $A$ leakage.
- **Old-task retention:** Check both $B$ (must stay intact) and paraphrased $A$ (must be genuinely erased, not resurface).

### LM26 · Probe-verifiable capability-card handshake between heterogeneous agents
- **Mechanism:** Equip each model/agent with a calibrated *capability card*—an introspective summary plus a compact failure-boundary probe set emitted by an introspection adapter—advertising what it learned since its base, where it is reliable, and which domains it perturbs. Learner $L$ screens peers' cards *before* querying or distilling, turning peer discovery into a cheap metadata handshake backed by spot verification.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Introspection Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16812) train LLMs to report learned behaviors for auditing; [Audit Before You Merge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10684) probes adapters externally. Using *self-reported, spot-verifiable capability cards* as a continual peer-selection protocol is the new object.
- **Cheapest first test:** Pool of 12 diverse peer models (2 relevant to $C$, 2 miscalibrated/adversarial); compare exhaustive behavioral probing, random peer trial, and card-screened selection + spot checks at equal total calls. Kill if small-probe screening ties at equal query cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify that spot-checked capability cards filter out peers whose updates would conflict with $L$'s old $A/B$ domains.

## E · Predecessors, successors, and cross-generational continual handoff

### LM27 · Tandem-oversight handoff from a weak adapted predecessor to a strong successor
- **Mechanism:** When upgrading from a continually adapted weak predecessor $M_{\text{old}}$ (rich in deployment skills $A/B$) to a stronger fresh base $M_{\text{new}}$, run *tandem token-level interleaving* during handoff: $M_{\text{old}}$ takes control on known deployment-edge tokens while $M_{\text{new}}$ generates the bulk, and $M_{\text{new}}$ continually distills the tandem trajectory—inheriting $M_{\text{old}}$'s hard-won edge habits without capping $M_{\text{new}}$'s reasoning ceiling.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Tandem Training for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13551) interleaves weak and strong models for oversight/interpretability; weak-to-strong generalization studies static label transfer. Using *tandem interleaving as a continual predecessor-to-successor upgrade operator* is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Weak adapted $M_{\text{old}}$ (knows $A/B$ quirks, weak on reasoning $C$) + strong fresh $M_{\text{new}}$ (strong on $C$, blind to $A/B$ quirks). Compare full distillation from $M_{\text{old}}$, log SFT, and tandem-interleaved handoff at equal steps. Kill if log SFT or full distillation preserves $M_{\text{new}}$'s $C$ ceiling equally well while matching $A/B$ inheritance.
- **Old-task retention:** Score *both* $M_{\text{old}}$'s inherited $A/B$ quirks and $M_{\text{new}}$'s pre-existing strong $C$ capabilities (neither may drop).

### LM28 · Portable skill bequest: training a predecessor to be cheap for an unknown successor to inherit
- **Mechanism:** While continual learner $M_{\text{old}}$ learns tasks $A, B, C$ during its lifetime, add a *bequest regularizer*: maintain a compact model-agnostic teaching packet (minimal pairs + boundary witnesses) and penalize updates that a small held-out proxy architecture cannot reconstruct from the packet within $K$ steps. When an unseen successor architecture $M_{\text{new}}$ later arrives, it inherits $M_{\text{old}}$'s lifetime in $K$ steps.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Dataset condensation condenses a *fixed dataset*; `root-collective-agents` #31 rewrites a shared text library for a same-family newcomer; [Skill Transfer: Strong to Weak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18624) adapts skills post-hoc. Regularizing a *living continual learner's trajectory* for *cross-architecture successor inheritability* (tested on an architecture unseen during $M_{\text{old}}$'s life) is the proposed primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Train $M_{\text{old}}$ (MLP) on 5 sequential tasks with and without bequest regularization (using a linear/small-MLP proxy); hand off to an unseen architecture $M_{\text{new}}$ (tiny transformer or gated net) with fixed $K$ steps. Kill if a post-hoc equal-byte coreset from $M_{\text{old}}$'s stream ties on $M_{\text{new}}$'s inheritance speed.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify bequest regularization does not hurt $M_{\text{old}}$'s own $A/B$ retention during life, and that $M_{\text{new}}$ retains its pre-handoff skills after absorbing the bequest.

### LM29 · Asymmetric strong-to-weak down-compilation with explicit capability-gap escalation
- **Mechanism:** When a smaller continual learner $L_{\text{weak}}$ learns new skill $C$ from a frontier model $M_{\text{strong}}$, partition $C$ into (i) sub-cases $L_{\text{weak}}$ can represent natively and (ii) the *irreducible capability gap* where $L_{\text{weak}}$'s capacity fails, compiling the gap into a lightweight escalation/tool trigger instead of forcing $L_{\text{weak}}$ to overfit unrepresentable targets (which erases $L_{\text{weak}}$'s old skills).
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Skill Transfer: Strong to Weak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18624) adapts textual agent skills from strong to weak agents; [Denser ≠ Better](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01763) shows capacity-mismatched OPD causes forgetting. Explicitly *splitting foreign-teacher ingestion into native internalization + compiled gap escalation* to prevent capacity-overload forgetting is the new operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Strong teacher teaching a tiny bottlenecked student $L_{\text{weak}}$ a mixed easy+hard task $C$ while $L_{\text{weak}}$ holds $A/B$. Compare full distillation, loss-clipped distillation, and native+escalation compilation at equal escalation rate. Kill if loss-clipped distillation + entropy deferral ties on $C$ utility and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure $A/B$ collapse in $L_{\text{weak}}$, directly testing whether fitting the unrepresentable hard slice of $C$ is what drives $A/B$ forgetting.

### LM30 · Predecessor-delta triage across a base-model upgrade
- **Mechanism:** When upgrading from adapted model $M_{\text{old}} = B_0 + \Delta_0$ to a new base $B_1$, do not blindly migrate $\Delta_0$. Evaluate $B_1$ on $\Delta_0$'s behavioral witness set to partition $\Delta_0$ into: (a) *already absorbed* by $B_1$ (drop), (b) *still needed* (migrate), and (c) *contradicted by $B_1$'s improved knowledge* (flag for verification). Migrate only (b), preventing stale-workaround overrides and double-dosing.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18638) and [Transporting Task Vectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12952) transport the entire adapter/vector across bases; [Harness-Model Collaboration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34724) and NOOA #4 note the base-upgrade frontier. Triaging a predecessor's learned delta into *absorbed / needed / contradicted* prior to migration is the candidate primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** Base $B_0$ adapted with 20 rules ($A/B$); upgraded base $B_1$ natively knows 10, improves on 4 (where $B_0$'s rule was a workaround), and lacks 6. Compare full $\Delta_0$ transport/distillation versus triaged migration at equal probe budget. Kill if naive OPD onto $B_1$ ties on $B_1$'s improved tasks + inherited tasks.
- **Old-task retention:** Score all three partitions (10 absorbed, 4 improved, 6 still-needed) plus unrelated $B_1$ capabilities after migration.

### LM31 · Reverse-generational back-porting from a live successor into a legacy model
- **Mechanism:** When a lightweight legacy model $M_{\text{old}}$ must keep serving (edge/latency budget) alongside a heavy new successor $M_{\text{new}}$, capture *only* the sparse error delta where $M_{\text{new}}$ succeeds on cases $M_{\text{old}}$ failed and back-port those targeted fixes into $M_{\text{old}}$ as a rolling residual patch—turning the successor into an error-driven continual tutor for its predecessor.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** Speculative decoding and online distillation pair small and large models; [Continually Post-training Small Models via Synthetic Self-distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13113) uses self-distillation. Framing *asymmetric error-gated back-porting from a live successor into a continually-serving legacy model*—preserving legacy-calibrated behaviors under tight capacity—is the proposed operation.
- **Cheapest first test:** Tiny legacy $M_{\text{old}}$ (serving $A/B$) paired with larger successor $M_{\text{new}}$ on shifting stream $C$; compare periodic full distillation from $M_{\text{new}}$, error-triggered KD, and residual-patch back-porting at equal update steps. Kill if standard error-triggered KD ties on $M_{\text{old}}$'s $C$ gain and $A/B$ retention.
- **Old-task retention:** Evaluate $M_{\text{old}}$'s latency-critical old $A/B$ tasks, including cases where $M_{\text{old}}$'s calibrated behavior intentionally differs from $M_{\text{new}}$.

### LM32 · Lineage-aware behavioral contract for continual model replacement
- **Mechanism:** Represent a continual learner's persistent identity *not* as a parameter tensor, but as a compact, executable *behavioral contract* (invariant input–output inequalities, rare-task witnesses, and failure-mode prohibitions) handed from generation $G_t$ to $G_{t+1}$. Any candidate successor (different architecture, vendor, or fine-tune) replaces $G_t$ only by passing the contract and extending it with new witnesses from its own deployment.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Continual Learning in Transition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06216) and [Harness Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19013) shift CL beyond parameters; `root-collective-agents` #24 proposes a living shared exam for a symmetric fleet. Making the *contract itself* the persistent continual-learning object that survives complete architecture swaps across 3+ generations is the overarching primitive.
- **Cheapest first test:** 4-generation sequence of *different* tiny architectures ($G_0$ linear $\to G_1$ MLP $\to G_2$ gated MLP $\to G_3$ tiny transformer), adding one new task per generation; compare naive behavioral distillation, random replay handoff, and minimal-contract + witness-guided handoff at equal byte budget. Kill if an equal-byte random replay buffer ties across all 4 generations.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure generation-0 ($A$) and generation-1 ($B$, including a rare slice) accuracy after generation-3 replacement.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can a continual learner import only what a foreign model *changed* without importing who the foreign model *is*? (LM01, LM04, LM05, LM10)**
   Formalize *relative behavioral transport*: given a foreign pair $(M_0, M_1)$ and an unrelated learner $L$, characterize when $L$ can acquire $M_1 - M_0$ without inheriting $M_0$'s priors, reasoning dialect, or subliminal traits. Start with two different tiny transformer families that disagree on $A/B$ tie-breaks while $M_1$ adds $C$. Drop the line if full distillation from $M_1$ plus equal-budget $A/B$ replay matches both $C$ acquisition and zero tie-break/subliminal drift.
2. **Do failed, intermediate, or weaker models teach things a strong final teacher cannot? (LM02, LM03, LM07, LM09, LM25)**
   Test whether *non-optimal* external models—aborted reward-hacked checkpoints, emergence-ordered intermediate snapshots, capacity-bottlenecked siblings, foreign crash logs, and naive unadapted peers—carry causal information (repulsion boundaries, prerequisite curricula, simplicity filters, clean unlearning anchors) that strictly beats distilling from the strongest final teacher at equal compute and samples.
3. **When does latent or structural ingestion beat black-box text/logit distillation across heterogeneous models? (LM15, LM16, LM17, LM18, LM19, LM20)**
   With cross-model KV-cache translators and cross-architecture crosscoders now available, test whether consolidating translated latent state, aligned sparse features, or reversible scaffold bridges into persistent weights yields a genuine retention–plasticity advantage over text/logit distillation once translator training, anchor drift, and storage bytes are honestly charged.
4. **What is the minimal state that must cross a full base-model or architecture replacement? (LM27, LM28, LM29, LM30, LM32)**
   Compare weight/adapter transport, predecessor-delta triage, tandem-oversight handoff, and bequest-regularized behavioral contracts across a multi-generation sequence of *different* architectures. Identify the smallest generational handoff object that preserves rare deployment skills across 3+ complete model swaps without capping each successor's new capabilities.
