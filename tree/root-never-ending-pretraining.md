# Root · Never-ending pretraining

**Question.** What can one training lineage preserve, transfer, or measure while the corpus, tokenizer, model shape, and usable knowledge frontier change on different clocks? A learner here is one continuously updated next-token model. Shadow branches below are experimental controls, never a serving population. The stream is exogenous; the model neither changes the world that generates it ([learner–world](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/363)) nor invents tasks ([open-ended](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/384)). Inference does not update weights or retrieve evidence ([test-time](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/373)); no skills migrate between deployed learners ([population](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/379)).

**Bar.** The 34 cards are ambitious-but-doable **primitive candidates**, not novelty verdicts or experiment results. Read the [ledger](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/f4cad623b5d976a6369c7bd1e69d267a5f0288bf/reviews/idea-ledger.md) and [triage](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/f4cad623b5d976a6369c7bd1e69d267a5f0288bf/reviews/flood-triage.md) first: a reusable object, operation, or law could earn 4; a useful schedule, initialization, or benchmark alone is at most a direction (3). In particular, do not re-propose log-partition conservation or simple tangent-matched widening ([interface change](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/115)), quotienting away a token ([evolving representations](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/325)), or delayed edge credit ([architecture](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/335)).

**Shared first-test contract.** Start with an enumerable model or a 5–30M decoder and two or three short stream slices, not a frontier run. Unless stated otherwise compare branches from the same checkpoint and match *new-domain gain* as well as the relevant resource (FLOPs, original bytes, examples read, or archive bytes); report the unmatched resource rather than pretending that token counts survive retokenization. Before any branch, lock a disjoint old set: stable facts, older valid versions of changed facts, and ordinary old text or code. Evaluate cold, with no retrieval or online updates, immediately after a transition and after a later common stream slice. Use bits per original byte for cross-tokenizer text and identical byte-string answer choices for QA. A first-test win also requires no material old-task loss versus the old checkpoint on the named retention slice (suggested gates: ≤1 percentage point or ≤1% relative bits/byte, declared before training). In intentionally destructive diagnostic arms, measure that loss; only the proposed protected arm can pass the retention gate. Thresholds below are proposed go/no-go rules, not observations. Literature deltas are scoped to the linked sources, not claims that no one has tried the idea.

**Scale gate.** A toy survivor should predict a held-out change in size, history length, or shift type before a long run. For comparison at scale, use a chronological web/code slice as in [TiC-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02107), and retain strong rewarm/decay/replay and continual-schedule baselines from [Simple and Scalable CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763). Count archive/index I/O, virtual steps, optimizer state and vocabulary-induced sequence-length changes against claims of efficiency. A final union re-train is an oracle for tradeoffs, not a free baseline or a replacement for old cohorts.

## A · The stream is not a partition

### NP01 · Exposure-null twins
- **Mechanism.** A twin of a stream slice preserves its style, document positions and lengths but replaces *all* new occurrences of one randomized fact, including paraphrases, with a matched decoy. Pair these twins to separate survival without exposure from survival through implicit rehearsal. The portable object is the difference between the two retention curves, indexed by what evidence the twin intervened on.
- **Nearest art.** [Time-Incremental CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23916) explicitly treats overlapping web snapshots and analyzes sample-level acquisition and forgetting (§3.2); [Train Once, Answer All](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23383) embeds controlled exposures within one pretraining run. Neither comparison by itself subtracts randomized, proposition-level implicit replay from a time-overlapping stream.
- **First test / kill.** Plant randomized aliases and facts in 100 synthetic page families; compare fact-null edits with URL exclusion and exact deduplication. Kill if the twin contrast cannot separate planted zero from nonzero implicit replay on blind families, or URL/exact deduplication explains it equally well.
- **Old-task retention.** Score untouched old facts and held-out old prose in both arms to detect a corpus-wide effect of making the twin.

### NP02 · Replay with a signed revision boundary
- **Mechanism.** Replace an old/new document pair by a scoped transition: positive evidence for the replacement after a validity boundary, positive evidence for the old claim before it, and negative relative evidence only for the reversed time–value pairings. Negative here means a contrast in answer log odds, not removing the old text globally. The replay unit is an amendment, not either document.
- **Nearest art.** [CKL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03215) separates invariant, updated, and new knowledge; [TempLAMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15110) conditions predictions on time; [Learning and Unlearning of Fabricated Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21750) studies removal of injected false facts. The residual claim is that a *paired historical update* needs signed, scope-bound terms beyond positive timestamped examples.
- **First test / kill.** Use two revisions for fictional offices; compare signed amendments with the same positive dated examples, and with timestamp shuffling. Kill if the contrast cannot reduce post-revision stale answers beyond the positive-only control at equal new-answer gain.
- **Old-task retention.** Require the prior officeholder on pre-revision dates and unchanged answers on unrelated offices; test unseen wording.

### NP03 · Complementary replay pairs
- **Mechanism.** Make a pair of ordinary old batches the replay unit when its members reinforce a named old behavior but cancel their *side effects* on the same new behavior. Neither batch alone should qualify. Estimate the pair's finite effect using a virtual joint step, including a check beyond gradient cosine for nonlinear interference.
- **Nearest art.** [Replay and Gradient Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01908) combines replay and MER-style gradient alignment at language-pretraining scale; [Fisher-guided selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02593) already selects sets with a submodular objective. The candidate is an indivisible compensating pair for a specified behavior, not selection by alignment or diversity.
- **First test / kill.** Construct a two-feature text grammar with individually harmful old batches of opposite sign. Compare the pair to efficient MER, the best independently scored batches, and both singleton ablations, charging the virtual steps. Kill if paired cancellation fails on unseen batches at equal old benefit or MER matches it.
- **Old-task retention.** Check the old behavior both partners support plus a second archived skill neither partner was selected on.

### NP04 · Replay by residual evidence
- **Mechanism.** Learn the *difference between contextual claims* in successive near-duplicate documents without repeatedly paying for their shared scaffold. Weight changed spans plus the earliest unchanged span whose conditional meaning depends on the edit; that dependency is part of the unit, so this is not changed-token masking alone.
- **Nearest art.** [TiC-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02107) builds chronological web distributions and trains with older-data replay; [Time-Incremental CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23916) studies successive overlapping snapshots. These make overlap real, but do not define an update by the boundary between a textual diff and the conditional meaning it changes.
- **First test / kill.** Use versioned manuals where one value change alters the meaning of one later unchanged sentence; compare whole pages, plain diffs, and same-size random unchanged spans. Kill if dependency-selected spans do not improve unseen new-version entailment versus the best control per training FLOP.
- **Old-task retention.** Require old-version entailments when a version is named and unchanged command behavior in a legacy manual.

### NP05 · Replay exchange witnesses
- **Mechanism.** Define a directed, conditional exchange rate: how many exposures to stream fragment B replace one replay exposure to A *for a specified old behavior at matched new gain*. Learn sparse witnesses from short randomized replacements. B can buy protection for A without A buying protection for B; do not assume that rates compose.
- **Nearest art.** [Skill-it!](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14430) induces ordered skill dependencies and uses them for data selection; [Replay on Demand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40089) allocates replay using forgetting and learning potential. The object here is an experimentally identifiable replacement rate, transferable to a later stream, not a skill graph or source score.
- **First test / kill.** Have a new-language corpus carry only one of two old algorithmic regularities. Estimate B→A on short windows and predict a held-out dosage curve; compare to the Skill-it! interaction signal and loss-based replay. Kill if the exchange rate cannot predict the dose needed on the next slice better than either control.
- **Old-task retention.** Test both the substituted regularity and the one not covered by B, on disjoint old examples.

### NP06 · Learning-equivalent deduplication
- **Mechanism.** Call two documents redundant only if substituting one for the other leaves *both* old-behavior protection and subsequent new learning unchanged under a short common continuation. This is a local equivalence test on training effects, with contextual counterexamples; copies and paraphrases can be different, and different-looking documents can be equivalent.
- **Nearest art.** [Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264) measures diminishing returns of repeated data, and [How Do LLMs Acquire Factual Knowledge?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11813) links duplicate data to faster factual forgetting in its controlled setup. Neither makes redundancy depend on indistinguishable future learning under a swap.
- **First test / kill.** For a tiny stream of paired documents, derive equivalence classes using two continuations and predict outcomes of deduplicating under a third. Compare near-text deduplication and immediate gradient similarity at equal corpus budget. Kill if the proposed classes do not better predict which removals are safe.
- **Old-task retention.** Test old rare answers carried redundantly across sources and one deliberately nonredundant old answer that the deduplicator must not erase.

## B · A moving alphabet changes the training problem

### NP07 · Byte-prefix update conjugacy
- **Mechanism.** Define a tokenizer migration to act on *training*, not merely on predictions. Fit a local map so that “one ordinary minibatch step, then migrate” and “migrate, then train on the same raw bytes” induce the same change in byte-prefix log probabilities on unseen old contexts. A low-rank map suffices for the first test; it is indexed by migration, not by corpus label.
- **Nearest art.** [Exact Byte-Level Probabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09303) gives the correct pushforward to byte events; [Approximate Likelihood Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20083) and [Byte-Prefix Marginalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22334) align outputs across tokenizers. [An Equivariance Toolbox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21447) relates parameter equivariances to learning dynamics. This tests equivariance of *subsequent updates* across a change in the token prediction problem, even when initial byte probabilities already match.
- **First test / kill.** Enumerate byte prefixes of a two-merge toy language. Match cold byte KL first, then train both versions on one unseen old batch and one unseen new batch. Kill if the learned map cannot halve held-out update disagreement versus byte distillation plus a tuned scalar step size, or loses its effect after a few steps.
- **Old-task retention.** Check old continuations starting mid-merge and an old script whose common splits did not change.

### NP08 · Segmentation disagreement debt
- **Mechanism.** A proposed merge creates a measured debt on byte prefixes where a short and a long parse now learn incompatible continuations. Temporarily expose *both* parses of only those prefixes and pay the debt through held-out byte-event agreement. Compression alone cannot retire a debt, nor can agreement on the training prefixes.
- **Nearest art.** [FLEXITOKENS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12720) learns adaptive byte boundaries; [Teaching Old Tokenizers New Words](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03989) extends BPE and prunes leaves; [Dynamic Tokenization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18553) generates merged embeddings on the fly. Here the objects are incompatibility witnesses and a release condition for a *permanent* vocabulary change, not learned segment boundaries or initial compression.
- **First test / kill.** Introduce two equally frequent merges, one deliberately consuming a disambiguating boundary. Compare targeted dual parses to continued BPE, random subword dropout, and equally many randomly dual-parsed examples. Kill if the measured debt does not predict which migration harms held-out byte predictions, or targeting gives no protection at equal compute.
- **Old-task retention.** Score archived ambiguous continuations in their original raw bytes, including prefixes ending inside the new token.

### NP09 · Transport optimizer moments by boundary role
- **Mechanism.** During a merge, transport moments for a new embedding from *conditioned occurrences* of its constituents in that span, not global row averages; include their gradient cross-moment in the new row's second-moment estimate so reinforcing and cancelling gradients differ. The object is a boundary-conditioned state pushforward, independent of the new embedding initializer.
- **Nearest art.** [ZeTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07883) predicts embeddings for new tokenizers; [Mundra et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05841) compare convex-hull initializers. [Staged Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06211) already treats optimizer state as part of structural growth (§3.3). The delta is transporting span-conditioned covariance across a many-to-one *input/output alphabet* edit.
- **First test / kill.** With identical initial embeddings and schedules, compare resets, global moment averaging, scalar rescaling and conditional transport on two merges with opposite covariance. Kill if conditional transport predicts neither the sign nor relative size of the early byte-loss shock better than scalar rescaling.
- **Old-task retention.** Score an old language containing the constituents mostly outside the new merge, including rare constituent roles.

### NP10 · Occurrence credit conserved across tokenizers
- **Mechanism.** Give each witnessed raw span one training-credit number and apportion it over whatever tokens currently cover it, so splitting a datum into more predictions does not itself amplify that datum's replay dose. Debit only target bytes supervised by the span; prefix bytes used as context earn no target credit.
- **Nearest art.** [FLEXITOKENS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12720) removes a fixed compression-rate constraint, and [Exact Byte-Level Probabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09303) makes evaluation comparable. Neither defines replay exposure as a conserved quantity across successive redrafts of the tokenization; simple per-byte normalization is the control, not the claim.
- **First test / kill.** Split frequent substrings only in one of two old domains; compare token-normalized, globally byte-normalized, and span-credit-matched replay with identical raw slices. Kill if span credit does not better predict and prevent the relative old-domain degradation, or if global byte weighting suffices.
- **Old-task retention.** Track each old domain separately and check rare old spans of the shifted domain, not just the byte-weighted mean.

### NP11 · Retirement with a future-learning witness
- **Mechanism.** Retire a merge only when some small split-path model reproduces both past byte behavior and the reference merge's *response to a family of new-context minibatches*. Keep the future-response counterexamples, not the retired token, as the witness of why the factorization can or cannot survive. The two branches are only a first-test fixture.
- **Nearest art.** [Picky BPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04599) prunes undertrained tokens during tokenizer fitting; [Teaching Old Tokenizers New Words](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03989) prunes leaves post hoc; [ER01](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/325) already proposes quotienting by future *continuations*. This card tests future *gradient-driven learning* after the token is gone, not forward equivalence under a new name.
- **First test / kill.** Train two old rare merges, equalize post-removal byte likelihood, and expose both to unseen uses with composition only for one. Compare leaf pruning plus continued training and byte likelihood distillation. Kill if witnessing future steps cannot predict which pruning causes later forgetting after matching cold scores.
- **Old-task retention.** Test archived uses of both merges and old recombinations of their parts after the novel uses arrive.

### NP12 · Tokenization as a reversible instrument
- **Mechanism.** Impose a short split/merge/split pulse while training on a fixed raw stream. Project both final branches to the same alphabet. The residual after subtracting conversion-only and no-pulse controls estimates what was *learned because of factorization*, rather than apparent improvement from shorter examples or a friendlier readout.
- **Nearest art.** [ZeTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07883) enables low-cost tokenizer swaps, and [Vocabulary Expansion and Initialization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05841) compares outcomes after an expansion. The proposed primitive is a matched reversible intervention and its end-alphabet residual, not faster transfer.
- **First test / kill.** Use a prefix grammar and identical byte windows. Match FLOPs by shortening the pulse branch's unrelated work or by giving the control equivalent useful updates; report both raw bytes and FLOPs. Kill if the residual is accounted for by conversion-only or by a fixed tokenizer with matched context/window and compute.
- **Old-task retention.** Return to the original tokenizer and test old long-form dependency and low-frequency next-byte prediction without changing evaluation prompts.

### NP13 · Capacity transpositions instead of vocabulary growth
- **Mechanism.** Under a fixed row budget, lease a row by pairing one token split with one merge. Enumerate small coalitions when no single swap preserves old behavior. The indivisible object is a *budget-neutral change of factorization* with a measured cross-term against executing the surgeries independently, not a policy that alternates growing and shrinking.
- **Nearest art.** [Teaching Old Tokenizers New Words](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03989) already provides continued BPE and leaf pruning, while [Gold Panning in Vocabulary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01188) adapts domain expansion. Neither motivates a merge–split cross-term that is necessary for retention under a fixed vocabulary budget.
- **First test / kill.** Enumerate simultaneous and sequential swaps among six toy tokens with overlapping contexts; use the same final rows and initialization for sequential controls. Kill if no held-out old/new Pareto gain depends on the coupled swap versus best separate prune-and-grow at matched compute.
- **Old-task retention.** Test old contexts sensitive to the removed row and old contexts of the merge's constituents, separately.

## C · Growth changes which writes are possible

### NP14 · Two-shock growth witness
- **Mechanism.** Compare opposite orders of two ordinary stream batches after a width or depth insertion. A growth operator can preserve predictions and each single-batch response while changing the old-task effect of the order swap. Treat that difference-of-differences as a witness for what the surgery inserted into the update rule.
- **Nearest art.** [Composable Function-preserving Expansions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06103) proves cold forward preservation; [Staged Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06211) goes further and preserves loss and its rate of decrease (§2.2). [MER for CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01908) uses cross-batch gradient alignment. The extra invariant is a held-out *conditional order effect across a shape change* after one-step effects have already been matched.
- **First test / kill.** Insert a residual block into a small decoder using two initializations. Match zero-step old scores and one-step responses; with SGD, measure A→B versus B→A and compare second-order witnesses to one-step matching. Kill if order effects vanish at equal useful step size or a simple LR change predicts the retention gap just as well.
- **Old-task retention.** Check an old syntactic skill after both shock orders and after a common neutral slice, not just at growth time.

### NP15 · Symmetry-safe optimizer state
- **Mechanism.** At head growth, define a transport on the *clone subspace* using a tiny matrix second moment. Different orthogonal descriptions of the same two clones must induce the same byte-level response to a gradient and an inverse basis change. This targets a coordinate artifact of optimizer inheritance, not the perturbation from growth itself.
- **Nearest art.** [Staged Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06211) transforms existing optimizer state on width/depth changes; [The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam Does](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05136) already demonstrates basis-dependent Adam updates and studies sharing moments. The delta is a local, migration-scoped transport that preserves the old response under arbitrary clone gauges without changing the optimizer elsewhere.
- **First test / kill.** In a factored toy attention block pick two output-identical clone gauges. Compare copy, reset, shared scalar and block transport; charge the latter's extra state. Kill if shared scalar or reset removes gauge-dependent forgetting as well at matched new gain.
- **Old-task retention.** Test the archived attention-dependent rule after several new-stream steps in *both* gauges, not their mean.

### NP16 · Grow in the disagreement of clones
- **Mechanism.** Factor a cloned feature into a preserved common response and a signed difference writable only from contexts where two newly relevant meanings disagree. Use paired minibatches to identify the antisymmetric update; a clone has no independent job until the signal survives on unseen contexts.
- **Nearest art.** [Net2Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05641) and [Grow, Don't Overwrite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08647) already replicate parameters with corrective scaling. [SparseGrow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10566) gates sparse updates to control growth-induced forgetting. The risky new part is a contrast-defined write into the antisymmetric factor rather than random clone differentiation or mask gating alone.
- **First test / kill.** In a homonym grammar add a context where an old feature must split. Compare exact cloning plus random symmetry breaking, gradient gating with the same trainable dimension, and paired contrast. Kill if either ordinary clone control learns the new distinction at the same retention and cost.
- **Old-task retention.** Check the original shared meaning in contexts where there is no new distinguishing cue, including unseen old paraphrases.

### NP17 · A local lower bound on how much to grow
- **Mechanism.** Form a response matrix whose columns are desired finite changes on distinct new minibatches, after forbidding motion of held-out old scores. Its residual rank outside the old model's allowed update image lower-bounds added *effective tangent dimensions* in the linearized fixture (not raw parameters or a global capacity bound). Use the rank to choose between one broad attachment and several independent ones.
- **Nearest art.** [GradMax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05125) initializes new units to maximize gradient signal while not perturbing predictions; [Sculpting Subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07097) constrains full fine-tuning for retention. The candidate is a deficit certificate across *several independent desired responses*, not a gradient-maximizing direction.
- **First test / kill.** Use grammars whose new responses have equal norm but planted residual ranks one and three. Fit the certificate on half the examples; sweep added effective ranks against GradMax and a loss/gradient-norm rule. Kill if the predicted rank cannot identify the smallest successful width on held-out responses better than the controls.
- **Old-task retention.** Recompute old rule accuracy on contexts excluded from the constraints; a lower bound fitted to tokens it can trivially protect does not pass.

### NP18 · Temporary width as a learning instrument
- **Mechanism.** Borrow width for one stream pulse, then return to the original shape. Distinguish capacity that is necessary to *acquire* a new dependence from capacity needed to *represent* it. The primitive is an acquisition-only loan with a same-shape outcome; the loan has value only when spending the same compute directly in the small model cannot buy that outcome.
- **Nearest art.** [Stacking Your Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15319) evaluates atomic growth operators for efficiently reaching larger models; [LiGO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00980) learns a linear map to a larger initializer. Teacher–student compression is not novel; the extra claim is that transient within-lineage width beats a compute-matched small run and ordinary final compression for a dependence that is representable at the old size.
- **First test / kill.** Learn a planted compact parity-like language rule through a short widened phase and shrink using a fixed shared projection/distillation budget. Compare a small-only branch and a separately trained wide teacher compressed at the same total FLOPs, including projection. Kill if the advantage requires remaining wide or either control matches the same-shape endpoint.
- **Old-task retention.** Score old compositional patterns and old text after the width has been fully returned, without the larger branch available.

### NP19 · Split learning traffic without splitting prediction traffic
- **Mechanism.** Duplicate an expert, initially read the same mean of both outputs, and use a backward-only assignment to send each new example's update predominantly to one clone while still exposing both clones in the forward pass. Compare assignments with the same mean gradient but different within-clone covariance. Prediction and learning then have separately testable allocation rules.
- **Nearest art.** [Lifelong-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12281) adds regularized distribution-specialized experts; [Gradient Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04332) already applies data-dependent backward masks without changing that forward pass. The narrower candidate is covariance as a degree of freedom specific to clone growth: keep the first moment of the projected update fixed and vary its second moment to change interference.
- **First test / kill.** Split one tiny expert across two confusable stream sources. At matched forward FLOPs and measured initial mean update in function space, compare tied updates, standard forward routing, and backward masks with shuffled versus structured assignment. Kill if covariance does not predict which assignment preserves old behavior better, or an ordinary routed control matches it.
- **Old-task retention.** Test old examples that previously used the parent expert and mixed-source old examples near the assignment boundary, without task IDs.

### NP20 · The mixed effect of a vocabulary edit and a width edit
- **Mechanism.** Run “merge then widen” and “widen then merge” with no data between operations. They end at the same alphabet and shape. Define the mixed residual as their difference in induced response to subsequent old and new raw batches, after matching each operation's cold output and subtracting the respective no-edit controls. If material, treat joint state transport as an operation in its own right.
- **Nearest art.** [Masked Structural Growth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02869) provides strict forward-preserving growth operators; [In-Place Indic Tokenizer Expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15373) grows vocabulary without changing the backbone. The candidate is an interaction in inherited training state that neither separate intervention can diagnose.
- **First test / kill.** Duplicate one head and add one merge in both orders. Normalize cold byte scores and compare copied versus freshly reset moments, with a joint transport as the proposed repair. Kill if the order effect is negligible or if independently transporting states gives the same old/new trajectory.
- **Old-task retention.** Hold out old text using the new merge and old examples that exercise the widened head but cannot use the merge.

## D · Forgetting laws should survive a change of clocks

### NP21 · A composable forgetting impulse
- **Mechanism.** Given an old fact and a short pulse of new data, record the induced change in its log odds with its sign and lag. Try composing independently measured pulses to predict the effect of an unseen alternating stream; keep a signed interaction term only if independent superposition fails systematically. This is a proposed response law, not a bespoke mixture schedule.
- **Nearest art.** [Factual Knowledge Acquisition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11813) models gains per exposure diluted by later power-law forgetting; [Learning Dynamics in CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07796) separates annealing from distribution shift and extends its law to model size and replay (§3.4). The delta is out-of-order prediction of multiple identifiable shocks, including opposite signs, on the same old behavior.
- **First test / kill.** Estimate isolated pulses from two synthetic update sources; predict a blind pulse train at two spacings. Compare elapsed tokens, a per-fact power law and a fitted CPT curve. Kill if signed impulses give no held-out prediction gain without refitting on the train.
- **Old-task retention.** Use the fitted response to choose one protective replay slot and check untouched stable facts plus the targeted old fact after the train.

### NP22 · Scaling laws for jointly losing requirements
- **Mechanism.** Predict the probability that a *whole old bundle* remains usable, allowing failures to correlate within a dependency family. Fit its joint survival as size and replay increase rather than multiplying marginal retention rates. A scaling regime change would be growth improving mean retention while leaving the probability of “all required rules still work” flat.
- **Nearest art.** [Scaling Laws for Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05605) fits aggregate forgetting under LoRA fine-tuning; [Replay and Gradient Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01908) varies model size and replay for CPT. Neither provides a joint-survival exponent for sets of old rules whose failures can be dependent.
- **First test / kill.** At three small widths and two replay doses, fit joint retention on some skill bundles and predict all-pass on held-out bundles after a new domain. Compare a marginal-product and a matched empirical correlation baseline. Kill if no transferable joint term improves held-out prediction; do not extrapolate a scale law if the small widths fail it.
- **Old-task retention.** Test constituent rules alone and as a fixed chain that needs every old link, including a chain withheld from the fit.

### NP23 · An optimizer age independent of calendar time
- **Mechanism.** For a frozen new-stream direction, define effective moment age as the number of fresh batches a reset optimizer would need to produce the same preconditioned probe update (projected onto that direction). Declare age unidentifiable if that calibration curve is nonmonotone or misses the observed value. Rewarming starts a new directional clock only where stale moments delay adaptation.
- **Nearest art.** [(Re)warm Your Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04014) varies checkpoint, max LR and warmup length; [Why Warmup the Learning Rate?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09405) already ties warmup to conditioning and proposes an Adam variance initialization. The delta is a transferable age coordinate for a subset of directions across *repeated* shifts, not a favorable variance reset.
- **First test / kill.** Stagger two matched shifts into orthogonal toy domains. Compare no reset, a global moment reset, and a directional age estimated from the first shift; match peak LR and new gain. Kill if age does not collapse early-loss trajectories on the second shift better than the best calendar-based control.
- **Old-task retention.** Measure the untouched direction's old degradation and an old direction that overlaps the changed data, separately.

### NP24 · A retention hysteresis loop
- **Mechanism.** Move the same two data sources through A→B→A and vary reversal speed. At matched instantaneous mixture, learning rate, and new-domain score, measure the area between outgoing and returning old-performance paths. Test whether a nonvanishing loop after slower reversal distinguishes path-dependent loss from a transient delay.
- **Nearest art.** [Learning Dynamics in CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07796) models a domain transition and annealing; [Beyond Endpoint Scores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03900) establishes why whole time/rank trajectories matter. The candidate is a controlled loop and a slow-reversal limit, not collecting more checkpoints.
- **First test / kill.** Ramp the mixture at three speeds in two tiny grammars; compare a one-variable relaxation model fitted on the outgoing leg. Kill if the loop shrinks as predicted by mere lag or disappears when also matching optimizer state or using plain SGD.
- **Old-task retention.** Check old composition and archived old text after returning, not only matching loss on the same mixture.

### NP25 · A rewarm shock spectrum
- **Mechanism.** Probe how short learning-rate pulses of equal area affect forgetting across old cohorts after an identical copy of a checkpoint. Estimate which durations, not just peak LR, excite persistent loss. The transferable object is a cohort-by-duration response surface with budgets, not a schedule recommendation.
- **Nearest art.** [(Re)warm Your Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04014) studies transient loss increases as warmup length and peak LR vary; [Simple and Scalable CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763) tests rewarming/decay/replay at up to 10B and discusses schedules without a fixed token budget. The extra test matches aggregate dose and new gain and predicts *cohort-specific* response to unseen pulses.
- **First test / kill.** Fit short versus long pulse responses for old facts and old syntax at one boundary; predict a mixed pulse at the next. Compare peak LR, integrated LR, and the CPT loss curve. Kill if the surface fails to predict the ranking of protected pulse schedules better than those controls.
- **Old-task retention.** In the proposed low-damage pulse, check old reasoning syntax and old terminology independently at peak shock and after recovery.

### NP26 · Separate drift dose from diffusion dose
- **Mechanism.** Hold mean parameter movement on new data approximately fixed while changing gradient-noise covariance, and ask whether old forgetting follows noise projected into old-sensitive directions rather than tokens or step count. A candidate law has two knobs: deterministic drift and stochastic diffusion; matching either alone need not match retention.
- **Nearest art.** [On the Origin of Implicit Regularization in SGD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12176) analyzes finite-step minibatch effects; [Factual Knowledge Acquisition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11813) observes greater robustness to forgetting at larger batch size. The delta is an identified projected noise dose under matched drift, not the observation that batch size matters.
- **First test / kill.** Compare full-batch updates with known zero-mean artificial noise parallel versus orthogonal to old sensitivity, then test minibatch noise at matched drift, FLOPs and new gain. Kill if the same dose does not predict the held-out retention ordering or a scalar noise norm does as well.
- **Old-task retention.** Read old fragile relations and a control relation insensitive to the injected direction; only the protected arm must meet the shared floor.

### NP27 · The upkeep cost of an infinite horizon
- **Mechanism.** Define marginal upkeep of a retention target as the minimum recurring replay compute needed to keep its survival above a floor, given that new-domain gain cannot stop. Estimate whether required cost grows with protected history or saturates because new exposures substitute for old replay. This produces a feasibility bound for a nonterminating learner, not a policy for distributing replay.
- **Nearest art.** [Optimal CL Has Perfect Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05188) shows worst-case memory requirements, not an upkeep-compute law; [Spaced Repetition for CPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17530) schedules reviews; [Forgetting in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26097) reports limited remaining capacity can defeat replay. The delta is a measured *minimum recurring compute price* across histories, with a lower bound in a planted independent-data case.
- **First test / kill.** Start with an expiry model: if each of `n` independent facts needs one exclusive refresh every `h` steps, at least `n/h` refreshes per step are necessary. Then enumerate short replay schedules in a tiny decoder on independent facts versus shared rules. Fit the price on two history lengths and predict the third. Kill if it cannot bound the feasible budgets better than extrapolation from fixed-ratio or spaced replay, or if the apparent savings require starving the new stream.
- **Old-task retention.** Enforce a floor for each age band, particularly the oldest; do not let the two youngest bands hide its collapse.

## E · A moving cutoff is not one date

### NP28 · Bitemporal corrections in the weights
- **Mechanism.** Train control pairs on two independent axes: *valid at* and *knowable by*. A late correction may change today's answer about yesterday while leaving intact what could reasonably have been reported yesterday. Build crossed contrasts so moving either time changes only its intended dimension; serving needs no database or retrieval.
- **Nearest art.** [TempLAMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15110) jointly models time and text (§2.3), while [temporal databases](https://doi.org/10.1145/140979.140996) already distinguish valid and transaction time. That distinction is not new; the candidate is learning independently movable axes through retroactive, append-only updates in one model.
- **First test / kill.** Plant changes that take effect late and corrections announced late, including identical valid times with different report times. Compare one date, two date tags with plain CPT, and crossed contrasts at equal data. Kill if simple two-date conditioning matches held-out crossed queries and current accuracy.
- **Old-task retention.** Check what an earlier record actually said and what was historically valid, using the correct time interpretation for each; score unrelated old facts separately.

### NP29 · Measure the frontier with unguessable reveal events
- **Mechanism.** A reveal event binds a randomly drawn answer to a plausible question at a randomized ingest step; the fact's event date and wording are held fixed. Run two branches with different reveal offsets but the same ambient chronology. The contrast measures when the model can demonstrate *exposure-dependent* knowledge, distinct from how recently its answers sound.
- **Nearest art.** [Dated Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12958) estimates effective cutoffs by probing versions of a resource; [Train Once, Answer All](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23383) implants controlled facts. The new object is paired reveal-time perturbation at fixed apparent date, used to calibrate the lag and false positives of a cutoff estimator.
- **First test / kill.** Use nonce outcomes attached to shuffled dates with two reveal offsets and a never-revealed arm. Compare against date-only and passive rolling-window estimators. Kill if it cannot recover the offset on unseen templates or declares comparable learning for the never-revealed arm.
- **Old-task retention.** Test earlier independently revealed facts after the late reveals, plus a dated old rule not involved in the intervention.

### NP30 · A cutoff for a join, not its operands
- **Mechanism.** Define the frontier of a compound query by the most recent verified *coherent join* of its prerequisites. Two facts can be individually current yet never simultaneously valid. A join witness requires correct answers under one common as-of condition and rejection of planted non-overlaps; this can label training pairs, not only grade them.
- **Nearest art.** [Dated Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12958) defines resource-level effective cutoffs; [RippleEdits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976) tests dependent facts after edits. The candidate is a failure of composing independently up-to-date facts because their support intervals do not intersect, even with perfect marginal recall.
- **First test / kill.** Stagger a fictional vendor's contract and component approval windows. Compare the minimum of marginal cutoffs, date-tagged rehearsal, and join-witness training. Kill if the latter cannot reject nonoverlap while answering valid overlaps on unseen relation pairs at equal marginal accuracy.
- **Old-task retention.** Require earlier valid joins as well as both single-relation answers, including old contracts whose approvals never changed.

### NP31 · A partial order on usable snapshots
- **Mechanism.** Rank checkpoints by inclusion of verified (relation, validity band) cells rather than by issue date or latest observed fact. Two successive snapshots can be incomparable: each answers an interval the other cannot. Estimate a sparse set of separating cells that predicts dominance or incomparability on a much larger hidden grid.
- **Nearest art.** [Dated Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12958) already recognizes topic- and resource-level effective cutoffs, and [ChroKnowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09870) partitions knowledge by domain and temporal state. This proposes a nonmonotone set order with interior holes and falsifiable separating witnesses, not simply more cutoff dates.
- **First test / kill.** In three increments plant a skipped middle era and a dormant early interval in otherwise familiar relations. Compare one cutoff per model, one per relation, and age-binned accuracy. Kill if witnesses cannot predict held-out interval reversals better than the best summary.
- **Old-task retention.** Include early stable cells and early valid-but-no-longer-current values; a later date cannot compensate for losing either.

### NP32 · An omission that is allowed to teach
- **Mechanism.** A missing claim is negative evidence only within a source-declared complete enumeration, for its stated scope and validity interval. Convert disappearance between complete versions into a signed, dated constraint; identical omission from a partial source must teach nothing about the claim's truth.
- **Nearest art.** [Predicting Completeness in Knowledge Bases](https://doi.org/10.1145/3018661.3018739) already treats completeness as local rather than global; [TiC-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02107) uses temporal web snapshots where disappearance alone is not a label. The candidate is compiling a local completeness warrant into a dated negative *pretraining event*, not inventing the closed-world assumption or treating every absence as contradiction.
- **First test / kill.** Interleave full and visibly abridged fictional product catalogs with matched omission rates. Compare positive-only versions and unlikelihood on every disappearance. Kill if scoped omission cannot remove truly discontinued items without also removing items omitted solely from abridgments on new catalog templates.
- **Old-task retention.** Check legitimately listed old items as of their original dates, plus current old items absent only from partial lists.

### NP33 · Shift a history without shifting its rules
- **Mechanism.** Treat a calendar translation as a paired transformation of facts *and their validity predicates*, not masking or replacing a date token. Learn an equivariance only for rules that should translate; explicit counterexamples protect rules tied to fixed dates. This aims to learn how to extend an era rather than what ordinal string to emit.
- **Nearest art.** [TimeStress](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01220) varies distance and granularity around validity intervals; [TimeShift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13338) probes day-granular recall and paraphrase robustness. The delta is training with composition and falsification under held-out calendar translations, not reporting sensitivity to a date paraphrase.
- **First test / kill.** Shift synthetic histories by unequal offsets; interleave a moveable ten-day rule and a fixed statutory date. Compare date augmentation and explicit date tokens; hold out a sum of two seen offsets. Kill if the model cannot apply the translated rule at the novel offset without translating the anchored rule as well.
- **Old-task retention.** Test original-era translations, old anchored dates, and an unrelated succession rule on unchanged dates.

### NP34 · Propagation time for derived facts
- **Mechanism.** After an exogenous update, directly taught facts and never-taught consequences have distinct effective adoption times. A propagation operator maps the first stable adoption of a dated fact to a distribution of first stable adoption across dependency types. The predicted *lag* can guide what derivative evidence is worth adding, not what to retrieve at inference.
- **Nearest art.** [RippleEdits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976) evaluates implied changes after discrete edits; [Beyond Endpoint Scores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03900) measures month-dependent updating behavior. The candidate is a transferable latency law *from independently timed exposure to untaught consequences*, not endpoint propagation or more frequent evaluations.
- **First test / kill.** Use two private relation graphs with updates to one leaf, then a common neutral stream; sample answer odds frequently, requiring two successive checkpoints above threshold for adoption. Compare graph depth alone and direct-fact adoption alone. Kill if lags on the second graph cannot be forecast better than both at matched direct adoption.
- **Old-task retention.** Track older valid consequences and same-depth unaffected branches; later stabilization is not a win if unrelated inferences drift.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Is there a portable invariant of tomorrow's learning across today's surgery?** Start with byte-prefix update conjugacy (NP07). At matched cold outputs, find one sequence that distinguishes otherwise identical migrations, then ask if its witness predicts pruning and mixed surgeries (NP11, NP14, NP20). Stop if initial byte matching plus an LR/moment adjustment predicts all the differences.
2. **When can indefinitely growing history cost bounded replay?** Compare independent old facts to shared latent rules using replay exchange and upkeep witnesses (NP05, NP21, NP27). Aim for a tight finite bound in the independent fixture and a testable saturation condition in the shared one. Stop if held-out price is no better predicted than by per-example review or if the bound only holds as new learning tends to zero.
3. **Is an effective cutoff a composable object with two time axes?** Use late corrections, unguessable reveals, and nonoverlapping joins (NP28–NP30, NP34). See whether a compact witness set predicts both answerability and adoption lag without oracle metadata at inference. Stop if per-resource dates and ordinary two-date CPT match crossed, derived and historical probes.
4. **Which aspect of forgetting actually scales?** Compare marginal to joint skill survival, drift to diffusion, and calendar age to optimizer age (NP22–NP26). Require one proposed law to predict an unseen size *and* a reversal or pulse, not just fit a falling curve. Stop if step count, peak LR and independent marginal failures suffice.
5. **Can capacity be necessary for a write but unnecessary for the resulting memory?** Compare local dimension bounds and width loans (NP16–NP18) on the same planted dependence. First establish representability at the smaller size, then seek an acquisition barrier no compute-matched small baseline clears. Stop if the gain disappears on shrinkage or comes entirely from ordinary teacher distillation.
