# Deployment feedback 4: Renew witnesses and block collisions in the learner

Child of [PR #354](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/354) ([parent node](deployment-feedback-3.md)) · Depth 3 · 26 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** A feedback-trained model can lose a valid explanation without receiving any evidence against it: a shared layer moves, a router sends the same prompt to a different head, or the optimizer stops letting a rare feature respond. Conversely, two updates can leave every explicit correction action available yet jointly teach the model to erase the information those actions expose. Keep a small repertoire of learning operations that renew an opposed witness; block pairs by the *training interaction* that destroys the final distinguishing route, not by their names, apparent parameter overlap, or independent scores.

**Scope.** [Flood-triage row 486](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/70c958ab13c64cee4395f9fbd18403b2baf5807b/reviews/flood-triage.md#L486) kept D3-01 (renew a dying witness via a certified signed pivot) and D3-15 (store a joint blocker rather than stacking vetoes), neither in the top ten. This node develops only those two. **Learning-only gate:** match a learned system and a frozen export on old labeled scores, current routes, and immediate new-task gain. Vary what training actually does to a representation, an update map, or a distribution of future outputs. A frozen verifier can check a realized candidate; if a monitor over fixed features and predeclared edit effects can also *construct* the same object and get the same result, treat the card as old work. A verifier that models the learning dynamics explicitly is a legitimate strong oracle, not something this node claims to rule out.

**Common first assay and controls.** In a tiny enumerable environment use a trainable trunk with a serving head and two small shadow hypothesis heads, or a stronger learned model if the mechanism requires it. The heads are *world models*: each predicts permitted feedback on held-out interventions as well as a preference order; two arbitrary neural seeds with opposite answers are not witnesses. Require the old certified heads to fit the authorized measurements within declared error bounds and predict opposite answers to the same old comparison. Distinguish a broken certificate from a proved decision. Use row 486's four cells: match visible old A/B scores and immediate benefit from C/D, independently vary silent closure and lawful reachability, then reveal a witness on one old scope only. Check old A/B, the still-open twin, correct licensed closure, and per-cohort diagnostic power at a matched false-alarm ceiling after C and D. An impossible-to-detect reversal must remain reported as infeasible. A synthetically injected witness may test closure but does not establish deployable reachability.

## A · Renew a dying witness using operations that training creates

### D4-01 · Reconstruct the lost side from a gradient difference
- **Primitive:** When the trunk learns a new task, estimate the differential effect of the trunk update on the two worlds' old responses. Cache a small corrective head update driven by the *difference* of their evidence residual gradients, and certify feasibility and opposed sign after applying it to the failing witness only.
- **Learning dependence:** The direction is a comparison of backpropagated residuals in the newly learned representation. A verifier on frozen rows can see which sign is gone, but has no differential response to learn or transport.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Semantic Drift Compensation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00440) adjusts prototypes following estimated feature drift; [MEND](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11309) transforms gradients for local editing. The update here is selected to recover one evidence-compatible *side*, with the surviving side serving as a live residual control.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Train a two-layer trunk on C, holding two old heads initially valid; D shifts the activation of a cue shared by both. Compare drift-compensated prototypes and independently refit heads with the same examples. Kill if the differential pivot cannot beat refreshed LP and head refit at equal evidence access and compute.
- **Old-task retention:** After C and D test A/B serving, both worlds' fit and disagreement, then whether the authorized observation closes only its own comparison.

### D4-02 · Renew a witness by crossing the learned activation boundary
- **Primitive:** Keep a one-step training intervention that moves a previously useful unit back into its informative regime for the failing hypothesis without changing the other one's activation pattern on old probes. Recompute signed feasible increments on both sides of the learned boundary.
- **Learning dependence:** An apparently unchanged output can acquire a zero Jacobian when training pushes a hidden unit inactive; fixed-route verification under the current output map cannot construct the update that makes that feature trainable again.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Local ReLU region complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18283) describes the network's local geometry; [AlphaEdit](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HvSytvg3Jh) constrains edits to preserve knowledge. The new candidate is a guarded crossing to make a previously admissible side learnable again, not just restricting edits to the current activation region.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** In a four-unit ReLU model have C turn off the feature that represented the old negative side, and D tighten a fit constraint. Compare smooth gradient repair, exhaustive tiny weight-space repair, and reinitializing the dead feature. Kill if the targeted crossing does not preserve more verified witnesses at equal intervention cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B across both activation regions; cover an untouched case sharing the unit that should not switch decision.

### D4-03 · Keep a learned feature only if it repairs the missing sign
- **Primitive:** Learn a small dictionary of candidate repair features across prior live corrections. Admit a new atom only if an ordinary constrained update in its span renews a failing witness on unseen prompts without moving the opposite one out of evidence bounds.
- **Learning dependence:** An atom's value is learned transfer of repair gradients to unobserved prompts. A fixed verifier can check supplied atoms, not discover which feature representation makes useful future correction gradients recur.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dictionary learning for features](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features) decomposes activations; [Learning Where to Learn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14402) learns which parameters to update. Here atoms are trained and selected for renewing a specific lost decision side under evidence constraints.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Learn atoms on several tiny feedback streams, then hold out a new scope and drift direction. Compare equal-width random, PCA and gradient-sparsity bases. Reject if there is no transfer to withheld prompts after matching rank and inference cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old labeled tasks and unaffected open comparisons after learning the atom and again after using it on the held-out scope.

### D4-04 · Relearn the signed side in the learner's own update metric
- **Primitive:** Keep a small signed repair direction together with the optimizer-state coordinates that govern it. After drift, compute a candidate *raw gradient* whose actual preconditioned step falls inside the verified repair range; release neither the raw gradient nor the range without checking the resulting endpoint.
- **Learning dependence:** Identical weights and gradients with different Adam moments can take different steps; a verifier of the frozen serving function cannot infer which seed gradient will renew the witness.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980) adapts steps from gradient moments, and [Continual Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02754) carries second moments across tasks. The candidate inverts the actual update to land in a side-specific feasible region rather than selecting a moment policy for aggregate retention.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use the same warm-start head under two moment histories. Compare preconditioned projection, moment reset, and Continual Adam with equal evidence. Kill if the inverse-step pivot ties these at signed renewal and compute, or needs essentially a full moment backup.
- **Old-task retention:** Retest both cohorts' old actions and each held-out witness after the state-dependent step; audit the next ordinary optimizer step too.

### D4-05 · Teach an ordinary correction to renew a witness
- **Primitive:** Learn a shadow-hypothesis initialization at which a newly authorized example yields, by one normal update, a valid replacement for a failing witness on its original side. Do not train the example to reveal which side should be true; train it to restore fit while the ambiguity remains.
- **Learning dependence:** The retained state is judged by what a new labeled example's gradient does, not by the current predictions. No static witness validator can compile the initial weights without modeling or learning the gradient response.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MAML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400) learns fast adaptation; [CE4-03 in #351](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/351) asks a common write to complete from one authorized example. Here the correction must restore the failing side while keeping its opposite admissible, without settling the comparison.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Train a tiny meta-initialization on drifting contexts; test on withheld contexts where the correction does not determine the old contrast. Compare ordinary MAML on fit, replay-based one-step updates, and exact refit. Kill if side-aware initialization gives no additional renewable openness at the same fit and steps.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and paired disagreement after the one-step renewal, then confirm a different genuinely decisive label is not ignored.

### D4-06 · Change the learning feature while holding the witness output fixed
- **Primitive:** Move a surviving witness within a low-loss region to a functionally matched parameterization whose gradient on a future correction can reach a replacement for the dying side. Check both endpoints on old evidence and test the new correction before retaining the move.
- **Learning dependence:** The object is a pair of output-matched networks with different future learning responses, not two fixed equal functions. Their different update Jacobians vanish from a static input-output equivalence check.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Mode connectivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06977) finds low-loss paths; [weight disentanglement in tangent space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827) edits in a linearized network. The candidate chooses a low-loss continuation for the *response* to a legitimate upcoming gradient, not for current average loss.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Find matched nonlinear fits near either side of a feature competition in a shallow network. Compare a random low-loss walk, tangent editing, and exact small-network search. Kill if any gains disappear against a static output-equal initialization with ordinary meta-training.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B before and after the move and after the next correction, with old pair validity scored independently of old logits.

### D4-07 · Restore a witness with a retained teaching gradient, not its old weights
- **Primitive:** Distill a small synthetic gradient carrier for a shadow hypothesis, using only authorized old evidence, whose effect on a future trunk recovers the missing sign and preserves evidence fit. Reject carriers that simply store served answers or become invalid after a representation shift.
- **Learning dependence:** The carrier is defined by an actual update on regenerated features; in a fixed model it is neither a transition nor evidence that a world is valid.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dataset condensation by gradient matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05929) and [trajectory matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932) reproduce training behavior. The target is a certified renewal direction under representation shift while another hypothesis stays plausible, not reproducing a teacher or trajectory globally.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Distill one carrier in a synthetic preference stream; compare the same bytes of real old data and ordinary gradient/trajectory condensation after a held-out feature rewrite. Reject if the carrier has no relative benefit or needs old data it claims not to retain.
- **Old-task retention:** Test A/B and both fit/opposition after using the carrier; verify a new authorized witness can still eliminate its side.

### D4-08 · Recover a witness by unsharing one feature factor
- **Primitive:** In a low-rank shadow model, split a single learned feature factor only if the two witness gradients demand incompatible changes on that factor while sharing the rest productively. Use the new factor for the signed pivot, not to add a new serving route or expert.
- **Learning dependence:** Joint factor learning makes two independently useful correction directions share an update bottleneck. A verifier on fixed factor values has no estimate of which feature would benefit from being learned separately.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [O-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14152) preserves tasks in distinct low-rank spaces; [LoraHub](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13269) composes adapters. This split is triggered by opposing evidence-fit repairs while both world hypotheses still describe one old decision, not by old-task interference alone.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two shadow heads need opposite early-feature repairs while a third dimension should remain shared. Compare independent adapters, a uniform rank increase, and an orthogonal rank increase. Kill if the selective split fails at matched peak rank and future renewal.
- **Old-task retention:** After C/D check A/B, fit of the old unchanged head and whether both old-world signs remain feasible.

### D4-09 · Renew the side from its response variance, not its mean gradient
- **Primitive:** For a near-zero mean repair gradient, retain a few signed directions with high conditional probability of satisfying the old evidence constraints under small legal batches. Validate the resulting update before adoption; do not certify an arbitrary favorable seed.
- **Learning dependence:** The viable pivot depends on the distribution of training steps induced by resampling, even when the serving law and expected raw gradient agree. A fixed verifier of mean behavior has no such object.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Empirical large-batch training](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06162) studies gradient noise scale; [MER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11910) seeks transfer through gradient alignment. The retained object is a sign-conditioned distribution of viable *next repairs*, not a rule for reducing variance or maximizing average transfer.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Form paired batches with equal mean gradients but different projected covariance onto an old contrast. Compare deterministic mean, stratified replay, and full gradient resampling under a cap. Kill if conditional directions cannot reduce failed re-solves at the same number of simulated and real updates.
- **Old-task retention:** After C/D check A/B and signed renewal rates over allowed batch draws; never count a single lucky draw as a route or validity guarantee.

### D4-10 · Distinguish evidence-driven death from learning-driven death
- **Primitive:** Replay only the last learned representation move on the two hypotheses, with evidence fixed, then replay the evidence addition with representation fixed. Use the two counterfactuals to choose transport, a signed training repair, or an exact closure check; do not infer death of a side from a failed approximate repair.
- **Learning dependence:** Separation requires counterfactual *training* transformations and their effect on the representable hypothesis family. A frozen verifier can test the two current witnesses but cannot attribute their loss without the learning replay.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [TracIn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08484) attributes predictions along training checkpoints; [exact recursive set membership](https://doi.org/10.1109/9.29443) attributes feasible-set contraction to new measurements in a fixed model. The candidate distinguishes a change in available neural explanations from additional information about the old decision.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Engineer identical terminal margins from a genuine new comparison and from trunk collapse. Compare a frozen-feature inference arm, full counterfactual retraining, and compact learned-update replay. Kill if the compact experiment cannot recover the correct repair choice.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old labeled A/B and the open pair in the learning-only event; demand authorized selective closure in the evidence-driven event.

## B · Block training interactions that erase the last distinguishing route

### D4-11 · Block the cross-update term, not either gradient
- **Primitive:** Measure the mixed change in an old diagnostic contrast when each peer recomputes its gradient after the other's update. Store the smallest interacting parameter block that explains loss of attainable contrast; admit either correction with peers that lack that interaction.
- **Learning dependence:** The recomputation term contains derivatives of one training gradient evaluated after another write. That term disappears with fixed additive deltas, though the diagnostic contrast can still be nonlinear in those deltas.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [First-order meta-learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02999) analyzes cross-batch gradient effects; [Second-Order Influences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10177) analyzes successive replay selections. The blocker is tied to report separability and can depend on update order even when task-gradient alignment is unchanged.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use two gradient pairs with the same dots and same singleton endpoints on old tasks but different mixed effects after a real second step. Compare PCGrad, frozen-delta superposition, trajectory influence and full pair retraining. Kill if the mixed-block estimate does not predict held-out route failures at useful cost.
- **Old-task retention:** After both C/D orders check ordinary A/B, route-hitting and non-hitting peers, and old diagnosis power at the same false-alarm level.

### D4-12 · A collision can be stored in optimizer state before it appears in weights
- **Primitive:** Jointly tag two training requests only if their updates push a shared second moment into a regime that suppresses later authorized correction along a protected contrast. Audit the next correction from that moment state, not just the present weights.
- **Learning dependence:** Two runs can share serving weights and scores but have different latent moment accumulators and therefore different responses to the same next gradient. A fixed verifier cannot discover that future loss without modeling the optimizer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Continual Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02754) pools previous-task second moments to improve aggregate retention. Here the stored state explains a prospective, contrast-specific blocker that declines only peer combinations which disable an otherwise useful diagnostic response.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match current snapshots by weight-only restoration in the toy while retaining distinct accumulated moments; replay the same lawful third correction. Compare updating with separate moments, reset, pooled moments, and refreshed exact trajectory simulation. Kill if the collision is fully predicted by a single largest moment or if resetting it safely dominates.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B and both diagnostic signs before and after applying the identical third correction, reporting ordinary service of the non-hitting peer too.

### D4-13 · Block a paired training update that coalesces the two report routes
- **Primitive:** Estimate the paired displacement of a learned router's gates on cases that would produce opposite reports. When training C and D together would send both cases to an expert lacking the distinguishing feature, emit a router–expert blocker; don't ban a peer that causes the same gate shift toward a competent expert.
- **Learning dependence:** Assignment boundaries and expert competence both change with training. A router exported as a fixed transition graph contains neither the gate gradient nor the jointly changing expert response.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [StableMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08396) stabilizes learned routing, and [MoE token gradient conflict](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19905) tackles conflicting gradients. The blocker is specific to the *loss of a report distinction* after joint routing and expert training, not router stability by itself.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two experts, both safe on old scores; C shifts gate margins, D removes a feature from one expert. Compare frozen routes, permanent expert isolation, gradient surgery and exact paired training. Kill if gate-only or expert-only signals predict all harmful peers as well.
- **Old-task retention:** Score old normal and minority A/B workflows, plus the report distinction before and after both changes under each route.

### D4-14 · Detect a shared feature trained to explain away both sides
- **Primitive:** Track a learned nuisance feature whose activation predicts both available report paths after C and D; block the pair if training on both makes the report head ignore *both* of the separate distinguishing cues. The blocker attributes the collapse to the competing feature rather than merely an output correlation.
- **Learning dependence:** Feature competition emerges because the head and encoder co-adapt. A fixed effect table with two valid cues cannot predict when joint training will make a shortcut dominate gradients.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Gradient starvation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09468) shows feature competition under cross-entropy; [Semantic Drift Compensation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00440) restores shifted representations. This blocker permits singleton shortcut learning but tests whether the pair removes all old cue-dependent evidence.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Train a small classifier to express two alternative reports; C and D correlate the same nuisance with opposite contexts. Compare shortcut-invariant training, removal of either cue and gradient-sign/block overlap. Reject if a cue knockout cannot causally release the predicted blocker on held-out histories.
- **Old-task retention:** Test A/B with nuisance varied independently, including quiet old users whose cue remains usable in a single-write arm.

### D4-15 · Predict when two local edits jointly saturate the report decoder
- **Primitive:** Learn a paired-update expansion for report log odds in the actually reached rare states, then check whether both interventions enter a saturated region where opposite histories yield indistinguishable reports. Reserve the blocker for peers that shrink separation below the required power, not just peers that increase confidence.
- **Learning dependence:** The decoder and features move together during C/D. If a fixed decoder on frozen features reproduces the saturation from adding fixed biases, this is not a learning-only effect and the card dies.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Task arithmetic in tangent space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827) isolates tasks in linearized effects; [TIES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708) resolves sign interference in merged vectors. The proposed signal is the nonlinear *report-law* response, conditioned on reached states and checked against actual sequential retraining.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match old labels, logit margins, and independent report-law changes; let one nonlinear two-step training pair alone saturate both rare reports. Compare a temperature-matched arm, exact frozen-logit composition and a mixed-curvature estimate. Kill if frozen composition predicts the same failures or the estimate misses held-out pairs.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B with both rare and common histories after the joint write, matching ordinary service and output entropy.

### D4-16 · Block an edit pair only if their training distributions feed each other
- **Primitive:** Store a two-edge feedback cycle when C's learned output moves D's next training sampler into a narrow region and D's update in turn eliminates visitation of the old informative region. An edit with the same immediate effect but a fixed sampler is not a member of the blocker.
- **Learning dependence:** Data collected after training is part of the next update. Replaying fixed edit effects cannot reconstruct the occupancy-conditioned gradients or the cycle they create.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Stateful performative prediction](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/brown22a.html) studies stateful responses to predictions; [second-order replay selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10177) studies interference between selections. The sparse object is a joint train–sample–train failure of one old test with direct mediation checks.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** In a three-state simulator, cross actual on-policy collection and a matched frozen log with the same sample count. Compare a performative model that re-solves each pair and a fixed-graph controller. Reject if freezing either edge does not restore diagnosis or if a one-step proxy predicts the same failures.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B across both occupancy distributions and verify actual attainable power under the intervened and original samplers.

### D4-17 · Treat a shared normalization update as a separate writer
- **Primitive:** Attribute a joint blocker to training-induced normalization statistics only if resetting or routing those statistics restores the old report distinction while leaving both peer weight changes and new-task benefits in place.
- **Learning dependence:** Running normalization state and the representations it normalizes are learned from each peer's data. At equal current scores and pre-update export, different statistic streams can produce opposite route outcomes.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Continual Normalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16102) reduces cross-task normalization effects; [invariant confluence](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf) flags unsafe merges of known writes. The hypothesis is a reusable, causally isolated *joint* blocker for rare diagnostic reports, not a new normalization layer.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use two distributions with matched aggregate scores whose sequential norm updates erase a minority report. Compare continual normalization, per-task norm statistics, and freezing all normalization. Kill if the isolated intervention doesn't rescue diagnosis at the same A/B score and new gains.
- **Old-task retention:** Check labeled A/B and rare contrast power under live and held-out statistic histories; include a peer whose data does not hit the implicated feature.

### D4-18 · Detect route loss caused by student learning, not teacher incompatibility
- **Primitive:** Treat two individually useful teacher corrections as a joint blocker only if the student fitting both compresses all distinguishing reports into the same learned feature, despite each teacher and both separate students retaining a route. Identify the collision with a student-only feature intervention.
- **Learning dependence:** Both evidence routes exist in the fixed teacher policies; the new collision is created during fitting of a shared student representation, not by composing declared teacher transitions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Policy distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06295) compresses policies and [DER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07211) rehearses teacher logits. The blocker concerns a specific lost diagnostic route at a given student capacity, even when teacher outputs and mean distilled performance remain stable.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Distill two tiny teachers into a capped two-feature network with or without a diagnostic report loss. Compare separate student heads at equal total rank, replay with all known report strings, and exact student feature ablation. Kill if either ordinary report replay or teacher compatibility suffices.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify A/B service plus report-law separation in the two singleton students and their joint student, including an uninvolved old group.

### D4-19 · Block a capacity reallocation that consumes both spare diagnostic features
- **Primitive:** In a sparse learner, identify two training requests whose prune/regrow steps consume different reserve weights supporting alternative report cues; forbid the pair when neither cue can regrow under the remaining allowed gradient budget. A peer preserving one viable regrowth direction remains admissible.
- **Learning dependence:** The reserve depends on gradients of currently inactive weights and the model's learned regrowth policy. A graph of current active weights or explicit actions misses whether an alternate report can be relearned cheaply.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Dynamic sparse continual learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14831) adapts sparse masks online. The candidate reserves future gradient accessibility of at least one *distinguishing cue*, not network sparsity or static parameter ownership.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use a two-cue sparse MLP with learner-chosen regrowth under equal-size peer steps. Compare protected mask, random regrowth, and identical fixed-mask updates. Kill if a simple per-route mask is sufficient or gradient accessibility does not predict restoration.
- **Old-task retention:** After C/D test old A/B and authorized diagnosis with actual bounded regrowth, including one spare cue that must still work in the non-hitting control.

### D4-20 · Identify an interaction that destroys the next teaching example
- **Primitive:** Block a pair of writers if it preserves current diagnostic outputs but makes the same next legal feedback example produce indistinguishable updates under the two world hypotheses. Register the local loss of *learning response* only if the route uses that response and the user can still request it lawfully.
- **Learning dependence:** The planned experiment diagnoses from a lawful supervised update followed by an independent probe. A frozen report graph can faithfully represent today's outputs and still omit the difference between tomorrow's two learned update responses.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [La-MAML](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/85b9a5ac91cd629bd3afe396ec07270a-Paper.pdf) optimizes through later training; [active diagnosis](https://home.lmf.cnrs.fr/downloads/SergeHaddad/A15-CDC.pdf) assumes defined controlled and observed events. The blocker concerns training steps that erase the measurement process itself, even though its pre-update prediction is unchanged.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Find networks with identical pre-update probe accuracy and different one-example response; compare fixed behavioral diagnosis, a random matched-norm update, and exact update-then-probe across peers. Kill if the measured difference is only norm or service disruption, or if an ordinary static comparison resolves the same ambiguity at equal burden.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B before and after the diagnostic update, require unchanged unrelated skills, and audit realized diagnosis after the non-hitting peer.

## C · Use learning to make both objects cheaper

### D4-21 · Renew a witness in a direction that also releases a joint blocker
- **Primitive:** Choose between feasible signed gradient pivots by which one restores a diagnostic feature that two upcoming training operations would otherwise erase. Require the same bounded pivot to renew the hypothesis and release a blocker; no extra edit or fallback route is free.
- **Learning dependence:** The coupling is between the pivot's training gradient and the future peer gradients on the same learned feature. A fixed verifier can check whether the resulting route works but cannot produce the jointly useful gradient from predeclared route changes.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MEND](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11309) learns gradient transformations and [Pacti](https://doi.org/10.1145/3704736) composes known guarantees. The claim is shared learning work: one update can repair evidence fit and leave a viable separating report after both peers, better than independently chosen repairs.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Construct two equally valid signed pivots differing in a feature that the joint pair will later use. Compare separately optimized renewal plus blocker repair, a random eligible pivot and exact joint low-rank search. Kill if there is no savings with learning, or the same savings persist in a frozen supplied-feature control.
- **Old-task retention:** Test A/B and lawful closure for the previously open pair after both writes; report the alternative old pair without maximal protection.

### D4-22 · Learn the report feature that separates two replacement witnesses
- **Primitive:** If a joint write invalidates a diagnostic feature but leaves both signs feasible, train a small report readout on lawful differences between *repaired* world predictions; then revalidate both learned worlds and report power on out-of-sample interventions.
- **Learning dependence:** The replacement report depends on generalization to unseen histories from a learned representation of where repaired hypotheses differ, not selection of a new path from a fixed list.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [StableMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08396) learns stable routing and [disagreement compression](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume16/wiener15a/wiener15a.pdf) characterizes a version space from compact data. The candidate uses disagreement to train a replacement measurement without treating synthetic world predictions as new user evidence.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Couple semantic feature drift with a broken readout in a small learner; compare independent head refit and exact diagnosis over frozen report summaries. Reject if a new readout cannot generalize beyond the training histories or if fit to synthetic predictions is mistaken for closure.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B serving and report power on unseen old histories; require open status to remain until independent lawful evidence arrives.

### D4-23 · Keep gradient evidence that generalizes across model versions, not across users
- **Primitive:** Learn a signed repair basis from repeated permitted evidence on the same old scope before and after unrelated edits, then store only the components that predict the next valid pivot across versions. Test whether interference from a peer can be located by the basis' loss of predictive power.
- **Learning dependence:** The signal is the part of an evidence gradient that survives a sequence of learned representations. A fixed verifier has no across-training transfer distribution to infer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Model stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) compares representation compatibility; [TRAK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14186) attributes predictions to examples at scale. The basis is retained for signed *future repairs* and uses a matched authorized scope rather than inferring that user evidence transfers across cohorts.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match old heads across two feature rewrites and introduce a third learned rewrite that hits the repair basis. Compare stitching, individual gradients at equal bytes and exact re-search/diagnosis. Kill if the basis cannot distinguish a future-valid renewal from an output-matched but fragile one.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure A/B plus real renewal/diagnosis after each rewrite; confirm the basis does not implicitly resolve the unwitnessed old comparison.

### D4-24 · Test whether a low-loss peer leaves a future gradient usable
- **Primitive:** Project candidate signed repairs through the actual differentiable update of the peer rather than transporting them only to its final feature coordinates. Block the pair only if the transported gradients no longer contain any verified renewal on one side within the declared budget.
- **Learning dependence:** The pushforward of an update through another training step depends on the update Jacobian and the learned metric; fixed endpoint substitutions cannot differentiate that response.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [La-MAML](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/85b9a5ac91cd629bd3afe396ec07270a-Paper.pdf) differentiates through an online inner loop; [tangent-space editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827) linearizes model behavior. Here prospective interaction is gated on retaining a *certified side-specific repair*, not aggregate future task loss or a linearized task-vector match.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Construct equal-output peer updates with different curvature along a signed repair. Compare endpoint gradient recomputation, independent blocker checks, and full unrolled verification. Reject if unrolled transport cannot save validation compute or if the apparent gain comes from granting additional repair steps.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B after both peers and after an ordinary new example attempts to renew the old side.

### D4-25 · Renew by relearning only the feature both peers leave plastic
- **Primitive:** Track a small space of features whose gradients remain effective under both pending training requests, and try the failing witness's pivot in that space. A joint blocker depends on the *lack of a plastic repair*, not on representational rank or the existence of two nominal routes.
- **Learning dependence:** The feature's response to a bounded number of actual learning steps may vanish while static rank and route reachability are unchanged. A verifier of the static export cannot construct this response space.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [O-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14152) assigns orthogonal task subspaces; [optimizer effects on plasticity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01559) study learning dynamics. This card explicitly avoids treating generic curvature or effective rank as a proxy: it requires a measured, sign-specific adaptation under both peer updates.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match two learned features' activations/rank but suppress learning of one through optimizer history. Compare projected gradients, equal-rank random basis and actual exact small-budget unrolling. Kill if adapting in the measured space cannot improve signed renewal more than one extra unbiased update at the same cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B accuracy, surviving opposition and untouched old-route power after both writes and the attempted pivot.

### D4-26 · Retain a learned counterexample to the blocker, not a new veto
- **Primitive:** Once a candidate joint blocker is found, seek a pair with the same static effect summaries but different learning interaction and show that it preserves a separating witness after real training. Learn a sparse blocker predicate that distinguishes the two cases and abstains outside the tested update family.
- **Learning dependence:** Input to the predicate includes a measured cross-training response, such as a change in a witness's repair gradient. Static confluence or graph summaries are deliberately identical for the closest positive and negative cases.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Invariant confluence](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf) checks safety of merging predefined operations; [Second-Order Influences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10177) estimates effects of successive sample selection. The candidate generalizes a *contrast-preserving training exception* to a joint veto, benchmarked against exact pair retraining.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Hold current scores, observed route graph and singleton effect summaries fixed, but vary training order or shared feature plasticity. Compare a statically learned veto, independent witness renewal plus D3-15, and exact paired retraining. Kill if the learned exception cannot admit more truly safe pairs at matched false admits and total compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Run the original four cells after C/D; audit old A/B, selective closure, and per-cohort report power in both the genuine blocker and its counterexample.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can training dynamics separate the four failure cells when fixed effect summaries cannot? (D4-10, D4-11, D4-21, D4-26)** Begin with the retained assay from row 486. Construct matched sibling learners whose frozen routes, old scores, and singleton learned effects agree but whose second trained step differs. Test incremental set membership and LP with refreshed actual feature rows, exact retrain-then-diagnose, and the best independent witness repair and blocker. Require a lower-cost sound distinction between a dying witness, a proved closure, and a joint loss of the only lawful diagnostic route. A frozen export that ties the learned assay closes this line.
2. **What state makes a signed pivot renewable across a learned feature change? (D4-01, D4-04, D4-05, D4-07, D4-09)** Find a family where a few retained gradient or optimizer directions can renew both sides with less held state than full re-search, and a matched example where the summary fails. Demand equal bytes, numeric precision, authorized data and old A/B scores against warm-started LP and the strongest refit/meta-learning control. Separate genuine lack of information from inadequate trainability.
3. **Which real training interaction makes the last report route fail? (D4-11–D4-20)** Intervene on features, optimizer state, or data collection independently until the proposed mediator is necessary in at least one matched case and absent from a non-hitting peer. Compare to PCGrad, one-step versus retargeted exact safe diagnosis, a Pacti quotient with the same known effects, and a confluence check. A fixed additive-delta or static-route explanation is enough to reject a card.
4. **Can one learning operation renew a witness and release a blocker more cheaply than the two separate operations? (D4-21, D4-22, D4-24, D4-25)** Include an explicitly uncoupled negative case; match combined state, optimizer steps and lawful queries. If the best independent stack matches cost and held-out retention, keep the parent objects separate rather than advancing a merged mechanism.
