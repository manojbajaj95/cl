# Check as work: let the first useful batch verify, then teach away the receipt

**Status:** Ideas only; two exact paper fixtures, no learner runs or GPU work. **Parent:** [Verified renewal](verified-renewal.md) ([PR #243][parent]), cards **10** and **16**. **Review seed:** [Flood triage, row 356][triage] scores both 3: make the incoming partner's first truthful C batch itself check the uncertainty claim, with no extra step or stored check receipt; choose successor batches so a younger shared carrier holds the defaulter's acquired skill and its old receipt can be dropped. **Read first:** [Idea ledger][ledger]: test ordinary old tasks, match starting margins, reject wins built into the fixture, and make a merge beat the strongest stack of its parts at equal budget.

**Verdict:** A useful batch can double as a check only if its ordinary observable outcome separates honest histories from harmful lies *before* anyone relies on the claim or erases the evidence. A younger carrier can replace the old dependency only if it preserves service when the old backing is actually reused, not just while both carriers are intact.
The joint bet is a choice of ordinary C word that improves both tests at matched C utility and clocks, beating a separate check plus the best receipt-retirement method. Neither seed is new in isolation: checking is system identification or segment verification; moving a skill is teaching or distillation. The proposed operation uses the same needed work to change what can be verified and what future service needs to remember.

## Contract

- **Fix the interfaces before blind arrivals.** A = original tasks, Bᵢ = installed writes including the defaulter's useful fragment B_d, C = installed and incoming successor tasks, W = paired service. Freeze truthful data, precedence, optimizer and clocks, ordinary observable outputs and their precision, admitted continuations, held-out slices and acquisition-anchored floors. A batch must have been needed for C without the audit; changing its order is legal only inside that menu. No audit-only examples, fabricated gradients, resets, private slot reads, extra forward passes, or retry-until-pass. The verifier must trust or independently obtain the ordinary observation; a publisher's self-reported update size does not authenticate itself.
- **A check is a decision, not a transcript.** At the handoff, consume the live uncertainty claim against the observation already exposed by the first committed C step. For the frozen menu and decision rule, let H be lawful pre-states inside the claim and L be reachable out-of-claim states on which a named backed obligation would fail. Require separation of their **observable** first-step images after measurement noise and quantization, or refuse the claimed right. Truthful data alone does not guarantee separation. A failing batch need not be undone: admit it only if the step itself is safe on the union of plausible histories, and route the suspect B_d certificate to the parent's clearing path before it can be spent or evidence is lost. No extra step or durable receipt **for the check**; the incoming claim must still be available when consumed, and any pre-existing live entitlement record is charged as before. Do not keep a replay log, digest, decision bit, or reconstructed proof specifically for historical checking. If consequences require later evidence, this is outside the receipt-free variant.
- **Retire the defaulter's receipt, not the skill.** This is a separate deletion: R_d is live until the claim is resolved, downstream obligations no longer consult it, and every publicly possible post-transfer state has a common admitted continuation that delivers B_d and W by their deadlines without the old backing. Give the younger carrier genuine installed or incoming C duties too. Test a lawful reuse of the old backing, not just hidden deletion of R_d while the old model route still serves. Freeze later defaults, rollback boundaries, lawful reuse and suffixes in scope. If rollout is finite, state expiry and what safely takes over; do not widen the language after deletion without a new independently justified guarantee.
- **Compare at equal service.** Use identical legal menus, retained views, inference and optimizer access. Stack the cheapest lawful public-consistency check ([#216][consistency]), an authenticated claim chain (integrity alone does not establish truth), [Proof-of-Learning-style][pol] segment replay or a separate audit, then the best of shared constrained fitting, [GEM][gem], [DER][der], fresh-head [distillation][distill], [#163][witness] witness replacement and [#226][escrow] backing reassignment. Let the stack select its best order, reuse ordinary statistics where legal, and safely collect its own dead records. Include reset and fresh reservations, unchanged receipt-backed service, each seed alone, and an exact same-information planner as a ceiling. Match C progress and useful pairs, acquisition-anchored A/B/installed-C floors, deadlines and starting per-item margins. Report false acceptance, false refusal, safe useful single arrivals, timely pairs, peak and cumulative bytes (including claims, logs, scratch, routing and pinned support), verification computation, optimizer steps and clocks separately. Test at least three adaptive defaults and a later blind successful pair; do not confuse zero extra optimizer steps with zero planning or checking cost.

## Two exact paper checks

### W1. A useful opener can separate or alias without changing the held-out C gain

Heavy-ball with unit step: `m′=m/2+∇L(w), w′=w−m′`. Start at `(x,y)=(0,0)` with `m_y=0` and `m_x=1` (honest) or `2` (lie). These aliases are reachable from zero by two earlier x-gradients `−s, 3s/2` for `s=1,2`. The claim `m_x=1` backs a separate idle-x fallback promising `|x′|≤1/2`; its true next `|x′|` would be 1 for the lie. Freeze the ordinary serving read to squared update size and take the first C batch from two truthful quadratics sharing teacher `(1,1)`, `L_a=a(x−1)²/2+(y−1)²/2`. The held-out C score is the loss on y; both options reduce it from `1/2` to zero. The ordinary A panel here is only `|x|≤1/2`, which holds at the opener on all four paths.

| C opener | Honest `(x′, y′)` | Lie `(x′, y′)` | Ordinary squared update, honest / lie |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a=1/2` | `(0,1)` | `(−1/2,1)` | `1 / 5/4` |
| `a=3/4` | `(1/4,1)` | `(−1/4,1)` | `17/16 / 17/16` |

The first orderable batch detects and the second cannot, even though both teach y. Rounding squared size to the nearest integer breaks even the first check. Full signed x read would separate both arms; if lawful at the same cost, it kills the toy's batch-selection advantage. This is observability under a stipulated read, not evidence that update size is enough for an open-world claim, that the fallback can already be cleared, or that the check has broad old-task retention.

### W2. Reusing the old backing exposes whether transfer really happened

Use the same heavy-ball law. Old backing `o=1`, younger shared weight `y=0`, both slots zero. Toy B_d score `max(o,y)` has acquisition floor 1; held-out C targets `y=1`; toy A requires `0≤y≤3/2`. First take a truthful C step `a(y−1)²/2`, then a required unrelated reuse batch `o²/2` (target zero for a new job). Initially only o can meet B_d's floor on its own.

| C teaching before reuse | `(o,y)` before reuse | `(o,y)` after reuse | B_d after reuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a=1` | `(1,1)` | `(0,3/2)` | `3/2`, passes |
| `a=1/2` | `(1,1/2)` | `(0,3/4)` | `3/4`, fails |

Both pass B_d before reuse, both make C progress before reuse, and both keep A at both cuts. The strong arm lands with `m_y=−1/2` after reuse. Two more ordinary C steps on `(y−1)²/2` give y of `5/4` then `7/8`; o coasts to `−1/2` then `−3/4`, so B_d fails on the second step. A stays safe across those two steps. A one-cut certificate therefore cannot authorize deletion for this longer suffix. This deliberately stipulated read is a test of the deletion criterion, not a matched-C win or a new training method: the arms differ in C loss at the teaching cut and a plain fit with the same strong batch ties. In a first real discriminator, hold C utility fixed and require shared features and rare B_d contexts not encoded in the teaching batch.

## A. Make the first useful update carry the check

### 01. A truthful opener consumes the claim — primitive candidate (parent #10)
**Mechanism.** Bind acceptance to a prediction for the ordinary output of the first required C step; consume the claim in that transition, with no audit batch or stored check receipt. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Proof-of-Learning][pol] checks training segments by replay; the candidate operation is a one-use check on a bounded uncertainty claim inside live training, not training provenance. **Cheapest first test.** W1 plus a separate probe and segment replay at equal C gain; refuse when only the aliasing opener is legal. **Old-task retention check.** A, B_d fallback, earlier Bᵢ and installed C after acceptance and rejection.

### 02. Trade off separation against the weakest old margin — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose an opener by the minimum distance between honest and harmful images, subject to the *whole* reachable set sharing safety and C utility. A maximally diagnostic opener that jeopardizes one old slice cannot be used. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Active fault diagnosis selects informative actions; the candidate is diagnosis under a constraint that the same action is a required continual-learning write. **Cheapest first test.** Three quadratic candidates with informativeness inversely related to retention; compare Fisher-information selection plus a [GEM][gem]-style guard and same-information exact search. **Old-task retention check.** Match A/B/installed-C starting margins and score their worst slices after the first step.

### 03. Check the bad consequence, not the whole hidden slot — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Group pre-states by which fallback or paired promise they break; permit overlaps inside a class and separate only outcomes that require different immediate clearing actions. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Set-membership diagnosis and [#232][shrink] harmful under-reporting already define bad states; this proposes compiling a service decision into the measurement of an ordinary C transition. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate four aliases with two different failed fallbacks and a benign out-of-claim state; compare full slot identification and [#216][consistency] public consistency. **Old-task retention check.** Every implicated B fallback, including the benign alias, plus A and installed C.

### 04. Let an existing output carry a signed contrast — improvement
**Mechanism.** When a signed pair of output changes is already emitted as part of the ordinary C interface, choose a batch whose contrast cancels public nuisance drift and exposes the harmful alias. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Residual parity checks do this already; the potential improvement is reusing exactly those reads without a new probe. **Cheapest first test.** Add a common offset to W1's two-channel variant and compare absolute size, signed contrast and a dedicated read at matched precision. **Old-task retention check.** Ordinary A/B/C readouts in both channels before and after the nuisance shift.

### 05. Match the check to the published precision — improvement
**Mechanism.** Select batches whose honest and harmful images occupy different bins of the existing C report, allowing bounded rounding and drift. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust quantized diagnosis is established; the question is how much useful C supply survives the quantized constraint. **Cheapest first test.** Sweep predeclared bin widths and offset in W1; compare best legal opener, higher-precision paid reporting and non-checking order. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed-C near their margins for each precision and any refused batch.

### 06. Commit the opener before the claimant can tailor the hidden state to it — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Seal the incoming claim and backing first, then use an ordinary public draw among admissible C openers without retaining a draw record for the check. If any opener can miss a lie, predeclare a probabilistic bound rather than claiming deterministic verification. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Challenge-response and [Proof-of-Learning][pol] use delayed challenge selection; the challenge here must itself deliver useful C and expire immediately. **Cheapest first test.** Three openers and three harmful alias choices with cyclic overlaps; compare announced deterministic scheduling, a private-choice ceiling and a paid random challenge. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/C on every draw, with worst-case rather than average floors.

### 07. Make failure useful without making it spendable — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Accept C progress on a safe write that rejects the incoming claim, but never let its conditional entitlement go live; clear B_d through the independent route. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Optimistic transactions and the parent's clearing step already abort rights; this splits the truth of a claim from the usefulness of the actual training transition without reverting C. **Cheapest first test.** Overlap a mistaken claim with one lawful C opener and a later blind arrival; compare full rollback and a safe-write guard plus separate audit. **Old-task retention check.** B_d under clearing, every earlier Bᵢ, A and installed C immediately and one batch later.

### 08. Prove no-op clipping is not a valid check reorder — improvement
**Mechanism.** Reuse the same C step on both sides of a proposed optimizer shrink and look for collision of honest and harmful outputs before allowing reordering. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#232][shrink] and parent V1 already show detect-before-shrink; this is a mandatory test for an embedded check under a real legal batch menu. **Cheapest first test.** Add an honest no-op hull clamp to W1 before the opener; compare orders, reset and unshrunk C progress. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/C and the fallback certificate invalidated on the merged history.

### 09. Audit-path null: use the best lawful passive checker — improvement
**Mechanism.** Give the baseline every ordinary read the joint method has, including post-step activations if already emitted, and permit a fixed passive verifier with no special training order. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Public-consistency checking ([#216][consistency]); this asks whether choice of first batch matters at all, rather than claiming a new check. **Cheapest first test.** Run W1 with signed x lawful and unlawful; test refusal on the indistinguishable case. **Old-task retention check.** Identical per-cut A/B/C panels and actual B_d fallback.

### 10. Find the boundary with no possible receipt-free check — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Construct a menu-wide indistinguishability certificate: for each legal opener find an honest/harmful pair giving the same observation but demanding different decisions. The contract then requires a paid check or refusal. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Observability lower bounds are standard; the candidate is a refusal certificate over a constrained *useful* batch menu and declared backing obligations. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two truthful openers with different overlapping pairs; add signed output or one paid probe as an escape, and distinguish per-opener collisions from a common collision that defeats both together. **Old-task retention check.** Match A/B/C at refusal and after the baseline's lawful recovery.

## B. Make younger shared work carry the defaulter's skill

### 11. Transfer the floor, then delete the receipt — primitive candidate (parent #16)
**Mechanism.** Select ordinary successor batches whose young shared route serves B_d after actual reuse of its old backing, then retire R_d after successful checking or independently revalidated clearing. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [Distillation][distill], [#163][witness] and [#226][escrow] replace teachers, witnesses or backing; the candidate operation discharges a live provenance dependency without keeping a teacher or permanent head. **Cheapest first test.** W2 with actual reuse and a blind suffix, compared with receipt-backed service and shared constrained distillation at matched C utility. **Old-task retention check.** B_d rare slices at both cuts, A, other Bᵢ and installed C.

### 12. Transfer a bundle whose members cannot migrate separately — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose C work that makes two B skills jointly realizable on one carrier, then release both old reservations together only if their shared fallback remains feasible. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Multi-task distillation shares a student; the bet is simultaneous closure of cross-backed service obligations that neither one-at-a-time release can satisfy. **Cheapest first test.** Two old carriers with overlapping fallback obligations and a shared rank-limited learner; compare joint distillation under the same fallback constraints and sequential [#226][escrow] reassignment. **Old-task retention check.** Each B slice individually and the alternating B/W workload, plus ordinary A/installed C.

### 13. Locate the carrier by transplanting only the learned change — improvement
**Mechanism.** In the offline test, separately cut old support and transplant the putative young change into a matched host; measure necessity and sufficiency without counting either intervention as lawful online work. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Mechanistic ablation and causal interchange are established; this prevents confusing an untouched old route with real migration. **Cheapest first test.** A small shared ReLU learner, with equal-sized sham cuts and transplants; compare [DER][der] and selected C orders. **Old-task retention check.** Held-out A/B/installed-C on the ordinary reuse path and report intervention damage separately.

### 14. Replace fragile inheritance with a common fallback — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select C batches that leave one public continuation available across every residual post-check alias, even if their individually best B_d actions differ. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust control and [#226][escrow] prep quotients cover aliases; here the common action needs to survive withdrawal of the old backing while using the young shared carrier. **Cheapest first test.** Two aliases with equal visible B_d scores and opposite hidden drift; compare point-estimate migration, worst-case constrained distillation and receipt-backed action choice. **Old-task retention check.** Worst-alias B_d, A and installed C throughout the common suffix.

### 15. Teach the shared feature behind the rare slice — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Among equally useful C items, favor those that move a shared feature used by B_d's rare slice; use no B_d labels during the write and keep evaluation slices hidden. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [GEM][gem] and [DER][der] keep old information explicit; the bet is acquisition-floor transfer from ordinary counterpart work, not selecting replay with a different score. **Cheapest first test.** Two-factor classification with common and rare old contexts; separate naturally aligned from marginally matched but nonaligned C pools, and give a same-byte replay baseline its best rare examples. **Old-task retention check.** B_d rare and common gaps separately, A, other Bᵢ and installed C after old reuse.

### 16. Require the next renewal to preserve the new carrier — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Select the migration word against a public envelope of next-cycle writes and defaults; deletion requires the young carrier to keep its promise even while it is used as shared backing again. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** [#226][escrow] bounds a rolling escrow; the candidate here is a renewal-closed learned carrier invariant, not bounded entitlement by itself. **Cheapest first test.** Three blind default cycles alternating first partners; compare independent per-cycle migration, best same-budget [DER][der] and a reserved carrier. **Old-task retention check.** Every installed Bᵢ and A/C at every reuse and default, including the oldest generation.

### 17. Spend on the bottleneck that outlives the old right — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Move only the B_d capability whose certified future is still receipt-sensitive; let robustly retained outputs stay on existing shared weights. Select C examples by whether they remove the last live reader of R_d, not by average imitation error. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Targeted [distillation][distill] minimizes chosen errors and garbage collection frees unreachable state; the candidate learning operation changes reachability of proof dependencies at a fixed service interface. **Cheapest first test.** Two B outputs whose gradients compete, only one receipt-sensitive; compare full and slice-targeted shared distillation plus normal safe garbage collection. **Old-task retention check.** Both B outputs and all A/installed-C panels after the old route is reused.

### 18. Replace per-receipt proofs with a reusable carrier rule — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Certify a public family of useful C words that migrate any B skill in a specified service class. At retirement, the same rule applies from the live public state without a per-B retained proof, and outside the class it refuses. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Inductive invariants and [#226][escrow] continuation equivalence already compress proofs; the test is whether the class rule can describe learned substitution while retaining independent C work and rare B guarantees. **Cheapest first test.** Two migrated jobs and a blind third outside a predeclared low-rank class; charge the rule, class labels and any routes against per-job certificates. **Old-task retention check.** A/C and every B job after reuse, including the refused job on its retained fallback.

## C. Make the two moves earn their coupling

### 19. Let the separating opener create the route for migration — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Choose an opener whose live update both separates and moves the young carrier into a region where later ordinary C batches can finish B_d migration, unlike the most diagnostic opener alone. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Dual control and [#241][loan] ordinary-work handoffs already reuse actions; the new claim is that separability and later proof-dependency removal share a reachable region under a fixed deadline. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate two-step truthful words on a coupled optimizer host with diagnostic-only, transfer-only and joint feasible words; compare both seed arms and the best non-interacting stack, all with the same menu. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C at both steps, plus rare B_d and W after reuse.

### 20. Reject early without pinning a receipt until the carrier is ready — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Arrange first-step check and late migration so live entitlement status—not a historical decision log—suffices across an interrupted suffix. On rejection, the independently cleared obligation must already be in force before the claimed route becomes unavailable. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Transactional compaction and optimistic clearing track decisions; the candidate is a safe split between an ephemeral check and a later learned retirement, with no check-specific persistent evidence. **Cheapest first test.** Enumerate defaults immediately before and after the check, arrival of a second partner mid-prep and reuse before migration; compare safe transactional garbage collection with the same transition boundaries. **Old-task retention check.** A/C and all B floors at interruption, restart and later reuse, with W delivered on time.

### 21. Choose a word that budgets observability for the following partner — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Teach B_d into the young carrier without placing the next partner's useful opener in a state where honest and harmful images necessarily collide. Check and migration may change which later aliases are reachable. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Persistent excitation maintains system identifiability; here it must arise from useful successive work while preserving retirement and future admission. **Cheapest first test.** Three-hop 2D host where two equal-utility migration orders differ in next-hop observation overlap; compare greedy local checks, separate audit plus replay, and exact planning. **Old-task retention check.** A/C and every Bᵢ across all hops, including after actual reuse of each retired route.

### 22. Detect harmless completion but dangerous renewal — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** Compile the first-batch check against actions that may become available *because* a carrier was migrated. A lie that leaves C progress intact can still invalidate the newly freed reservation. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Parent GPR11 already checks harmful claims before shrink; the proposed extension ties the harmful set to specific post-release capabilities, not merely today's B_d score. **Cheapest first test.** Two states tied on migration and current service, but one fails a blind pair enabled by release; compare a current-floor checker and a continuation-aware stack. **Old-task retention check.** A/B/installed C before reuse and at the blind pair, particularly the fragment no longer backed by R_d.

### 23. Use a shared-carrier cut to test failure of the merge — improvement
**Mechanism.** Compare coupled scheduling to its best independent stack under identical observations, costs and C utility; intervene by replacing the first update with an equal-utility truthful opener that changes only the tested separation or downstream reachability. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** A matched interaction test, not a new learner; if the stack or the simple substitution ties, keep only the surviving part. **Cheapest first test.** Exact two-coordinate search with successful and impossible menus, authenticated claims, passive or paid checking, best safe deletion and legal reset with its lost evidence scored. **Old-task retention check.** Match all A/B/C margins at every cut and require per-slice noninferiority under reuse before counting any resource win.

### 24. Separate an impossible coverage target from a bad scheduler — primitive candidate
**Mechanism.** For blind arrivals, construct a frontier of menus with a safe separating opener and a reachable receipt-free suffix, then issue the strongest supportable admission guarantee rather than silently excluding hard requests. **Novelty vs nearest prior art.** Robust viability and [#226][escrow] admission track feasible futures; this adds an ephemeral evidence test and a learned substitution obligation to the same feasibility decision. **Cheapest first test.** Exhaust a small finite menu with independently varying first-step collisions and carrier capacity; compare online selection to exact same-information and privileged hindsight ceilings. **Old-task retention check.** Report A/B/C on accepted and refused branches and retain ordinary fallback service for refused C arrivals.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Is there a useful C word whose first step separates harmful aliases and opens otherwise unreachable receipt-free service at the same budget?** Start with cards 19 and 23. Kill the merge if the best passive/paid checker plus shared migration stack matches detection, deadlines and reuse at matched retention and C utility.
2. **Which harmful claims can ordinary first-step observations actually disambiguate?** Cards 03, 05, 06 and 10. How much blind coverage survives finite precision, nuisance, adaptive alias choice and deadlines? If lawful collisions are widespread, state the restricted menu where the premise holds rather than add a hidden audit.
3. **What is the broadest suffix for which the younger shared carrier can replace R_d without forgetting rare B_d behavior, even under another default?** Cards 14–18. Adversarial lawful reuse, shared capacity and changed optimizer drift need testing before deletion; a good one-cut score is insufficient.
4. **Can rejection remain operationally safe without retaining history?** Cards 07, 20 and 22. The check must not slip behind a spend, leave a later party needing a transcript, or serialize useful arrivals enough that a paid challenge window wins.

Prior-art comparisons are scoped, not priority claims. HF Papers surfaced GEM and DER; the arXiv abstract verifies the Proof-of-Learning reference. The repository comparisons are the closest baselines for obligation scope and renewal.
