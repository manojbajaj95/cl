# Verifier-backed forks → can they compose to predict effects on unseen tasks?

Parent: [`tree/model-rl-ood.md`, PR #51](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/51/files), ranked branch 1 ("verified-fork algebra") · Depth 3 · Sibling: [`tree/rl-discriminating-experiments.md`, PR #57](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/57/files) (branch 3)

A **fork** here is PR #51's unit: at one decision state `s`, two concrete alternatives `a`, `a'`, each completed by a fixed continuation policy `π`, scored by a verifier, giving a measured effect `Δ(s; a, a' | π, V)`. The question is whether a pile of such measurements on training tasks can be **composed** to predict the sign of `Δ` on a task none of them was measured on, and say when it cannot.

## Root cause

**A fork records *that* an action helps under one verifier and one continuation policy, not *through which check* or *which part of the policy* the help travels. With neither of those indices, there is no join key between tasks. Composition then falls back to similarity interpolation, and the marginal effects that are measured do not identify the composed effect even in principle.**

Three separate gaps, each enough on its own:

1. **No join key (what to compose *on*).** Current tree-structured RL computes fork values and spends them immediately as advantages ([ASCT 2609.35215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35215), [PTGPO 2610.14737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14737), [hindsight tree credit 2611.02706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02706), [belief-shift branching 2609.11061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11061)). The scalar `Δ` is indexed by a concrete prefix, so a new task shares no index with it. ASCT reports transfer studies of the *trained policy*, not reuse of its fork tables.
2. **Marginal effects do not identify joint effects (what composition *needs*).** If an unseen task's verifier is `V₁ ∧ V₂`, its success under `a` depends on the *coupling* of the two checks, which single-check forks never observe. Worked example below: the sign of the composed effect is not identified. This is the finite-sample face of what composition algebras assume away. Successor features and the Boolean task algebra need rewards to be linear or Boolean in shared features, and the BTA collapse fails in stochastic MDPs, where optimal composition can need exponentially many policies ([2606.04053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04053)). [PoEM 2609.30226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30226) combines policies in log-prob space, exactly only when the new reward is a linear combination of old ones. Measured non-additivity is real but **input- and format-conditioned**: a task-vector interaction contrast moves from +6.9 to +0.3 points when the prompt is wrapped in the training template ([2608.09490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09490)). Transfer is **directed**: A helps B while B hurts A ([2609.39702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39702)). RL shows a decomposed-to-composed asymmetry: training on parts does not reliably transfer to composed tasks, but the reverse does ([2609.19465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19465)).
3. **Effects expire with the policy (what composition is *of*).** `Δ` is a difference of `Q^π`, so it is a property of the task *and* the continuation policy. Once the learner trains on forks, `π` moves and every stored `Δ` is stale. Transportability theory gives conditions for carrying an effect across domains, but only with known causal structure, or with target samples bounded by the size of the smallest composable circuit ([2512.22777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22777), [2611.14066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14066), [causal abstractions 2608.15645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15645)). No current method transports across a task shift and a policy shift at once. [One-step Bellman alignment 2601.21924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21924) corrects source targets for task mismatch alone.

A deeper reason the gap does not close by itself: [2611.03861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03861) proves that any observational predictive advantage over the direct-cause predictor requires dependence on non-causal inputs, and gives a lower bound on intervention-response error. Forks are interventions on the *agent's action*, not on the *task mechanism*. They therefore escape the bound for the action but inherit it for "which mechanism made the action good". A fork model trained to predict `Δ` well on source tasks will use the same shortcuts PR #51 wanted forks to remove, one level up.

### Worked example (arithmetic, not an experiment)

Two checks. Action `a`: `P(V₁)=0.9, P(V₂)=0.6`. Action `a'`: `P(V₁)=0.7, P(V₂)=0.8`. Forks on the single-check tasks say `a` wins `V₁` by 0.2 and `a'` wins `V₂` by 0.2. On the unseen task `V₁ ∧ V₂`:

| Coupling of the two checks | `P(V₁∧V₂ | a)` | `P(V₁∧V₂ | a')` | Sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent | 0.54 | 0.56 | `a'` wins |
| Comonotone (max overlap, Fréchet upper) | 0.60 | 0.70 | `a'` wins |
| Countermonotone (Fréchet lower) | 0.50 | 0.50 | tie |
| `a`'s checks comonotone, `a'`'s countermonotone | 0.60 | 0.50 | `a` wins |

The intervals `[0.50, 0.60]` and `[0.50, 0.70]` overlap, so the marginal forks do not identify the sign. The *joint* fork at one state does: one rollout pair scored on both checks. Composition needs a small second-order object (the coupling) and a rule for when the first-order one suffices. Every idea below either supplies that object or a coordinate system in which it vanishes.

### Nearest baselines a composed-fork predictor must beat

- kNN over fork tables by state embedding (similarity interpolation).
- PoEM-style log-prob policy combination.
- A per-task transfer map ([2609.39702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39702)) or a kernel task surrogate ([2602.03783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03783)).
- PR #51's grammar-aware canonicalization.
- Fresh verifier calls on the target at the same budget as the forks used.

Ledger caveats that apply: three cl predictor pilots died because the target barely varied, so gate on effect variance across held-out compositions exceeding seed variance first. B3's lesson-interference predictor lost to function-vector cosine. "Composition contracts" was killed because atomic outputs label hybrids for free. Here the composed target is a *causal effect under a verifier*, which atomic outputs do not label.

## Raw ideas (unfiltered)

Each: mechanism · **why new** · *cheapest first test (with retention check)*. Shared testbed unless stated: a CPU string-function DSL in the style of [2509.25123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25123). Tasks are verifiers built as conjunctions/compositions of predicates over `f(g(x))`, the policy is a tiny router or Qwen2.5-0.5B with a constrained action menu, and held-out tasks are unseen predicate combinations. "Retention check" = sign accuracy on the old tasks' forks, plus old-task success after the learner trains on the composed predictions, on an A→B→A stream.

1. **Clause-indexed forks.** Score each fork rollout on every predicate of the verifier separately and store the vector `Δ⃗` of per-clause effects, not the scalar. Predict a new task by composing its clauses' entries. **Why new:** the verifier's own syntax becomes the join key, so forks gain a task-independent coordinate for free. *Test: 6 predicates, train on single and pair tasks, predict sign on held-out triples. Compare scalar-fork kNN at equal rollouts. Retention: clause signs on A after training on composed-B targets.*
2. **Fréchet fork calculus.** From per-clause forks, compute the guaranteed interval of each composed effect (Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds for ∧/∨, chained for sequences). Commit to a sign only when the intervals separate; otherwise request the *one* joint fork that closes the gap. **Why new:** a sound "can't predict" verdict with a costed repair, instead of a calibrated-looking guess. *Test: fraction of held-out compositions decided, error rate on decided ones (should be ~0), and verifier calls vs. always-measure. Retention: unchanged by construction; check that bounds stay sound after policy updates.*
3. **Coupling sketches.** Store a tiny per-state estimate of pairwise clause correlation under each action (the second-order object from the worked example) and compose with it. **Why new:** an explicit "interaction memory" for verifier checks, not data-example interactions ([2605.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15675)). *Test: sign accuracy on held-out triples with independence, sketch, and oracle coupling. Retention: sketch error drift over the stream.*
4. **Log-odds effect space.** Compose effects in `log Q/V` (PR #51's Bayes identity) or per-clause logit space, where independent checks add exactly. Measure additivity residual in probability, logit and `log Q/V` space. **Why new:** picks the coordinate that makes the fork algebra linear, rather than assuming linear rewards. *Test: residual of the pair-from-singles prediction in each space. Dead if probability space is as good. Retention: residual on A after B training.*
5. **Learned coarse-graining for additivity.** Learn a causal abstraction (map token-level forks onto a few strategy variables) whose objective is the *composition residual* on training compositions, not reconstruction. **Why new:** "find the variables in which effects compose" as the learning target ([2608.15645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15645) assumes the abstraction is given). *Test: residual on held-out compositions vs. PCA/k-means abstractions of the same forks. Retention: abstraction stability across stream phases.*
6. **Fork tensors with a policy axis.** Measure each fork under 2–3 continuation policies (current, older checkpoint, temperature 1.5) and store `∂Δ/∂π` along that path. Predict `Δ` after the learner's next update. **Why new:** an effect with a declared policy-drift slope, so it expires predictably instead of silently. *Test: predict post-update fork signs vs. stale `Δ` and fresh refit. Retention: old-task fork drift predicted vs. measured.*
7. **Task × policy factorization.** Fit `Δ(s,a,a'|π) ≈ Σ_k τ_k(s,a,a')·ρ_k(π)` across checkpoints. `τ` is a task-side effect, portable across learners; `ρ` is a policy-side gain. **Why new:** separates "what the task rewards" from "what this learner can exploit", which is the transport problem in gap 3. *Test: hold out a checkpoint, predict its fork signs from 5 forks plus `ρ` fitting. Compare PoEM low-rank. Retention: `τ` reused across A→B→A.*
8. **Difference-in-differences forks.** Run the same action pair on two tasks with synchronized seeds (common random numbers, cf. [counterfactual quotient models 2608.22092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22092)) and record how the *task difference* modulates the effect. **Why new:** the measured object is the interaction "task component × action", the term composition needs, at matched noise. *Test: variance and sign accuracy of DiD vs. separately measured forks at equal calls. Retention: DiD reuse after B.*
9. **Fork epistasis graph.** At two decisions in one trajectory, run the 2×2 factorial of alternatives. Store the interaction (epistasis). Compose only along decision pairs with near-zero epistasis, and flag the rest. **Why new:** a sparse map of which credit is separable, learned from experiments, not assumed by per-step advantage. *Test: predict which held-out compositions break additivity (AUROC) vs. state-overlap heuristics. Retention: epistasis graph stability.*
10. **Sheaf of local effects.** Treat each task's fork table as a section over its states. Overlapping tasks must agree (glue) on shared states after the abstraction map. The gluing residual (an obstruction) says where a global effect model cannot exist. **Why new:** non-composability as a computable topological invariant of the fork collection. *Test: compute the residual on training task overlaps; does it predict held-out composition failures better than per-task error? Retention: obstruction stays localized after updates.*
11. **Transfer-matrix composition for sequential tasks.** For `f` then `g`, summarize stage effects as matrices over a small set of intermediate-state classes. The composed effect is their product, with the abstraction from idea 5. **Why new:** the physics transfer-matrix method applied to credit, so depth-k compositions come from 1-step forks. *Test: predict depth-3 composition effects from depth-1 forks vs. kNN and fresh calls. Retention: depth-1 matrices after depth-3 training.*
12. **Certified-orbit transport.** Record with each fork the set of verifier-preserving transforms (renaming, cue swaps) under which its sign held. A new task inherits the sign only if it lies in the group closure of certified transforms. **Why new:** a fork carries a domain of validity, giving a symbolic transport check instead of a similarity score. *Test: precision on the inherited set and coverage vs. embedding-distance thresholds. Retention: certificates re-verified after B.*
13. **Verifier-code-conditioned effect model.** A small model reads the verifier's *source code* plus state and pair and predicts `Δ`. Trained on forks; evaluated on unseen verifier programs. **Why new:** conditions on the task's specification, not its samples, so an unseen task is a new program, not a new distribution. *Test: held-out verifier programs, sign accuracy vs. the same model conditioned on 10 sample inputs. Retention: accuracy on old programs after adding new ones.*
14. **Effect Jacobian of writes.** For a candidate weight update (one RL step on task A), predict the change in fork effects on task B from per-fork gradient inner products. Forks become the probe set for transfer and forgetting. **Why new:** forgetting measured as a change in *causal effects*, not loss or accuracy, which is the quantity that decides later choices. *Test: predicted vs. measured sign change of B-forks after A-steps, against gradient-cosine on B loss ([2608.03573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03573)-style). This idea is itself a retention check.*
15. **Fork basis as a retention contract.** Low-rank-decompose the effect matrix (tasks × action-pair types) and keep the k forks that span it. Every consolidated write must preserve their signs. **Why new:** a minimal behavioural test set *derived from* effect geometry, not a replay buffer or static probes. *Test: k-fork basis vs. equal-size random forks and replay as a write gate, measuring forgetting caught per verifier call.*
16. **Composition fuzzer.** Search the space of unseen compositions for the one where the fork model is most confidently wrong (verifier-checked). The archive of failures maps the composability boundary. **Why new:** targets the *effect model's* closure, not a rule-vs-rival split as in PR #57. *Test: failures found per call vs. random composition sampling. Retention: re-check archived failures after updates.*
17. **Target-directed fork acquisition.** Given one named unseen composition, choose which training-task forks to run to minimize predicted-effect variance *on that target*. **Why new:** active learning of the measurement *basis* for a composition, not for task performance. *Test: calls to reach a target sign accuracy vs. uniform forks and uncertainty sampling. Retention: chosen forks reused later.*
18. **Skills ship with effect tables.** In an agent skill library, each skill carries the clause-indexed fork table measured when it was called. Composing skills composes tables, and a mismatch on first use flags the composition. **Why new:** measured semantics as a skill's type signature for continual agents. *Test: skill-composition success prediction in the cl skill-memory testbed vs. description similarity. Retention: tables survive base-model swaps or are flagged.*
19. **Self-forecast head.** Train the policy to predict its own fork outcomes before running them. Prediction error on a new task triggers forking and marks the task novel. **Why new:** the learner owns a model of its own interventional effects, a self-model of credit. *Test: does self-forecast error rank held-out compositions by true effect-model error? Retention: self-forecast accuracy on old tasks after B.*
20. **Cross-model fork transport.** Forks measured with model M₁'s continuation predict M₂'s after an upgrade through the `τ/ρ` split (idea 7): `τ` stays, `ρ` is refit with a few forks. **Why new:** continual learning across model versions via task-side effects, not via weights or lessons (task-vector transport is prior art; see memory). *Test: Qwen2.5-0.5B→1.5B, refit `ρ` with 5 forks, sign accuracy vs. refitting all. Retention: M₁ effects still hold for M₁.*
21. **Imagined advantages.** Use composed effect predictions as RL advantages on unseen tasks with no verifier, and spot-check a random 10% with the verifier as an unbiased correction (control variate as in PR #51). **Why new:** training on a task you have never verified, with a built-in bias meter. *Test: OOD success per verifier call vs. GRPO and PR #51's corrected proxy on the same task. Retention: A-task success on A→B→A.*
22. **Fork-to-fork influence graph.** Estimate which training forks, when learned from, flip which test forks (second-order interaction-aware influence over forks, cf. [2605.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15675)). **Why new:** attribution between interventions, not data examples. It predicts directed transfer at the decision level. *Test: predict asymmetric transfer pairs vs. a transfer map ([2609.39702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39702)). Retention: graph reused across stream phases.*
23. **Effect laws with domains.** Compile fork clusters into symbolic laws ("in states with feature φ, a beats a' by δ±ε on clause c"). Compose laws logically, and contradictions between laws on a new task signal OOD before acting. **Why new:** a compact, inspectable, contradiction-checkable effect theory, beyond a learned regressor. *Test: law precision and coverage on held-out compositions vs. decision-tree regression on the same forks. Retention: laws revised, not overwritten, under B.*
24. **Weight-resident effect adapter.** A separate low-rank adapter is trained so that its readout equals the predicted `Δ⃗`, while the policy stays frozen. Composing tasks composes these adapters, which are judged only on effect prediction. **Why new:** a weight-space object whose composition semantics is checkable by forks, unlike task vectors judged on accuracy ([2608.09490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09490)). *Test: effect-adapter sum vs. per-task regression on held-out pairs; interaction contrast under template change. Retention: old adapters' predictions unchanged.*

## Ranked deeper sub-questions

Ranked by how likely each leads to a new primitive.

1. **Is there a coordinate system (per-clause log-odds, `log Q/V`, or a learned abstraction) in which verifier-backed fork effects add across task compositions, and does the leftover residual flag exactly the compositions that cannot be predicted?** Ideas 1, 2, 4, 5, 10. The primitive would be an *effect algebra* with a sound abstain rule. Cheapest test: the DSL with 6 predicates. First gate on held-out effect variance > seed variance. Dead if probability-space kNN ties the best space on held-out triples, or the residual's AUROC for failures is < 0.7.
2. **Can a fork be split into a task-side effect and a policy-side gain, so it survives the learner's own updates and a model upgrade?** Ideas 6, 7, 20, 14. The primitive would be a *portable effect* `τ` that outlives the policy that measured it, which gap 3 says nothing provides today. Dead if refitting `ρ` with 5 forks is no better than stale `Δ` after one update.
3. **Is the second-order coupling between checks sparse and cheap enough to store, so a few joint forks plus marginals decide most composed signs?** Ideas 3, 8, 9, 2. The primitive would be an *interaction memory* for verifiers. Dead if closing the Fréchet gap needs joint forks on most held-out compositions (> 50%).
4. **Can composed fork predictions stand in for verifier calls on unseen tasks without OOD credit bias or forgetting?** Ideas 15, 21, 22. This is closer to an improvement (a cheaper estimator) unless 1 or 2 succeed. Dead if the spot-check correction shows bias beyond seed CI, or A-task retention drops vs. GRPO.
5. **Can the verifier's source code, rather than its samples, index effects?** Ideas 13, 18, 23. Specification-conditioned credit is a moonshot until a DSL with many verifier programs exists. Dead if conditioning on code is no better than 10 sample inputs.
