## What might be new?

[Heegard–Berger coding](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1985.1057112) (rate-distortion and lossless coding with multiple decoders having different side information) and [Kaspi / Gray–Wyner](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1974.1055283) already price a *fixed* multi-decoder joint distribution; budgeted multi-objective scheduling and submodular/knapsack selection already pick one item that scores well on two *independent* objectives. Both are conceded as controls. On the dual-site side (`#302#10`), the fifth-level bet is that required C can change *inter-site structure*—creating cross-site residual coupling, conditional fiber degradation, heterogeneous key roles, or breaking an **inter-site alternating odd cycle** invisible to either site alone—rather than just shrinking a residual at each site. On the dual-repair side (`#302#18`), the bet is **non-additive dynamical coupling** between graph repair and residual repair: causal chaining inside one update, cross-channel damage invariants, non-monotone borrow-and-repay or catalyst records, and coupled infeasibility witnesses that additive multi-objective scoring misses. Where both seeds meet, the bet is that one required C action (or colour-gauge choice) discharges a graph obstruction at one site and a residual obligation at another so one shared key answers both, beating `#302#10` and `#302#18` stacked.

## Contract and first gates

- Inherit the [parent contract](route-merged-folds-4.md#contract-and-first-gates): A is old-task service, B is successor service, C is required labelled work. Freeze the discrete update, admissible C orders, legal request/arrival supports **per history**, route frequencies, deadlines, horizon, arithmetic, expiry and fallback before selecting C. No C action may alter the request law, relabel a record, insert dummy work, or peek at unobserved future arrivals.
- Freeze every weight-read site (disjoint parameter blocks or distinct existing read interfaces), its precision, and its fixed reconstruction law `g_i(W_i, k)` before C. Never materialize a second weight read on a single-site route, widen weights, add a pairing/route tag, or mutate weights at encode time. Report marginal weight-read fibers per site separately from joint fibers: credit only genuine conditional simplification or cross-site alignment, never disguised single-site fiber refinement or trivial demand collapse (`m_1 = m_2`).
- For dual-repair claims, price every evidence query, witness table, and candidate-evaluation step inside the decision budget, and verify exact post-C graph colouring and exact conditional reconstruction on the unfolded discrete host (including the first state-mutating B step and any later in-horizon read). Report clique number, per-site chromatic numbers `chi(G_1), chi(G_2)`, union chromatic number `chi(G_1 ∪ G_2)`, exact residual width, both decoder table sizes, peak allocated bytes, and the concrete B write admitted by any released byte.
- Score held-out A at entry, after each C batch, at the cut, after each legal B read at every site, and after the write that spends any released capacity; each card adds targeted A probes. Require pinned A/B/C floors and matched service in addition to exact replay. Include a null where no due C order aligns the two sites and a null where no single record serves both obligations.
- **Stacked control:** causal `#302#10` (dual-site shared key without graph steering), causal `#302#18` (single-record graph+residual repair on one site), `#298#03` + `#298#13` run sequentially on separate records at equal compute, static Heegard–Berger / optimal multi-decoder coding on the untrained and post-C weights, budgeted multi-objective (scalarized and Pareto-greedy) scheduling on the same C pool, equal-byte quantization, and a slot reset with scored retention. Give exact static coding the candidate C order too: a tie there credits C selection, not a new codec.

## One exact paper check (not a learner result)

Take five histories `0, 1, 2, 3, 4` and two existing weight-read sites `W1, W2`. At site 1, all five histories fall in one coarse weight fiber and four fixed legal request pairs `{0,1}, {2,3}, {0,4}, {0,3}` are each legal only for their two endpoints. At site 2, `W2` partitions histories into three fibers `{0}, {1,2}, {3,4}`, with demanded contributions disagreeing on `{1,2}` and `{3,4}` so `G2 = {1–2, 3–4}` (2 edges, `chi(G2) = 2`, 1 bit). In table X, site 1 disagrees on `{0,1}, {2,3}, {0,4}` and agrees on `{0,3}`, giving `G1 = {0–1, 2–3, 0–4}` (3 edges, `chi(G1) = 2`, 1 bit); the union `G1 ∪ G2` is the five-cycle `0–1–2–3–4–0` (`chi = 3`, 2 bits for one shared key). In table Y, site 1 swaps only `{0,4}` for `{0,3}` (`G1' = {0–1, 2–3, 0–3}`, still 3 edges, still `chi(G1') = 2`), leaving `G2` untouched. Now `G1' ∪ G2` is the four-cycle `0–1–2–3–0` plus `3–4`, which has bipartition `{0, 2, 4}` / `{1, 3}` (`chi = 2`, 1 bit for one shared key).

Both tables have identical per-site edge counts `(3, 2)`, identical union edge count `5`, clique number `2`, and identical per-site chromatic numbers `(2, 2)`. Neither single-site selector sees an obstruction because both sites already fit in 1 bit alone. These are answer tables, not outcomes of lawful C updates; the check motivates card 04 and says nothing about learner reachability, A retention, decoder overhead, or allocated bytes.

## Idea cards

### 01. Anti-align cross-site residuals when neither weight predicts its own moment (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** When `W1` alone poorly predicts `m1` and `W2` alone poorly predicts `m2`, choose required C so the post-C discrepancy at site 2 becomes a fixed deterministic map (e.g., negated or offset) of the discrepancy at site 1. One stored key `k` then carries the shared residual while `g1(W1, k)` and `g2(W2, k)` apply fixed opposite signs, even though neither site's marginal weight–moment correlation improves.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Heegard–Berger and `#302#10` rely on each site's side information shrinking its own uncertainty or matching a common residual. Here C manufactures *cross-site residual correlation* across disjoint blocks while leaving both marginal weight fibers and single-site conditional entropies unchanged.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a two-block discrete momentum host where each block's weight fiber leaves 2 residual states (4 joint states). Compare C aimed at cross-site anti-alignment against `#298#13` per-site residual shrinking, static Heegard–Berger on untrained weights, and a null where block 1 and block 2 updates decouple.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score held-out A on probes reading block 1 alone, block 2 alone, and both blocks jointly, before C, at the cut, and after each site's first B update.

### 02. Make incomparable weight blocks effectively degraded on the reachable set (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** Disjoint weight blocks `W1, W2` normally induce incomparable partitions of histories, forcing Heegard–Berger overhead. Use required C so that, conditioned on the shared key `k`, the histories colliding under `W1` become a coarsening of those colliding under `W2` on the post-C reachable set—creating lawful degradedness without shrinking either site's marginal fiber count.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Degraded side information (Wyner–Ziv / Heegard–Berger) is a classical property of a *fixed* channel. The candidate operation uses ordinary C to move the reachable state set into a degraded-fiber regime across existing parameter blocks.
- **Cheapest first test:** Build a tiny finite host whose pre-C partitions `W1, W2` cross (requiring 2 joint bits despite 1-bit marginals). Search a due C menu for an order whose post-C conditional partitions nest, dropping the shared key to 1 bit; compare static Heegard–Berger, separate per-site keys, and a fiber-preserving non-nesting null.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test A across every crossing cell of `(W1, W2)` before and after C and after a B write that touches only the finer block.

### 03. Assign heterogeneous semantics to one shared key across sites (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** Freeze `g1` so `k` acts as a fine additive residual correction at site 1 (where `W1` identifies the coarse regime) and freeze `g2` so the *same* bitstring `k` acts as a coarse regime selector at site 2 (where `W2` identifies the fine offset). Choose required C so each history's fine residual at site 1 matches its coarse regime index at site 2.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#10` uses `k` in the same residual role at both sites; polymorphic/overloaded coding for fixed sources assumes fixed roles or a mode tag. Here C aligns two structurally different missing coordinates (fine vs coarse) onto one pre-request bitstring with zero tag bits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct a two-block discrete host with `(regime, offset)` state where `W1` exposes `regime` and `W2` exposes `offset`. Enumerate due C orders that couple `offset` at block 1 to `regime` at block 2; compare separate keys, single-role `#302#10`, and static joint coding on the untrained host.
- **Old-task retention check:** Probe A on both regime-sensitive and offset-sensitive old tasks at both sites after C and after each site consumes the shared key.

### 04. Break an inter-site alternating odd cycle invisible to either read site alone (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** Form the union conditional confusion graph `G(W1) ∪ G(W2)` for one pre-request shared key. Even when `G(W1)` and `G(W2)` are each bipartite (1 bit each), their union can contain an odd cycle alternating between site-1 and site-2 edges. Target required C to delete an edge of one site specifically because it closes that inter-site cycle, dropping the shared key from 2 bits to 1 with no single-site chromatic change.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#07` merges two *ingress history families* without weight reads; `#302#10` shrinks dual-site residuals without targeting inter-site parity cycles. The primitive is a cross-site graph witness that neither site's own confusion graph nor local residual entropy can detect.
- **Cheapest first test:** Use the paper check above as the target structure on a tiny two-block discrete host. Compare union-cycle C selection against per-site `#298#03`, per-site `#298#13`, `#302#10` residual-only steering, and static Heegard–Berger; include a null where the inter-site cycle is untouched by any legal C order.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test A on all five cycle histories at both read sites, including histories on the untouched site `W2` and after the admitted B write.

### 05. Survive a Site-1 B write so the same immutable key still answers Site 2 (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** Route 1 reads `(W1, k)` and executes a real state-mutating B write to block 1 before route 2 reads `(W2, k)`. Choose required C so block 1 lands in a basin where its B write either leaves site 2's demanded moment contribution invariant or shifts it into exact agreement with `g2(W2', k)`, keeping `k` immutable with no key refresh or pre-write snapshot.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#11` keeps a single-site residual valid across its own weight mutation; recursive filtering updates state at runtime. Here C immunizes *site 2's* shared-key decoding against a *disjoint site 1's* B write through downstream/optimizer coupling.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a two-block coupled host (e.g., shared downstream layer or coupled optimizer step) with sequence `C → B1(read W1, k; write) → B2(read W2', k)`. Compare immutable-key C steering against a paid key refresh after `B1`, a saved pre-`B1` snapshot, and `#302#10` evaluated only before `B1`.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score A immediately after `B1`'s write and after `B2`'s read, specifically on histories whose `W2'` values coincide after `B1` despite differing before `B1`.

### 06. Tag-free quorum sharing across three weight sites (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** With three existing read sites `W1, W2, W3` where no legal C prefix aligns all three to one key, use required C to align the cheapest feasible pair (say `W1, W2`) to the shared key `k` while steering the third site `W3` into a weight fiber where its fixed decoder `g3(W3, k)` ignores `k` (self-sufficient fiber). No pairing-selector tag is stored because `W3`'s own lawful weight value tells `g3` that `k` is unnecessary.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Dynamic multi-decoder grouping normally pays a subset-identity header. Using C to land the excluded site in a self-sufficient fiber eliminates the header bit while keeping all three decoders static.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a three-block discrete host where any 2-of-3 pair can share a 1-bit key but all 3 cannot. Compare tag-free quorum C steering against 3 separate keys, a tag-priced dynamic pair selector, and fixed-pair `#302#10` on held-out arrival menus where the feasible pair varies.
- **Old-task retention check:** Probe A on all three blocks—especially the excluded block whose weight moved into a self-sufficient fiber—before C, at the cut, and after B writes on all three sites.

### 07. Onboard a new read site onto an already-frozen live key (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** Key `k` is already live and pinned for site 1 (re-encoding violates the migration cap). When a successor route introduces read site 2, use required C to steer block 2's weights and moments so the *existing* pinned key `k` answers `g2(W2, k)` with zero extra key bytes and zero modification to site 1's key assignment or reads.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#05` extends a precolouring on a single graph; `#302#10` chooses `k` jointly for both sites from scratch. Here `k` is an immutable legacy constraint, and C performs one-sided side-information alignment on the newly onboarded parameter block.
- **Cheapest first test:** Freeze a valid 1-bit key assignment for block 1 on a tiny discrete host, then attach block 2 with a due C menu touching only block 2 (or shared layers). Compare pinned-key C onboarding against paying a second 1-bit key, paid migration/re-encoding of site 1, and random C; include an infeasible pinned assignment as a null.
- **Old-task retention check:** Reissue A reads on site 1 (verifying zero regression on the pinned legacy route) and on site 2 after onboarding and after site 2's first B update.

### 08. Self-certifying dual-site key that flags single-site drift without a checksum bit (P; `#302#10`)
- **Mechanism:** Choose required C so valid `(W1, k)` and `(W2, k)` pairs occupy strict subsets of each decoder's input domain, leaving unused syndrome cells that catch unexpected single-site drift. If an intervening unscheduled update knocks block 1 out of alignment, `g1(W1, k)` detects the invalid syndrome and triggers fallback while `g2(W2, k)` still decodes cleanly from the same `k`.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Error-detecting codes add parity bits; `#302#10` maximizes sharing with no integrity check. Here C carves a *free detection margin* inside the existing fixed weight-read domain so one shared key simultaneously answers two sites and localizes single-site staleness.
- **Cheapest first test:** On a finite two-block host with an injected single-block drift event between C and B, compare syndrome-carving C steering against `#302#10` plus an explicit 1-bit checksum/CRC (at equal total bytes) and against `#302#10` with silent corruption.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score A on clean trajectories (no false fallback) and on drifted trajectories (fallback restores A floor without breaking the intact site 2).

### 09. Causally chained double-duty record: residual collapse deletes the odd-cycle edge (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** Rather than picking one C record that happens to score well on two *independent* obligations, select a required C record whose weight/moment step resolves a within-fiber residual ambiguity that *is itself* the last conditional witness of a blocking odd cycle. Neither a pure graph selector (looking at blind edges) nor a pure residual selector (minimizing average conditional entropy) picks this record because the graph deletion only appears *through* the residual collapse.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Budgeted multi-objective scheduling and `#302#18` treat graph and residual obligations as separate additive targets; `#302#17` hits a conditional witness but does not exploit a single record's dual role across both residual width and odd-cycle parity. The primitive is a coupled causal witness where one repair triggers the other inside one discrete step.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a discrete momentum host where a blocking odd cycle survives in the blind graph but has one edge whose sole remaining conditional witness is a residual ambiguity. Compare causal-chain selection against `#298#03`, `#298#13`, additive multi-objective `#302#18`, and a null where residual collapse leaves a second witness intact.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test A on all odd-cycle histories and on histories sharing the collapsed weight fiber, before C, after the single record, and after the first B step.

### 10. Cross-channel damage-safe repair via a coupled preservation invariant (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** When the best graph-repair record destroys an intact weight–moment relation (adding 1 residual bit) and the best residual-repair record closes a new odd cycle (adding 1 colour bit), enforce a hard coupled invariant: admit a required C record only if it repairs one channel *without* crossing a width threshold on the other. Certify safety with a small cross-channel witness (the intact channel's tightest near-obstruction) rather than a full rebuild.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Additive scalarization in budgeted multi-objective scheduling trades one metric smoothly against another and accepts a net-zero swap that still fails the cut. This primitive enforces discrete cross-channel non-interference between graph parity and residual multiplicity.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct a tiny due C menu containing (a) a graph-fixer that breaks the residual, (b) a residual-fixer that closes an odd cycle, and (c) a slower/less-local record that repairs the tight channel while preserving the intact one. Compare coupled-invariant selection against weighted multi-objective `#302#18`, sequential `#298#03` + `#298#13`, and random eligible records.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score A on both the repaired channel's histories and the preserved channel's histories after C and after the admitted B write.

### 11. Non-monotone borrow-and-repay across two due records (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** When two due C records are available before the cut and no single record satisfies both obligations, deliberately pick record 1 even though it *worsens* residual ambiguity (and makes zero immediate graph progress), because it moves the discrete momentum state into a basin where record 2 simultaneously breaks the odd cycle and collapses the residual below the original width.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Greedy/myopic budgeted multi-objective scheduling and `#302#18` reject strictly dominated first steps. This primitive exploits discrete optimizer non-commutativity to borrow temporary residual width and repay both channels on the second due record.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a 2-step due C menu on a small finite momentum host where record 1 is strictly dominated on `(colours, residual_bits)` yet `(record 1, record 2)` is the unique 2-step prefix that beats the cut. Compare 2-step non-monotone witness search against greedy multi-objective `#302#18`, Pareto-frontier 1-step pruning, and exhaustive 2-step search (pricing search queries).
- **Old-task retention check:** Check A in the *intermediate* state after record 1 (ensuring the temporary residual worsening does not breach mid-window A floors) as well as at the cut and after B.

### 12. Catalyst record that scores zero on both channels yet unlocks a joint repair (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** Identify a due C record `c1` whose own immediate effect on both the odd cycle and the residual ambiguity is `(0, 0)`, yet running `c1` first shifts curvature/momentum so a subsequent due record `c2` (which alone hits neither obligation) jointly clears both. Index `c1` by the *sensitivity* of the coupled witness to `c2`, not by `c1`'s own immediate progress.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Multi-objective scheduling ranks `(0, 0)` records dead last; lookahead planning in general MDPs is expensive. Here a tiny coupled witness (one odd-cycle edge + one residual pair) acts as a cheap local precondition detector for catalyst records.
- **Cheapest first test:** On a quantized two-step momentum host with 4 due records, plant a unique `(c1, c2)` catalyst pair where `c1` and `c2` individually have `(0, 0)` effect. Compare witness-sensitivity catalyst selection against `#302#18` multi-objective ranking, random 2-record prefixes, and full 2-step brute force; include a null with no catalyst.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test A after `c1` (checking that the zero-progress catalyst causes no silent A drift), after `c2`, and after the admitted B write.

### 13. Cross-cut split of one double-duty record across an intervening B write (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** Cut 1 is graph-bottlenecked (odd cycle) and cut 2—occurring after a state-mutating B write—is residual-bottlenecked. Select one pre-cut-1 required C record that breaks cut 1's odd cycle *now* and leaves the post-B-write momentum aligned with cut 2's fixed weight read, discharging cut 2's residual obligation before cut 1 even happens.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#18` solves both obligations before a *single* cut; `#302#19` releases capacity at cut 2 via a parity transition without pre-aligning a residual via a pre-cut-1 double-duty record. Per-cut multi-objective scheduling ignores cut 2 until after cut 1, missing the pre-B leverage.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate a tiny host with `C1 → cut1 → B1(write) → cut2` where no C record is due between `B1` and `cut2`. Compare cross-cut double-duty selection against per-cut `#302#18`, `#298#03` at cut 1 plus paid residual carry at cut 2, and a null where `B1` erases cut 2's alignment.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score A at cut 1, immediately after `B1`'s write, at cut 2, and after spending cut 2's released capacity on a real `B2` write.

### 14. Co-batch non-commuting graph and residual repairs to cancel cross-damage (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** When two due C records `c_g` (fixes the odd cycle, breaks the residual) and `c_r` (fixes the residual, re-closes the odd cycle) fail in *both* sequential orders `(c_g, c_r)` and `(c_r, c_g)`, combine them into a single lawful mini-batch update (at the same total record service and gradient count) whose summed gradient cancels the cross-damage term so both obligations hold at the cut.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `park-crossings` (`#296#11`) co-batches an unsafe A repair with a B crossing to stay inside a park; `#302#18` uses one record, not batch-composition cancellation of two non-commuting discrete fold repairs. Standard multi-objective scheduling chooses record sets, not batch granularity against discrete graph/residual cross-damage.
- **Cheapest first test:** On a small discrete momentum host with batch-size-1 vs batch-size-2 lawful updates, construct `c_g` and `c_r` that fail in both serial orders but pass when co-batched. Compare co-batching against both serial orders, `#302#18` single-record search, GEM projection, and a null where cross-damage adds rather than cancels.
- **Old-task retention check:** Probe A after the co-batched step and after the admitted B write, compared against both serial orders at matched total C records served.

### 15. Certify coupled infeasibility of single-record joint repair to trigger early split (P; `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** Extract a minimal coupled obstruction—one blocking odd cycle plus one conflicting within-fiber residual pair—and test whether the discrete update cones required by the two witnesses are disjoint across the due C menu. If disjoint, immediately certify that no single record can do `#302#18` and pivot to a two-record split or fallback without paying full multi-objective enumeration.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#04` certifies infeasibility of a family of C prefixes for *graph* colouring alone; `#302#21` audits causal menus offline. This is an online *coupled negative witness* separating single-record joint feasibility from forced sequential splits.
- **Cheapest first test:** Build a menu suite mixing single-record-solvable and cone-disjoint instances on a finite host. Measure exact continuation queries and decision bytes for coupled-cone refusal plus early split vs exhaustive `#302#18` enumeration and blind fallback.
- **Old-task retention check:** Verify A on both the early-split path and the fallback path, ensuring early refusal never drops A below the exhaustive selector's floor.

### 16. Single C record breaks an inter-site odd cycle and aligns site 2's residual (P; both seeds)
- **Mechanism:** In a dual-site host sharing one pre-request key `k`, the joint bottleneck consists of an inter-site alternating odd cycle in `G(W1) ∪ G(W2)` *plus* a within-fiber residual ambiguity at site 2. Select one due C record whose update breaks a site-1 edge of the inter-site odd cycle while simultaneously collapsing site 2's residual ambiguity onto site 1's key label, unlocking a 1-bit shared key before a 1-record deadline.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** This is the strict fifth-level interaction of `#302#10` and `#302#18`: `#302#10` alone stalls on the inter-site odd cycle, `#302#18` alone operates on one site and still pays a second key for site 2, and stacking them sequentially misses the 1-record cut.
- **Cheapest first test:** Extend the paper check's two-block discrete host with a 2-way residual ambiguity at site 2 and a 1-record pre-cut budget. Compare the joint selector against `#302#10` alone, `#302#18` on site 1 alone, sequential `#302#18 → #302#10` (2 records), static Heegard–Berger, and a null where no single record hits both.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test A on every history in the inter-site odd cycle and in site 2's ambiguous weight fiber, before C, at the cut, and after B writes on both blocks.

### 17. One C record does a graph repair at Site 1 and a residual repair at Site 2 (P; both seeds)
- **Mechanism:** Site 1's bottleneck is purely a non-clique odd cycle (`G(W1)` is 3-chromatic, residuals trivial) while site 2's bottleneck is purely a within-fiber moment ambiguity (`G(W2)` is bipartite, residual width 2). Choose a single required C record that repairs the graph obstruction at block 1 and the residual ambiguity at block 2 so one shared key answers both disjoint sites.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#18` repairs graph and residual obligations for the *same* reader; budgeted multi-objective scheduling over disjoint blocks misses the *shared-key coupling* that requires the two sites' post-repair labels to agree without an extra tag.
- **Cheapest first test:** Build a two-block host where block 1 has a 5-cycle and block 2 has a within-fiber 3-way moment split, with a 1-record deadline before the cut. Compare cross-site split-type selection against `#302#18` per site, `#302#10` without graph repair, two separate keys, and budgeted multi-objective scheduling ignoring label agreement.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score A separately on block 1's cycle histories and block 2's fiber histories before C, at the cut, and after each block's B step (do not average across blocks).

### 18. Align the post-repair colour gauge at Site 1 to discharge Site 2's residual without touching Site 2 (P; both seeds)
- **Mechanism:** After a required C record breaks an odd cycle in `G(W1)`, the resulting 2-colouring is not unique (disconnected components or Kempe chains can swap colours `0 ↔ 1`). Jointly choose the due C record *and* the colour-gauge assignment on site 1's repaired graph so the resulting 1-bit label coincides with the exact residual bit demanded by site 2's fixed decoder `g2(W2, k)`—satisfying site 2 with zero updates to block 2.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#18` requires the C record itself to alter the residual state; `#302#10` trains both blocks. Here the *discrete symmetry (colour gauge)* unlocked by site 1's graph repair absorbs site 2's residual obligation for free, without mutating block 2's weights or moments.
- **Cheapest first test:** Construct a two-block host where two candidate C records both make `G(W1)` bipartite, but only one induces a component structure whose 2-colouring gauge matches site 2's fixed residual table. Compare gauge-aware selection against `#298#03` (arbitrary 2-colouring), `#302#10` modifying block 2, and `#302#18` single-site repair.

### 19. Migrate a blocking edge from the tight site to a slack site via a single C record (P; both seeds)
- **Mechanism:** Site 1 has an odd cycle (needs 2 bits) while site 2 has slack (1 bit with unused colour-class margin). Select one due C record that shifts the distinguishing witness across the coupled residual from site 1 to site 2—deleting site 1's odd-cycle edge while adding a *harmless* edge between already opposite-coloured histories at site 2. Total edges across both sites stay constant, yet the shared key drops from 2 bits to 1.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#298#04` trades edges within one graph; `#302#10` aligns fixed-graph residuals. Migrating a disagreement witness *across read sites* to exploit asymmetric colour slack has no analogue in single-site graph repair or static Heegard–Berger coding.
- **Cheapest first test:** On a finite two-block host, compare cross-site edge-migrating C against `#298#03` edge deletion at site 1, `#302#10` residual alignment, `#302#18` joint repair, and a null where the migrated edge closes a new odd cycle at site 2.
- **Old-task retention check:** Test A on the endpoints of both the deleted site-1 edge and the newly added site-2 edge, before C, at the cut, and after B writes at both sites.

### 20. Bank site 2's shared-key alignment during site 1's earlier graph-repair cut (P; both seeds)
- **Mechanism:** Site 1's cut arrives after 1 due C record and is blocked by an odd cycle; site 2's cut arrives after a second due C record (with an intervening B step at site 1). Choose record 1 to repair site 1's odd cycle *and* pre-position the state so that after site 1 commits key `k`, record 2 aligns site 2 to that *same* already-issued key `k`, avoiding a second key or migration at cut 2.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** Combines staggered-deadline cross-cut repair (card 13) with pinned-key onboarding (card 07) under a single causal schedule; neither per-cut `#302#18` nor simultaneous `#302#10` plans for a key that is frozen at cut 1 and reused at cut 2.
- **Cheapest first test:** Enumerate `C1 → cut1(issue k for W1) → B1 → C2 → cut2(reuse k for W2)` on a tiny two-block host. Compare banked-alignment selection against myopic `#298#03` at cut 1 + second key at cut 2, `#302#10` forced at cut 1, and a null where no `C1` preserves cut-2 reachability.
- **Old-task retention check:** Score A at cut 1, after `B1`, after `C2`, and after `B2` consumes the reused key `k`, including histories that collide at `W1` but separate at `W2`.

### 21. Refuse dual-site credit when weights leak across blocks or demands collapse (I; `#302#10` + `#302#18`)
- **Mechanism:** Extend `#302#16`'s matched-fiber audit to two read sites: verify (a) neither `W1` nor `W2` gained hidden cross-block history identification via fiber splitting, (b) `m1` and `m2` remain genuinely distinct on the post-C reachable set (ruling out trivial demand collapse), and (c) static Heegard–Berger on fiber-matched weights does not tie the steered arm. Deny dual-site credit if any check fails.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#16` audits one site's weight fibers; Heegard–Berger rate bounds assume fixed sources. This audit catches the two failure modes unique to dual-site steering: cross-block leakage disguised as key sharing, and degenerate `m1 = m2` collapse.
- **Cheapest first test:** Build three synthetic negative controls with matched marginal weight histograms: (1) cross-block fiber permutation that leaks block-2 identity into `W1`, (2) C order that collapses `m1 = m2` by erasing block-2 specialization, and (3) valid dual-site sharing with distinct `m1 != m2` and fixed fibers. Confirm the audit rejects (1) and (2) while passing (3).
- **Old-task retention check:** Require A probes that specifically test block-2 specialization (catching trivial `m1 = m2` collapse even when shared-task A passes).

### 22. Price both decoders, coupled-witness search, and slot padding against a real B write (I; both seeds)
- **Mechanism:** Charge the full fifth-level overhead—both fixed reconstruction tables `g1, g2`, any coupled graph+residual witness cache, decision-time continuation queries, and alignment padding—in one exact CPU byte ledger. Count a fifth-level win only if the net freed bytes admit a predeclared B write before expiry that beats `#298#13` (two tiny site-specific keys) and sequential `#298#03` + `#298#13` at equal compute.
- **Novelty vs nearest prior art:** `#302#22` prices a single-site joint fold. Dual-site shared keys double the fixed-decoder footprint and coupled-repair selectors add search overhead; this kill test checks whether any net real-write capacity survives once both are priced.
- **Cheapest first test:** Run an exact byte-and-query ledger across a packed-slot boundary comparing (a) fifth-level joint cards (04, 16, 17, 18), (b) two independent `#298#13` keys, (c) sequential `#298#03` + `#298#13`, and (d) static Heegard–Berger, including a case where a 1-bit raw key saving is wiped out by the second decoder table or padding.
- **Old-task retention check:** Run the same held-out A suite with and without the admitted B write, plus the expiry/fallback arm when net freed bytes fall short of the B write.
