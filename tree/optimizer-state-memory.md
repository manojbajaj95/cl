# Node: can an optimizer keep a behavioral write alive?

Parent: [PR #106, training-invariant edit content](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/106), especially *optimizer-state completion* and the question of selectively writing a learner's invariant.

**Question:** Can quantities the optimizer itself keeps fixed (momentum invariants or other conserved quantities of the training dynamics) be written so later learning cannot erase their effect on behavior?

## Root cause: the optimizer has to protect the behavior, not just a number

[PR #106](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/106) gives a conditional positive control, not a failed experiment: future inputs restricted to a subspace cannot update its orthogonal complement in a linear SGD learner. An old readout can use that complement only if it sees it. That argument does not supply a writable invariant under ordinary nonlinear training, or under momentum, decay, or a stream that expands the feature span. The failed concrete candidate is **Noether/balancedness memory** in the [PR #6 review](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6).

**First failure: flow conservation is not update conservation.** For a ReLU unit `a ReLU(u·x)` (include the incoming bias in `u` and a constant in `x`), `(u,a) → (cu,a/c)` for `c>0` preserves the function. For a loss depending only on the network outputs, this implies `u·g_u = a g_a` on each batch. Consequently `d = ||u||² − a²` has zero time derivative in Euclidean gradient flow. The exact Euler/SGD step instead gives `d⁺−d = η²(||g_u||²−g_a²)`. Even when every batch preserves the symmetry, nothing forces those second-order terms to cancel over the stream. With momentum, the update uses the accumulated velocity; its inner-product cancellation is not guaranteed at the *current* weights. Coordinate-wise Adam scaling need not preserve the cancellation either. Uniform decoupled weight decay adds a factor `(1−ηλ)²` on `d` in the plain SGD case, even with zero gradient. Ordinary changes in the data do not by themselves break the exact ReLU rescaling symmetry of an output-only loss. They do change the finite-step drift and can break *support-dependent* invariants (e.g., when a later feature leaves the parent's protected subspace); state-dependent masks or losses that see internal activations can also remove the symmetry assumption.

**Second failure: the demonstrated write moves only the gauge.** Rescaling writes `d` while leaving the prediction unchanged on every input. A second rescaling can set `d=0` without changing that prediction either. This is a failure of the particular write/read pair, not a theorem that all conserved quantities are function-invisible. Rescaling can change subsequent learning under a non-equivariant optimizer, but that requires a causal behavioral read through the dynamics, then a test against effective-LR matching; neither follows from extracting a bit from `d`. A decoder that reads the gauge directly changes the inference system and must be counted, then survive an equivalent reparameterization of the full system or admit that it depends on the chosen gauge.

**What the reviewer measured.** In `reviews/pilots/noether_check.py` and its results on [PR #6](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6), a 256-unit ReLU student learns A and then three nonlinear-teacher tasks, 3,000 steps each; the optimizer is recreated per later task. At the largest write, SGD at 0.05 and momentum SGD at 0.01 (`β=0.9`) give 0.516 and 0.504 balancedness-bit accuracy respectively; old-A accuracy is 0.20 in both arms (five classes). At SGD 0.01 both balancedness and the non-conserved incoming-norm channel decode at 1.000, despite old-A accuracy of 0.24 and 0.22 respectively. A function-preserving rebalance sets the balancedness channel to zero in exact arithmetic; residual signs in finite precision are not a decoder. The reported `erase` column reads balancedness after rebalance even in the incoming-norm arm, so it is not an incoming-norm erasure test. This evidence rejects this carrier and protocol, not all continuously carried optimizer state. Slower updates can preserve many statistics while forgetting tasks.

**The parent's momentum invariant has an exact boundary.** With `v⁺ = βv + g`, `w⁺ = w − ηv⁺` and constant `η, β` (`0≤β<1`), the parent's `I = w − ηβv/(1−β)` obeys `I⁺ − I = −ηg/(1−β)`. In a gradient-null coordinate it records where the weight will stop after the velocity decays; it does not fix the weight or its predictions along the way. Even there, zeroing the velocity shifts `I` by `ηβv/(1−β)`. A nonzero gradient on a later batch breaks conservation without needing a reset, and treating decay as part of the gradient is one way to introduce such a term. The buffer does not make a trainable coordinate immune to subsequent gradients.

**Scope of a genuine positive.** Let `z` include parameters, optimizer buffers and any schedule state, and `T_b` be the actual discrete update. Specify which future batches, optimizer transitions and restarts are legal. A native invariant `I(T_b z)=I(z)` must be nonconstant on reachable writes; changing it must serve an old obligation on unseen inputs without an external rewrite. If a legal continuation takes every written state to the same limit, any continuous exact invariant has the same value on all writes (iterate the equality and take the limit). For example, an allowed stream repeating a full-rank quadratic has that property for fixed-step SGD with `0<η<2/λ_max`; stable momentum has the same obstruction when its *augmented* map contracts. Excluding contradictory labels is necessary but does not certify a useful channel under all remaining updates. Conversely, a deliberately modified optimizer can enforce a behavioral invariant by construction; its claim is cheaper preservation at matched plasticity, not discovery of a native conservation law.

### Screen and shared CPU gates

The [ledger, flood triage and top 10 in PR #6](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/6) already rule out naked balancedness, gauge preconditioning without an effective-LR advantage, and Noether-keyed restore when the key can travel with the adapter. Gauge-orbit metadata, winding/holonomy bits and relearning as a read head are already in the pool. Transferring ordinary optimizer state (SM25) overlaps trajectory/optimizer-state transport; fractional momentum and frozen slots are not new primitives. Also distinguish the ideas below from [#106](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/106)'s joint weight/momentum lift, commutation of training and edits, and direct update projection. Novelty claims here are hypotheses against these controls, not a literature clearance.

Use a two-feature linear oracle, then a small ReLU stream with held-out teacher inputs and no task IDs at inference. Measure separately: decodability of any payload, direct old-task behavior without a restore step, and (if offered) recovery cost after a fixed new support budget. Match initial edit success/logit margin, new-task progress and compute; report mean and worst old-task loss at every boundary and worst checkpoint. Count buffer bits, projector/decoder state, support examples, and protected parameter capacity; compare with equal-byte replay or explicit stored intent, and an optimizer tuned to the same new-task progress. For any native-charge claim include finite-step SGD, momentum carried *and* reset, the non-conserved norm control, and function-preserving rebalance with the appropriate state transform declared. Include a fresh held-out support expansion or conflicting-label arm as a boundary test, not as a task the method must magically solve. These are proposed first tests, not results.

## Raw ideas (no self-filtering)

### A. Use statistics the optimizer actually updates

1. **Behavioral sufficient statistics at the fixed point.** *Mechanism:* for a streaming ridge head, pair accumulating Gram and response statistics so compatible later samples change both but keep a target readout of the solved head; seek a much smaller paired accumulator for an old output slice. *Why it might be new:* the conserved object is a behavior of the solve while both sufficient statistics change, not a stored old coefficient; only interesting if the compressed pair beats recursive least squares (RLS) at matched bytes. *First test:* 8-D ridge with compatible and misspecified teachers, sweeping accumulator rank. *Retention:* held-out old strata after each solve, including ones never seen by the new teachers.
2. **Separate gradient mean from gradient covariance.** *Mechanism:* pair write batches so their mean gradient cancels at install but their squared gradients change Adam's denominator selectively; use that change to resist misleading future updates on an old feature. *Why it might be new:* writing through a second-moment channel without an initial parameter effect is distinct from importing moments; kill if a diagonal LR schedule suffices. *First test:* two-coordinate regression with matched weights at the branch point, then match effective step sizes on ordinary batches and swap only second moments. *Retention:* direct old-task error on a held-out stream at fixed new-task loss, including after the moment's predicted lifetime.
3. **Learn from the first disagreement between two memories.** *Mechanism:* retain two low-rank optimizer responses to the same gradients at two time constants; the first sign disagreement triggers a test for old-rule contradiction, not an automatic freeze. *Why it might be new:* a local hypothesis test using disagreement across identical evidence, not a fractional-momentum optimizer; a tuned gradient change detector is the hard null. *First test:* interleave transient label bursts, compatible shifts and real revisions in scalar-plus-nuisance regression. *Retention:* held-out old-rule error during bursts and false restores of superseded labels after revisions.
4. **Task interference as an impulse response.** *Mechanism:* inject one small known moment pulse, observe its time-resolved old-output response, and use the response to choose when *not* to consolidate a new write. *Why it might be new:* predicts delayed forgetting from an intervention on optimizer state rather than ranking initial gradient conflicts. *First test:* a two-mode quadratic with equal immediate changes but different slow tails; compare to static Hessian and gradient-similarity predictors. *Retention:* worst old loss over the full tail after held-out writes, at matched time-to-new-target.
5. **A mount-point test for optimizer-only memory.** *Mechanism:* permute or swap buffers between prediction-identical forks and swap back before the claimed read; identify which state components are necessary during write, storage and read. *Why it might be new:* an experimental separation of buffered content from a past optimization advantage, not optimizer-state transfer as a method. *First test:* plant one buffer-resident bit, one effect already baked into weights and one effective-LR decoy in a linear simulator. *Retention:* old tasks under each intervention and under the same intervention on a bit-free matched control.
6. **Discharge a moment into an ordinary predictor.** *Mechanism:* use a bounded per-feature gate that transfers a pending moment into weights on the first sufficiently informative batch, then zeroes that moment and admits normal training. *Why it might be new:* learning state is temporary escrow with a measurable release condition, not permanent storage or a constantly used adaptive LR. *First test:* delayed-feature regression where early samples cannot distinguish two old hypotheses; compare ordinary momentum, a gradient norm gate and an explicit equal-byte deferred update. *Retention:* held-out old error just before and after release and many batches after the buffer is cleared.

### B. Make an optimizer that protects a behavior by construction

7. **Paired actual updates, not projected proposals.** *Mechanism:* split each step into adaptive descent and a correction chosen *after* momentum and decay, with a discrete chain rule that makes the net change of an old nonlinear contrast exactly zero. *Why it might be new:* the protection is finite-step and accountable after preconditioning; kill if exact projection onto the same level set performs as well at the same cost. *First test:* conserve the product of two predictor coefficients while changing their ratio in a two-output toy. *Retention:* unseen old inputs probing the product, plus unrelated old groups not included in the correction.
8. **Protected overlap instead of protected parameters.** *Mechanism:* co-update a basis and its readout so their overlap matrix on chosen old feature modes is exactly unchanged, while both factors and all parameter locations stay writable. *Why it might be new:* optimizer enforces a bilinear interface rather than freezing the basis; distinguish it from parent basis refresh by requiring a continuous learning path that changes both factors. *First test:* rank-two factorized regression, compare with orthogonal-gradient descent (OGD) on the same modes and a frozen subspace. *Retention:* unseen old mixtures, including near the boundary of the protected span.
9. **Protection as a budget the stream spends.** *Mechanism:* use a nonnegative per-obligation drift allowance and debit a certified upper bound on each accepted step's worst change; batch arrival can spend but cannot silently reset that allowance. *Why it might be new:* offers a stream-level contract instead of an EWC weight or per-step trust region; useful only if the bound avoids consuming the budget on benign traffic. *First test:* enumerate near-threshold steps of tiny logistic regression against static trust region and replay. *Retention:* check budget violations and worst unseen old classification margin, alongside rejected beneficial updates.
10. **A dual variable that can buy back a write.** *Mechanism:* when a new update necessarily degrades an old contrast, record its signed dual debt; a later compatible batch must preferentially pay debt if it provides the right feature span. *Why it might be new:* makes forgetting a reversible, optimizer-enforced obligation across two different data distributions, not immediate projection; compare with standard primal–dual CL using the same anchors. *First test:* a stream with an unavoidable conflicting step followed by a compatible correction direction not present earlier. *Retention:* report peak old loss, debt repayment time, and final old accuracy without explicitly replaying the old examples.
11. **Mutation that preserves a decision region, not a logit.** *Mechanism:* accept any preconditioned step that keeps an old task's decision inequalities valid on a certified input cell; adjust confidence freely inside the cell. *Why it might be new:* exact discrete old decisions under nonzero parameter motion may buy more plasticity than protecting old gradients or logits; constrained margin training is the strong null. *First test:* enumerate vertices of small polytopes in two-feature classification with conflicting calibration but compatible labels. *Retention:* score unseen interior and boundary points, and separate calibration drift from class errors.
12. **One protection rule across different optimizers.** *Mechanism:* permit momentum, reset, or Adam as proposal engines but require each to emit the same verifiable finite-step accounting for a protected output; test whether different engines can exploit different slack. *Why it might be new:* an optimizer-independent behavioral contract, not a recipe for porting the buffers; an ordinary constrained solver with the same warm starts is the hard rival. *First test:* alternate a momentum phase with a sign-preconditioned phase in constrained two-layer linear regression. *Retention:* measure old-task degradation immediately before and after switches and at matched end-of-segment new loss.

### C. Look for categorical or probabilistic protection, not constant moments

13. **Noise-stable basins with a behavioral label.** *Mechanism:* write a predictor into one of two behaviorally distinct basins whose exit barriers differ under the legal minibatch noise, without requiring individual weights to be stationary. *Why it might be new:* the putative storage variable is an old decision stable under measured training-induced escape, not a winding bit or an arbitrary flat minimum. *First test:* small double-well predictor under controlled Gaussian and minibatch noise, versus a matched margin/curvature baseline. *Retention:* old accuracy and survival quantiles through held-out noise schedules, including early escape checkpoints.
14. **Write a safety probability.** *Mechanism:* estimate `p(z) = P_z(hit the compatible region before the failure region)` under a declared stochastic training kernel; on nonterminal states `p` is harmonic, so `p` along the stopped trajectory is a martingale, not a pathwise invariant. Choose writes with high `p` under held-out shifts. *Why it might be new:* a writable lower bound on the probability of surviving a training phase, not conservation of an expected tensor or an edit half-life. *First test:* exact absorbing-chain oracle with equal mean initial outcomes and unequal failure probabilities, against margin and expected loss. *Retention:* calibrate old-task violation rates and measure wrong-terminal behavior; reject a high average that masks a bad old group.
15. **Certify a sticky label through optimizer switches.** *Mechanism:* find a behavior-defined region closed under each allowed update, including a reset and decay, but not under arbitrary task updates; write by entering it. *Why it might be new:* closure of a set of parameters, rather than fixed parameters or a fixed scalar, yields a certificate that tolerates switch order. *First test:* enumerate reachable regions in a quantized two-neuron learner and compare certificates for each optimizer alone to certificates for arbitrary switching. *Retention:* check the old examples used to define the region and unseen old examples; count trajectories the certificate cannot cover.
16. **Watch first-passage failures instead of average drift.** *Mechanism:* allocate reversible writes using estimated probability that an old decision crosses its margin floor even once, with correlated minibatch noise measured from actual update forks. *Why it might be new:* optimizes worst-time service rather than final retention or average stability, potentially distinguishing schedules with identical terminal distributions. *First test:* compare block-correlated and independent gradient noise with matched final variance; benchmark against empirical rollout screening at the same compute. *Retention:* first old-task failure time, fraction of stream spent below threshold, and final accuracy.
17. **Protected coarse state, disposable fine state.** *Mechanism:* search for a low-bit partition of augmented optimizer state whose transitions depend on the coarse label but not on the fine state, and require that label to directly predict an old behavior. *Why it might be new:* a stochastic aggregation specifically for durable, writable optimizer regimes; not a new Nerode quotient or just one-step clustering. *First test:* construct a small grid with an exact partition and a lookalike broken only by a reachable rare batch; test held-out paths. *Retention:* track old-task violations conditional on rare transitions, not just averaged within coarse cells.
18. **Redundant prediction paths with different failure modes.** *Mechanism:* install two old decision paths whose failures under the same new updates are anticorrelated; use an inference-time agreement or confidence rule chosen before training rather than fixing both paths. *Why it might be new:* allocate redundancy by the joint update-failure law, not by sparse corruption assumptions or arbitrary ensemble diversity. *First test:* two small heads on complementary features with planted correlated and anticorrelated interference; compare equal-capacity ensemble, equal-byte replay and shared-head training. *Retention:* evaluate old disagreement cases, common-mode failure under held-out streams, and the cost of abstentions.

### D. Make the boundary of the channel measurable

19. **Optimizer-invariant portability matrix.** *Mechanism:* give each candidate write three distinct claims—same optimizer, legal state reset, optimizer swap—and identify the first operation that loses the old behavioral distinction under matched training progress. *Why it might be new:* a causal portability diagnostic rather than a method for carrying state; it can distinguish robust weight-mediated protection from serialization of a buffer. *First test:* cross reset, LR change and momentum-to-SGD switch in a linear model with a planted protected coordinate and a transient buffer control. *Retention:* old-task performance after every operation, with unchanged predictions at the moment of state reset.
20. **Forecast when the feature span will exhaust protection.** *Mechanism:* track principal angles between the protected old readout and the span of *realized preconditioned steps* as tasks accumulate, and certify failure when overlap is forced by a new feature. *Why it might be new:* a forecast of channel lifetime under update geometry rather than an importance score; kill if the same-budget raw gradient span predicts it just as well. *First test:* rotate a sequence of rank-one feature streams with anisotropic preconditioning, predict the first failing task before training it. *Retention:* test worst-group loss before and after the predicted crossing plus an unseen order of the feature rotations.
21. **Price an optimizer restriction in examples, not weight distance.** *Mechanism:* replace the first legal batch that destroys a candidate invariant with a safe multi-batch schedule, then price its sample overhead to reach the same new-task accuracy. *Why it might be new:* measures when a protected behavior is possible only by changing the curriculum; random rejection and ordinary projected updates are the nulls. *First test:* enumerate short schedules in a linear stream where a single high-value batch also corrupts an old direction. *Retention:* require old performance during the replacement schedule, not just after an offsetting last batch.
22. **Recover an old rule with evidence that cannot teach it.** *Mechanism:* build a support set on which two distinct old rules agree; ask whether a learner can recover the right rule from that same support using only its current learning state. *Why it might be new:* an identifiability gate for optimizer-state recall, not relearning from labels that already tell the learner the answer. *First test:* train the rules `x₁` and `x₂` in separate forks, then offer both the same support with `x₁=x₂`; compare carried and reset buffers at matched fork weights. *Retention:* score zero-update accuracy first, then score unseen examples with `x₁≠x₂` and unrelated old groups at equal numbers of adaptation steps.
23. **Find the precision at which an apparent invariant becomes a reset.** *Mechanism:* use the actual checkpoint rounding and optimizer restart implementation as legal transitions, then search for two behaviorally distinct writes that collide under their composition. *Why it might be new:* isolates erasure due to representation of optimizer state rather than to numerical drift in weights; it can provide a concrete lower-bit failure witness. *First test:* enumerate 4–8 bit momentum coordinates under periodic serialization and compare with full precision at matched writable capacity. *Retention:* measure old-decision loss at the first collision, plus worst loss on unrelated old tasks as precision changes.
24. **Allocate memory by the optimizer's failure certificate.** *Mechanism:* when an exact or empirical continuation witness destroys a behavioral distinction, store only the missing distinguishing constraint in a bounded external memory, then re-test native and stored parts separately. *Why it might be new:* the replay allocation criterion is a demonstrated optimizer failure, not per-sample surprise or estimated edit half-life; the external recovery is explicitly not native conservation. *First test:* stream a 4-D regression with one protected direction, one slowly drifting direction and one immediate collision, against equal-byte reservoir replay and parent-style split by fixed invariant span. *Retention:* measure direct and repaired held-out old loss separately, including untouched tasks and a future unanticipated collision.

## Deeper sub-questions, ranked by chance of a new primitive

1. **Can finite-step updates preserve an old decision region while all parameters remain learnable?** (7–12, 15.) Best bet: a discrete behavioral contract that proposals from different optimizers can satisfy, without requiring constant logits. Start with exact inequalities on a tiny predictor; kill if exact projection or matched replay has the same retention–plasticity frontier, or the practical certificate protects only its anchor examples.
2. **Can behavior live in a reachable, writable basin or survival probability instead of a conserved scalar?** (13–18.) Start with an exactly enumerable training kernel and held-out shifts; a mean martingale is not a guarantee, so require calibrated old-task survival in the tails. Kill if margin, curvature or a same-cost ensemble explains the benefit.
3. **Can optimizer buffers serve as temporary escrow that leaves ordinary predictions intact after the buffer is gone?** (2–6, 19, 22.) The positive is direct retained behavior once the moment has been discharged and deleted, not an intact optimizer checkpoint or a speedup on old support. Demand buffer swaps, effective-LR matching and an explicit deferred-write baseline; check that no improvement is due to suppressing new learning.
4. **Can we locate the first loss of a writable distinction early enough to allocate storage only where dynamics fail?** (1, 19–24.) Start by planting a support expansion and a restart/rounding collision in a linear oracle; demand actionable, prospective witnesses and fewer stored bytes than replay at equal old and new performance. If the best outcome is a correct no-go or a diagnostic, report it as such, not a new memory primitive.
