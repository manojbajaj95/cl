# Deployment feedback 5: Keep the next teaching example informative

Child of [PR #355](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/pull/355) ([parent node](deployment-feedback-4.md)) · Depth 4 · 26 candidate primitives · Ideas only; no experiments run

**Thesis.** A serving checkpoint can look fine while the learner has already lost an old means of finding out it is wrong. Two individually admissible writes can leave a shared Adam accumulator that suppresses the next teaching step. They can also leave a perfectly usable optimizer but make the teaching step reveal the same thing under two still-plausible explanations. These are separate failure modes: a large state change is not proof of either, and a small update is not proof that no better authorized probe exists.

**Scope.** [Flood-triage row 488](https://github.com/manojbajaj95/cl/blob/a6e0e2990624251722e45b0ac556e974918d0a36/reviews/flood-triage.md#L488) kept only D4-12 (latent optimizer-state collision) and D4-20 (joint loss of the next teaching example), neither advanced into the top ten. The second already converges with top-ten #5, relearning as the read head. The target here is not the readout alone: it is how to detect a *pair of writes* that makes a specific legal readout unusable, without excluding unrelated peers.

## Literature check

These are nearest-prior comparisons against the papers' stated mechanisms, not claims of priority.

| Closest line | What the new object needs to do |
|---|---|
| [Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980) carries first and second moments and bias correction. [Continual Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02754) pools past second moments; [Resetting the Optimizer in Deep RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833) tests state resets under shifting training targets. [Continual pretraining with state resets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763) resets at phase boundaries, while [quantization-aware continuation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.694/) explicitly compares retaining optimizer state at transition. | Neither carry nor reset by default. Identify a conditional two-write loss of an authorized diagnostic, beyond ordinary adaptation and retention. If resetting safely dominates, don't store a blocker. |
| [Stored in Optimizer State, Valued by Later Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20442) is a close null: it transports a training perturbation through optimizer state, including weight-matched moment transplants, and finds a first-moment carrier in its setting. [Loss of Plasticity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7) measures diminished ability to learn new tasks. | A state swap by itself is not novel. Ask whether a shared *second* moment, after two admissible writes, selectively disables a particular lawful readout while generic plasticity survives. Attribute effects to the first moment, clock, weights or data if those explain them better. |
| [Decoupled Neural Interfaces](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jaderberg17a.html) predicts downstream gradients so modules can update before receiving backpropagated gradients; [dataset condensation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05929) learns examples by matching gradients. [Iterative Machine Teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10470) chooses examples to teach a target quickly. | A predicted gradient may triage which probes need auditing; a synthetic gradient or example must never count as a real report. The protected object is the causal response difference to an actual authorized teaching opportunity, not update accuracy or rapid acquisition. |
| [Fisher identifiability](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913267) concerns local identification under regularity assumptions, and the [NTK](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07572) describes linearized gradient-training dynamics. [Empirical Fisher limitations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12558) explain why squared observed scores should not be casually equated to Fisher curvature. | Compare current-weight report-likelihood Fisher, unweighted cross-NTK from teaching to readout examples, and a state-conditioned local update map; evaluate all against actual post-teaching report laws. Neither local rank nor a large kernel value proves finite-query diagnostic power. Adam's second moment is not assumed to be a Fisher matrix. |
| [Interleaving and relearning savings](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3tb956hb) compares recovery to initial learning, [binding-specific savings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22381) uses matched never-learned bindings, and [frozen BERT with refit probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01081) tests what remains accessible in representations. [MAML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400) directly trains for fast adaptation. | The first test must beat equal-query relearning, not just accuracy or a frozen probe. Distinguish learned familiarity from evidence about a live competing explanation, and a difficult learning step from loss of the last informative legal experiment. |

**Common first assay.** Use a tiny trainable model and two evidence-compatible worlds for an old comparison, with enumerable lawful teaching actions and reports. Match siblings on starting weights, scores, routes, and the measured singleton effects of C/D where the mechanism permits it. Run no-write, C, D, C→D and D→C. A teaching route is one authorized teaching opportunity, the ordinary update, and a separately authorized readout. Compare the two worlds' laws of the observable transcript with and without that step, at a fixed query budget and false-alarm ceiling. A different parameter delta is not itself a report, and a teaching label that gives away the world before the step is not evidence for an update-dependent readout. If the learner starts in the same full state and the entire authorized signal law is identical, deterministic processing cannot create a distinction; any apparent one is leakage. On a finite toy, compute the best attainable power for each allowed route; a failed candidate alone warrants abstention, not a veto. A joint blocker requires both singletons to pass, their combination to fail on all declared routes under budget, and at least one non-hitting peer to pass. An untested action family stays out of scope.

**Controls inherited by the cards.** For moments, cross weight-matched Adam moment swaps (including first versus second), a full optimizer reset, momentum-free SGD, PCGrad, one-step lookahead, Continual Adam, and generic plasticity measures (matched new-task learning curves). Distinguish a swap on an artificial checkpoint from a collision observed on an ordinary trajectory. Hold step counters, bias corrections, schedules, gradient clipping and the next data and RNG fixed or explicitly factor them out. Compare resets and SGD at matched useful new-task gains, not nominal learning rate. For teaching, compare current-weight Fisher or contrast Jacobian if the world is discrete, cross-NTK, a frozen report graph, ordinary MAML, each write alone, and relearning from matched never-taught and previously taught controls at equal lawful queries. Include a readout-only control matched on action budget and transcript access, an exact actual-update oracle, and the parent D2-02 per-edit veto. For both halves check old per-cohort A/B, unresolved twins, and diagnostic power after both writes and after the protected correction; include EWC or replay at matched retention and new-task service. Match retained bytes, training and verification cost, delay, and false alarms. No tests or experiments were run.

## A · Find joint collisions carried in optimizer state

### D5-01 · Hold the weights still and move only the suspected accumulator
- **Primitive:** Store a minimal set of second-moment coordinates whose transplant from C→D switches the protected teaching route from informative to uninformative at the same weights, first moment and clock. Demand a reverse transplant that rescues it, and check the same loss on ordinary trajectories before registering the blocker.
- **Learning dependence:** The serving function and its current Fisher stay fixed while the update produced by the next authorized example changes; a frozen verifier cannot see the transplant.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Optimizer-state transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20442) already uses surgical swaps. The proposed certificate is bidirectional and pair-conditional, for loss of usable diagnostic power rather than inheritance of a behavioral trait.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two features and one protected report; swap one coordinate, its largest unprotected rival, all moments, and only the first moment. Kill if either singleton also fails or the bidirectional intervention does not replicate across held-out seeds.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure A/B after C/D and after the teaching step in all swap arms, including an unrelated peer at matched service.

### D5-02 · Cache a bound on the next useful contrast, not on the largest moment
- **Primitive:** Retain intervals for the few Adam denominator coordinates that contribute to the readout-weighted teaching step. Bound the remaining coordinates' contribution using a residual norm; send uncertain pairs to exact unrolling rather than vetoing them.
- **Learning dependence:** The bound refers to the product of the report Jacobian and the optimizer's future, example-dependent step, not to current outputs or parameter norms.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980) gives the update; [Adafactor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04235) compresses second-moment estimates for updating. Here compression must preserve a decision about a *contrast* and may decline to decide; it does not replace Adam.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Compare top-moment and top-contrast sketches at equal bytes against exact one-step enumeration. Kill if error intervals remain too wide to save audits or if a locally bounded step is misused as a finite-query certificate.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and both route outcomes after C/D and one step; include a large but irrelevant moment on an old common case.

### D5-03 · Learn the earliest time a protected correction works again
- **Primitive:** Attach to a blocker the earliest verified reopening time under a declared intervening update schedule; compare deferring the second write with delaying the probe. Without knowledge of the actual gradients, a bound that depends on moment decay alone must abstain.
- **Learning dependence:** Reopening depends on accumulation and decay during actual updates, not on wall-clock time or a static route schedule.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Optimizer resets in RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833) address contamination by dropping state. Here a limited wait is proposed as a cheaper alternative to reset or veto, with the same authorized probe and deadline.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Try one quiet gradient stream and one matched-gain stream that replenishes the protected accumulator. Compare a fixed cooldown, reset and exact scheduled unrolling. Kill if the observed window cannot beat a fixed delay at matched admitted service.
- **Old-task retention:** Track A/B and the unresolved pair during the wait, then test recovery and the unrelated cohort after the correction.

### D5-04 · Detect suppression caused by sign-canceling writers
- **Primitive:** Flag pairs whose gradients counteract in the signed numerator yet reinforce in the squared accumulator along the teaching direction. Require the joint trace to explain a loss not predicted by either singleton or their summed parameter delta.
- **Learning dependence:** The hazard comes from separate first- and second-moment histories even when C/D nearly cancel in weights; a score- or delta-matched export throws these histories away.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [PCGrad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782) removes conflicting gradient components; [Continual Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02754) reuses second moments. Neither objective protects this particular downstream teaching response in a canceling pair.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match final weights and net gradient in two one-dimensional synthetic sequences; only one loads the denominator. Compare PCGrad, gradient variance and exact Adam step. Kill if matching or swapping the first moment explains the failure instead.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B, one protected and one orthogonal teaching route after the pair and after the same correction.

### D5-05 · Reset only the state necessary to recover the old diagnostic
- **Primitive:** Before vetoing a pair, test replacing the implicated second-moment slice with its pre-pair value while retaining weights and other state. Commit a repair only if the same legal next example recovers and a collateral-update audit passes.
- **Learning dependence:** Selective replacement changes Adam's response without changing serving behavior; a fixed verifier cannot identify which accumulated gradient history can be discarded.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Resetting the Optimizer in Deep RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833) resets optimizer state on iteration boundaries. Here a pair-specific state repair must preserve the adaptation that a whole reset would lose; no resetting rule wins by definition.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use two coordinates with opposite reset needs; compare full reset, Continual Adam and random partial reset, preserving or explicitly compensating the step clock. Kill if full reset is equally safe and cheap or slice replacement induces spikes later.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B after the intervention, the protected step, and the first unrelated future correction, not merely at the unchanged checkpoint.

### D5-06 · Admit the safe order rather than veto the unordered pair
- **Primitive:** Store a directed blocker only when the two orderings leave distinguishably different second-moment states and swapping just those states transfers the teaching failure. Permit the better order if causal and service constraints allow it.
- **Learning dependence:** Moment decay gives the two training requests different weights in the recurrence; an unordered composition of identical static singleton effects lacks this difference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [First-order meta-learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02999) analyzes cross-batch gradient effects, and [invariant confluence](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p185-bailis.pdf) tests safety of independent merges. Here the predicted reversal is specifically mediated by optimizer state rather than the order of final model deltas.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use two matched-effect sequences with a high protected-coordinate gradient early versus late. Compare noncommuting weight-only SGD, first-moment swaps and exact replay of both orders. Kill if the same safe order is obvious without moments.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure A/B and teaching power after each order, not just the preferred one; check ordinary service latency from ordering.

### D5-07 · Budget denominator load per teaching route, not per writer
- **Primitive:** Account for how much each incoming update raises the next-step denominator in a protected route's supporting coordinates. If another legal teaching route has redundant support, do not veto until the pair uses up both margins.
- **Learning dependence:** The margin is the effect of accumulated squared gradients on achievable future learning, not a count of static routes or the parameters assigned to a writer.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adafactor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04235) tracks adaptive scale, and [SafeMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04753) maintains access in a fixed unknown MDP. The object is a budget shared through training state across ostensibly different probes.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Two legal probes supported by disjoint or overlapping features; compare per-edit budgets, exact joint oracle and a single global norm cap. Reject if a simple static overlap threshold makes the same admissions.
- **Old-task retention:** Test A/B and the power of each remaining probe after C/D; verify that nominal redundancy was not just duplicated access to one suppressed feature.

### D5-08 · Preserve scale differences when global learning-rate rescue is misleading
- **Primitive:** After a suspected collision, find whether any allowed scalar step-size adjustment restores separation without overstepping a different old contrast. Store the two conflicting bounds only if a targeted moment change restores compatibility.
- **Learning dependence:** Opposing constraints concern responses to the same gradient under a history-dependent diagonal preconditioner, not two static output margins.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Rewarming continual pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04014) studies restoring learning rates after distribution shifts; [natural gradient](https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article-abstract/10/2/251/6143/Natural-Gradient-Works-Efficiently-in-Learning) changes the geometry of steps. The blocker tests a route-specific incompatibility under a scalar rescue, not whether adaptive rates generally improve learning.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Sweep safe learning rates in a two-feature toy with one suppressed probe and one fragile old decision. Compare scalar rewarming, a local second-moment intervention and exact finite-step grid search. Reject if any scalar schedule rescues both within budget.
- **Old-task retention:** Require old A/B on each side of the safe learning-rate bound and preserved power on the newly recovered route.

### D5-09 · Detect a hidden collision when optimizer state is quantized or factored
- **Primitive:** If one writer changes the state scale or factor statistics shared with another, forecast how the *stored* state will change the protected step; compare to the same history in full precision. Separate blocking the pair under this storage format from claiming it is intrinsically unsafe.
- **Learning dependence:** Quantization or factorization of the evolving preconditioner can couple separate gradient histories even at matched exported weights.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adafactor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04235) reconstructs second moments from matrix marginals; [8-bit optimizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02861) quantize optimizer statistics blockwise. The target is selective loss of a particular lawful teaching route, not average optimizer fidelity.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** In one small matrix, load different coordinates of a state block with C and D; compare matched-precision routes, changing block boundaries, and high precision for the implicated block alone. Kill if the same loss appears with exact state or scales alone flag too many peers.
- **Old-task retention:** Compare per-cohort A/B and the protected step at each precision, charging any extra bytes needed for a rescue.

### D5-10 · Pair moments only in a shared feature's actual update coordinates
- **Primitive:** Before carrying moment state across a learned feature remapping, test whether the protected teaching direction still uses the carried coordinates. Represent the joint blocker by the mismatch between the state left by C and the feature-aligned update learned by D.
- **Learning dependence:** The collision depends on sequential feature reuse and the coordinate dependence of a diagonal accumulator. Two fixed functions related by feature relabeling do not determine what optimizer state accompanied the relabeling.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Model stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) tests compatibility of representations; [Continual Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02754) studies carrying state between tasks. The hypothesis is a specific loss from misaligned transport, not generic representation drift.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Start with exact channel permutations so the correct moment transport is known, then try learned alignment only where validated. Compare carrying both moments, properly permuting them, and resetting. Kill if a weight-only feature-alignment diagnostic ties it.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and the matched report law before remapping and after the protected update; track a feature reused by an unrelated peer.

### D5-11 · Locate the first harmful moment deposit in a batched stream
- **Primitive:** Keep a short provenance trace of cumulative second-moment increments on protected directions. Identify the least subset of microbatches whose removal restores diagnosis under an actual replay, and offer the remaining parts of C/D rather than rejecting both entire writes.
- **Learning dependence:** Removal changes gradients on future minibatches. An additive trace is only a search heuristic; the repair must survive genuine retraining and cannot be obtained from a static log of edit effects.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [TracIn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08484) attributes predictions to training examples; [optimizer-state transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20442) allocates delayed effects across trainer state. The proposed primitive makes a minimal, legally editable portion of a pair admissible, scoped to a future diagnostic.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Enumerate deletions from tiny C/D batches; match training compute against influence ranking, largest gradient norm and random subset deletion. Reject if the trace cannot find a correct subset faster than enumeration or the deletion sacrifices new-task gain.
- **Old-task retention:** Test A/B and the actual teaching step after partial admission, including a held-out old cohort absent from the trace.

### D5-12 · Tell a selective collision from a generally untrainable checkpoint
- **Primitive:** Pair the protected route with an equally hard, authorized control update using similar gradient magnitudes but disjoint support. Store a blocker only if the readout loss follows the implicated state while the control still learns normally.
- **Learning dependence:** Selectivity is measured in two responses to ordinary training, not in the static checkpoint's feature rank or current test scores.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Loss of Plasticity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7) measures a learner's ability to learn a new task; [binding-specific relearning savings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22381) controls for generic familiarity. The pair-specific claim requires a selective moment-mediated failure with generic plasticity held fixed.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Cross diagnostically targeted moment contamination with an unrelated loss of plasticity, matching visible score and average learning progress. Reject if random fresh-task probes or the reset alone identify every pair at the same cost.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B and new service, then compare post-correction power for the protected old cohort and the matched control cohort.

## B · Detect when two writes destroy the next teaching example

### D5-13 · Compare teaching to simply reading the very same example
- **Primitive:** Keep an update-dependent route only if its increase in best achievable power over reading the same label and making a matched follow-up inquiry disappears specifically after both writes. Measure the loss through an update/no-update intervention, not by comparing different feedback examples.
- **Learning dependence:** The update changes the model's next query or the representation used for the next report; a frozen graph omits the update-dependent observation. If observations are just a deterministic recoding of the same transcript, the card must fail.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Iterative Machine Teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10470) optimizes training inputs for rapid acquisition; [experimental information](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177728069) compares allowable experiments. The object is the *incremental* diagnostic value of turning a real example into a training action.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use a shared nondiagnostic teaching label that changes how the learner elicits the follow-up report. Enumerate all budget-matched label-only and static policy alternatives. Kill if any frozen policy has the same power across the pair split.
- **Old-task retention:** Check old A/B and the still-open twin with both protocols; neither gets free access to an extra user query.

### D5-14 · Gate an audit with a synthetic gradient trained to flag collisions
- **Primitive:** Train a DNI-style side head to predict each world's backpropagated teaching gradient from partial network state. Run the predicted gradients through the actual optimizer state on shadow copies to estimate the post-update contrast. Send uncertain or out-of-family pairs to the exact oracle; no synthetic update licenses a veto by itself.
- **Learning dependence:** Inputs and targets include how backpropagated teaching responses change after C/D, which a frozen report graph does not expose.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [DNI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jaderberg17a.html) predicts gradients as substitutes for backpropagation, and [BADGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03671) selects samples from approximate gradient embeddings. The head predicts whether to *spend a real diagnostic audit*, not a gradient to execute.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Hold out a peer family on a two-layer learner; compare true-gradient linear lookahead, random audits and direct post-update contrast prediction for a fixed audit budget. Kill if triage only works by giving familiar peers more audits or silently increasing false admissions.
- **Old-task retention:** Verify A/B and actual held-out report power for accepted and audited pairs; report the worst old cohort, not just the mean.

### D5-15 · Expose the teaching-to-readout direction hidden by full local rank
- **Primitive:** Compare current-weight Fisher/Jacobian to the cross-response from the legal teaching gradient to the allowed readout. Flag a pair if the latter loses the distinguishing direction although both local objects remain individually rich.
- **Learning dependence:** Coupling is mediated by learned sensitivities between the current teaching examples and a distinct set of downstream readout examples, not the rank of a fixed report menu.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [NTK](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07572) connects training and test Jacobians; [Fisher identifiability](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913267) gives a local statistical criterion. A cross-kernel computation is not new; test whether it predicts a *joint loss of legal finite-step diagnosis* beyond using either local object alone.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Make equal-rank Jacobians whose teaching–readout alignment rotates under C/D; compare Fisher rank, cross-NTK at the current checkpoint, and full update then observe. Retire the proposed blocker summary if the ordinary cross-NTK already gives equally selective admissions.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and post-step power for the rotated direction and an orthogonal unaffected old comparison.

### D5-16 · Separate a lost learning direction from saturation of its readout
- **Primitive:** At matched pre-update reports, measure the induced activation change and the actual report law. If C/D leaves the distinguishing activation change intact but the ordinary step pushes it through a saturating readout, classify it as a readout failure and check lawful alternatives before blocking.
- **Learning dependence:** The interaction is between a real supervised step and the output head on the newly reached parameter path. A frozen verifier can flag a saturated current output but not this post-teaching collision.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [NTK](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07572) is a linearized description; [optimizer reset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833) changes later steps. Neither a healthy tangent nor a moment reset guarantees that an actual finite step remains observable through the reporting interface.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Cross a nonsaturating and saturating learned decoder, matching initial Jacobians and steps. Compare forward exact unrolling, temperature calibration, and an authorized alternate readout. Kill if a fixed postprocessing explanation from current scores suffices.
- **Old-task retention:** After both writes verify A/B and the downstream report at the actual step size, not just a stronger unlicensed readout.

### D5-17 · Protect a report difference only when it generalizes past the teaching template
- **Primitive:** Use a joint blocker when a writer pair changes a teaching example into a memorization event: its own training score still improves and its gradients differ across worlds, but the legal held-out report loses the binding-specific distinction.
- **Learning dependence:** The failure is a loss of transfer from the learned update to distinct queries. A frozen service verifier can see neither the new fit nor the change in its generalization.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Binding-specific savings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22381) uses counterbalanced learning histories and exposure controls; [BERT probe refitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01081) assesses accessible prior knowledge. The object is the loss induced *jointly* by two otherwise useful writes, not whether a binding was ever stored.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match entity, slot, value and template exposure; counterbalance which old binding was correctly taught and hold out report templates. Compare same-template savings and equal-query generic relearning. Kill if the savings difference persists equally on never-taught controls.
- **Old-task retention:** Report A/B on held-out aliases after C/D and verify that closure does not transfer to an unqueried binding.

### D5-18 · Replace a ruined teaching example with an equally lawful neighbor
- **Primitive:** When C/D erases one teaching response, search the authorized menu for an example whose update reaches the missing observable contrast. Store which learner-induced route failed and which validated substitute prevents the whole edit pair from needing a veto.
- **Learning dependence:** Neighbor selection is by transfer of a genuine update to a distinct report; lexically similar or statically adjacent actions can send gradients in opposite directions.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Iterative Machine Teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10470) chooses inputs that speed convergence; [active diagnosis](https://home.lmf.cnrs.fr/downloads/SergeHaddad/A15-CDC.pdf) chooses experiments on declared dynamics. The hypothesis is reusable selection across changing learned update maps at matched user burden.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Hold static availability constant; use two equally cheap legal examples, one knocked out only by the pair. Compare unweighted cross-NTK, nearest-template retrieval and exact all-probe unrolling. Reject if existing teaching selection finds the same replacements at equal compute.
- **Old-task retention:** Audit old A/B and check that switching examples resolves only the originally authorized old comparison.

### D5-19 · Test power lost in the tails when mean teaching responses agree
- **Primitive:** Retain a short paired distribution of report changes over ordinary training randomness, then test whether the pair makes informative trials too scarce at the error budget even when the mean response is unchanged.
- **Learning dependence:** Random training masks, batch order or other permitted randomness helps determine whether the teaching example affects the readout. A fixed output law before learning and an expected-gradient map can both miss this.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Large-batch gradient noise](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06162) studies the effect of stochastic-gradient variance; [Dataset Cartography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10795) measures confidence variability during training. Here the distribution drives a matched-false-alarm diagnosis decision, not general example difficulty.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Enumerate masks in a two-feature toy trained with ordinary dropout; create equal expected logit changes but different observable power. Compare mean step, variance alone and the exact discrete transcript law. Kill if a cheap moment bound ties the full histogram.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B and old-cohort power over the real randomness law, not selected seeds, including a non-hitting peer.

### D5-20 · Protect information in joint readouts that marginal probes miss
- **Primitive:** Given an authorized post-teaching report with two fields, detect a loss of the worlds' dependence structure even if both marginal report laws survive. Require that dependence to appear after a real update and to disappear after the writer pair, not after either singleton.
- **Learning dependence:** A shared learned update coordinates how two outputs respond to the same example. A graph that stores only each pre-update field separately cannot see new class-conditional coupling.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Experimental information](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177728069) applies to joint laws, and [stateful performative prediction](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/brown22a.html) models response to predictions. Evaluating joint power is old; the hypothesis is a predictable loss from jointly trained response coupling.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Use a binary two-field report with matched marginals and a shared hidden training feature; compare full static joint score, update/no-update and exact joint likelihood. Kill if pre-update joint reports or label-only access recover the same distinction.
- **Old-task retention:** Check both approved fields together, A/B per cohort, and the privacy/scope budget against any alternative requesting two reports.

### D5-21 · Preserve teaching distinctions only outside the directions needed for current service
- **Primitive:** Test whether C/D makes the one still-distinguishing teaching response effective only along directions that break old service. Store the joint incompatibility; avoid declaring failure when an ordinary permitted update policy retains both.
- **Learning dependence:** The tradeoff is between reachable training displacements and learned old-task sensitivities; static report and accuracy values do not determine it.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [OGD](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/farajtabar20a.html) projects gradients to preserve earlier outputs; [PCGrad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782) projects interfering gradients. The candidate protects *future information* with old A/B as a constraint, rather than keeping all previous output gradients unchanged.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Create two jointly adapted checkpoints with identical old scores and raw probe power but different power after imposing a matched retention limit. Compare OGD, replay and exact constrained one-step unrolling; kill if standard OGD recovers all legal diagnostics.
- **Old-task retention:** Score A/B and information after the protected update; rule out any apparent recovery purchased by sacrificing a small old cohort.

### D5-22 · Calibrate a probe against the wrong lesson, not just no lesson
- **Primitive:** Use a matched incompatible teaching history to test whether the real probe is diagnosing a particular old explanation or merely displacing whichever answer was most recently reinforced. Block a pair only if it destroys the binding-specific response with competitor effects matched.
- **Learning dependence:** Two matched displayed answers can have different learned competition after a new example. The control measures their subsequent update rather than guessing memory from current preference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Binding-specific savings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22381) includes a wrong-binding history and notes its competitor interference. The additional object is a *pair-conditioned change* in specific savings under fixed current output and a fixed legal teaching action.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Cross correct, matched wrong, and never-taught histories with C, D and C/D. Use balanced teacher labels and a disjoint readout. Kill if generic relearning or interference from the wrong history explains the complete effect.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B after the pair and require a confirmed report to settle at most the taught scope, not the competing explanation for an independent scope.

### D5-23 · Audit a diagnostic MAML does not optimize
- **Primitive:** Hold immediate adaptation gain fixed and find pairs that collapse world-specific differences in the next update. Select or reject meta-initializations by this joint failure rate, without encoding the world ID in the initialization or treating it as evidence.
- **Learning dependence:** Meta-initializations can yield equal immediate task losses but distinct learned responses on a separate lawful probe. A verifier inspecting only the few-shot loss or initial policy misses the difference.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [MAML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400) targets post-adaptation loss; [Meta-Experience Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11910) favors future gradient transfer. Fast or noninterfering adaptation is the control, not the contribution.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match ordinary MAML and a non-meta learner on one-example task gain; use counterbalanced world identities and hold out peer pairs. Kill if protecting the reaction adds nothing at equal query count or if all gain can be explained by higher generic adaptability.
- **Old-task retention:** Check post-C/D A/B and per-cohort power after the identical authorized example, including held-out writers and worlds.

### D5-24 · Preserve the direction of the answer, not just detect change
- **Primitive:** Distinguish a probe rendered uninformative from one whose two-world response polarity flips after C/D. Salvage the latter only if an independent lawful calibration certifies the new interpretation within the original query budget.
- **Learning dependence:** The sign can reverse when a learned readout feature and the teaching-step transfer relation change while current report laws stay fixed. A static report label cannot tell whether the direction of response remains valid.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Model stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07682) tests representational compatibility; [optimizer-state transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20442) shows continuations can value trainer-state perturbations differently. The primitive distinguishes real destruction from a scope-certified recoding of a learning-mediated experiment.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Match pre-update logits; engineer one pair that flips the sign of transfer and another that yields zero separation. Compare calibrated static report decoding, absolute change thresholds, and exact signed post-step likelihood; kill if the sign calibration costs more than fresh diagnosis.
- **Old-task retention:** Check that recalibration neither inverts untouched A/B outcomes nor causes false closure for an unrelated open twin.

## C · Decide whether to repair state, replace the lesson, or block

### D5-25 · Route the same failure to the least expensive causal repair
- **Primitive:** Build a small table of crossed second-moment interventions and authorized teaching substitutes. A pair needing neither is admitted; a pair rescued by one is offered that repair; charge both costs when only the combination restores power. Record a blocker only when all admissible options in scope fail.
- **Learning dependence:** One intervention changes the historical preconditioner, the other changes the gradient that runs through it. Their effects need not add; a static diagnosis graph given only singleton effects cannot select which rescue works.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Optimizer reset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833) and [Iterative Machine Teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10470) optimize different pieces. The test is whether causal attribution reduces actual audits or queries relative to enumerating reset–example combinations.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** Construct one moment-only, one lesson-only, one mixed, and one irreparable pair at matched current diagnostics. Compare full reset then any example, equal-query relearning, and exact enumeration. Kill if either repair alone or a fixed repair order is just as good.
- **Old-task retention:** Check A/B, the protected report and the untouched twin after the actual selected repair and update in every cell.

### D5-26 · Certify which teaching responses survive a state compression
- **Primitive:** Store a small state-conditioned sketch of the next-step responses on a declared legal teaching basis. For two checkpoints whose sketches match, reuse a diagnosis decision only if the unrepresented response is bounded tightly enough; seek a same-sketch counterexample and abstain outside the basis.
- **Learning dependence:** The sketch compresses the composition of learned teaching gradients, optimizer state and the post-update readout, not the current feature rows or reports alone.
- **Nearest prior / gap:** [Adafactor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04235) compresses the update state and [NTK](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07572) approximates local linearized training response. Here a compression is judged by the pair decisions and real teaching power it preserves, not global update reconstruction.
- **Cheapest first test / control:** With two legal teaching directions and a held-out third, compare equal-byte moment-only, NTK-only and actual-response sketches against exact replay from the full trainer state. Kill if a same-sketch miss inside the declared scope falls outside the bound, or if verification costs erase savings.
- **Old-task retention:** Measure A/B and report power after both writes and the protected step; count out-of-family abstentions separately from failures or safe admissions.

## Ranked deeper questions

1. **Can two writes destroy a lawful teaching route through the second moment alone, before their weights reveal the collision? (D5-01, D5-04, D5-06, D5-12)** Match frozen routes, singleton effects, and immediate gains. Seek a joint-only failure on ordinary trajectories, then transplant second moments at matched weights, clock, first moments and next gradient. Try to disprove the mechanism with first-moment swaps, weight-only dynamics, reset, step-size matching and generic plasticity probes. Require selective A/B retention and loss of report power only in the protected correction after both writes.
2. **Does the authorized update create diagnostic value over the best legal no-update protocol? (D5-13, D5-15, D5-17, D5-22)** Compare exact finite-query transcript power to matched static reports, teacher-label access, Fisher/cross-NTK and equal-query relearning. The pair must specifically remove a distinction surviving after each writer alone. MAML and generic training familiarity are strong nulls; a real observation channel is mandatory.
3. **What is the cheapest representation that predicts a collision without replaying the whole learner? (D5-02, D5-07, D5-14, D5-26)** Match memory, query and audit budgets. Compare moment sketches with DNI-style triage and true local state-conditioned response; use a same-summary counterexample and report abstentions and missed pairs. An exact update-and-observe oracle supplies the decisions, not a performance target at equal approximation error.
4. **When should the learner repair the optimizer, switch examples, or veto the pair? (D5-05, D5-08, D5-18, D5-25)** Cross the interventions against exact search and independent vetoes. At equal user burden, allow a repair only if it preserves the protected response *and* old service after the next update. If a plain reset, lawful substitute or fixed ordering dominates, discard the more complex blocker.
